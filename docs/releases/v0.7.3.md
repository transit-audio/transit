# transit v0.7.3 (in progress)

This is a patch release (in progress). transit is alpha-quality software and the session format may still change, so back up anything you care about.

v0.7.2 ([v0.7.2 notes](v0.7.2.md)) fixed recording, saving and plug-in hosting. This one continues with fixes found after it.

⚠️ **Changed behavior** (details under Fixes): a stereo audio track whose input is a mono input records and hears that one channel on both sides; it used to take that channel and the next one. This includes a track made stereo from a mono track on a named mono path.

⚠️ **Changed behavior** (details under Fixes): MIDI file export leaves muted clips out.

⚠️ **Changed behavior** (details under Highlights): a MIDI take with notes played during the count-in now covers the whole count-in, so it overlaps an earlier clip on its track more often and merges into it or starts a take stack. Event > Time Operations > Move Song Start is gone.

⚠️ **Changed behavior** (details under Fixes): when the audio device runs at a rate other than the session's, the output and the live input are now converted by a band-limited converter. It sounds different (no more dull treble or aliasing), and its look-ahead makes live monitoring about 0.75 ms later (live MIDI into a software instrument about 0.36 ms) and adds about 0.75 ms to the round trip at 44.1 against 48 kHz: measure hardware inserts and External Instruments again at such a rate. A device at the session's rate is unchanged.

⚠️ **Older builds and sessions with a pre-count zone**: a session recorded with count-in notes from bar 1 has a clip before bar 1. A transit build from before this release cannot reach that part: it plays from bar 1, and moving the clip there puts it on bar 1 and shifts its notes later. Builds since v0.3.0 warn when they open such a session (it lists the `pre_count` need); keep it to this release or later.

## Highlights

- **Count-in pickups are kept from the start of the session.** Notes you play during the record count-in are kept where you played them, also when recording from bar 1 at the very start of a session. The take's clip then covers the whole count-in, so it starts before bar 1, in a pre-count zone whose bars count down (0, -1, ...); Return to Zero and Play from the start play the pickups, and a bounce or MIDI file export starts there. One rule everywhere: later in the session the clip also covers the whole count-in, where it used to start at the first pickup. Nothing is moved onto the beat. Controller moves during the count-in are kept too (hidden before the clip's start when no note was played), recording works from a cursor, a pre-roll or a cycle range in the zone, and a note played before the count-in started is said on the status line. Audio clips still start at bar 1: an audio take that starts in the zone keeps that audio in its file, before the clip's start, and the status line says so. The status line's "count-in notes before the session start were left out" is gone with the limit it reported (#441, #440).
- ⚠️ **Move Song Start is retired.** Event > Time Operations > Move Song Start and the `event.move_song_start` command are gone: it moved only tick-based material, parting MIDI from audio, and the pre-count zone replaces its use for room before bar 1. The Time Operations window's reply no longer carries `song_start` (#441).

## Fixes

- ⚠️ **Rate conversion between the session and the audio device is band-limited.** When the device runs at another rate than the session (it does not offer the session's rate, or another rate was saved for it in Audio Devices), the output and the live input (what you monitor, and an External Instrument's return, which a real-time render prints) went through linear interpolation, which dulled the treble (44.1 kHz on a 48 kHz device: -1.5 dB at 10 kHz, -6.3 dB at 20 kHz) and folded content above the device's limit back (96 kHz on a 48 kHz device at full level). Both now use the windowed-sinc reader built for the audio clip editor: its error stays below -73 dB of the signal up to 0.42 of the lower rate (18.5 kHz when either rate is 44.1 kHz, 20.2 kHz at 48 kHz), and content that would land above 0.58 of the device's rate is at least 73.5 dB down (78 dB when converting down by a ratio of 1.5 or more); measured at every common pair of 44.1, 48, 88.2 and 96 kHz, tones stay within 0.0003 dB with everything else at least 89 dB down, and a 30 kHz tone converted down folds back at least 82.9 dB down. Above 0.42 of the rate is a transition band (20 kHz from 44.1 kHz on a 48 kHz device reads -0.38 dB). The converter looks ahead about 16 frames each way. Playback, the timing of MIDI sent out to other gear and where a take lands do not move. What you play live through transit at such a rate is heard a little later: a monitored input by about 0.75 ms at 44.1 against 48 kHz (0.35 to 0.40 ms of look-ahead on the input, plus the output rendering 16 session frames further ahead, 0.33 to 0.36 ms; 16 times the rate ratio when the session runs above the device), and notes played into a software instrument by the output's part alone, about 0.36 ms. The round trip of a hardware insert or an External Instrument grows by the same 0.75 ms, so measure them again at such a rate. A device at the session's rate passes every sample through untouched, as before (#601).
- System Audio recordings keep their treble steady. The System Audio input (Windows) converts the captured output's rate to the input device's while it follows the drift between the two clocks, and it did that by linear interpolation at a position that never sits on a whole sample, so the treble swelled and dipped (at 48 kHz, up to 12 dB at 20 kHz and 5 dB at 15 kHz, about 2.4 times a second at a 50 ppm drift) and the result was recorded. It now uses the band-limited converter: a 15 kHz tone keeps its level within 0.001 dB in every 100 ms at drifts of 0, 50 and 1,000 ppm, a change of the drift trim never moves the read position (no click), and at the same rates with a drift trim of exactly zero every sample passes through untouched (on two real clocks the trim is rarely exactly zero, so the converter runs). The latency Audio Devices reports for System Audio is unchanged: the converter's look-ahead sits inside the ring's 30 ms margin (#603).
- Linux, JACK system: the input device picked in Audio > Audio Devices is the one that opens. transit used to take the input from the output's device whatever was picked, and said the picked input was not available. If the output device has no inputs and none is picked, the first device that can record is used (#682).
- A stereo audio track can take a mono input. On a device with one input channel its input menu used to list nothing; now a stereo track's input menu lists the stereo inputs, then the mono ones, and a mono input is recorded and heard on both sides. A stereo track with no input set reads "In 1" on such a device, which is what it records. Make Stereo on a mono track whose input has no stereo pair (the one input of a one-input device, or the last input of an odd count) keeps that input on both sides instead of refusing; a numbered input with a pair still becomes the pair ("In 3" becomes "In 3-4"), and a named input path is kept. Tracks wider than stereo and FX channels are unchanged. Changed behavior: a stereo track whose input is a mono input, including a track made stereo from a mono track on a named mono path, now records that one channel on both sides where it used to take that channel and the next (#681).
- Audio Devices > I/O: a device whose saved I/O table had no inputs (saved while its input did not open) gets the default input paths ("In 1" and so on) when it opens with inputs. Paths you made stay, and a default you removed stays removed (#690).
- Baking a looped MIDI clip no longer moves notes that sit before the loop start onto the clip's first tick: they stay where they are, as playback plays them (#441).
- Dragging several clips toward the start now previews every clip stopping together, where the drop has landed since v0.7.1 (#441).
- ⚠️ **In a session with clips before bar 1, the consolidated and DAWproject exports start earlier and Bounce in Place prints from bar 1.** Consolidated tracks' stems, MIDI files and tempo file all start at the bar line where the pre-count zone begins, so they line up with each other; bar 1 falls that many bars into each file, where they used to start at bar 1 and leave the zone out. Bounce in Place of clips that start before bar 1 prints from bar 1, with the part before it kept hidden in the printed file; with the source muted, deactivated or hidden (Mute is the default) that part is no longer heard, and the status line says so (choose Leave to keep it sounding); clips that end before bar 1 are refused. A DAWproject export starts at that same bar line: every time in the project moves later by those whole bars and the stems start there, so nothing sits before the project's start. The session length (the open log line and `session.inspect`'s `length_samples`) counts the zone, and `session.inspect` reports `start_samples` and `end_samples`. Sessions without clips before bar 1 export and print exactly as before (#441).
- ⚠️ **MIDI file export writes each clip as it plays, and leaves muted clips out.** Notes that start past a clip's end are left out, notes held over the end stop there, and a looped clip's repeats are written out. It used to write every note a clip held, past its end too, and a looped clip's content only once. A muted clip is no longer written, because a MIDI file cannot mark it muted and it would sound wherever the file is opened; a muted track is still written (#441).
- ⚠️ **A start trim of a MIDI clip hides notes instead of deleting them, and notes stored before an unlooped clip's start are now silent.** Dragging a MIDI clip's start edge later, Trim Start to Insertion and Trim to Selection keep the notes and controller moves they pass, hidden: dragging the start edge back out brings them back exactly where they were played. A note that starts before the edge is silent as a whole, even when it is held past the edge, as it was when a trim deleted it. Existing sessions sound different in one case: a clip that already holds notes before its start without a loop (made by Clear Loop, or by dragging the loop handle back to the first cycle, on a looped clip whose start was trimmed) used to play those notes all at once on its first tick; they are now silent. Copies, pasted ranges, DAWproject exports and clip group files carry nothing the start edge hides; the session file keeps the hidden notes and lists `midi_hidden_notes`, so an older build that reads session markers warns before it plays them on the clip's first tick (#441).
- A MIDI clip's start edge drags back as far as the session start, or further to the clip's earliest hidden note or controller move when that is earlier, and stops exactly there, unless another clip on the track is in the way, so a trim back reaches what a start trim hid; it can bring a pre-count zone back before bar 1. The trim drag's preview now also stops at a neighboring clip, where the drop does. The end edge and the loop handle of a clip that ends before bar 1 no longer stretch it to bar 1 (#441).
- A whole-track print (Track > Bounce in Place on a track, Commit, `track.bounce` and `track.commit`) of a track with content before bar 1 says so when it silences that part: the print starts at bar 1, so with the source set to Mute, Mute and deactivate, or Hide, the part before bar 1 no longer plays, and the status line and the command's reply now say that Leave keeps it sounding (#700).
- Linux, ALSA system: one audio dropout (an input overrun, or an output underrun while the audio thread waits) no longer ends the input for good with a CPU core left busy; the stream recovers after one gap and capture restarts, so monitoring and recording go on. Covered by tests on Linux; awaiting confirmation on hardware (#586).
- Linux, ALSA system with a separate input device: pressing Record no longer fails at once with "recording start fell outside retained input history" when the start maps less than one input block (at most 100 ms) before the first frame captured after arming; the take starts at that frame and is marked approximate. A start on a separate input that no current input clock map can place (a late input callback, say) waits up to a second for one, then is placed by the last validated map and marked approximate, where it used to fail with "could not be mapped"; a device that records with its output is placed as before. Covered by tests; awaiting confirmation on Linux hardware (#587).
- After recording with notes played during the count-in, the view scrolls back to show the take's start when the pickups landed left of it, including the first take that makes the pre-count zone. The view used to stay where it was, so the pickup was off screen and looked lost (#707).
- Linux: a plug-in editor embedded in its window is put back in place when another program moves it there, instead of staying offset or clipped until the window is next resized. (#697)
- Recording says when a take lost input, and where. When the take writer falls behind until the input queue is full, or the audio system (WASAPI) fills lost input with zeros, the take keeps its timing with silence there, as before; now a loss of 1 ms or more goes to the log when it begins and the status line counts it while recording, and Stop names how long and from how far into the take, for each cause (or that it started before the take began, in a count-in or pre-roll). Stop used to say only "the disk was too slow", a guess, and said nothing at all when a take file also failed; WASAPI's zeros were not reported per take. Invalid input samples (not a number or infinite) recorded as silence are counted too and named at Stop. Not counted: a JACK input port or an ASIO input channel without a buffer, which silences the whole stream rather than a stretch of it. The log also notes any stretch of exact digital silence of 100 ms or more between signal in a take, which names an interface that went silent while still running (#184).
- ⚠️ **ASIO: closing, reopening and stopping streams no longer risk a crash or replayed audio when a driver or a plug-in misbehaves.** transit takes the driver's buffers out of use before the driver frees them, also when reopening fails. When a buffer switch arrives while transit is still busy with the previous one, that part of the buffer plays silence instead of repeating the audio from two buffers earlier, and a slow error report no longer makes buffer switches miss their turn. Stopping a stream while transit's ASIO thread is busy (for example while a driver's control panel is open) silences that stream at once and succeeds; the driver stops as soon as the thread is free. A start that finds an audio callback still running fails with a message saying so instead of hanging. If an audio callback (often a plug-in) still has not returned two seconds after its streams close, the driver is left loaded instead of being freed under it, and no ASIO driver opens again until transit restarts; the error says so (#524).
- A hosted plug-in's own state is better protected. A change a plug-in reports itself (a patch or sample loaded in its own browser) marks the session modified, so Quit, New and Open ask to save; it used to go unnoticed until the next save. Every two minutes transit reads the plug-ins' current settings, also in a session with no unsaved changes, and autosaves when they changed, so a plug-in that never reports its changes is covered too; that read is held to about a tenth of a second, and plug-ins it did not reach are read at the next one. Quit, New and Open also read every plug-in's settings before asking. Removing a plug-in, replacing an instrument (by another instrument or an External Instrument), deleting or ungrouping its track, changing the track's width, and an undo or redo (one step or a jump in Undo History) that takes a plug-in away now read its current settings first, so Undo or Redo brings it back as it was at that moment, not as of the last save; changing a track's width also no longer resets its plug-ins to the last saved settings. If the audio engine is still applying an earlier change, transit waits a moment for it; if that is not enough, the change is not made and the status line says to try again. A plug-in that does not answer in time (busy in a dialog, or stuck) keeps the settings it last stored: the change goes ahead with those, and the status line names the plug-in. A plug-in that reports a change all the time keeps its session reading modified (#574).
