Audio clips with a stretch other than 1 (Conform to Tempo; Elastic Properties on a track without an Elastic algorithm) are read through ADR 0056's band-limited reader since #604, in playback and in every render, from memory and from disk alike. Its measured limits: below 0.42 of the session rate (20.2 kHz at 48 kHz, 18.5 kHz at 44.1 kHz) the error is within -73 dB (-78 dB for a clip sped up 1.5 times or more), and a clip sped up rejects what would play at 0.58 of the rate or above to -73.5 dB (-78 dB from 1.5 times); between 0.42 and 0.58 of the rate is the transition band, where a slowed clip's treble is attenuated and a sped-up clip's content is only partly rejected (it aliases only above 0.42 of the rate). A clip sped up more than 32 times (a stretch below 1/32, which no command sets: they keep the stretch within 0.05..20) plays as silence, and the audio health check names it. Reading a stretched clip costs processor time in proportion to how far it is sped up: the read alone, for a stereo clip at 48 kHz, takes about 0.7 percent of one core near normal speed and about 12 percent at 20 times, the most a command sets (the reader's own bench, on a loaded machine).
