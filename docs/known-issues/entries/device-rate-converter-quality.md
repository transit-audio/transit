Closed in v0.7.3 (#601): device-rate conversion, and the live input's conversion into the session (what is monitored and what the plan reads, External Instrument returns included), used linear interpolation with no anti-alias filter, so the treble drooped (44.1 kHz on a 48 kHz device: -1.5 dB at 10 kHz, -6.3 dB at 20 kHz) and content above the device's Nyquist folded back (96 kHz on a 48 kHz device at full level). Both now use the band-limited reader of [ADR 0056](../../adr/0056-audio-clip-editor.md) decision 8. Measured limits, by design: the passband holds tones up to 0.42 of the lower of the two rates (18.5 kHz when either is 44.1 kHz, 20.2 kHz at 48 kHz), level within 0.0003 dB and the tone at least 89 dB over everything else at every common pair (44.1, 48, 88.2 and 96 kHz, both ways); content that would play at 0.58 of the device rate or above is at least 82.9 dB down (30 kHz from 88.2 kHz into 48 kHz). Between 0.42 and 0.58 is the transition band: 20 kHz from 44.1 kHz on a 48 kHz device reads -0.38 dB with its images 27 dB down, and a 23 kHz tone from 48 kHz on a 44.1 kHz device folds to 21.1 kHz at -13.1 dB. At a converted rate the reader looks ahead about 16 frames on each side of the conversion (16 times the rate ratio when it converts down): the output renders that much further ahead and the live input keeps that much more queued, which adds about 0.75 ms to the round trip of a hardware insert or an External Instrument at 44.1 against 48 kHz (measure again at that rate). Equal rates are unchanged and pass every sample through bit for bit.
