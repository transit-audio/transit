Closed in v0.7.3 (#601): device-rate conversion, and the live input's conversion into the session (what is monitored and what the plan reads, External Instrument returns included), used linear interpolation with no anti-alias filter, so the treble drooped (44.1 kHz on a 48 kHz device: -1.5 dB at 10 kHz, -6.3 dB at 20 kHz) and content above the device's Nyquist folded back (96 kHz on a 48 kHz device at full level). Both now use the band-limited reader of [ADR 0056](../../adr/0056-audio-clip-editor.md) decision 8. Limits, by design. The tests assert the reader's bounds: in the passband, up to 0.42 of the lower of the two rates (18.5 kHz when either is 44.1 kHz, 20.2 kHz at 48 kHz), an error below -73 dB of the signal (-78 dB at a ratio of 1.5 or more); content that would play at 0.58 of the device rate or above at least 73.5 dB down (78 dB at a ratio of 1.5 or more). Reported test observations, not acceptance bounds: at every common pair (44.1, 48, 88.2 and 96 kHz, both ways) tones within 0.0003 dB and at least 89 dB over everything else; 30 kHz from 88.2 kHz into 48 kHz at least 82.9 dB down; between 0.42 and 0.58 (the transition band), 20 kHz from 44.1 kHz on a 48 kHz device reads -0.38 dB with its images 27 dB down, and a 23 kHz tone from 48 kHz on a 44.1 kHz device folds to 21.1 kHz at -13.1 dB. At a converted rate the reader looks ahead about 16 frames on each side of the conversion (16 times the rate ratio when it converts down): each callback renders only up to the output converter's reach past its last frame, and the live input reads the input converter's look-ahead. So what is played live through transit at a converted rate is heard later than at the session's rate, by an amount that depends on the converters' relative phase, the captured input history kept at alignment and the callback endpoints met afterward; the device buffer influences those but does not determine the result alone (v0.7.3, #724). In the paired fake-device tests, with capture starting into an empty ring, the hardware probe reports these extra plan frames over the simulated hardware delay, each a range the test allows at callbacks of 32 to 1,024 frames and three patterns of changing size, not two values both observed (session on device): 48 kHz on 44.1 kHz, 34 to 35 (0.71 to 0.73 ms); 44.1 on 48, 32 to 33 (0.73 to 0.75 ms); 48 on 96, 31 to 32 (0.65 to 0.67 ms); 96 on 48, 61 to 62 (0.64 to 0.65 ms); 48 on 88.2, 32 to 33 (0.67 to 0.69 ms); 44.1 on 88.2, 31 to 32 (0.70 to 0.73 ms); 48 on 176.4, 32 to 33 (0.67 to 0.69 ms); 48 on 192, 31 to 32 (0.65 to 0.67 ms); equal rates, 0. The probe adds its click after the output converter, so these are not the exact delays a monitored input, a hardware insert's send or an External Instrument's return sees. The live input reads silence for at most 18 session frames while the converter starts (at most 31 at 96 kHz on 48 kHz), then at most one frame. By reading the code, with no test yet: an input attached with audio already queued can sit later (32.5 session frames at 48 kHz on 96 kHz with 64-frame callbacks). With a 48 kHz session, a live note played into a software instrument with no look-ahead lands 17 session frames (0.35 ms) late on a 44.1 kHz device and 16 on a 96 kHz device, and on time with the live input's 128-frame look-ahead, at callbacks of 64 to 512 frames. Measure hardware inserts and External Instruments again at the rate you use. Playback, MIDI sent out and take placement do not move. At equal rates, speed 1 and a whole-frame converter phase, finite samples pass through unchanged.
