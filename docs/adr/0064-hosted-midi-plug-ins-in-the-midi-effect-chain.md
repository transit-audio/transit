# ADR 0064: Hosted MIDI plug-ins in the MIDI effect chain

- **Status:** proposed 2026-11-27 (issue #671; builds on ADR 0057 and ADR 0059, both accepted 2026-11-26; revised twice and settled in three judging rounds on 2026-11-27, then reopened the same day to cover plug-ins with no audio bus, plug-ins with no editor and listing by capability, and to order hosting before ADR 0057's built-in devices; a confirmation round is pending; nine product choices decided 2026-11-27 by delegation, each marked where it is written and listed under For the maintainer to confirm or overturn). Acceptance will not remove the maintainer's right to overturn any of those choices. The maintainer's hands-on check with a real third-party note-writing plug-in in each format (VST3 and CLAP) and his Mac check of the card and the editors are prerequisites of the visibility slice VH. Routing to other tracks and external ports is the named next record, "MIDI routing between tracks and to external ports" (decision 11).
- **Date:** 2026-11-27
- **Implementation:** not started. Slice cards in the Slices section; a contributor can take any slice whose needs have landed.
- **Reopen when:** the MIDI routing record named in decision 11 is written (it may change where a chain's output can go, never what a stage guarantees); ADR 0036's rack stage lands (decision 10 then admits hosted stages on racks); a supported plug-in is found that writes its notes on more than one output and expects them merged (decision 3); a public Steinberg document states the value layout of a legacy pitch bend or poly pressure output event (decision 3 then admits them); a VST3 plug-in is found that writes output events outside `0..numSamples` on purpose (decision 3); a plug-in that qualifies by the capability rule is found to misbehave in the slot in a way the category would have predicted (decision 2); Audio Unit MIDI processors are wanted (Out of scope); the stage ledger (decision 5) is measured refusing notes in ordinary use; a reported hang or crash of a hosted MIDI plug-in makes the in-process limit of decision 7 unacceptable; or Ableton Live and Logic Pro are found, in their documentation, to agree on a behavior this record chose differently.

## Context

**The request.** Issue #671: a third-party MIDI plug-in (a chord generator, an arpeggiator, a note processor) loads in transit, but the notes it produces go nowhere. An outside contributor offered to build it, so this record is written as a specification to build from, slice by slice. Plug-ins reported so far show the range the rules must cover: a VST3 generative chord performer with no sound of its own, listed by other hosts as an instrument, whose user wants its notes to drive synths on other tracks (the issue's case); and a set of note effects with no audio bus at all and no editor of their own, shipped many to a file, the VST3 ones categorized as instruments. Neither is a design center: the rules below are written for whole classes of plug-in (with or without audio buses, with or without an editor, one or many classes per file), and the evidence is fixture plug-ins written for the tests. The first slices deliver a plug-in's notes to its own track; other tracks are the next record (decision 11), and these slices do not complete the issue's reported request.

**What this record builds on.** [ADR 0036](0036-midi-effect-slot.md) made the slot and its host. [ADR 0057](0057-midi-devices-chord-arpeggiator-humanize.md) (accepted 2026-11-26) rebuilt the host's contract for its three devices, and its decision 16 lists what a hosted stage in the chain inherits, enforced by the host: final acceptance per hop (16.1), the host owning delivery to the instrument with its `out` table and refused-note accounting (16.2), every chain edit, Stop, panic and safety reset ending the chain's notes from the host's own table and releasing every pedal at an edit (16.3), ownership inside a stage being the stage's own (16.4), time and between-blocks rules (16.5), latency (16.6), capacity (16.7), refusal by the instrument (16.8, owned by [ADR 0059](0059-midi-event-delivery-under-refusal.md)) and Capture (16.9). Decision 16.2 states the hole this record must narrow or state: the host matches releases by identity and oldest-first, which proves an entry exists, not that a release answers it. This record inherits all of decision 16 and does not restate it; where it adds a rule, the rule is written here.

**What the code does today** (read on the branch carrying ADR 0057 and ADR 0059, head 40992792; line numbers are that tree's).

- **The MIDI effect host runs built-ins only.** A stage is `Stage { uid, plugin, bypass, fx: Option<Builtin>, carry, carry_len }` (`crates/mix/src/midi_fx.rs:126` to `135`); `begin` compares the chain by uid, bypass and an FNV hash of the id, and on a difference ends the chain's notes and makes every stage afresh with `Builtin::new(&w.plugin)` (`:294` to `327`), on the audio thread, in place. An id that `Builtin::new` does not know gives `fx: None`, and that stage passes its input through (`:131`; ADR 0036). A stage's output is collected, then sorted stably by offset (`:526`). Events sent between blocks run through the chain at once with `run(plugin, 1, false)` (`:377` to `381`). The host lives in the instrument's event state (`crates/mix/src/events.rs:548`), which travels with the instrument instance.
- **The trait and the event type.** `MidiEffect` (`crates/dsp/src/midi_fx.rs:146` to `178`) has no notion of a hosted plug-in; `Builtin` (`:194` to `200`) is a closed enum. `PluginEvent { channel: u8, note_id: i32, message }` (`crates/dsp/src/event.rs:146` to `150`) has unsigned MIDI channel and key and no port, so it cannot carry a CLAP wildcard. The model already reserves room: `MidiEffectSlot::plugin` is "Registry id of a built-in ... Hosted MIDI effects are not accepted yet", and `state` is "Opaque effect state (hosted MIDI effects, later), standard base64" (`crates/model/src/midi_effect.rs:23` to `33`). The add command accepts only ids the built-in registry knows (`crates/engine/src/cmd/midi_effect.rs:76` to `80`) and only on an instrument track or a rack (`:60` to `69`).
- **Hosted plug-ins are `Box<dyn Plugin>`,** made on the plug-in host thread (ADR 0018, `crates/plugin-host/src/lib.rs`; on macOS the host runs inline, making every instance on the calling thread, `lib.rs` module documentation, "Inline mode"), wrapped by `Hosted` (`crates/plugin-host/src/hosted.rs`), and given to plans by `(track, slot)` through `InstanceSpec` (`crates/mix/src/instance.rs:6` to `23`) and the player's `Handles` (`crates/playback/src/player/handles.rs`), which show a slot as Loading, Slow or Not responding while its instance is on its way (`SlotLoad`, `handles.rs:70` to `79`). Slot keys are insert indexes, `INSTRUMENT_SLOT = usize::MAX` (`crates/mix/src/lib.rs:243`), `RACK_SLOT = usize::MAX - 1` (`crates/mix/src/rack.rs:30`) and clip-effect slots with the top bit set (`lib.rs:252` to `272`; `clip_fx_of_slot` excludes only `INSTRUMENT_SLOT`, so any other key with the top bit set reads as a clip slot).
- **Lifecycle signals the adapters already keep.** VST3: `Shared::activate` (main thread, holding the instance's core) reconfigures and bumps `activation` whether the activation succeeded or failed (`crates/vst3-host/src/plugin.rs:274` to `305`); `service` calls it for `kLatencyChanged` or `kIoChanged`, latency changed or not (`:356` to `362`); a refused activation leaves the instance inactive and its calls skipped (`:890` to `898`). CLAP: a restart parks the audio side and reactivates (`restart_when_parked`, `restart`, `crates/clap-host/src/plugin.rs:332` to `402`). Both count live state loads, bumped while holding the core (`loads`, VST3 `plugin.rs:180`, CLAP `plugin.rs:165` to `167`). None of these is visible through the `Plugin` trait.
- **VST3 note output is handed over and ignored.** Each native call gets an output event list (`outputEvents`, `crates/vst3-host/src/ffi/instance.rs:507`), cleared before the call (`:486`) and never read; its `addEvent` accepts while there is room, with the comment "SoundCraft ignores them" (`crates/vst3-host/src/ffi/events.rs:281` to `297`; capacity `EVENT_CAP = 1024`, `crates/vst3-host/src/ffi.rs:44`). `configure` counts and activates only the event *input* bus (`instance.rs:368`, `:379` to `382`). A block is processed in native sub-calls of at most the prepared block (`crates/vst3-host/src/plugin.rs:911` to `990`), each with its own offset; a block of zero frames makes no call (`:877` to `880`). A call is skipped, keeping its input events at their offsets, when the main thread holds the instance (`plugin.rs:883` to `887`); the next call clamps them to its last frame (`:901` to `909`). When `process` returns an error the adapter counts nothing (ADR 0059, Context). The process mode is always `kRealtime`, offline renders included (`instance.rs:394`, `:496`). The adapter's `reset` keeps queued releases, keeps release-class controller points and clears timed points, and calls nothing in the processor (`plugin.rs:1028` to `1034`; ADR 0059, 3d). The transport sets playing, recording, tempo, meter, `projectTimeSamples`, `projectTimeMusic` and `barPositionMusic` from the signed `Transport` fields, and no cycle flag (`instance.rs:35` to `54`).
- **CLAP note output is not read.** The host's `try_push` keeps only `CLAP_EVENT_PARAM_VALUE` (editor edits) and accepts and ignores everything else (`crates/clap-host/src/ffi/events.rs:190` to `213`). The host reads only *input* note port 0 (`note_port`, `crates/clap-host/src/ffi/instance.rs:220` to `232`), from which it picks the CLAP or MIDI dialect for input (`note_mode`, `crates/clap-host/src/plugin.rs:755` to `761`). Controllers reach a CLAP plug-in only as MIDI on an input port that supports the MIDI dialect (`plugin.rs:1068` to `1119`). `reset` is requested for the next actual call (`plugin.rs:946` to `954`, `:815` to `817`); a skipped call keeps note events (`:795` to `799`). The transport converts signed positions to fixed point by rounding (`ffi/events.rs:138` to `170`) and carries no loop flag or loop points.
- **The scanner reads factory class information only, one reply per file.** A plug-in file is loaded in a child process (`--scan-vst3 <file>` / `--scan-clap <file>`, answered by `transit_ui_studio::plugin_scan::scan_child`, `crates/ui-studio/src/plugin_scan.rs:34` to `44`), which lists each class's id, name, vendor, sub-categories or features, category and `is_instrument` (`load_bundle`, `crates/vst3-host/src/registry.rs:86` onward; `ClapDescriptor`, `crates/clap-host/src/lib.rs:58` to `72`); no component is created. The child answers one line for the whole file; a child that crashes, prints no reply or runs past `CHILD_TIMEOUT` (120 s for the file) is recorded as a file error with no plug-ins (`crates/host-common/src/scan_cache.rs:29` to `30`, `:178` to `186`, `:210` to `275`). Results are cached by file size and modification time, a successful entry reused by every later build, a failure retried by another build, and the whole cache ignored when its `VERSION` differs (`:21`, `:114` to `130`, `:169` to `174`). The "+" menus list built-ins, then VST3 and CLAP entries grouped by vendor (`plugin_menu`, `crates/ui-studio/src/devices.rs:1011`), from `CachedPlugin { id, name, vendor, instrument }` (`plugin_scan.rs:171` to `179`).
- **Automation of hosted parameters** goes through `send_points`, which hands each lane point to `set_param_at` at its offset (`crates/mix/src/automation.rs:202` to `220`), and a lane that names a parameter the current plug-in lacks is inert, because "replacing an insert keeps its lanes" (`automation.rs:182` to `190`). MIDI effect lanes use slots 240 to 247; `remove_midi_effect` drops the removed effect's lanes (`crates/model/src/midi_effect.rs:85` to `96`); there is no replace for a MIDI effect.
- **Hosted inserts are suspendable when their input is silent; instruments and clip effects are not** (`suspendable`, `crates/mix/src/lib.rs:537`).
- **Fixture plug-ins exist.** The VST3 and CLAP hosts build a fixture plug-in per format from a cdylib outside the workspace in their integration tests (`crates/vst3-host/tests/fixture`, `crates/clap-host/tests/fixture`; for example "Fixture Events", which writes every event it receives into its audio output, `crates/vst3-host/tests/events_e2e.rs`). The CLAP fixture's `lib.rs` is 1,246 lines, the VST3 fixture's 920. Mix tests never load third-party plug-ins (`create_plugin` returns `None` for them under `cfg(test)`, `crates/mix/src/instance.rs:203` to `215`).

**What the formats say** (public documentation, read for this record on 2026-11-27; a quote is verbatim from the page named).

- *VST3* (Steinberg's API reference, `steinbergmedia.github.io/vst3_doc`, and developer portal). `ProcessData::outputEvents`: "outgoing events for this block (optional)". `Event`: `busIndex`, `sampleOffset` ("sample frames related to the current block start sample position"), `ppqPosition`, `flags`, `type`. `NoteOnEvent`: `channel`, `pitch` (0 to 127), `tuning`, `velocity` ("range [0.0, 1.0]"), `length` ("optional, Note Off has to follow in any case!"), `noteId` ("note identifier (if not available then -1)"); `NoteOffEvent`'s `noteId` is the "associated noteOn identifier (if not available then -1)". `LegacyMIDICCOutEvent` is "reserved for generating MIDI CC as output event for kEvent Bus during the process call", with `controlNumber`, `channel`, `value` ("value of Controller [0, 127]") and `value2` ("[0, 127] used for pitch bend (kPitchBend) and polyPressure (kCtrlPolyPressure)"); in the `ControllerNumbers` enum `kAfterTouch = 128`, `kPitchBend = 129`, `kCtrlProgramChange = 130`, `kCtrlPolyPressure = 131`. **No page read states which of `value` and `value2` holds the low and high seven bits of a pitch bend, or which holds the key of a poly pressure;** the documented helpers (`Helpers::getPitchBendValue`, `setPitchBendValue`) do not say either. A MIDI port maps to a bus of media type `kEvent` ("About MIDI"); a bus's direction is `kInput` or `kOutput`. `getBusCount` and `getBusInfo` are "Called after the plug-in is initialized"; `activateBus` and `setActive` are UI-thread calls. Categories are strings (`Fx`, `Instrument`, `Instrument|Synth`, `Fx|Generator`, `Fx|Instrument`, ...); no MIDI effect category exists. `setProcessing`: "this could be used to reset some buffers (like Delay line or Reverb)", callable "in the UI or in Processing Thread"; the call sequence page: it "may be called from real-time Audio Thread (must be lock-free and without memory allocation!)" and the "plug-in has to reset its inner processing state". Not stated on any page read: whether output events must lie in `0..numSamples` or be sorted; whether `projectTimeSamples` or `projectTimeMusic` may be negative.
- *CLAP* (the MIT-licensed headers, `include/clap/`). `note-ports.h`: `count(plugin, is_input)` and `get(plugin, index, is_input, info)`, both main-thread; `clap_note_port_info { id, supported_dialects, preferred_dialect, name }`; dialects `CLAP_NOTE_DIALECT_CLAP`, `_MIDI`, `_MIDI_MPE`, `_MIDI2`; "If the plugin does not implement this extension, it won't have note input or output"; ports change only while the plug-in is deactivated. `events.h`: `time` is the "sample offset within the buffer for this event"; "The plugin must insert events in sample sorted order"; `clap_event_note { note_id, port_index, channel, key, velocity }`, each address field `-1` for "unspecified or wildcard", a wildcard matching every value of its field (the header's example: port 0, channel 3, key and id wildcards match all voices on channel 3 of port 0); a note-on with `-1` for port, channel or key is invalid; a note-on of velocity 0 "is valid and should not be interpreted as a NOTE_OFF"; `NOTE_CHOKE` "can be sent by the host to the plugin"; `NOTE_END` "is sent by the plugin to the host" to report a voice's end; note expressions (volume, pan, tuning, vibrato, expression, brightness, pressure); `CLAP_EVENT_MIDI`, `_MIDI_SYSEX`, `_MIDI2`; one note must not be sent both as a note event and as MIDI. `try_push` "returns false if the event could not be pushed to the queue". `plugin-features.h`: `note-effect` is "a note effect or a note generator/sequencer"; `instrument` "can process note events and then produce audio". `plugin.h`: `reset` "Clears all buffers, performs a full reset of the processing state ... and kills all voices" (audio thread, active); latency and ports "must remain constant, until deactivation". Whether `song_pos_beats` may be negative is not stated.

**What other hosts document.** Ableton documents taking the MIDI output of a VST plug-in to another track ([Accessing the MIDI output of a VST plug-in](https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/209070189-Accessing-the-MIDI-output-of-a-VST-plug-in)). No Logic Pro documentation was consulted for this record. No decision below rests on another host's behavior except where a cited document is named.

## Changed during review (2026-11-27)

The first draft was revised twice before acceptance. What changed in the design:

- The plug-in's output is kept in an intermediate event with signed address fields until the stage ledger has matched it; a native note-on needs a concrete port, channel and key, and its note id may be `-1` (decision 3).
- The ledger matches in event-time order, and the provenance claim is limited to exact downstream addressing once an entry is selected, with every ambiguous case defined (decision 5).
- Restarts and state loads are observed through a lifecycle generation on both state-load paths, and output is accepted only from a call under the current epoch whose reset ran first (decision 6).
- The scan lists a file's classes first and probes each class in a child process of its own (2.4).
- HM1 and HM2 need ADR 0059's slice R1; the failing block is defined (7.4); exports report a silent stage (7.5).
- Undocumented VST3 legacy pitch bend and poly pressure output is dropped and counted (decision 3); "nothing hangs" is conditional on the host continuing to execute (decisions 6, 7).
- Five product choices were decided by delegation (decisions 2, 4, 7, 11), listed under For the maintainer to confirm or overturn.
- After acceptance, the record was reopened to cover whole classes of plug-in it had left implicit: plug-ins with no audio bus are hosted as they are (4.4); a hosted plug-in without an editor gets a permanent host parameter editor, which also serves editor-less hosted inserts and instruments (9.1); a class with a note output and no audio output is listed only as a MIDI effect (2.7); the CLAP input dialect is read again at every activation (4.1); and hosting is ordered before ADR 0057's built-in devices, with Capture no longer a prerequisite of visibility (Slices).

## Decision

### 1. Scope of the first cut

A hosted VST3 or CLAP plug-in that declares a note output transit can read can be a stage of an instrument track's MIDI effect chain, anywhere a built-in can sit, under every rule of ADR 0057 decision 16 and the rules below. Its notes go to the next stage or the instrument of the same track (decision 11 for other destinations). Audio Units are out of scope (Out of scope).

### 2. Which plug-ins qualify: capability, not category

1. **The rule (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** A plug-in qualifies when **any** of its outputs carries notes in a form transit reads: a VST3 class whose initialized component reports at least one event bus in the output direction (`getBusCount(kEvent, kOutput) >= 1`); a CLAP plug-in that, created and initialized, reports at least one output note port (`note_ports.count(plugin, false) >= 1`) whose supported dialects include the CLAP, MIDI or MIDI MPE dialect. A CLAP plug-in whose only output ports speak MIDI 2 alone does not qualify (transit carries no MIDI 2 message); one whose first port is MIDI 2 only and whose second supports the CLAP dialect qualifies, and the stage reads the second (decision 3). Whatever the plug-in calls itself: an instrument, an effect, a generator, a note effect.
2. **Categories are a hint and decide nothing.** VST3 sub-categories and CLAP features keep doing what they do today (the category and the instrument flag that place a plug-in in the instrument and insert menus); they never admit or exclude a plug-in from the MIDI effect slot. So the reported case (a VST3 class whose sub-category is an instrument, with an audio output bus and an event output bus, as the issue describes it) qualifies, and appears in both the instrument menu and the MIDI effects menu. A CLAP `note-effect` without an output note port does not qualify (it cannot write notes the host reads).
3. **A declared output that stays silent is fine.** A plug-in that declares a readable note output and never writes to it passes nothing (decision 4: its output replaces its input). That is the user's choice to make, not the host's to guess.
4. **The scan: list first, then probe each class on its own.** Creating a component can crash, hang or open a dialog, and today's child protocol loses a whole file's reply when its child does not return (Context), so a probe added to the listing child could remove instruments and inserts that list today, other classes of the same bundle included. The scan therefore runs two phases per changed file:
   - **Listing**, unchanged: the existing child (`--scan-vst3 <file>`, `--scan-clap <file>`) reads the factory and answers the file's classes. Its result is cached as today. Today a format's list (`cached()`) stays empty until that format's whole scan ends (`crates/ui-studio/src/plugin_scan.rs`); HM0 adds publication of the listing phase as soon as it ends, before any probe runs, and of each probe outcome as it arrives. That incremental publication is new work, not today's behavior.
   - **Probing**, after the listing is published: one child process per class (`--probe-vst3 <file> <class id>`, `--probe-clap <file> <plugin id>`), which creates only that class, reads its capability, and exits without activating, processing or showing anything. VST3: `createInstance` of the component, `initialize` with the host context, `getBusCount` of event buses in both directions, `terminate`. CLAP: `create_plugin`, `init`, the note ports in both directions with each port's dialects, the audio output port count, `destroy`; the VST3 probe also counts audio output buses. The child answers `note_output`, `note_input`, `audio_outputs` and the index of the first readable output; or an error.
   - **Outcomes per class,** each a field of that class's cached descriptor: `note_output: Some(true | false)` when the probe answered; `None` with the probe's error text and the build that tried it when it returned an error, crashed, printed no answer or ran past its timeout. Probing never deletes the factory listing or changes a class's identity or category: whatever a probe does, the class keeps its name, id, category and cache entry. A successful probe's capability results decide its menu placement under 2.6 and 2.7; a failed probe keeps today's category-based placement. So if every probe of a file fails, its classes keep exactly today's menus (instruments stay instruments) and gain no MIDI effect entries; the diagnostics report and an explicit rescan are the recovery. A failed probe is retried by another build or when the file changes, as file failures are today, and the explicit rescan probes every class again. The diagnostics report lists every class whose probe failed with the reason.
   - **Cost.** Every class of a changed file is created once in a child of its own, once per change of that file (the cache keeps the outcome). Each probe has its own timeout of 120 s, the listing child's budget (`CHILD_TIMEOUT`), so a bundle of `n` classes can take up to `n × 120 s` of background probing in the worst case (a file of eleven classes is eleven probe children per change of the file, up to 22 minutes in the worst case and seconds for classes that create quickly); probes run on the existing background scan threads, never on the UI thread, and the menus fill as probes answer. The cache's `VERSION` goes from 1 to 2, so the first scan of a build with this slice lists and probes every file once: the existing invalidation mechanism, not a migration shim (alpha: no migrations).
5. **Checked again at creation.** An instance made for a MIDI slot whose configured instance has no readable note output (a plug-in updated since the scan) is a stage the user did not ask to be silent, so it is silent (decision 7) with the state "Does not write notes", named in the card and the load note; the session keeps the slot unchanged.
6. **Where a qualifying plug-in appears.** In the MIDI effect group's "+" menu (ADR 0057, 14.7): built-in MIDI effects first, then VST3 and CLAP entries with `note_output == Some(true)`, grouped by vendor, through the existing `plugin_menu` with the list filtered. A class whose probe failed (`None`) is not listed there (its probe failure is in the diagnostics report); a rescan (Audio > Scan Plug-ins) probes again. The add command accepts a hosted id only when its cached descriptor says `note_output == Some(true)`; until the visibility slice, only through the control channel (decision 12, slice VH).
7. **Instrument and insert menus by capability (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** The probe also records the class's audio output count (`audio_outputs: Option<u32>`: VST3 `getBusCount(kAudio, kOutput)`, CLAP `audio_ports.count(plugin, false)`). A class whose probe succeeded and found a readable note output and **no** audio output is listed in the MIDI effects menu only, never in the instrument menu or an insert menu, whatever its category: it supplies no audio output, and it belongs in the note-processing chain. A class with an audio output keeps today's category-based placement in those menus (so a generator with an instrument audio bus is offered in both the instrument and the MIDI effects menus), and so does a class whose probe failed (`None`, 2.4). Today the instrument menu is filled from `CachedPlugin::instrument`, the category-derived `is_instrument` (`crates/ui-studio/src/plugin_scan.rs:171` to `179`), filtered in `instrument_lists` (`crates/ui-studio/src/devices.rs:957` onward); HM0 adds the audio count and HM4 the capability exclusion there. A session that already holds such a class as an instrument or insert loads it unchanged (alpha: nothing is migrated).

### 3. Reading note output

**Which output (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** The stage reads the plug-in's **first readable output** and no other: VST3 event output bus 0 (every VST3 event bus carries the same event types, so the first is readable), which the adapter activates in `configure` beside the input bus (`activateBus(kEvent, kOutput, 0, true)`, UI thread, inactive component, as the input bus is); CLAP the lowest-indexed output note port whose dialects include CLAP, MIDI or MIDI MPE (the index the probe recorded, read again from the instance at each activation). Events addressed to any other bus or port are dropped and counted (`other_output`). When a plug-in declares more than one note output, the card's state line says which one is read ("Reads note output 1 of 3") and the help says why: transit has one note port per instrument, and a plug-in that writes several streams means them for several destinations, which is decision 11's question.

**When it is read.** After each native sub-call, before the next: the adapter takes that call's output events, adds the sub-call's start to each offset, gives each a write-order sequence number for the block, and appends them to a fixed block output buffer of 1,024 entries (the native list's capacity; past it an event is dropped and counted). CLAP: the host's `try_push` writes into that buffer and returns `false` when it is full (the plug-in is told); VST3: `addEvent` already refuses at the list's capacity. A block of zero frames makes no native call and produces no output. A skipped call (main thread holds the instance, parked, inactive) produces no output for that block; the input it kept reaches the plug-in at offset 0 of the next actual processing call (ADR 0059, 3a, which slice R1 implements; HM1 needs it).

**Offsets: a host policy.** Neither format states the range of an output offset (Context), so the host decides: an offset below 0 becomes 0, and one at or past the sub-call's frame count becomes that sub-call's last frame; both are counted (`offset_clamped`). The host's carry is not used for hosted output, since carrying an event the plug-in placed outside its call would act on an offset no format defines. Clamping makes the result depend on how the plug-in's calls are partitioned, so a stream with malformed offsets is excluded from every partition-equality promise in this record.

**The intermediate output event.** The adapter does not translate into `PluginEvent`, which cannot carry a port or a wildcard (Context). It hands the stage `HostedOut { offset: u32, seq: u32, kind, port: i32, channel: i16, key: i16, note_id: i32, form, values }`, where `port`, `channel`, `key` and `note_id` are the plug-in's own signed values (`-1` kept as written), `form` is `Native` (a VST3 event or a CLAP note event) or `MidiBytes` (a CLAP MIDI event), and `kind` is one of: note-on, note-off, choke, poly pressure, controller, channel pressure, pitch bend, program, dropped (with its reason). Velocities and values stay in the format's units until the ledger has matched the event; the stage converts at output (below). A native note-on is admitted per field: its port, channel and key must be concrete valid addresses (the read port; channel 0 to 15; key 0 to 127), and any of them out of range, `-1` included, makes the note-on invalid (dropped, counted, as the CLAP header calls such a note-on invalid); its `note_id` may be any value, and `-1` is a valid anonymous identity (an anonymous note, 5.1), not an invalid address. A channel or key out of range in a MIDI-byte event cannot occur (seven-bit fields).

**The translation table.** Every row below is what the stage writes after the ledger (decision 5) has matched the event; every channel written is 0 to 15. Velocities: a float velocity `v` becomes `round(127 × v)`, half away from zero, clamped to 1..=127 for a note-on and 0..=127 for a note-off. A native note-on whose velocity rounds to 0 is a note-on of velocity 1, as both formats define it; a MIDI-byte note-on with velocity 0 is a note-off (MIDI's rule; ADR 0057, 2.2).

| Format event | Becomes |
|---|---|
| VST3 `kNoteOnEvent` | note-on (`tuning` and `length` ignored) |
| VST3 `kNoteOffEvent` | note-off for the entry the ledger selects (an anonymous release, 5.3) |
| VST3 `kPolyPressureEvent` | poly pressure `round(127 × pressure)` for each entry it matches |
| VST3 `kLegacyMIDICCOutEvent`, `controlNumber` 0 to 127 | `Control { cc, value }` from `value` |
| the same, `kAfterTouch` (128) | `ChannelPressure` from `value` |
| the same, `kCtrlProgramChange` (130) | `Program` from `value` |
| the same, `kPitchBend` (129) and `kCtrlPolyPressure` (131) | **dropped, counted**: no public document read states which field holds which bits or the key (Context). Admitted by an amendment that quotes one (Reopen when) |
| the same, any other controller number | dropped, counted |
| VST3 `kDataEvent`, note expressions, `kChordEvent`, `kScaleEvent` | dropped, counted |
| CLAP `NOTE_ON` on the read port | note-on |
| CLAP `NOTE_OFF` and `NOTE_CHOKE` | note-off for every entry the release matches (a wildcard release, 5.3) |
| CLAP `NOTE_END` | ignored (it reports the plug-in's own voices, not a note it writes) |
| CLAP `NOTE_EXPRESSION`, pressure | poly pressure for each entry it matches |
| CLAP `NOTE_EXPRESSION`, any other | dropped, counted (transit carries no per-note tuning, volume, pan or brightness) |
| CLAP `MIDI` on the read port, any dialect | MIDI 1.0: 9n, 8n, An, Bn, Cn, Dn, En; MPE is carried as what it is on the wire, per-channel notes and controllers (ADR 0057, 2.10) |
| CLAP `MIDI_SYSEX`, `MIDI2` | dropped, counted |
| CLAP `PARAM_VALUE` | as today (editor edits, `ffi/events.rs:199` to `211`) |

What a user loses, stated in the help and the card's help text: per-note tuning and the other per-note expressions except pressure, system exclusive, MIDI 2, VST3 legacy pitch bend and poly pressure output, and every output but the first readable one. The drop counters are shown with the chain's counters (`MidiFxCounts`, `EventStatus::midi_fx`) and in the diagnostics report.

**The trait seam.** `Plugin` gains, with defaults that change nothing for any existing plug-in: `fn note_output(&self) -> Option<NoteOutputInfo>` (`None`; the configured instance's readable output and how many note outputs it declares), `fn take_note_output(&mut self, out: &mut dyn FnMut(HostedOut) -> bool) -> CallReport` (hands the block's events in write order and clears the buffer; the report carries the lifecycle generation each call of the block ran under, the number of native calls made and skipped, and whether any reported an error, decision 6), `fn lifecycle(&self) -> Lifecycle` (`{ generation: u64, active: bool }`; decision 6) and `fn restart_processing(&mut self)` (decision 6). `Hosted` forwards all four to the plug-in it wraps; with no plug-in loaded it reports no output and an inactive lifecycle. The adapters' file budgets (`crates/vst3-host/src/plugin.rs` 1,340 lines, `crates/clap-host/src/plugin.rs` 1,337) mean the output path goes to new modules beside them.

### 4. What the plug-in hears, and what passes it

1. **Input.** The stage's input events go to the plug-in through the adapter's existing `Plugin::event`, exactly as an instrument receives them. **The input dialect, CLAP:** the adapter reads input note port 0 and delivers in the CLAP dialect when that port supports it, else in the MIDI dialect (`note_mode`, `crates/clap-host/src/plugin.rs:755` to `761`; the port's preferred dialect is not consulted). Today that choice is made only in `prepare` (`:933` to `935`): `Shared::restart` deactivates and reactivates (`:378` onward) without reading the note ports again, so after a restart in which the plug-in changed its ports the adapter can keep the old dialect. HM1 fixes this: every activation, the first and every restart, reads the input note port and its dialects again and publishes them together with the lifecycle generation's bump (decision 6), so no event is translated for the new activation before the new dialect is in force. Ports may change only while the plug-in is deactivated, so the dialect is fixed for each activation, and a change is a restart, a barrier. The stage reads either form on its read output (decision 3). Then, by format: VST3 notes and poly pressure on event input bus 0 with the chain's voice ids as `noteId`, controllers, channel pressure and pitch bend as points on the parameters the plug-in maps them to (`IMidiMapping`), program changes not delivered; CLAP notes on input port 0 in its dialect, controllers only as MIDI to a port that supports MIDI. A plug-in with no event input (a pure generator) hears no notes. Admission and refusal at this input are the adapter's, under ADR 0059's slice R1; a refused input event is counted like any stage refusal.
2. **Output replaces input, controllers and pedals included (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** What the plug-in writes is the stage's whole output: an input event the plug-in does not write again (a note, a controller, pitch bend, a pedal) does not reach later stages. A MIDI effect transforms its whole stream, and forwarding the input around it would undo processing the plug-in does on purpose (a generator's Thru control, a controller remapper). Stated limit, with no pedal-up invented by the host: a pedal-down the plug-in forwards without the matching pedal-up sustains at the instrument until the next barrier; an edit releases every pedal the instrument holds (ADR 0057, 2.13), and Stop releases pedals and resets the instrument as for any chain (ADR 0057, 2.14).
3. **Transport and tempo.** The same `Transport` the built-ins see (ADR 0057, 16.5): playing, recording, tempo, meter, sample position, quarters and bar start, which the adapters already map (Context). No loop or cycle information is passed (none exists in `Transport`); a loop wrap appears to the plug-in as a jump of the position. While stopped, playing is false and the position does not advance; whether the plug-in plays while stopped is its own behavior, not gated by the host (a chord performer must answer keys while stopped), so Stop ends what the stage had sounding but does not promise that such a plug-in stays silent afterwards. **Negative positions.** The pre-count zone (ADR 0052) reaches the plug-in as true negative values: VST3 `projectTimeSamples` and `projectTimeMusic`, CLAP `song_pos_beats` and `song_pos_seconds` (rounded to fixed point as today), never clamped. Neither format documents negative positions, so a plug-in may misread them; that is a stated compatibility limit, not something the host repairs.
4. **Its audio, if it has any (zero audio: Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** The stage owns a two-channel scratch buffer of the session's largest block, allocated when the stage's instance is prepared, because the `Plugin` trait's `process(io, frames)` is how an adapter is called and both adapters return without a native call when `io` is empty (VST3 `plugin.rs:875` to `880`, CLAP `plugin.rs:767` to `772`); the stage prepares the instance with two channels. What the plug-in sees depends on its own buses, not on the buffer: a plug-in **with** audio buses gets the buffer on its main input marked silent (VST3 silence flags, CLAP constant flags, as today's `set_input_silent`), and its output is computed and not used. A plug-in with **no** audio bus or port is hosted as it is, with no audio invented for it. Verified by reading the adapters: a VST3 class with no audio buses configures with empty arrangements (`setBusArrangements` with zero counts, `crates/vst3-host/src/ffi/instance.rs:321` to `358`), gets empty bus buffers and is processed with `numInputs` and `numOutputs` 0 and null bus arrays (`instance.rs:494` to `503`), and nothing is copied back (`has_output` false, `plugin.rs:972`); a CLAP plug-in with no audio ports activates with empty port buffers (`Core::activate`, `crates/clap-host/src/plugin.rs:108` to `130`) and is processed with zero audio input and output counts (`ffi/instance.rs:351` to `358`), nothing copied back (`out_main` none). No running test covers either today; zero-audio fixtures in both formats are part of HM1 (the adapter) and HM2 (the first slice that hosts a stage). A synth that also writes notes may sit in the slot: the capability rule admits it, and refusing it would refuse the reported case, which declares an instrument audio bus. A sidechain key is never routed to a MIDI stage. The card says "Audio from this plug-in is not used here" (decision 9). The plug-in's processing cost is paid in full; the help says so.
5. **Never suspended.** A hosted MIDI stage is not suspendable (a generator writes notes with silent input); its slot is created with `suspendable` false.
6. **Between blocks.** A hosted stage is never processed between blocks: a native call advances the plug-in's time, and ADR 0057's call "in which no time passes" (slice P2) has no native form. An event sent between blocks waits in the stage's input and reaches the plug-in at offset 0 of the next actual processing call, after the retained events of skipped calls and before that block's own events; a skipped call extends the wait. Until P2 lands, the host's between-blocks call `run(plugin, 1, false)` skips hosted stages the same way.
7. **Offline rendering.** Bounce, freeze and, once ADR 0057's slice CAP lands, Capture render with fresh instances restored from the stored state (`crates/mix/src/offline.rs`), through the same stage code. The VST3 process mode stays `kRealtime` in offline renders, as for every VST3 plug-in today (Context; changing it for all VST3 plug-ins is not this record's).

### 5. Note pairing and provenance: the stage ledger

ADR 0057 decision 16.4 leaves ownership inside a hosted stage to this record. The host puts a **stage ledger** at every hosted stage's output. It is host code, so the plug-in is held to it without being trusted; what it can and cannot establish is stated in 5.4 and 5.5.

1. **Entries and host ids.** Every accepted note-on the stage writes opens an entry `{ channel, key, plug-in note id or -1, host id, seq: u64, state }` and leaves the stage carrying a fresh **host id** in `note_id`, unique among the stage's open entries: a 31-bit counter, skipping `-1` and any id still open. Every note-off and poly pressure written for an entry carries that entry's host id. The port is not stored: only the read port reaches the ledger.
2. **Event-time order.** The ledger matches the block's whole output after the block's last native call, in the order of `(offset, seq)`: the events are sorted stably by their offset (sub-call start added, clamped, decision 3) before any of them changes an entry, so events at one offset keep the plug-in's write order and an event written later in time but earlier in write order is matched in time. Example, within one native call: the plug-in writes on at 10, then off at 0; the ledger sees off at 0 first, which matches nothing and is consumed (5.3), then on at 10, which opens an entry that sounds until the next barrier. The output the stage writes is in the same order, so the host's later stable sort changes nothing.
3. **Matching a release.** By form:
   - *An identified native release* (VST3 note-off or CLAP note-off or choke with a note id other than `-1`, and channel and key given): closes the **oldest** open entry with that channel, key and plug-in id, and only that one.
   - *An anonymous single release* (VST3 note-off with `noteId` -1; a CLAP MIDI-byte note-off or 9n with velocity 0): closes the **oldest** open entry of its channel and key, identified or not (VST3: the id is "not available"; MIDI bytes have none).
   - *A CLAP native release with any wildcard field* (`note_id`, `channel` or `key` is -1): closes **every** open entry that matches every field that is not a wildcard, oldest first, each with its own note-off, as the CLAP header defines wildcards; all fields wildcards closes every open entry. A port of -1 or the read port applies; another port drops the release (counted).
   - Poly pressure (VST3 `kPolyPressureEvent`, CLAP pressure expression, MIDI An) goes to every open entry its fields match by the same rules (a single entry for an identified address), each event with that entry's host id. It changes no ownership.
   - A release or pressure that matches no open entry and no rejection record (5.6) is consumed and counted (`unmatched_releases`).
4. **What the host guarantees.** Once the ledger has selected an entry, everything downstream addresses exactly that note: the note-on and its note-off carry one host id, and every later stage, the host's `out` table and the instrument pair them by identity. For a plug-in whose identities are unambiguous (every note-on with a plug-in id not in use by an open note, and every release with that id, written once), each note ends at its own release, a wildcard that covers it, or a barrier, and no note-off leaves the stage for a channel and key with nothing open. For every plug-in, no note-off leaves the stage without an open entry, and no entry gets two note-offs.
5. **What it cannot guarantee, and what happens instead.** A release carries only the plug-in's address, so where the plug-in's addresses are ambiguous the ledger picks by the rules above and the wrong note can end early. Nothing is left sounding past the next barrier in any of these cases (decision 6, under its conditions). Each case is defined and has a fixture script (Slices):
   - *A reused id.* `on(7)`, `off(7)`, `on(7)`, a duplicate `off(7)`: the duplicate closes the second note early, and the second note's own off is consumed.
   - *Duplicate open ids.* `on(7)`, `on(7)` on one key while the first is open: two entries; `off(7)` closes the older; a second `off(7)` the newer.
   - *An anonymous release meeting an identified note.* VST3 `on(7)` then an anonymous off of the same key closes the identified note (the oldest open entry of its key); the later `off(7)` is consumed.
   - *A wildcard release.* A CLAP note-off with id -1 on a key closes every open note of that key, identified ones included.
   - *Anonymous duplicates.* An anonymous C on, its off, another anonymous C on, then a duplicate of the first off: the duplicate closes the second C early.
   - *A stale release across a barrier.* The ledger is cleared at a barrier (decision 6). A release written afterwards for a note from before the barrier is consumed if it matches nothing; if the plug-in has since opened a note it matches (the same id reused, or the same key for an anonymous or wildcard release), it closes that note early.
6. **Capacity and rejected notes.** 256 open entries (`MIDI_FX_NOTES`). A note-on is **rejected** when 256 entries are open or when the stage's output refuses it (5.7); a rejected note-on is not written, is counted as a refused note-on, and leaves a rejection record `{ channel, key, plug-in id or -1, seq }` in a table of 256, so that its release finds it in its place: an identified release closes a record with its channel, key and id before falling back to nothing (records are not matched by the fallback of anonymous releases to identified notes); an anonymous single release closes the oldest among the open entries and records of its key by sequence number; a wildcard release clears every matching record as well as every matching entry. A release that closes a record writes nothing. With the record table full, a further rejection is only counted (`rejected_unrecorded`); its release then closes the oldest open entry it matches, ending that note early, or is consumed: saturation can shorten a note and never leaves one sounding past a barrier or writes a note-off twice.
7. **The stage's output buffer.** The stage's output is admitted by ADR 0057's P3b rule (16.1). A note-on the output refuses is rejected (5.6). A note-off the output refuses (only possible at 512 events in the buffer) leaves its entry open in the state **release owed**: the note-off is written again at offset 0 of the next block, ahead of every other event of the stage's output in that block, and the entry closes when the output accepts it; a later note-on of the same channel and key opens its own entry after it. An entry owed a release does not match later releases. At a barrier, owed releases are dropped with the ledger, because the barrier ends the note from the host's `out` table.
8. **Its own table.** ADR 0057's kit ledger (K1) matches an anonymous note-off against anonymous entries only; the formats' rules need an anonymous single release to close an identified note and a wildcard release to close several, so the stage ledger is its own table in the host (`crates/mix/src/midi_fx/hosted.rs`), with the same capacity and fail-closed bound. `NotePairing` is not used.

### 6. Barriers: every way a stage stops owning its notes

Each event below is a **host barrier**: the host ends every note it delivered from the chain (`out`), releases every pedal at an edit and presses none again, resets every stage, clears every stage ledger, rejection table and owed release and the delivery refused table, discards every hosted stage's retained input and buffered output, and starts a new epoch, exactly as ADR 0057's chain edit and reset barrier (2.13, P3a, P3b) do; all of it at offset 0 of the barrier block, before any output of that block from a stage reaches downstream. This record adds to the list of events that cause one, never to what one does.

| Event | Barrier |
|---|---|
| A hosted stage added, removed, moved, bypassed or un-bypassed | edit (ADR 0057, 2.13) |
| A hosted stage replaced by another effect (decision 8) | edit |
| Stop, the end of a play range, the All MIDI Notes Off action | as ADR 0057 2.14 and ADR 0059 4 |
| A hosted stage's instance arrives in the plan, or leaves it | edit; the stage comparison in `begin` gains "instance present" |
| A hosted stage enters a state the user did not ask for (decision 7) | edit, then the stage is silent |
| A hosted stage's lifecycle generation changes (a restart, a refused reactivation, a state load) | edit |
| A parameter change, from a lane, a hand, MIDI Learn or the plug-in's editor | none: each plug-in pairs its own notes across its own parameter changes; the ledger catches what it does not |

**The lifecycle generation.** A reactivated plug-in has forgotten the notes it was holding, and a loaded state can drop them, so their releases will never come. Each adapter keeps one counter, bumped while it holds the instance's core, at:
- every activation attempt: the first, every restart whatever the reason (latency changed or not), and a refused one, which also marks the instance inactive (VST3 `Shared::activate`; CLAP `restart`);
- every state load applied to the processor, on **both** load paths: the direct `Plugin::load_state` (VST3 `plugin.rs:1217`, and the CLAP twin) and the state reader's remote load (VST3 `plugin/state.rs`, CLAP `plugin/editor.rs`). Today's `loads` counters are bumped on the remote path only (VST3 `plugin/state.rs:45`, CLAP `plugin/editor.rs:33`), so the generation counter is a new counter bumped on both, not a rename of `loads`. This covers a preset recall, an undo that restores a state, a state set through the control channel, and a state job that ran while processing was skipped;
- every `prepare`.

Both adapters hold the core across the whole native sub-call loop of a block (`process_keyed`), and activation and state loads take the same core, so a generation cannot change inside a native call. `Plugin::lifecycle()` reads the counter (one atomic load) with the instance's active flag. `take_note_output`'s report gives, for each native call of the block, the generation it ran under and whether the stage's pending reset (below) ran before that call's `process`, both read under the core lock inside the call.

**One rule for accepting output.** The stage keeps a current **epoch**: the generation it expects and whether that epoch's reset has completed. It accepts a native call's output only when the call ran under the epoch's generation **and** the epoch's reset had completed before that call's `process` (in an earlier accepted call, or at the start of this one). Every other call's output is discarded, whole. So no output of a new epoch is accepted before its reset has completed, and none of an old epoch after its barrier: the barrier's "discard buffered output" has no exception. The stage applies the rule twice per block:
1. **Before handing input,** it reads `lifecycle()`. A generation other than the epoch's runs the barrier at offset 0 of this block, starts a new epoch for that generation with its reset pending, and requests the reset (`restart_processing`); the reset then runs at the start of this block's first actual call, under the core, before `process`, so that call's output can be accepted.
2. **After the block's calls,** it reads the report. If a call ran under a generation other than the epoch's (the main thread restarted or loaded the instance between step 1 and the call), that call's output and the output of every later call of the block are discarded, the barrier runs at offset 0 of this block (if step 1 did not already run one in this block), a new epoch starts for the reported generation with its reset pending, and the reset is requested for the next actual call. The stage resumes from the first call that satisfies the rule. Example: the stage checks generation G; a state load moves the instance to G + 1 before the adapter takes the core; the call under G + 1 writes a note-on and keeps its release scheduled inside the plug-in; the report shows G + 1, so the note-on is discarded and never reaches downstream, the barrier runs, and the reset runs before the next call; the release the reset may erase belongs to a note nobody received, so nothing is left without its release and no extra Stop is needed.
3. **Discarded calls' input** was handed to the plug-in and is lost with the output: a note played into such a call does not sound, and its later release is consumed (decision 7.2's rule that nothing is reconstructed for held keys applies).
4. **An inactive instance** after a refused reactivation is a state the user did not ask for: the stage is silent with the state "Could not restart" (decision 7) until a later activation succeeds, which is a new generation and so another barrier and epoch.

The same rule governs every barrier, not only lifecycle ones: every barrier starts a new epoch with its reset pending, so after an edit, Stop or a recovery the stage accepts output only from a call whose reset ran first.

**The stage's own reset at a barrier.** Before its next actual processing call, a hosted stage is reset by `Plugin::restart_processing`, a new trait method whose default calls `reset`. CLAP: `reset`, requested for the next actual call as today (documented to kill all voices). VST3 override: first everything today's `reset` does to the adapter's queues (queued releases kept, release-class controller points kept, timed points cleared, `plugin.rs:1028` to `1034`), then, under the core lock at the next actual call, `setProcessing(false)` followed by `setProcessing(true)` before `process`, which the format documents as the place a plug-in resets its inner processing state and allows from the processing thread. Results: `kResultOk` and `kNotImplemented` are success (the adapter already treats `kNotImplemented` from `setProcessing` as normal, `instance.rs:465` to `468`); any other result is counted and is a processing error of the stage (decision 7.4). The cycle is used for hosted MIDI stages only; VST3 instruments and inserts keep ADR 0059's reset as written (3d). Whether a VST3 plug-in actually forgets pending notes at that cycle is the plug-in's: a fixture that retains them shows what the host does then (its stale releases follow 5.5's stale case).

**Nothing hangs, under conditions.** While the host keeps executing (every native call returns, the process lives) and downstream delivery succeeds within ADR 0059's limits, every note a hosted stage let through to the instrument is ended by its own release or a wildcard (5.3), by a barrier (above), or by the instrument's reset at Stop (ADR 0057, 2.14); a note-on the plug-in writes without its note-off sounds until the next barrier, and Stop is always one. Outside the guarantee (decision 7.6): a native `process` call that never returns (Stop and every barrier wait on the audio thread behind it), a plug-in that crashes the process, and an editor or state call that holds the instance's core indefinitely (the stage's calls are skipped, so its reset cannot run; the barrier still ends what the host delivered).

### 7. Stage states, failures and the in-process limit

1. **User bypass passes notes through (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** A bypassed hosted stage is transparent: its input is its output, as for any bypassed effect (ADR 0036). The user asked for it.
2. **Every state the user did not ask for is silent (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** Loading (and Slow), Not responding, Not installed (missing on load), Could not be created, Does not write notes (2.5), Could not restart (6) and Failed (7.4) make the stage **silent**: it passes nothing; entering the state is a barrier, which ends the notes the chain had sounding; its input while silent is consumed (input releases of notes it consumed are consumed too); the card shows the state plainly; and the user can bypass the stage to hear the notes without it. Leaving the state (the instance arrives, a restart succeeds, Stop ends a failure) is another barrier; nothing is reconstructed for keys held through it (a held key sounds again when struck again). Reason: a failed or loading arpeggiator must not suddenly play the raw held chords into the synth; there is no automatic audible change the user did not ask for. A silent stage is not a silent track: a built-in generator after it in the chain keeps writing its own notes, and the record promises nothing about the track beyond the stage. ADR 0036's pass-through for an unknown *built-in* id is unchanged by this record (#641 owns it).
3. **Missing on load (#641).** A session whose MIDI effect slot names a plug-in that is not installed loads with the slot unchanged (id, parameters, state and pages written back on save as read), the stage silent, and a load note naming the plug-in and the track: "<name> (MIDI effect on <track>) is not installed. The stage is silent; bypass it to hear the track's notes without it." `Session::needs()` lists `hosted_midi_effects` when any slot holds a hosted id, so an older build warns instead of passing the notes through silently (ADR 0023). What a hosted *insert* shows today when its creation returns nothing was not read for this record; the slice reuses that slot state if one exists and adds the load note either way.
4. **A processing error; the failing block exactly.** The adapter reports, per block, whether any native call returned an error (a VST3 `process` result other than success, counted by ADR 0059's slice R1; CLAP `CLAP_PROCESS_ERROR`; a failed `setProcessing` cycle, decision 6). In that block: the stage's whole output for the block is discarded (the host cannot know which events a failing call meant, and earlier sub-calls of the block are discarded with it, so the block's result does not depend on where the native calls split it); the barrier runs at offset 0; the stage's input of the block from offset 0 on is consumed; and the stage is **Failed until the next Stop**: silent (7.2), its card and the status line saying "<name> on <track> reported a processing error. It is silent until you press Stop; bypass it to hear the notes without it." At Stop the failure ends (a barrier), and the stage runs again from the next block. Built-in stages after it in the chain keep running and may generate notes of their own after the barrier.
5. **Exports report a silent stage.** An offline render (bounce, freeze, export, and Capture MIDI Effect Output once it exists) in which a hosted MIDI stage is silent for any part of the render (not installed, could not be created, does not write notes, could not restart, failed) completes, and its result carries one entry per such stage naming the plug-in, the track and the state, for example "Keys: <name> (MIDI effect) is not installed; the export has no notes from it." The offline render does not report this today, so the result entry is new work (slice HM3). A bypassed stage is not reported: the user asked for it.
6. **The in-process limit (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** Hosted plug-ins run in the transit process, as instruments and inserts do. A creation, editor or state call that stops responding is handled by the existing plug-in host machinery (ADR 0018: Not responding, Retry, giving up on a stuck thread), unchanged. A native `process` call that never returns hangs the audio thread, and a plug-in that crashes ends the process; this record does not claim otherwise, and the guarantees of decisions 5 and 6 hold only while the host keeps executing.

### 8. Session, parameters, automation, presets

1. **Model.** A hosted stage is a `MidiEffectSlot` with the plug-in's id in `plugin`, its state in `state` (base64, as the field was reserved for) and its host-set parameter values in `params`, stored as a hosted insert stores them; `pages` as for any device (ADR 0047). No new field. The state is read and written by the state jobs of [ADR 0025](0025-plug-in-state-jobs-and-preset-containers.md) at save, as for inserts.
2. **Instances through the plan.** A hosted stage's instance is an `InstanceSpec` made off the audio thread by the existing `Handles` path (on macOS, inline on the calling thread, as every hosted instance is there), restored and prepared there, and carried by the plan to the instrument's event state, where `begin` takes it into its stage instead of calling `Builtin::new`. Its slot key is in a reserved range the slice defines (eight keys, one per chain position), disjoint from insert indexes, `INSTRUMENT_SLOT`, `RACK_SLOT` and clip-effect slots, and recognized by `clip_fx_of_slot` as not a clip slot; its `uid` is the slot's `InsertUid`, so a moved stage keeps its instance as a moved insert does. A displaced instance retires like a replaced instrument (back to its host thread through `Hosted`'s drop). Built-ins stay values made in place (ADR 0036's alternative "MIDI effects as plan cells" is taken for hosted stages only, where it was to be revisited).
3. **Parameters and automation.** A hosted stage's parameters are its plug-in's (`PluginInfo::params` of the instance) and are automated exactly as a hosted insert's: each lane point through `set_param_at` at its offset (`send_points`), so automation is placed by sample and the same at every block partition; lanes use the stage's slot 240 + index, as today. Editor edits are reported and written to the session as for hosted inserts.
4. **Replace keeps the lanes.** A new command, `mix.midi_effect_replace {track?, index, plugin}`, puts another effect (hosted or built-in) in an existing slot in one undo step, keeping the slot's automation lanes; a lane that names a parameter the new effect lacks stays and is inert, as for a replaced insert (`automation.rs:182` to `190`), and works again if the original plug-in returns. Removing a slot still removes its lanes (`remove_midi_effect`, unchanged). The card's `⋯` menu gains Replace with the same menu as "+".
5. **Presets.** A hosted stage's presets are its plug-in's own preset files (`.vstpreset`, `.clappreset`) through the existing plug-in preset commands (`crates/engine/src/cmd/plugin_presets.rs`), whose slot address gains the MIDI effect positions (`midi1` to `midi8`, the names the automation lane ids use). ADR 0057's JSON factory and user presets are for built-ins and do not apply (a hosted state is opaque). A preset recall is a state load, so a barrier (decision 6).
6. **Racks.** See decision 10.

### 9. The card, the editor window, the help

1. **Card.** A hosted stage is one card in ADR 0057's card row (K2a), at the shared height: the header every card has (power for bypass, the name, fold, `⋯` with Replace, Presets and Remove; Capture MIDI Effect Output joins that menu when ADR 0057's slice CAP lands, and until then there is no Capture entry), the Edit button that opens the plug-in's editor window, or the host parameter editor for a plug-in without one (below), the macro strip page when the stage has one (ADR 0047), a state line (Loading, Slow, Not responding with Retry, Not installed, Could not be created, Does not write notes, Could not restart, Failed until Stop; for each silent state the words "silent; bypass to hear the notes without it"; "Reads note output 1 of n" when the plug-in declares several) and the line "Audio from this plug-in is not used here". No parameter rows on the card: a hosted plug-in's interface is its own window or the host parameter editor.

   **A plug-in without an editor: the host parameter editor (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** Many hosted plug-ins ship without a custom editor and expect the host to show their parameters. Today transit shows nothing for them: a hosted insert or instrument without an editor has its Edit button disabled with the tip "This plug-in has no editor" (`crates/ui-studio/src/devices.rs:600` and `:628`), and its parameters are reached only through automation lanes. ADR 0047's macro strip does not solve this: it shows eight controls per page, derives hosted pages only for a table of at most 64 parameters (none above that; the landed resolver, `crates/engine/src/device_pages.rs`), takes a CLAP plug-in's remote pages as the author's selection, and fills user pages by touching controls in the device's editor, which such a plug-in does not have. So the strip never guarantees access to every parameter. Decision: when a hosted plug-in has no custom editor, its Edit button is **enabled** and opens the **host parameter editor**: a device window holding a scrollable list of every editable parameter in the plug-in's registry table (hidden and read-only parameters left out, as the registries already drop them), each row a choice list, a toggle or a value slider with its formatted value, double-click resetting to the default. It is a permanent surface, kept after the macro strip lands, so it is not an interim one. It is built by extracting the generic rows that built-in editors already use (`param_row` and the scrollable grid in `crates/ui-studio/src/builtin_editor.rs:233` to `300`: choices, toggles, values, reset, current-value feedback through `followed_param`, MIDI Learn's context menu and marks) into a component with device-specific command dispatch: `mix.insert_param` and `mix.instrument_param` for hosted inserts and instruments (which already accept hosted parameters through the registry tables, `crates/engine/src/cmd/mix.rs:435` onward, `require_info`), and `mix.midi_effect_param` for a hosted MIDI stage; undo merging of a drag into one step (`execute_ui_merged`, as `set_builtin_param` does, `builtin_editor.rs:108` onward) and the automation touch (`touch_param`) are kept. The same component serves editor-less hosted **inserts and instruments** in the same slice, HM4: their disabled Edit button is the same defect, and the component needs no plumbing for them beyond what exists. MIDI Learn reaches a hosted MIDI stage's parameters through the same addresses (`plugin:midi<n>:<id>`); the automation lane picker, which lists hosted insert and instrument parameters today (`crates/ui-studio/src/automation.rs:118` to `130`) but MIDI effects only from the built-in table (`:87` to `97`), is extended to hosted stages in HM3.
2. **Editor.** The plug-in's own editor window through the existing editor machinery (`EditorOpened { track, slot }` with the stage's reserved slot key; ADR 0018, [ADR 0030](0030-editor-window-cohesion.md)), with the same header, not-responding handling and window state as an insert's editor. ADR 0057's decision 14.1 (MIDI effects are cards, not device windows) is about built-in devices' panels and is not changed.
3. **Help.** One help entry for hosted MIDI effects: what qualifies, that the first readable output is read, that output replaces input (pedals included, with the sustain limit), that its audio is not used, the drop list (decision 3), pairing and its limits (5.4, 5.5), the barriers (6), the stage states and bypass (7), the latency caveat (10.1), determinism, that live playback and an export can give different notes for some plug-ins, and Capture with its own limits once it exists (10.3), and that its notes stay on its track until routing exists (decision 11).

### 10. Latency, racks, determinism

1. **Latency.** This is what lets a hosted note effect place notes early: a plug-in that reports a latency `L` buffers its input by `L` and writes each note up to `L` after the input that caused it; delay compensation shifts everything else on the track by `L` to match, so a note the plug-in writes less than `L` after its cause is heard early relative to the rest, by up to `L`. Nothing is emitted into time already processed: the early placement is compensation for the plug-in's own buffering, exactly as ADR 0057's centered Humanize does through decision 7 (requested and provisioned latency, deferred activation). A hosted stage's reported latency (VST3 `getLatencySamples`, CLAP `latency.get`, already read by the adapters into `Plugin::latency`) enters ADR 0057's MIDI effect latency `L_midi` (decision 7, slice P4) as a stage's requested figure, read when the plan is built. The formats report one latency for the plug-in and none for its note output, so the figure may describe its audio path and not its notes; for such a plug-in the track's compensation is wrong by the difference, and the help says so. A change of a hosted stage's latency arrives only through a restart (both formats; Context), which is a barrier (decision 6); the new figure is requested at the next plan build and provisioned when that plan is adopted. Unlike a built-in, a hosted plug-in cannot play at its provisioned figure until then (ADR 0057's deferred activation, `set_provisioned_latency`), so between its restart and that adoption the track is compensated for the old figure, for as long as the replanning takes; no bound on that interval is claimed.
2. **Racks: refused until the rack stage.** A rack's chain runs once per member until ADR 0036's rack stage exists (ADR 0057, 15), which for a hosted plug-in would mean one instance per member, each with its own internal state and its own processing cost. The add and replace commands refuse a hosted id on a rack ("Hosted MIDI effects go on an instrument track until racks run one chain for all members"); a session that has one (hand-edited) loads it silent with the state "Not available on a rack" and a load note. Reopens with the rack stage, which also gives one generator several synths without routing (decision 11).
3. **Play equals export, and what the host cannot do.** The host gives a hosted stage the same input, transport and parameter points at the same frames in playback and in an offline render, and matches its output by the same rules; a plug-in whose output is a function of those, from the same initial state, and does not depend on how its calls are partitioned, gives the same notes. A plug-in with internal free-running or random state (a generative performer, a random arpeggiator seeded from the clock) can give different notes in every render, every playback and every loop pass, and no host can make it repeat. Stated plainly in the help: for such a plug-in, live playback, an export and a later Capture can each produce different notes, and the release that makes hosted MIDI effects visible cannot record the hosted chain's heard MIDI performance. **Capture MIDI Effect Output** (ADR 0057, decision 9, slice CAP) is planned and is not a prerequisite of hosting: it renders the range offline and writes what the instrument accepted as an editable MIDI clip on a new track. When it lands, the help and its result line, for a source chain holding a hosted stage, say: the captured clip holds the notes of the capture's own render, which for such a plug-in is a new performance, not the one heard while playing; once captured, the clip's MIDI data is fixed, so it plays the same notes everywhere (the sound is then the destination instrument's, which may itself vary); and Capture's own limits apply (positions to the nearest tick, notes shorter than a tick written one tick long, the clip renderer's order at one tick, same-key overlaps replayed as re-strikes). Recording what the chain plays live, so the heard performance itself can be kept, is a separate feature (Out of scope).

### 11. Destination: the same track now, routing the next record (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation)

**Decision.** In every slice of this record a hosted stage's notes go to the next stage or the instrument of its own track, nowhere else. Routing a track's MIDI chain output to another track's input, or to an external MIDI port, is the next record, **"MIDI routing between tracks and to external ports"** (to be written; issue to be filed when the maintainer schedules it). That routing is the reported user's actual request (notes from the generator driving synths on other tracks), and the first slices do not complete it.

**Why it is its own record.** Ableton documents routing a VST plug-in's MIDI output to another track ([Accessing the MIDI output of a VST plug-in](https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/209070189-Accessing-the-MIDI-output-of-a-VST-plug-in)), so the workflow exists and transit should offer it; designing it touches more than this record does: the plan's render order (a destination must render after its source within one block, or the route costs a block and breaks partition independence), cycles, MIDI tracks with no instrument (today the chain is only on instrument tracks and racks; ADR 0036, [ADR 0022](0022-external-midi-output.md)), external ports and their clocking (ADR 0022, [ADR 0043](0043-hardware-sync-transport-and-clock.md)), identities qualified by their source, barrier propagation to each destination, and bounded fan-out admission. The stage is useful on its own track from the first slice, so it does not wait.

**Non-preclusion constraints** on these slices (they keep the seam open; they are not a routing specification).

1. The chain's output after the last stage is a per-block stream a second consumer could read without changing what the instrument receives; nothing in these slices may make it exist only inside the instrument's admission. Downstream of an active hosted stage that stream is identified by host ids; a bypassed hosted stage passes its input's identities, and built-in generators write anonymous notes, so the stream as a whole is not fully identified and a route must qualify identities by source.
2. Ownership stays per hop: a destination will own what it accepted from a route by its own tables, never by the source instrument's.
3. Barriers on the source end the source's notes from the host's tables; nothing here may put a destination's notes in those tables, and nothing here may assume a barrier has only one destination.
4. The tap is after the chain's stages, before the source instrument's admission, so a destination does not depend on the source instrument's refusals; Capture's tap (what the instrument accepted) stays as it is.
5. No interim surface: no "send to track" control, no hidden route, no per-stage destination field in the model before the routing record is accepted. Until then, the plug-in's notes reach another track only by putting the plug-in on that track, or, once they land, by Capture (an offline performance, 10.3) copied to the synth's track or by a rack (10.2).

## Out of scope

- Audio Unit MIDI processors (type `aumi`). The Audio Unit host has no MIDI output path, and an AU MIDI processor's output arrives through a callback the host does not register; not trivial, so not here.
- Routing to other tracks and external ports (decision 11's record). Recording what the chain plays live.
- More than one output bus or port per plug-in; MIDI 2, system exclusive, VST3 legacy pitch bend and poly pressure output.
- Hosted stages on racks (10.2), until ADR 0036's rack stage.
- Per-note expression other than pressure.
- Running a hosted plug-in out of process.

## Consequences

- Any VST3 or CLAP plug-in that declares a readable note output can feed a track's instrument from the MIDI effect slot, including plug-ins other hosts list as instruments.
- Every note a hosted plug-in writes is given a host id once the stage has matched it, so everything downstream addresses it exactly; where a plug-in's own addresses are ambiguous (reused or duplicate ids, anonymous or wildcard releases, stale releases after a barrier) a note can be cut short, and is never left sounding past the next barrier, subject to decision 6's conditions.
- A scan lists every file as today and then creates every class once in a child of its own; the first scan of a build with HM0 lists and probes every file again, and a probe that fails costs only that class's MIDI menu entry.
- A plug-in in the slot costs its full processing, its audio is discarded, and its reported latency, which may describe its audio rather than its notes, delays the track.
- Restarts and state loads of a hosted stage cut the track's sounding notes, like an edit.
- A loading, missing, failed or unrestartable stage is silent until it recovers or the user bypasses it; a failed stage stays silent until Stop.
- A pedal-down a plug-in forwards without its pedal-up sustains until the next edit or Stop.
- A plug-in with internal randomness is not repeatable: live playback and an export can differ, and the heard performance cannot be recorded in this release; Capture, when it lands, keeps one rendering of it as an editable clip.
- A hosted plug-in without its own editor, MIDI effect, insert or instrument, gets a host parameter editor listing every parameter.
- Notes stay on their track until the routing record; the reported other-track request is not complete with these slices.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Qualifying by category** (CLAP `note-effect`, a VST3 "Fx" sub-category). Excludes the reported case and every instrument-category note generator; categories are the vendor's label for menus, not a description of buses.
- **Probing inside the listing child.** A probe that crashes or hangs would lose the whole file's listing, instruments and inserts included.
- **Accepting file-level loss on a probe failure.** Rejected by delegation: a scan change must not remove plug-ins that list today.
- **Probing the capability only when the plug-in is placed.** The menu could not list what qualifies without creating every plug-in in the app process.
- **Reading the first output whatever its dialect.** A CLAP plug-in with a MIDI 2 port first would qualify and pass nothing.
- **Mixing a hosted stage's audio into the track, or refusing plug-ins with audio outputs.** Mixing makes the slot an instrument by another name and contradicts "before the instrument"; refusing excludes the reported case.
- **Forwarding input controllers and pedals around the plug-in.** Undoes processing the plug-in does on purpose; a host-invented pedal-up would be a change the user did not ask for.
- **Translating into `PluginEvent` before matching.** Loses ports and wildcards.
- **Matching in write order.** An off written before its on in write order but later in time would close the note before it opens downstream.
- **Trusting the plug-in's pairing (ADR 0057 16.2 as is).** Leaves downstream pairing oldest-first and every ambiguity unbounded.
- **Reusing ADR 0057's kit ledger.** Its anonymous offs close anonymous entries only; the formats' rules need an anonymous single release to close an identified note and a wildcard to close several.
- **Detecting a restart by its latency or by the instance's presence.** A restart with an unchanged latency keeps both.
- **Settling the legacy pitch bend layout against a fixture written from the same guess.** Proves agreement, not correctness.
- **Using the host's carry for out-of-range offsets.** Gives meaning to offsets neither format defines.
- **Calling a hosted plug-in for events between blocks.** Advances its time with no audio time passing.
- **Passing input through when a stage is loading, missing or failed.** Plays raw input (an arpeggiator's held chords) into the synth without the user asking.
- **A per-stage destination field now.** An interim surface the routing record would replace.
- **One instance per rack member.** Independent random states and N times the cost, for a structure the rack stage will replace.

## Choices made without a rule to follow (for the review)

Each is decided above; each is listed so a reviewer can test it.

1. Out-of-range output offsets clamped as host policy, not carried (decision 3).
2. Hosted stages wait for the next actual processing call instead of a between-blocks call (4.6).
3. The stage ledger's matching rules by form, and its own table instead of the kit ledger (5.3, 5.8).
4. Restart, refused reactivation and state load are barriers, observed through the lifecycle generation (decision 6).
5. The VST3 `setProcessing` cycle as the stage reset, for MIDI stages only, with `kNotImplemented` accepted and other results failing the stage (decision 6).
6. The failing block: the whole block's stage output discarded, not only the failing call's (7.4).
7. Reported latency taken at its word for a note generator (10.1).
8. Hosted stages refused on racks (10.2).
9. A failed scan probe hides the class from the MIDI menu, with the reason in diagnostics (2.6).
10. VST3 legacy pitch bend and poly pressure output dropped until documented (decision 3).

## For the maintainer to confirm or overturn

Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation, each in one sentence:

1. A bypassed hosted MIDI effect passes notes through, and a loading, not responding, missing, unrestartable or failed one is silent (it ends its notes and passes nothing until it recovers or you bypass it), with a failed one silent until Stop.
2. A hosted MIDI effect's output replaces its whole input, controllers and pedals included, so a pedal-down it forwards without the pedal-up sustains until the next edit or Stop, and the host never invents a pedal-up.
3. A plug-in qualifies if any of its outputs carries notes transit can read, and the stage reads the first such output and no other, saying so on the card when there are several.
4. The first slices deliver the plug-in's notes only to its own track, and routing them to other tracks and external ports, which is what the reported user asked for, is the next record.
5. Hosted MIDI effects run in the transit process like every hosted plug-in, so a plug-in whose processing hangs or crashes can stop audio or the app, and the note guarantees hold only while the host keeps running.
6. A note plug-in with no audio bus or port at all is hosted as it is, with no audio invented for it.
7. A hosted plug-in without its own editor, whether a MIDI effect, an insert or an instrument, gets an enabled Edit button that opens a host parameter editor listing every editable parameter, kept for good after the macro strip arrives.
8. A plug-in whose probe shows it writes notes and has no audio output appears only in the MIDI effects menu, never in the instrument or insert menus, while a plug-in whose probe failed keeps today's category-based place.
9. Hosted MIDI effects become visible without waiting for Capture, so for a plug-in with random or free-running behavior, live playback and an export can give different notes and the heard performance cannot be recorded in that release.
