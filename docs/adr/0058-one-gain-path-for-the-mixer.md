# ADR 0058: One gain path for the mixer: per-sample, block-partition invariant

- **Status:** accepted 2026-11-12 (proposed 2026-11-12; settled after six judging rounds with edits, the revisions listed under Judging rounds 1 to 5; the product choices decided by the maintainer on 2026-11-12: the recommendations of the eleven open questions, Decided by the maintainer, with the clip fade law the closed law as ADR 0056 decides it; after acceptance, the same day, a convention audit checked every decided item against what Ableton Live and Logic Pro document, and the maintainer scheduled the automation latency follow-up for v0.8.0 and had the convention statements corrected (After acceptance: the convention audit, below))
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Implementation:** not started. Slices in the Slices section. Fixes #501 (P1), #526 and #517; adds rows to #495.
- **Reopen when:** a cost benchmark of decision 17 exceeds its budget on the reference machine and the fallback also fails it (for G3, the uncached measurement misses, the qualified promise of decided item 9 applies, and the maintainer sets its uncached budget); a measurement on small hardware (ADR 0037's Pi 5 run) puts the gain path over a budget the maintainer sets for it; the maintainer wants mute automation to step exactly (decided item 1), different ramp times (decided item 2) or a different phase-invert behavior (decided item 3); Ableton Live or Logic Pro is found to document fader smoothing, mute fades or mute automation timing that disagrees with this record; built-in device parameters or rack macros are made sample-accurate, or sample-accurate device-parameter automation is taken up (they would join the block-size claim, and device lanes might then move to decision 2's evaluator); automation is moved to the latency-compensated position (Out of scope); ADR 0056's fade law changes (decision 13 follows it).

## Context

### The defects

The mixer applies gains in four places, and in three of them the gain a sample receives depends on how the timeline was cut into audio blocks. A professional checks this by rendering the same session at two buffer sizes, or bouncing it and recording playback, and subtracting: the result should be exactly zero, and today it is not.

**#501 (P1): fader, mute and send automation read at block edges.** `process_strip_fader` (`crates/mix/src/strip.rs`) reads the volume lane at the block's first and last sample (`volume_db_at`, `crates/mix/src/automation.rs`), adds the trim lane and the VCA's lane in `f32` dB, clamps at +12, and ramps linearly in gain between the two converted ends (`lerp_gain`, which converts both ends on every call, per sample per channel, through `transit_dsp::db_to_gain`, an `f32` `powf`). The lane itself is linear in dB. So a breakpoint inside a block is never read, the curve between the ends is a chord, and the level depends on the block size. The master fader (`process_master`) has the same shape without the +12 clamp. The mute lane is read once at the block start and applied as a hard step for the whole block (`strip.muted`, one `bool`). A send's level is read once at the block start and held (`send_level`, `crates/mix/src/automation.rs`), and a send whose level converts to 0 at the block start skips the whole block even if its lane rises inside it. The send mute and send pan lanes are never read at all: `route_into` (`crates/mix/src/routing.rs`) uses the stored `snd.mute` and `snd.pan`, although the UI can create both lanes.

When one block carries D dB of change, the chord's error at its midpoint is `20·log10((1 + 10^(−D/20))/2) + D/2` dB: 0.51 dB at D = 6, 6.5 dB at D = 24, 24.0 dB at D = 60 (computed).

**The worked example (#501's minimal case).** Constant input 1.0, one track, a volume lane with breakpoints (0, 0 dB), (100, 0 dB), (356, −24 dB). The lane's closed form is `gain(n) = 10^(dB(n)/20)`. Computed gains at the probe samples:

In a model with a stand-in `exp2`, each of its 4,096 samples came out as the correctly rounded `f32` of the closed form (computed; see Evidence); what is guaranteed is decision 3's contract, within one rounding step, not correct rounding.

**#526: hand moves click.** A fader, trim, pan or send move, a mute, an unmute or a solo made by hand reaches the audio thread with the next published snapshot and applies as a hard step at the next block's first sample. A mute of sustained material is a full-scale step. `Strip` keeps no gain from the previous block. The monitor stage in `transit-playback` already ramps from its previous gain, so the pattern exists in the codebase.

**#517: clip fades and envelopes anchored to the block.** `mix_clip` (`crates/mix/src/clips.rs`) evaluates the clip's gain every 32 samples and interpolates linearly between the first and last sample of each segment, and the segments start at each block's first frame. A curved fade or a gain envelope therefore renders differently at each block size, and a fade-out never reaches 0 on the clip's last sample (its last value is `gain(1/len)`). ADR 0056 (the accepted audio clip editor record) made the fix its slice 0 until its third judging round; it now states that clip fades and gain envelopes use this record's gain path and keeps only their clip-time placement and editing (decision 13).

**Track pan is the model.** Pan automation is held per 64-sample timeline cell (`pan_segment`, `PAN_STEP`), so it is already independent of the block size, but it is a staircase: for a full left-to-right sweep in 480 samples one cell step changes the near channel's gain by about 1.8 dB at the center (computed, −3 dB law).

### What "block-partition invariant" means, and why it is a null-test claim

For a fixed session snapshot S and a timeline range R rendered from live state at rest (fresh strips), cutting R into any sequence of blocks (any sizes up to the engine's largest block, including the sub-blocks `render_active` makes at tempo and meter changes, `crates/mix/src/tempo_split.rs`) yields bit-identical output from the gain path, and a render started at any sample inside R yields the same samples as the full render from that sample on. It is a property a professional can check without access to the code: bounce the session at one buffer size, play it at another, subtract. This record makes it true for the mixer's gain path and says so on the claims page (#495). It does not make a whole session block-invariant: built-in device parameters and rack macros still take automation at block starts (ADR 0055 says so in its promise; Out of scope).

Live state (hand ramps, the hand gate, Touch returns) is not a function of the snapshot, so the claim for it is narrower and still testable: for an identical sequence of snapshot events arriving at identical samples, the output does not depend on how the samples between the events are cut into blocks (decision 1).

### What the reference workstations document

- **Logic Pro** documents a setting, Sample Accurate Automation, with the options Off ("Minimal overhead on computer performance for automation playback"), "Volume, Pan, Sends" ("Only these parameters are automated with sample accuracy") and "Volume, Pan, Sends, Plug-in Parameters" (Logic Pro X 10.4 help, General Audio preferences: https://help.apple.com/logicpro/mac/10.4/en.lproj/lgcp0ed343a9.html, read for this record and confirmed again in the convention audit on this archived page only; the current guide was not checked). The same 10.4 help documents Plug-in Latency Compensation and its scopes but says nothing about whether automation is compensated. So the professional expectation for the mixer's volume, pan and sends is sample accuracy. Mute is not in that list, and the page says nothing about smoothing or ramp times.
- **Ableton Live**: the Delay Compensation FAQ (https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/209072409-Delay-Compensation-FAQ) is indexed as saying Live "automatically compensates audio, automation, and modulation by offsetting all tracks". The page refused a direct fetch four times (HTTP 403, at the URL above, three times for this record, the last during the second judging round, and once more in the convention audit), so the wording comes from a search index, not a reading, and stays **unverified**; forum posts attributed to Ableton staff say the opposite for older versions. It is cited only as the reason automation timing against latency is a real question (Out of scope), not as a convention this record follows. Ableton documents one automation resolution: its manual's Audio Fact Sheet chapter says "Volume automation curves are updated for each audio sample" ([Audio Fact Sheet](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/audio-fact-sheet/), found in the convention audit), which supports this record's per-sample volume; it says nothing about pan, sends, smoothing or mute.
- **Not found** for either product, after searching for this record: whether fader moves are smoothed and over what time, whether mute and solo fade, and whether mute automation steps or ramps. **The 20 ms and 5 ms ramp times and the mute ramp in this record are engineering choices, not a cited convention**, decided by the maintainer on 2026-11-12 (decided items 1 and 2). 20 ms equals the write pass's point spacing (`WRITE_STEP_S`, `crates/ui-studio/src/automation.rs`), so a pass records no finer than the smoothing it hears; it does not make a recorded pass reproduce the hand performance (decision 8). 5 ms is long enough to keep a switch on sustained low-frequency material from clicking and short enough to sound like a switch.
- **Touch** follows Logic and Ableton as today (`docs/contributing/knowledge/automation-modes.md`, #188): a held control's lane is not read; on release the parameter returns over the session's Touch return time (that Logic names its setting Ramp Time is not verified).

### Ranges already settled

The fader strip's taper tops out at +6 dB (the design language, `crates/ui-studio/src/widgets/fader.rs`), while stored volume and send levels, volume and send lanes, and the model's inherited taper (`fader_pos_to_db`, `crates/model/src/mixer.rs`) reach +12 dB. The maintainer settled #357: a stored gain above +6 dB is shown as it is and never clamped or rewritten; new edits from lanes, MIDI Learn and send drags agree with the +6 dB fader. So the render ceiling stays at the stored range, +12 dB: a stored +9 dB plays at +9 dB. Restricting new edits is the UI's work (#357), not the mixer's. Clip gain is converted with a +48 dB cap today (`crates/model/src/clip.rs`); that cap stays. The static trim is stored up to +24 dB.

## Decision

Notation: `n` is an absolute timeline sample (`Samples`, `i64`). "Rounded once" means an `f64` value converted to `f32` with `as f32` (round to nearest, ties to even). `C = DB_TO_LOG2_F64` = log2(10)/20 as the `f64` literal `0.16609640474436813` (`transit_math::consts`). A *stage* is one gain the mixer applies: a strip's fader stage (level and gate), its static trim, the master's level, each send's gain, each pan matrix, each clip's gain. An *in-place* stage rewrites a buffer (trim, fader stage, master); a *summing* stage adds into a destination (a clip into the strip buffer, a send or the output pan matrix into a bus or the main mix).

### 1. The rule

Every gain the mixer applies is a pure function of the session snapshot and the absolute timeline sample, evaluated per sample from its closed form in `f64` and rounded once to `f32`: each stage's gain is one `f64` expression of its factors (decisions 4, 6, 7, 11, 13), and only its result becomes `f32`. Every intermediate (a lane value, a dB sum, a converted level, a hand-smoothed level, a gate ratio, a Touch blend, a pan coefficient between knots, a fade factor) stays `f64` until the stage's final expression. The only `f32` values that enter a stage's expression are intentional inputs: stored session values (lane points, static levels, curvatures, clip gain, the Touch base) widened exactly, and the pan law's outputs at its knots (decision 10), because that law is an `f32` function. No gain that was rounded to `f32` is used again in gain arithmetic; decision 14 lists the audit. Two stages applied one after the other to the audio (a send's gain, then its pan coefficient; the fader stage, then the output pan matrix) are two gains, each rounded once, applied to `f32` audio in a fixed order. A block is only a loop bound: nothing is read at a block's ends, nothing is interpolated across a block, and no decision about a gain is taken once per block unless the value is constant over the whole block by construction (decision 5). The only inputs that are not a function of the snapshot and `n` are the live state of decisions 6 to 8 (hand ramps, the hand gate, Touch returns), which a fixed snapshot rendered from rest never sets in motion, and which advance one step per rendered sample, so for an identical sequence of snapshot events at identical samples they too are independent of the block partition.

### 2. The lane value

For a lane with points sorted stably by time `(a_i, v_i)` (stored `f32` values, widened exactly to `f64`):

- **Breakpoints sharing a time** form a *group*. A group's value at its own sample is its **first** point's value; the segment after it starts from its **last** point's value. This is today's meaning in the clip envelope (`env_value`) and the one ADR 0056 9.6 c requires; for mixer lanes it replaces `value_at`'s mixed rule (first value at the lane's first time, last value at any later shared time).
- Let `g_0` be the first group (time `a_0`) and `g_last` the last (time `a_last`). `L(n)` = the first value of `g_0` for `n ≤ a_0`, the last value of `g_last` for `n > a_last`; at `n = a` of any group, its first value.
- For `a < n < b`, with `a`, `b` consecutive group times, `u` the last value of the group at `a` and `w` the first value of the group at `b`: `L(n) = u + (w − u) · ((n − a) as f64 / (b − a) as f64)`, in that order (difference, ratio, product, sum).
- Step lanes (Mute, SendMute) give `u` on `a < n < b` and the group's first value at `n = a`.
- **Incoming and outgoing values.** Each group time `a` has an *incoming* value (the group's first value: what the lane is at `a` and what the segment arriving at `a` ends on) and an *outgoing* value (its last value: what the segment leaving `a` starts from); for a single point they are equal. Any consumer that interpolates a quantity derived from the lane between group times (the pan knots of decision 10) uses the outgoing value at the left end of an interval and the incoming value at its right end, so a vertical step keeps this rule: the step happens on the sample after the group's time, never smeared across the following interval.
- **Which lanes use it: the mixer's gain lanes and clip envelopes, nothing else.** This record is about the mixer's gain path; device-parameter lanes do not move to this evaluator. From G1a the evaluator exists in `transit_model` as a function over a slice of `(time, value)` pairs, so a lane's points and a clip envelope's `(Samples, f32)` pairs both use it, with `AutomationLane::gain_value_at` as the lane's wrapper. It serves the *mixer gain lanes*: Volume (a VCA's Volume lane included), the trim lane (`AutoParam::Plugin { slot: u8::MAX, param: "trim" }`, a mixer lane despite its plug-in encoding: today it is read through the mixer's reader, `strip.rs:313-317`), Pan, Mute, SendLevel, SendPan and SendMute (a predicate `AutoParam::is_mixer_gain` names exactly these), and clip gain envelopes (decision 13).
- **Device-parameter lanes keep today's evaluator and delivery, unchanged and separately named.** Every other lane (insert and instrument parameters, rack macros, MIDI-effect parameters) keeps today's `AutomationLane::value_at` arithmetic, moved verbatim under the name `AutomationLane::device_value_at` (a pure rename: `f32` interpolation, the first point's value at or before the lane's first time, the group's last value at an interior shared time), and today's timed delivery to plug-ins that take sample-accurate parameters stays as it is (`crates/mix/src/automation.rs:64-66` selects the breakpoints after the block's start value and before its end; `145-153` sends each at its offset). The runtime readers split cleanly by function, and none needs both evaluators: `touch::plugin_value` (with `apply_values`' timed points and `apply_idle_freeze`), the rack macro reads (`crates/mix/src/rack.rs:137, 158`) and the MIDI-effect parameter read (`crates/mix/src/midi_fx.rs:231`) call `device_value_at`; `touch::lane_value` (volume, trim lane, pan and send level: `automation.rs:178, 190, 219`, `strip.rs:316-317`), the mute read (`strip.rs:293`, until G2's gate replaces it) and the clip envelope call the gain evaluator. For a device lane this keeps today's behavior exactly. Example, the lane (0, 0.5), (64, 0), (64, 1), (128, 0.5), an interior group at sample 64 holding 0 then 1: a hosted plug-in rendering a block that contains 64 receives both events at offset 64 and holds 1 to the end of that block; a block starting at 64 starts it at `device_value_at(64)` = 1 and re-sends neither event. So every tested partition delivers 1 at sample 64. Subsequent block-start updates keep today's partition-dependent interpolation: a block starting at 65 starts at `device_value_at(65)` = 0.9921875, so sample 65 receives 1 with one 128-sample block but 0.9921875 with the partition 65 + 63, and one-sample blocks reach 0.5078125 at sample 127. Each partition's delivered values and events must match the base commit (reproduced in a model of today's `apply_params` block-start read and `apply_values` delivery over six partitions: 128, 64 + 64, 32 × 4, 63 + 65, 65 + 63 and 1 × 128). Moving device lanes alone to this evaluator would have broken that agreement: a block starting at 64 would start at 0 while a block containing 64 ends on 1.
- **Readers that serve every kind of lane select by the lane's kind.** `AutomationLane::value_at(at, default)` keeps its name and becomes a dispatcher on the lane's own `param`: for a mixer gain lane, the gain evaluator rounded once to `f32`; otherwise `device_value_at`. Each call evaluates one lane, so no caller needs both values for one lane. Through the dispatcher, unedited: the lane editor's readouts, curve and point display (`crates/ui-studio/src/automation/lane.rs:155-179`, and through it `crates/ui-studio/src/automation.rs:401, 719, 762`), so for a mixer lane the editor shows exactly what the mixer applies and for a device lane what the device receives today; and the edit commands that write anchor points from a lane's value (`crates/engine/src/cmd/edit_more.rs:308, 312, 788, 887-900`, `cmd/mix.rs:821, 875-876`, `cmd/track_more.rs:457-458`, `AutomationLane::write_range`), whose anchors on a mixer lane now take the played value (different from today's only at duplicate-time groups and by today's `f32` interpolation rounding). The one computation that combines two lanes (`track_more.rs:457-458`, a Volume lane plus a trim lane or a VCA's Volume lane) combines two mixer lanes.
- **Known limit, not changed here:** device parameters stay block-quantized (a built-in takes the block-start value; a plug-in with sample-accurate parameters also receives each breakpoint at its offset, but not the ramp between), with today's duplicate-point behavior (the group's last value at an interior shared time, where mixer lanes read the first) and today's `f32` positional division (decided item 7). Sample-accurate device-parameter automation, and whether device lanes then move to this evaluator with matching timed delivery, is a later record or issue (Out of scope); it is not designed here.

**Exact at breakpoints:** at a group's time the evaluator returns the stored first value itself, selected, not interpolated, so no interpolation residue exists (ADR 0056 9.6 a); a segment whose two values are equal gives that value exactly at every sample. The positions are integers and the division is in `f64`, so a lane hours long has no positional rounding (today's `value_at` divides in `f32`, which loses precision past 2^24 samples, about six minutes at 48 kHz).

**Bounded traversal:** points are stored as written (`Session::sanitize` sorts lanes stably and drops no duplicate; ADR 0056 9.2 keeps clip envelope points the same way). The plan compiler, off the audio thread, builds for every mixer gain lane and clip envelope the plan reads a group index (for each point, the index of its group's last member, a `u32`; not saved), as part of the plan's prepared storage and admitted against the plan limit before it is allocated (decision 16). Construction and admission are in slice G1a, which both G1 (lanes) and G3 (envelopes) depend on. Device-parameter lanes need no index (their readers keep today's `partition_point` slices). The audio thread finds its place with one `partition_point` per block per lane and then walks forward, crossing each group in one step through the index, so the work per sample is at most one group crossing, independent of how many points share a time. The index changes cost, never values: an evaluator off the audio thread (the editor's) may walk the points without it and returns the same value. A lane with more points than a `u32` can index refuses the plan (decision 16). Which lanes rule in which automation mode is unchanged: a lane rules when the track's mode reads (`AutomationMode::reads`), the lane has points, and its control is not held in Touch; the Volume lane is not read in Write.

### 3. One conversion: its domain, caps, floor and accuracy

- `transit_math::gain` gains `db_to_gain_f64(d: f64, cap: f64) -> f32`, and an unrounded form `db_to_gain_f64_unrounded(d, cap) -> f64` used inside the stage expressions of decision 1. Domain: every `f64`. `0.0` if `d` is NaN or `d ≤ −144` (−∞ included); else `exp2(min(d, cap) · C)` in `f64` through the seam (+∞ gives the cap), and the rounded form rounds that once.
- **Caps** are named constants, never computed: `FADER_CAP_DB` = +12 for the fader stage, the master and send levels (the stored range, #357), `CLIP_CAP_DB` = +48 for clip gain (today's clip cap), `TRIM_CAP_DB` = +24 for the static trim (its stored range). A cap is finite and at most `MAX_DB` (+200); the function debug-asserts it. An out-of-range value from a file renders at the cap.
- **Floor:** at or below −144 dB the gain is exactly 0.0. A lane fading to −144 dB therefore steps from about 6.3·10⁻⁸ (−144 dB) to 0 on the breakpoint, 144 dB below full scale.
- **Equivalence with today's `f32` helper, restricted:** `db_to_gain_f64(f64::from(x), cap)` is bit-identical to `transit_math::gain::db_to_gain(x.min(cap))` for every **non-NaN** `f32` `x` and every cap above, because widening is exact and `min` commutes with it (checked on 200,000 random inputs in the model). It fails for NaN: Rust's `f32::min` returns the other operand when one is NaN, so `NaN.min(12.0)` is 12 and `db_to_gain(12)` is 3.981071710586548, while the new function returns 0.0 for NaN before it clamps. The statement for every input is therefore: `db_to_gain_f64(f64::from(x), cap)` equals `db_to_gain(if x.is_nan() { x } else { x.min(cap) })`. G1a tests NaN explicitly (quiet and signaling patterns, both signs) instead of relying on random inputs. It is **not** equal to `db_to_gain(x)` when `x` exceeds the cap: with `x` = 20 dB and the +12 dB cap the new function gives about 3.9810717 where `db_to_gain(20)` gives exactly 10. So equivalence holds only for matching, explicitly clamped inputs. The mixer does not call `transit_math` today: it calls `transit_dsp::db_to_gain`, an `f32` `powf`, so the conversion of every gain the slices move changes to the exact path (decision 19, re-pins).
- **Accuracy contract for an arbitrary `f64` input.** ADR 0055's analysis (2.9) bounds the `f64` result's relative error by the argument's rounding (`|arg|·(2^−53 + 5.0·10⁻¹⁷)`, the product and the constant), propagated through `exp2` (factor ln 2), plus `exp2`'s own (below 0.503 `f64` ulp): below 2^−48.3 for `|arg|` ≤ 23.9 (−144 to +48 dB) and 2^−47.9 at 33.2 (+200 dB). That argument does not depend on the input being a widened `f32`, so it holds for every `f64` input in the domain: at most 6.4·10⁻⁸ of an `f32` ulp of the result. Rounding once then gives a **faithfully rounded** result: the `f32` returned is one of the two `f32` values that bracket the exact `10^(min(d, cap)/20)`, so it is within one `f32` ulp of the exact value, and it is identical on every platform (the seam). **Correct rounding is not claimed** for `f64` inputs: ADR 0055's exhaustive near-tie survey covers the roughly 2.2·10⁹ `f32` inputs only, and the `f64` inputs whose exact result lies within that bound of an `f32` midpoint are not a handful but dense (in the model, with the platform's `exp2` standing in for the seam, 20,000 inputs chosen at the nearest `f64` to the dB value of an `f32` midpoint all came out faithful, and only 13,467 of them correctly rounded). Slice G1a carries the tests: the decades and floor of ADR 0055 2.9 for an `f64` input, NaN and ±∞, monotonic over a sweep, the matching-clamp equivalence above, and adversarial inputs at the nearest `f64` to each midpoint of a set of `f32` gains across the range, each result checked against a higher-precision reference to be one of the two bracketing `f32` values.
- **Monotonicity, a requirement of its own.** Faithful rounding alone does not make the conversion monotonic: two inputs whose exact results straddle an `f32` midpoint by less than the error bound could round in the wrong order if `exp2` itself were not monotonic. So it is required separately: for `d1 ≤ d2`, `db_to_gain_f64(d1, cap) ≤ db_to_gain_f64(d2, cap)` (and the same for the unrounded form), over the whole domain, the floor's step from about 6.3·10⁻⁸ to 0 and the flat region above the cap included. **How it is evidenced:** the argument `min(d, cap) · C` is monotonic in `d` (rounded multiplication by a positive constant and `min` are monotonic) and rounding to `f32` is monotonic, so the property reduces to the seam's `f64` `exp2` being non-decreasing on the argument range [−23.92, 33.22]; that is a property of the seam's implementation, not of its error bound, and is evidenced, not proven. G1a checks, for each cap (+12, +24, +48) and for both the rounded and the unrounded form: **strict increase on the unsaturated grid through the selected cap** (ADR 0055's 0.01 dB grid from −143.99 dB up to and including the cap: no two of those grid points give the same value; in the model, with the platform's `exp2` standing in for the seam, this holds for all three caps, computed); **non-decrease over the complete grid, with explicit floor and cap plateaus** (every grid point at or below −144 dB gives exactly 0.0, and every grid point at or above the cap gives exactly the cap's value: with the +12 dB cap, 12.00 and 12.01 dB both give 3.981071710586548, which is correct behavior and is asserted as a plateau, not rejected); non-decrease over runs of consecutive `f64` inputs (one `f64` step apart) around each adversarial midpoint input, each decade, the floor at −144 dB and each cap; and non-decrease of the seam's `exp2` itself over consecutive `f64` arguments at those places. The consecutive-input runs can detect a local violation; they do not prove its absence. A violation found is a defect in the seam and stops the slice.

### 4. The fader stage

`D(n) = vol(n) + trimlane(n) + vca(n)`, summed in `f64` in that order, where each term is its ruling lane's value at `n` (decision 2, with the Touch return of decision 8 applied to the term) or else the static value widened: `vol` is the track's Volume lane or `mixer.volume_db`; `trimlane` is the trim lane (`AutoParam::Plugin { slot: 255, param: "trim" }`) or 0; `vca` is the VCA track's Volume lane or static volume, read with the VCA's Touch hold respected and no return ramp, as today. `level64(n) = db_to_gain_f64_unrounded(D(n), FADER_CAP_DB)`. Summing in dB before one conversion puts the only rounding at the end; a product of three converted gains would round three times. `level64` is the stage's target; the hand smoothing of decision 7 acts on it, and the gate of decision 6 multiplies the smoothed value, so the fader stage's gain is `f32(H(n) · gate64(n))` with `H` the hand-smoothed level (equal to `level64` when no hand ramp runs). A static VCA changes a member's level here too: today the sum is in `f32` (−24 dB with a static VCA at `f32(−1e-6)` dB sums to −24.000001907348633), under this record in `f64` (−24.000000999999997), so the converted gain moves from 0.06309571862220764 to 0.06309572607278824 (computed); this is in the re-pin inventory.

### 5. Applying a gain: one rule, and when a block may be treated as constant

**The application rule,** on every path (constant or moving, live or offline), per sample, with `g` the stage's rounded `f32` gain at that sample:

- **In-place stages** (trim, fader stage, master): `g == 0.0` writes `+0.0`, whatever the input; `g == 1.0` leaves the input's bits untouched; otherwise `x · g`.
- **Summing stages** (a clip into the strip buffer, a send's taps, the output pan matrix's taps), per destination sample: a term whose gain (or, for a matrix tap, whose send gain or coefficient) is exactly `0.0` at that sample does not contribute. **When no term contributes, the destination is not touched at all**, no addition of a zero accumulator included. Otherwise the contributing terms are added in today's order: for a clip, `dst += v · g`; for a matrix, `acc = +0.0`, then `acc += (x_s · s) · c_s` for each contributing source channel `s` in ascending order, then `dst += acc`, exactly as `apply_matrix_span` accumulates today (`crates/mix/src/matrix.rs:255-259`). There is no unity bypass on a summing stage: a term with gain 1.0 is still added.

**What is promised, and what is not.** *Exact gain unity* is a property of the gain: a stage whose gain is exactly 1.0 multiplies by nothing (in place) or adds exactly `x` as its term (summing). *Source bits preserved* is a stronger property, and it holds only in place: a sum is IEEE arithmetic, and `+0 + (−0 · 1)` is `+0`, so a summing stage at unity does not pass a `−0.0` source through an empty destination. The promise between paths is: **for every input whose result is not NaN, the constant path and the moving path give bit-identical output**, because both apply the same rule with the same gain in the same order, and both skip the destination in the same samples. **A NaN result stays a NaN on both paths; its payload is unspecified**, because Rust does not guarantee the payload or the signaling bit of a NaN produced by arithmetic (the `f32` documentation's "NaN bit patterns"). The in-place unity branch is not arithmetic, so it keeps every input bit, a signaling NaN's payload included. The in-place zero branch writes `+0.0` whatever the input: `x · 0.0` would be `−0.0` for a negative `x` and NaN for an infinite or NaN `x`. A muted path gives `+0.0` for NaN and infinite input too, because a mute is how a user isolates a misbehaving source, so a muted strip must not pass its NaN on, and `+0.0` is what a zero fill writes. On a summing stage a zero term never arrives, so a NaN on a muted send or a zero coefficient never reaches the destination, as `apply_matrix_span` already does for a zero coefficient (`crates/mix/src/matrix.rs:241-253`). A gain of exactly 1.0 or 0.0 is the rounded result, so the fader at 0 dB with the gate open passes its input bit for bit, also during a moving block and after a ramp.

**Constant and moving.** Per block each stage classifies itself. It is **constant** when every ruling lane is constant over the block (all points that bound the segments the block touches have bitwise-equal values, or the block lies wholly before the first or after the last group), no hand ramp (decision 7) or gate ramp (decision 6) is running or starts in the block, and no Touch return (decision 8) is running for any of its terms. Otherwise it is **moving**. A constant stage computes its gain once and applies it with the rule above; by decision 2 the per-sample formula gives the same bits at every sample, so the fast path is a proven-equal shortcut, not an approximation. A moving stage writes its per-sample `f64` factors into the strip's curve buffers (decision 16), forms its gain from them per sample and applies it with the same rule.

**Block-level shortcuts are allowed only where the value is constant across the whole block by construction**: a stage classified constant, or a snapshot field that has no lane and no ramp (a snapshot changes only between blocks). Decision 16 lists every per-block decision in the mixer today and what replaces it. In particular a summing stage may skip a whole block, or prune a tap, only when its gain or coefficient is exactly 0.0 at every sample of the block (a constant stage at zero); a coefficient that is zero at the block start but moves inside it is applied per sample.

### 6. The gate: mute and solo

The strip's gate multiplies its hand-smoothed level: `gain(n) = H(n) · gate64(n)` in `f64`, rounded once, computed once per sample for all channels (`H` from decision 7; `H = level64` when no hand ramp runs). The gate is never rounded on its way: the pre-fader sends read the same `f64` `gate64(n)` (decision 11) from the strip's `f64` gate curve. (Rounding the gate first changes results: at gate 1/240 and a −24 dB send level, one rounding gives 0.0002628988877404481 and rounding the gate first 0.0002628989168442786; computed.) `N_s` is the switch ramp length (decision 9).

- **Lane gate.** `c_a(n)` = the number of samples `k` in `[n − N_s + 1, n]` at which the Mute lane reads audible (value < 0.5; the lane rules as in decision 2; without a ruling lane every sample is audible, so `c_a = N_s`). It is a function of the lane and `n` alone, computed at the block's first sample by walking the lane's groups inside the window (at most `N_s` matter, each crossed in one step), then by one add and one subtract per sample. For an isolated mute at breakpoint `p`: `c_a = N_s` before `p`, `c_a(p + j) = N_s − 1 − j` for `0 ≤ j ≤ N_s − 1`, 0 from `p + N_s − 1` on; an unmute at `q` gives `c_a(q + j) = j + 1`, reaching `N_s` at `q + N_s − 1`. The ramp is anchored at the breakpoint: it starts on the breakpoint's sample and lasts `N_s` samples, at every block size, in a bounce, and from any start position. Dense toggles stay bounded at a slope of one count per sample. At a loop start the window reads the lane before the loop start, as every lane is read at its own position.
- **Hand gate.** `c_h` is an integer counter kept in the strip, stepping by one per sample toward its target: 0 when the track is muted by hand (`mixer.mute`), soloed out (below) or its VCA is muted, else `N_s`. A rebuilt strip sets `c_h` to its target with no ramp; Stop, a seek and a reused strip keep it (the table in decision 15). A mute pressed between blocks therefore gives `(N_s − 1)/N_s` on the next block's first sample and 0 on its `N_s`-th; a press reversed mid-ramp turns back from where it is at the same slope. Under a fixed snapshot the counter is at rest.
- **The gate value:** `gate64(n) = (c_h(n) · c_a(n)) as f64 / (N_s · N_s) as f64`, exactly 1 when both are at `N_s` and exactly 0 when either is 0. With the level at exactly 1 the stage's gain is the correctly rounded `c_h·c_a/N_s²`: the division is rounded to `f64` and then to `f32`, and that double rounding cannot go wrong here, because a ratio `p/q` with `q = N_s²` below 2^24 (true through 768 kHz) is either exactly an `f32` rounding midpoint or at least 2^−49 relative away from every one, more than the `f64` rounding's 2^−53. (In the model, `c_a/N_s` with `c_h = N_s` matched the correctly rounded value for every count at `N_s` = 240.) The hand gate and the lane gate combine as today's OR of mutes at rest.
- **Solo** is unchanged in meaning: a strip is soloed out when any active track is soloed and this one is not soloed, not solo-safe, and is not an aux whose input bus a soloed track feeds (`receives_solo`). Pressing solo ramps every affected strip's hand gate over `N_s`. Solo has no lane.
- **Sends and the gate:** a pre-fader send's gain carries the strip's gate (decision 11; today a muted or soloed-out strip skips every send, pre-fader ones included, `crates/mix/src/routing.rs:137-139`; that meaning stays, now ramped and per sample); a post-fader send reads the already gated signal.

### 7. Hand moves of continuous controls

Applies to the fader stage, the master level, the static trim (with phase invert), every send level, and every pan coefficient (track and send). The switch ramps of decision 6 cover mutes and solos.

- **Where the smoothing sits.** The hand ramp smooths a stage's **target before any gate multiplies it**: the fader stage's target is `level64` (decision 4) and its gain is `f32(H(n) · gate64(n))`; a send's target is its level and its gain is `f32(H_i(n) · sendgate64_i(n) · g_strip(n))` (decision 11); the master and the static trim have no gate. So a 20 ms level ramp running when a mute arrives cannot stretch the mute: the gate still reaches 0 in `N_s` samples, whatever the level ramp is doing.
- **State per stage** (per row for a pan matrix, below): `p`, the timeline position of the last sample it rendered (the parked position when stopped); `t_last`, the `f64` target it computed there (decision 4 for a fader, including any Touch return); `h_last`, the `f64` smoothed value `H` it used there (not a rounded gain, so no `f32` value re-enters); for each of its terms that can return in Touch, the term's Touch state at `p` (held, returning with weight `w`, or reading its lane); a ramp counter `k`, the ramp's length `N` and its `f64` start value `from`; and a valid flag. Fixed storage in `Strip` or in the plan's per-send state (decision 16), moved with the strip in `move_strip`; what survives Stop, seeks and plan adoption is the table in decision 15.
- **Detection,** only when the stage is valid and the block's snapshot is not the one the stage last rendered with: compute `T'(p)`, the target at `p` under the new snapshot with each term's Touch state at `p` held fixed (a term held at `p` and released in the new snapshot counts as returning with weight 0, which gives its static value). If `T'(p)` differs bitwise from `t_last`, a ramp starts: `from = h_last`, `k = 0`, `N = N_c`. A new snapshot that leaves `T'(p)` equal does nothing, bit for bit. This one rule covers a fader drag, a typed value, undo, a MIDI-learned controller, a lane edit or a write pass under the playhead, and a mode change; it does not depend on whether the new block follows `p` (a seek published together with a move ramps from `h_last`; a seek alone changes no target at `p` and ramps nothing).
- **The ramp:** at each sample `n` of the block, `k += 1`; while `k < N`, `H(n) = from + (T(n) − from) · (k as f64 / N as f64)` in `f64`, with `T(n)` the stage's unrounded `f64` target; at `k = N`, `H(n) = T(n)` (selected, not computed) and the ramp ends; with no ramp, `H(n) = T(n)`. `H` is never rounded by itself: it enters the stage's final expression (times the gates where the stage has them), and that is rounded once. The target may move during the ramp (a lane under it); the ramp follows it. Example, 0 dB to −6 dB at 48 kHz (`N` = 960), gate open: the gain at the first sample 0.999480426, at the 480th 0.750593603, at the 959th 0.501706839, at the 960th exactly `db_to_gain_f64(−6, 12)` = 0.501187205; strictly monotonic (computed).
- **A new move mid-ramp** restarts from the smoothed value at `p`: `from = h_last`, `k = 0`, full `N` again.
- **The static trim** has the target `T = ±db_to_gain_f64_unrounded(trim_db, TRIM_CAP_DB)`, the unrounded form (with the rounded form as the target, a unity to −6 dB trim ramp over 960 samples gives 0.9683045744895935 at sample 61 instead of 0.9683046340942383; computed). A level change alone ramps over `N_c`. A change of sign (the phase invert toggle), alone or together with a level change, ramps over `N_s` by the same linear law, from `from` to the new signed target. **Where it crosses zero:** the linear law's real zero lies at `k* = N_s · from / (from − T)`; a sample is exactly 0 only when `k*` is an integer, which for equal-magnitude opposite endpoints (`T = −from`) means `N_s` even, at `k = N_s/2` (at 48 kHz, `N_s` = 240, sample 120 is exactly 0). At odd lengths there is no zero sample: at 44.1 kHz (`N_s` = 221) a +1 to −1 ramp gives ±0.004524887073785067 (±1/221, correctly rounded) at samples 110 and 111, a dip to −46.9 dB rather than to silence. With a level change as well (for example +1 to −0.5) the zero moves to `k*` (here `2N_s/3`) and is a sample only when that is an integer. The record keeps this linear law, the same one every hand ramp uses, rather than a zero-forcing law (two half-ramps meeting at an exact 0); the maintainer decided it (decided item 3). A toggle reversed mid-ramp restarts a full `N_s` ramp from `h_last` (decision 7's rule), unlike the gate's counter, which turns back at the same slope.
- **Pan coefficients** ramp per **row** of the strip's output matrix and of each send's matrix: a row's coefficients depend on one pan parameter (decision 10), and a row's ramp state, detection and `from` are its own, detection comparing that row at `p` bitwise. So a move of `Pan(1)` on a stereo track ramps row 1 only; row 0 keeps following its own lane, its own return and its own ramp. A surround panner move changes every row's target, and each row detects it.
- **Clip gain does not ramp:** it is an edit, not a performance control, and a clip has no per-stage state. A clip gain changed during playback steps at the next block (decided item 4).

### 8. Touch, Latch, Write, and what a recorded pass reproduces

- **Touch, level terms.** A held control's lane is not read (its term is the static value, which the UI keeps at the held value); pressing it is a snapshot change, so the hand ramp of decision 7 glides from the lane's value to the held value. On release the term returns to its lane over the session's Touch return time (`touch_return_ms`, 0 to 2000 ms, default 200, rounded to samples as today): term `= base + (L(n) − base) · w(n)` in `f64`, `w(n) = 1 − left(n)/len` with `left` the return's remaining rendered samples at `n`, evaluated per sample. The release does not also start a hand ramp (decision 7's detection holds the Touch state at `p`). The return counts rendered samples, so it is live only.
- **Touch, pan terms, per parameter and per row.** Return state is kept **per pan parameter**, as today's `TouchRamps` keeps it per parameter key (`crates/mix/src/touch.rs:41-58`): `Pan(0)`, `Pan(1)` and each `SendPan(i)` have their own held flag and their own return weight `w_p(n)`. Each row `r` of a pan matrix is a function of exactly one pan parameter `p(r)` (decision 10; for a stereo source row 0 reads `Pan(0)` and row 1 reads `Pan(1)`, `crates/mix/src/matrix.rs:82-88`). During a return, row `r` is a per-sample blend: `M_r(n) = H_r + (K_r(n) − H_r) · w_p(r)(n)` per coefficient in `f64`, where `H_r` is the row built by the pan law from the held (static) value of `p(r)` alone, captured at that parameter's release, and `K_r(n)` is the row's knot interpolation of decision 10, in `f64` and unrounded, a function of the snapshot and the timeline position only. `M_r(n)` then enters the row's hand smoothing (decision 7) as its target and is rounded once at the end. While `p(r)` is held, `M_r = H_r`; a row whose parameter is neither held nor returning is `K_r(n)`. So two controls held and released independently compose: one stereo channel can be held while the other reads its lane, and two returns released at different samples run on their own weights; rebuilding a row from static values never moves another row. The live return state enters only as the weights, which advance one step per rendered sample whatever the timeline does; after a locate or a loop wrap `K_r` is read at the new position at once, as any lane is, and the weights carry on; while stopped every knot is the parked position, so `K_r` is constant and a return still completes over its rendered samples. The fixed table of eight parameters per track stays: past it a released parameter returns at once without a ramp, as today (`TOUCH_SLOTS`), which is deterministic for a given event sequence. The blend is in coefficient space, like the hand ramps of pan coefficients (decision 7), so a return from hard left to hard right passes through (0.5, 0.5) at its midpoint, where the −3 dB law gives 0.7079 per side: a dip of about 3.02 dB below the law there (20·log10(0.5/0.7079458) = −3.0206 dB, computed); today's code blends pan positions instead (decided item 10).
- **Latch and Write.** What the write pass records is unchanged: the hand target (`lane.static_value`), a point every 20 ms, thinned afterwards (`crates/ui-studio/src/automation/pass.rs`). In Write the Volume lane is not read (whatever `Mixer::touched` holds), so the fader follows the hand through decision 7. In Latch the mix reads the lane while the pass rewrites it under the playhead; each publish that changes the lane at `p` is a detected change, so the hand ramp smooths what is a small sawtooth today. Whether Latch should suspend the lane as Touch does is not decided here. Pan lanes are read in Write today (`pan_at` has no Write exception); that stays.
- **Return ramp in Latch:** none until Stop, as today.
- **A recorded pass does not reproduce the hand performance exactly.** What was heard is a ramp linear in gain over 20 ms from each move; what is recorded is points 20 ms apart that the lane joins linearly in dB. Halfway through a move from 0 to −6 dB the hand ramp is at 0.750593603 and the recorded lane at 0.707945764 (computed). Playback of the recorded lane is exact and repeatable; it is not a sample-for-sample copy of the live performance, and the claims page says so.

### 9. Ramp lengths in samples

`N = (rate · ms + 500) div 1000`, in integers (rounded half up; no floating point). `N_c` uses 20 ms, `N_s` 5 ms:

| Rate (Hz) | 44,100 | 48,000 | 88,200 | 96,000 | 176,400 | 192,000 | 352,800 | 384,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `N_s` (5 ms) | 221 | 240 | 441 | 480 | 882 | 960 | 1,764 | 1,920 |
| `N_c` (20 ms) | 882 | 960 | 1,764 | 1,920 | 3,528 | 3,840 | 7,056 | 7,680 |

At 44.1 kHz 5 ms is 220.5 samples; the rule gives 221. The lengths are fixed per plan (the plan's rate). The products `c_h · c_a` and `N_s²` stay far below 2^53 through the supported 768 kHz maximum (`N_s` = 3,840 there), so the gate's ratios are exact integers before the one division.

### 10. Pan: knots on a fixed grid

Track pan (`Pan(0)`, `Pan(1)`) and send pan (`SendPan(i)`, now read) are evaluated **per matrix row** at **knots**. In every branch of `build_matrix` where a lane-driven pan enters, row `r` depends on one pan parameter `p(r)` (row 0 on `Pan(0)` or the send's pan, row 1 on `Pan(1)`; `crates/mix/src/matrix.rs:77-88`, `147`, `183-191`; G2 asserts this), so each row has its own knots: every timeline multiple of 64 (anchored to sample 0) and every group time of `p(r)`'s lane. At a knot `a` the row has an **incoming** coefficient set `c_in(a)` (the pan law of the lane's incoming value there, decision 2) and an **outgoing** set `c_out(a)` (the law of its outgoing value); at a grid knot or a single point they are the same. Each lane value enters the law as an `f32` pan position, and the law's outputs are `f32`: these knot coefficients are intentional `f32` inputs (the pan law `build_matrix` uses is an `f32` function, today `transit_dsp::pan::gains`; ADR 0055 slice 4b moves it to `transit_math::gain::pan_gains`, and this record does not). At the knot's own sample the row is `c_in(a)`; between knots `a < n < b` each coefficient is `K_r(n) = c_out(a) + (c_in(b) − c_out(a)) · ((n − a) as f64 / (b − a) as f64)` in `f64`, **unrounded**: it is the row's target, the Touch return of decision 8 and the hand smoothing of decision 7 act on it in `f64`, and only the result is rounded once. So a vertical step keeps decision 2's rule: a group at 64 holding −1 then +1, followed by +1 at 128, gives hard left at 64 and hard right from 65 on; interpolating between single knot values instead would give (0.984375, 0.015625) at 65 (computed). A call to the pan law costs about 84 ns on the reference machine (`pan_gains`, bench median), so a call per sample is not affordable (a group whose first and last values differ costs two calls at its knot); the knot rule is a function of the snapshot and `n`, exact at every breakpoint and at the hard ends (pan ±1 is exactly (1, 0) or (0, 1) in both laws' branches), and equal knots give the constant exactly, so a hard-panned or unautomated track keeps its matrix bit for bit. Worst gain error against the exact law for a full left-to-right sweep, channels above −40 dB, −3 dB law: 0.146 dB over 480 samples (10 ms at 48 kHz), 0.022 dB over 4,800 (computed). Without pan automation, a hand ramp or a Touch return, the matrix is constant over the block and the path is today's. When stopped every knot is the parked position. A Touch return blends per sample and per row (decision 8). The surround panner has no lanes; its hand moves ramp by decision 7, every row, the LFE coefficient of a surround matrix (`matrix.rs:175-178`) included: that coefficient is constant only while no such ramp runs. Stereo sources keep their two balance-law pans.

### 11. Sends

For send `i` at sample `n`:

- **Level** `level64_i(n) = db_to_gain_f64_unrounded(sendlevel_i(n), FADER_CAP_DB)` from its SendLevel lane (with the Touch return) or its stored level; the hand ramp of decision 7 smooths it into `H_i(n)`, in `f64`.
- **Mute** is a gate exactly like decision 6: a lane gate from the SendMute lane (now read) and a hand gate from the stored `snd.mute`, each over `N_s`, giving `sendgate64_i(n)`.
- **Pan** as decision 10 for mono sources; stereo sources go straight to left and right as today.
- **The send's gain** `s_i(n) = H_i(n) · sendgate64_i(n) · g_strip(n)` in `f64`, left to right, rounded once, where `g_strip` is the strip's `f64` `gate64` (decision 6, read from the strip's `f64` gate curve, never rounded on its way) for a pre-fader send and 1 for a post-fader send.
- **Per tap,** as `apply_matrix_span` has it, with decision 5's summing rule: per destination sample, the taps whose `s_i` or coefficient is exactly 0 at that sample do not contribute; with none contributing the destination is untouched; otherwise `acc = +0.0`, `acc += (x · s_i(n)) · c(n)` in `f32` for each contributing source channel in ascending order, then `dst += acc`. A constant send and pan take today's arithmetic shape; the value of `s_i` changes where the exact conversion differs from today's `powf` (re-pins).
- **Delay history.** Post-fader sends and the output matrix read the strip after its delay tap (decision 14), so they receive the gated signal delayed by the strip's compensation delay. A mute brings the fader stage's output (and the meter's input) to `+0.0` when the gate reaches 0, but whatever is downstream of the delay line keeps playing the line's earlier contents for its length, earlier audio and any NaN it held included, and falls silent that many samples later. The mute guarantee is scoped accordingly (decision 15, claims rows); a pre-fader send carries the gate in its own gain and is silent when the gate is.

### 12. VCA, groups, the master

- **VCA:** its fader adds in dB to each member's `D(n)` (decision 4); its mute sets each member's hand gate target to 0. A VCA move reaches the member as a target change, so the member's own hand ramp smooths it; no gain state is kept on the VCA's strip. One VCA level, as today.
- **Group (folder) tracks** are aux-like strips with their own stages; nothing composes across strips.
- **The master** has a level stage only: `D(n)` is its Volume lane (with Touch) or static volume, cap `FADER_CAP_DB` (today it has no clamp, so a stored master level above +12 dB now renders at +12; re-pins), the hand ramp of decision 7, no gate; it writes the main mix with the in-place rule.

### 13. Clip gain, fades and the gain envelope (this record owns the arithmetic; ADR 0056 owns placement and editing)

ADR 0056 (the accepted audio clip editor record) states in its decision 9: "Clip fades and gain envelopes use the per-sample, closed-form f64 gain path, rounded once, defined by the mixer-gain record. This record defines their clip-time placement and editing behavior." This decision is that path, and it carries each of the eight requirements (9.6 a to h) that record places on it, cited where each is met. Forming the product in `f64` and rounding once costs no exactness claim: at 0 dB with no fade and no envelope every factor is exactly 1.0 (`exp2(0)` is exactly 1), so `Gc` is exactly 1.0; at the floor or at a fade's zero sample a factor is exactly 0.0, so `Gc` is exactly 0.0; no exception to the round-once rule is needed in either record. A clip's curves are anchored to the clip's own first sample, so a moved clip keeps its shape. For clip sample `k` (`0 ≤ k < length`, `k = n − clip.start`):

- **The rendered gain** `Gc(k)`: 0.0 when `gain_db + env(k) ≤ −144` (the floor, decided on the `f64` sum before anything else); otherwise `Gc(k) = (E64(k) · Fin64(k)) · Fout64(k)` in `f64`, rounded once, where `E64(k) = db_to_gain_f64_unrounded(gain_db + env(k), CLIP_CAP_DB)`. No factor is rounded to `f32` on the way: no `f32` fade argument, no `f32` shape arithmetic, no `f32` product. It is computed once per sample for all of the clip's channels and applied with the summing rule of decision 5: `out += v · Gc(k)` with `v` the read (and folded) sample, nothing added when `Gc(k)` is exactly 0.
- **The envelope, per sample:** `env(k)` is the envelope's value by decision 2 with clip time as the position (0 dB without an envelope), summed with the widened `gain_db` in `f64` (exact unless the two values' exponents differ by more than 28, and then within half an `f64` ulp). Without an envelope `E64` is one constant per clip. This replaces the 32-sample segments of today's code and of ADR 0056's first revision: one method everywhere, nothing interpolated in linear gain. With segments, a −144 to +36 dB envelope over 31 samples rendered sample 15 at 16.17 (30.53 when the segment runs past the breakpoint) against the exact 0.001428, and a split moved the segments (a −24 to 0 dB ramp over 100 samples split at 17 changed sample 16 from 0.107224 to 0.098175); per sample both pieces give 0.098175 there (computed).
- **Exact breakpoints** (ADR 0056 9.6 a): at a breakpoint's clip time `env(k)` is the stored first value of its group, so with no fade there `Gc(k)` is `db_to_gain_f64(gain_db + v, CLIP_CAP_DB)` of exactly the stored value plus the static gain. (Today's `f32` interpolation from −144 dB to `f32(0.1)` returns 0.100006103515625 at the later breakpoint; ADR 0056 round 3, computed.)
- **Breakpoints sharing a time** (ADR 0056 9.6 c): preserved as written, with decision 2's meaning (the group's first value at its time, the segment after it from its last) and its bounded traversal: crossing a group costs one step through the group index, built and admitted by the plan compiler from slice G1a on (decision 2), whatever the group's size.
- **The fade shapes, in stable `f64` forms.** The shape kernel `F(t)` is an `f64` function of an `f64` argument, with the forms and operation order fixed here (each chosen so that no subtraction cancels near either end, where a fade's gain is small and the relative accuracy matters):
  - Linear: `t`.
  - Equal Power: `sin(t · (π/2))`, with `π/2` the `f64` literal `FRAC_PI_2`.
  - S-Curve: `v = sin(t · (π/2))`, then `v · v`. (The same function as `0.5 − 0.5·cos(π t)`, but that form cancels near `t = 0`: for a closed fade of 1,000,001 samples, sample 1 gives 2.467415161078179·10⁻¹² in `f64` rounded to `f32`, 65 `f32` steps from the correctly rounded 2.467401066449937·10⁻¹², about 5.7·10⁻⁶ relative; the stable form gives the correctly rounded value; computed.)
  - Exponential: `(t · t) · t`.
  - Logarithmic: `t · ((3 − 3·t) + t · t)`. (The same function as `1 − (1 − t)³`, which cancels near `t = 0`: at fade lengths near 10¹² samples it is 306 `f32` steps off at the first sample, and one step off near 10⁹; the stable form was correctly rounded at every point tested; computed.)
  - Near `t = 1` every form's result is close to 1, where an absolute error of a few `f64` ulps is far below an `f32` step, so no form needs a second branch there.
  - **Curvature weighting,** unchanged in meaning: with `c` the stored `f32` curvature, sanitized as today (non-finite gives 0, then clamped to [−1, 1], `clip.rs:61-69`) and widened, and `b = F(t)`: if `c == 0`, `b ≤ 0` or `b ≥ 1` the result is `b`; else `w = b · exp2(4·c)` and the result is `w / ((w + 1) − b)`, in that order, through `transit_math::f64::exp2`. `exp2(4·c)` depends on the fade alone, so it is computed once per fade by the same call on the same argument and reused at every sample (the same value, so the result is identical): per sample a curved fade costs its shape (one `sin` for Equal Power and S-Curve, multiplications otherwise), a multiply, an add, a subtraction and a division. Near `b = 1` the subtraction loses relative accuracy in `1 − b`, but the result is then close to 1 and its absolute error stays below `16 ×` that of `b` (since `w ≥ b/16`), far below an `f32` step.
  - **Argument sanitization,** unchanged: a NaN argument is 0, and the argument is clamped to [0, 1] (`clip.rs:23`).
  - **Accuracy of a fade factor.** By the per-operation argument (each operation at most half an `f64` ulp; `sin` and `exp2` at the seam's accuracy, which ADR 0055 slice 2 measures and expects within one `f64` ulp; `sin`'s condition number on [0, π/2] is at most 1), every factor carries a relative error below 10⁻¹³ (a bound with a wide margin; the true figures are a few `f64` ulps). In the model, with the platform's `sin` and `exp2` standing in for the seam, the stable forms rounded to `f32` were the correctly rounded value against a 50-digit decimal reference at every point tested: samples 1 to 3, the midpoint and the last interior samples of eleven fade lengths from 2 to 2^31 samples for every shape at curvatures 0, ±0.5, ±1 and `f32(0.2632)`, and, uncurved, the first and last interior samples of fades of 2^32 + 1, 2^36 + 1 and 2^40 (`MAX_POSITION`) samples and the first samples of fades of about 10⁹ and 10¹² samples (computed). These are model results on the platform's library, not measurements of the seam: the exact expected values in G3's tests are fixed against the real seam (`transit_math`) when the slice is built, and the bound above is what the tests may rely on.
  Example of the difference from today's `f32` argument and arithmetic: an exponential four-sample fade-in at `k = 1` gives 0.037037041038274765 today and 0.03703703731298447 (the correctly rounded 1/27) under this evaluator (computed).
- **The fade law: the closed law** (ADR 0056's question 9, decided by the maintainer on 2026-11-12: a fade-in from exactly 0 to exactly 1 and a fade-out ending at exactly 0, mirror images; a one-sample fade is the midpoint gain). The half-open law is kept below only as the description of today's behavior, which G3 replaces and which its re-pins move away from. Common to both: the clip's length is `length ≥ 1`; the valid clip-sample domain is `0 ≤ k < length` (the renderer asks for nothing else; an editor caller outside it gets the value at the nearest end of the domain); `m_in = fade_in.len` and `m_out = fade_out.len`, each clamped to `0..=length`; fade-in and fade-out are independent factors, and where they overlap inside one clip both apply; `F_in` and `F_out` are each fade's shape with its own curvature, through the kernel above.
  - **Closed law (rendered from G3 on).** `Fin64(k)`: 1 if `m_in = 0` or `k ≥ m_in`; for `m_in = 1`, `Fin64(0) = F_in(0.5)` (the midpoint); for `m_in ≥ 2` and `k < m_in`, `F_in(k as f64 / (m_in − 1) as f64)`. `Fout64(k)`: 1 if `m_out = 0` or `k < length − m_out`; for `m_out = 1`, `Fout64(length − 1) = F_out(0.5)`; for `m_out ≥ 2` and `k ≥ length − m_out`, `F_out((length − 1 − k) as f64 / (m_out − 1) as f64)`. For `m ≥ 2` a fade-in starts at exactly 0 and reaches exactly 1 on its last sample; a fade-out starts at 1 and its last sample is exactly 0; a fade-out is the fade-in of the same length reversed sample for sample. In a 100-sample clip with a 100-sample linear fade-out, samples 96, 97 and 99 are 3/99, 2/99 and 0 (computed). The one-sample midpoint (ADR 0056 9.6 d) is a property of this law only.
  - **Half-open law (today's, replaced by G3; not rendered after it).** `Fin64(k)`: 1 if `m_in = 0` or `k ≥ m_in`; else `F_in(k as f64 / m_in as f64)`, so `m_in = 1` gives `Fin64(0) = F_in(0) = 0`. `Fout64(k)`: 1 if `m_out = 0` or `k < length − m_out`; else `F_out((length − k) as f64 / m_out as f64)`, so the fade-out's first sample is `F_out(1) = 1`, its last is `F_out(1/m_out)` (never 0), and `m_out = 1` leaves the clip unchanged. This is today's `gain_at_with_fades` with the arguments in `f64` (`clip.rs:296-310`).
- **One evaluator for the render and the editor** (ADR 0056 9.6 h). One function computes `(Fin64(k), Fout64(k))` for a clip sample from the clip's length and a pair of fades, with the clamps, the closed law and the sanitization above; one function, `Clip::gain_at_with_fades(k, fade_in, fade_out)`, combines them with `E64(k)` under the floor and rounds once, and `Clip::gain_at(k)` is that function with the clip's own fades. The renderer, the waveform drawing, the fade-curve overlay (which draws the fade factor at the clip samples its pixels map to, instead of calling `Fade::gain` on a screen-sampled argument as `crates/ui-studio/src/fades.rs:122-133` does today) and every preview with temporary fades during a drag call these and nothing else, on the same clip data. `Fade::gain` stops being a public evaluator: its `f64` kernel is internal to the factor function. So what is drawn is what sounds, sample for sample, once G3 lands.
- **Crossfade sums, per shape** (ADR 0056 9.6 e). Stated for an *isolated complementary pair* only: the fade-out of one clip and the fade-in of the next over the same samples, equal lengths, no other fade overlapping inside either clip, both clips at 0 dB with no envelope (so `E64` is exactly 1 and each clip's gain is its fade factor rounded once), summed in `f32` as the mixer sums. Complementary in **gain** (real arithmetic, under either law, because at aligned samples the fade-out's argument is the complement of the fade-in's): Linear with Linear, S-Curve with S-Curve (`sin²(π t/2) + sin²(π(1 − t)/2) = 1`), Exponential in with Logarithmic out and Logarithmic in with Exponential out (`Log(1 − t) = 1 − t³ = 1 − Exp(t)`), and each of these with curvatures `c` in and `−c` out: the weighting multiplies a gain's odds `b/(1 − b)` by `K = 2^(4c)`, so with `−c` it maps `1 − b` to the complement of the weighted `b` (checked in the model against a 50-digit reference at curvatures ±0.25, ±0.5, ±1 and `f32(0.2632)`: deviations below 10⁻⁴⁸ for all four, computed). Complementary in **squared gain**: Equal Power with Equal Power at zero curvature only (amplitudes sum to √2 at the midpoint). **Not complementary in gain or in squared gain:** Equal Power with opposite curvatures (at the midpoint, curvatures +0.5 and −0.5 give 0.9061636786 and 0.3763849674, a squared sum of 0.9627982562; ±0.25 gives a squared sum of 0.9854109755; computed), two Exponential fades, two Logarithmic fades, any pair of different lengths or with a third fade inside. **One-sample pairs depend on the law and, under the closed law, on curvature** (the half-open figures describe today's behavior): for an isolated pair of uncurved fades of identical shape, the one-sample gain sums in real arithmetic are: closed 1, √2, 1, 0.25, 1.75 for Linear, Equal Power, S-Curve, Exponential, Logarithmic, respectively; half-open 1 for every shape. With curvature, the closed-law sum comes from the two midpoint evaluations and need not equal these figures; the half-open sum remains 1. (Under the closed law both one-sample fades take the midpoint; an uncurved Exponential with a Logarithmic gives 1. With curvature: Exponential fades at +0.5 and −0.5 give midpoint gains 4/11 and 1/29, summing to 127/319 ≈ 0.398, and Equal Power at +0.5 and −0.5 gives 1.2825486460; computed from today's weighting `w = b · 2^(4c)`, `w/(w + 1 − b)`, `clip.rs`. Under the half-open law the fade-out's one sample is `F_out(1) = 1` and the fade-in's is `F_in(0) = 0` whatever the shapes and curvatures.)
- **Crossfade bounds, on the final `f32` sum.** The bound concerns the `f32` sum the mixer forms of the two rounded gains, not the real sum of two rounded values (`f32(1/3) + f32(2/3)` is 1.0000000298023224 in real arithmetic and exactly 1 as an `f32` sum). For a gain-complementary pair each `f64` factor is within 10⁻¹³ relative of its exact value and each rounding adds at most half an `f32` ulp of the factor; the larger factor lies in [0.5, 1], so the two roundings together are below `0.75 · 2^−24` plus the `f64` errors, and the `f32` sum is 1 or `1 − 2^−24`. In the model the `f32` sum was exactly 1.0 at every sample of fades of 2, 3, 7, 64, 441, 960 and 4,800 samples for the four pairs at curvatures 0, 0.25, −0.5, 1 and `f32(0.2632)` (125,540 sums; computed), which today's arithmetic does not achieve (±`f32(0.2632)` sums to 0.9999998807907104 at the midpoint). For Equal Power at zero curvature the squared sum, formed in `f32`, is within 2^−23 ≈ 1.2·10⁻⁷ of 1 (8.3·10⁻⁸ worst with the gains widened, in the model). G3's tests assert exactly 1.0 on a set computed against the real seam when the slice is built and the stated bounds everywhere else; the Exponential and Logarithmic cross pairs are tested with the other gain-complementary pairs; two Exponential fades, two Logarithmic fades and opposite-curvature Equal Power have no sum law and are not tested for one.
- **Accuracy against the closed form, with the floor** (ADR 0056 9.6 b). The oracle for clip sample `k` is `o(k) = 0` if `x(k) ≤ −144`, else `10^(min(x(k), 48)/20) · Fin(k) · Fout(k)`, with `x(k) = gain_db + env(k)` computed from the stored points in exact rational arithmetic and the fade factors from the exact shapes at the exact rational arguments of the closed law. Where `o(k)` and `Gc(k)` are both nonzero, `Gc(k)` is within one `f32` ulp of `o(k)`: the `f64` product carries decision 3's conversion error (below 2^−48.3) and the fade factors' (below 10⁻¹³ each), far below the 2^−24 that faithful rounding needs, so the rounded result is one of the two `f32` values bracketing `o(k)`. Where either is zero (the floor decided on opposite sides of −144 because the `f64` sum or a stored value rounded), the rule is absolute: the other is at most `10^((−144 + δ)/20)` with `δ` the dB error that decided it.
- **What a split does to the gain** (ADR 0056 9.5). The earlier piece evaluates the envelope and static gain identically to the unsplit clip at every sample, bit for bit; its fades are identical except where the split moved or clamped them (each piece gets no fade on its inner edge, ADR 0056 9.3), which is a different fade, not an accuracy question. The later piece starts from a breakpoint inserted at the cut, its value the envelope's `f64` value there rounded once to `f32` dB, so it differs from the true value by at most half an `f32` ulp of that dB value, `δ` ≤ 2^−17 ≈ 7.63·10⁻⁶ dB for values below 256 dB in magnitude, and the difference shrinks linearly to zero at the next breakpoint. So the later piece's gain is within a relative `10^(δ/20) − 1` ≈ 8.8·10⁻⁷ of the unsplit clip's plus one `f32` rounding on each side, where both are nonzero; where either is at the floor the absolute rule applies: the other is at most `10^((−144 + δ)/20)` ≈ 6.30957899·10⁻⁸. Example: a cut value of −143.999998 dB is stored as −144 dB, turning a gain of 6.3095749·10⁻⁸ into 0, a relative difference of 100 percent and an absolute one below that bound (computed).
- **Clip cost** (ADR 0056 9.6 g), landing-blocking, measured by slice G3 **with the fade cache disabled**: at most 0.5 % of one core at 48 kHz (2 % at 192 kHz) for a stereo clip with both fades curved and overlapping across the whole clip and a moving envelope, measured once per shape (both fades of that shape; Linear, Equal Power, S-Curve, Exponential, Logarithmic); a clip with no fade and no envelope no slower than the base beyond 5 %. The uncached path is the one the promise rests on: every fade a cache does not hold takes it, and admission may leave a cache out entirely (decision 16), so a benchmark that hits a cache cannot carry the promise.
  - **What the record's figures allow (computed from bench medians, not measured):** the budget is 104.17 ns per sample in total at either rate (0.5 % of 10⁹ ns over 48,000 samples; 2 % over 192,000). One exact conversion for the moving envelope is 3.90 ns (3.74 % of the budget), which leaves about 100.27 ns per sample for the two fade factors, the product and the two channels' multiply-add. The curvature's `exp2` is per fade, not per sample (above); even counted per sample, three `exp2` calls (8.58 ns) would be 0.041184 % of a core at 48 kHz, 8.24 % of the budget. The record has no bench figure for `sin` (Equal Power and S-Curve call it once per fade per sample) and no measured fade loop, so whether the uncached path fits is not known: G3 measures it first.
  - **If every shape passes uncached,** no cache is built and the promise is unconditional.
  - **If any shape misses uncached,** G3 stops and reports the per-shape figures, and the qualified promise applies (decided item 9; the alternative, holding G3 until a cheaper exact evaluation exists, was not chosen): a bounded cache is built; for the benchmark workload, the 0.5 % budget applies when both fades are resident in an admitted cache; otherwise the stated uncached budget applies, including when plan admission omits the cache (decision 16). Fitting the cache's capacity is not residency: a plan whose required storage is 1,040,187,392 bytes fits the 1,073,741,824-byte default limit, but adding the fixed 64 MiB cache needs 1,107,296,256 bytes, so the cache is omitted, and two two-second fades at 48 kHz (1,536,000 bytes of factors) run uncached in that plan. The resident workload is gated at the stated budget, the uncached cost at a budget the maintainer sets, and the claims page carries the limit in words like "A clip's fades and gain envelope cost at most 0.5 % of a core at 48 kHz when its fades are resident in the plan's fade cache (64 MiB, admitted only when the plan has room for it; about 8.4 million factors, 175 seconds of fade in total at 48 kHz, 44 at 192 kHz); otherwise, including when the plan has no room for the cache, up to X % of a core", X being G3's largest measured uncached figure. No incremental or recurrence form is part of this record: a recurrence for `sin` or for the shapes is not shown bit-identical to the closed form, and an evaluator that is not identical would make values depend on the cache state.
  - **Fades are evaluated per sample from the closed form;** no per-plan table of a fade's gains is required. A full table is not affordable in general: two hour-long fades at 48 kHz in `f64` take 2 × 3,600 × 48,000 × 8 = 2,764,800,000 bytes, more than the 1 GiB default prepared-plan limit (`PlanLimits::prepared_bytes`, `crates/mix/src/compile.rs:50`). A cache, built only if a shape misses uncached (decided item 9), is only a cache: a fixed per-plan byte budget counted in the plan's prepared bytes (decision 16), holding the `f64` factors of the fades that fit (two overlapping 88-second fades at 48 kHz already take 2 × 88 × 48,000 × 8 = 67,584,000 bytes, more than 64 MiB, 67,108,864); any fade it does not hold is evaluated per sample, with the same values. The cache changes cost, never a value.
- **No hand ramp** for clip gain (decision 7).
- What is promised for clips (ADR 0056 9.6 f): the clip source, gain and fade path is block-partition invariant for a fixed snapshot with available source data (in memory, or a disk ring that delivered), with decision 5's NaN qualification. A clip at 0 dB with no fades and no envelope has `Gc` exactly 1.0, so each term it adds is exactly its source value `v`; what lands in the strip buffer is the IEEE sum with what is already there. Alone on the strip (the buffer starts at `+0.0`), the result is the source bit for bit for every sample except a `−0.0` source sample, which becomes `+0.0` (`+0 + −0 = +0`), and a NaN, which stays a NaN with its payload unspecified. G3's test `unity_gain_and_no_fades_pass_the_source_bit_for_bit` (the name ADR 0056 refers to) asserts exactly that.
- **The interim state, until G3 lands.** ADR 0056's editor draws with today's nominal `Clip::gain_at` and envelope curves and claims no sample-exact agreement with playback, and a start trim or split inserts its cut breakpoint with today's `env_value` (ADR 0056 9.3, 9.4). G3 switches both in one landing: the drawing entry point becomes this decision's evaluator (`Clip::gain_at_with_fades` and the factor function), the cut breakpoint takes the envelope's `f64` value rounded once to `f32` dB (the split item above), and ADR 0056's `the_editor_draws_the_rendered_gain` changes its assertion from today's `gain_at` to this evaluator in the same landing.

### 14. Where each gain is applied, in order

Unchanged order: source (clips with decision 13) → static trim with phase invert (decision 7 ramps) → inserts → pre-fader copy → fader stage `buf = apply(buf, gain(n))` with `gain = level · gate` → meter → delay tap → sends (pre-fader from the copy, post-fader from `buf`, decision 11) → output pan matrix (decision 10). The master: inserts → level → main mix. Pan stays at the route stage after the delay tap (merging it into the fader would move the meter and the post-fader tap).

**Precision at each step.** Lane values, dB sums, converted levels (the unrounded form), the Touch blends, hand smoothing, ramp fractions, gate ratios, pan interpolation, fade arguments, shapes and curvature, and each stage's product of its own factors (smoothed level × gate; smoothed send level × send gate × strip gate; envelope × fade-in × fade-out) in `f64`; each stage's gain rounded once to `f32`; the stage's gain and, for a matrix tap, the pan coefficient applied to the audio in `f32`, left to right, by decision 5's rule. The audio is `f32` throughout the mixer; nothing is gained by a wider multiply into the audio.

**Audit: every place an `f32` value enters gain arithmetic** (re-checked for this revision across decisions 2 to 17):
- *Intentional inputs, widened exactly:* stored lane points, static volume, trim, send levels, the Touch base (the control's static value), clip `gain_db` and envelope points, fade curvature; the pan position given to the `f32` pan law and the law's `f32` outputs at knots and in held rows (decisions 8, 10); the LFE coefficient inside a surround matrix (an output of the same `f32` matrix builder).
- *Stored data, by design:* the cut breakpoint a split inserts (an edit, rounded once to `f32` dB, decision 13).
- *Two stages in sequence on the audio:* send gain then pan coefficient, fader stage then output matrix: each gain rounded once, applied to `f32` audio in a fixed order (decision 1).
- *Kept in `f64`, never rounded on the way (each was a double rounding in an earlier revision):* the gate (decision 6; the pre-fader send reads the `f64` gate curve), the static trim's target (unrounded conversion), the hand ramp's start value (`h_last`, not the applied gain), pan coefficients between knots and during a return (decisions 8, 10), the fader's level before the gate.
- *Outside the gain path, unchanged and listed in decision 16:* the clip-effect gain, the click level, the External Instrument return gain.

### 15. Transport, seeks, tempo splits and the offline bounce

- **Stopped** (`monitor_only`): every sample of a block evaluates at the parked position, so a stopped block no longer sweeps the lane across the block, block after block. Live state (hand ramps, the hand gate, Touch returns) still advances per rendered sample.
- **Tempo and meter splits:** each sub-block is a block like any other; nothing in the gain path sees the split.
- **Seek and loop wrap:** lanes, gates and knots are read at the new position at once; hand state is unaffected (decision 7); a Touch return carries on (decision 8).
- **What live state survives what.** Gain live state is not plug-in render state: Stop and panic reset plug-ins and notes (`MixEngine::reset` → `reset_render_state`, `crates/mix/src/lib.rs:1004-1026`, `crates/mix/src/render_pool.rs:256-273`) and leave it alone, as they already leave today's `TouchRamps` alone. So a hand ramp, a mute ramp or a Touch return running at Stop completes over the stopped monitoring blocks that follow. The one rule: state lives with the strip, and only a strip that is not carried over starts fresh.

  | State | Stop, panic | Seek, locate | Loop wrap | Reused-plan adoption (strip reused and moved, `move_strip`) | Rebuilt strip (new document, new strip, changed channel count or format, changed rate) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Hand ramp per stage and per pan row (`p`, `t_last`, `h_last`, `k`, `N`, `from`, valid) | kept; continues | kept; `p` is the last rendered sample, the target is read at the new position | kept | kept | invalid: the first block applies the target, no ramp |
  | Hand gate counter `c_h` (strip and each send) | kept; continues | kept | kept | kept | set to its target |
  | Lane gate count `c_a` | none kept: recomputed from the lane at every block start | same | same | same | same |
  | Touch return per parameter (held flag, samples left, length) | kept; continues | kept | kept | kept | cleared: lanes read at once |
  | Held pan rows `H_r` for a running return | kept with the return | kept | kept | kept | cleared |
  | Send state (hand ramp, `c_h`) for a send | kept | kept | kept | kept when the send slot and its target are unchanged; otherwise as rebuilt | as rebuilt |

  Test 3 covers each column for an identical event sequence.
- **Offline bounce, stems, freeze, commit:** they run the same strip code (`render_range`, 1024-sample blocks, `crates/engine/src/io/bounce.rs`) on fresh strips, so their live state starts at rest. The offline render compiles its plan from a copy of the session (`RangeRender::build_with`, `crates/mix/src/offline.rs:172`, `Arc::new(s.clone())`), and that copy carries the runtime `Mixer::touched` list; a fresh renderer does not unhold it. So the offline render **clears `Mixer::touched` on every track of its copy before compiling**: a bounce has no held controls, reads every lane eligible under its track's automation mode (in Write the Volume lane is still not read), and starts no Touch return, hand ramp or hand-gate movement.
- **Freeze's real boundary, stated as it is today.** Freeze is documented as the track's output "after its inserts and before its fader" (`crates/engine/src/cmd/freeze.rs:3`). What `render_track_pre_fader_timed` (`crates/mix/src/offline.rs:303-345`) actually renders for the frozen track: its clips or instrument, its static trim and phase invert (`strip.rs:207-214`), its inserts, its trim lane (a plug-in-parameter lane, kept with the freeze inputs), and a fader stage at 0 dB with mute and solo cleared but **with its VCA's level and mute**, because VCA membership is kept and the member strip reads the VCA track even though the render marks it inactive (`offline.rs:313-338`, `strip.rs:319-329`); pan is set to unity. During playback the frozen track (`Session::with_frozen_audio`, `crates/model/src/session.rs:519-543`) drops its inserts and plug-in lanes but keeps its static trim, phase invert and VCA membership, which are not freeze inputs (`crates/model/src/track.rs:363-375`), so those are applied a second time. This record does not change that boundary and claims no equality between a frozen and an unfrozen track; it promises only that the freeze render and the frozen playback each use this gain path (block-partition invariant). The double processing is a defect of today's code, to be fixed on its own. **What a bounce equals:** the gain path applies the same gain to the same sample as live playback of that snapshot at any buffer size, **from live state at rest (fresh strips) or once live state has settled** (no hand ramp, gate ramp or Touch return running and no control held). A bounce begun while a hand ramp is running applies the snapshot's target at once, and a bounce never reproduces a hand performance (decision 8). A whole bounce equals playback only where the rest of the session is block-invariant too (no built-in parameter automation, no hosted plug-in, no overrun).

### 16. Real-time rules, and every per-block decision replaced

- No allocation on the audio thread. Each strip gets, at plan compile on the control thread, three `f64` buffers of the plan's largest block: the smoothed level curve `H`, the gate curve `gate64` (read by the fader stage and the pre-fader sends), and one send-factor scratch reused send by send; each stage forms its gain from them per sample and rounds once. The strip's own hand state, gate counter, output-matrix rows at `p`, per-row ramp state and held rows `H_r` are fixed arrays in `Strip` (so `size_of::<Strip>()`, already counted, covers them), built with the strip and moved in `move_strip`. Per-send state (hand ramp, `c_h`, matrix rows, held rows) is a per-plan table sized by the strip's send count. Mixer gain lane and clip envelope group indexes are built by the plan compiler (decision 2; slice G1a).
- **Memory admission before allocation.** Every allocation this record adds to a plan is counted in the compiler's storage measure (`crates/mix/src/compile/storage.rs`, `measure` and `strip_bytes`) with checked arithmetic, before anything is allocated, exactly as the existing buffers and delay lines are: the three `f64` curve buffers per strip (3 × 8 bytes × block), the per-send state tables, the group indexes (4 bytes per point of each mixer gain lane and clip envelope the plan reads, admitted from slice G1a on, with or without any cache: a 100,000-point envelope takes 400,000 bytes), and the G3 fade cache if one is built (decided item 9; its fixed budget). A total over `PlanLimits::prepared_bytes` (1 GiB by default, `crates/mix/src/compile.rs:50`) or an arithmetic overflow refuses the plan with `CompileError::ResourceLimit`, before allocation, and the active plan keeps playing unchanged, as for any refused compile today (`compile.rs:663-690`). The fade cache is the one optional item: when it does not fit, the plan compiles without it (fades evaluated per sample, same values) instead of being refused. Building off the audio thread alone does not bound memory; the admission does.
- Work per sample is bounded: at most one `exp2` per moving level stage (fader, master, each moving send level, each moving clip envelope), one `sin` per moving Equal Power or S-Curve fade (a curvature's `exp2` is once per fade), integer arithmetic for gates, one multiply-add per pan tap, at most one group crossing per lane; per block, one `partition_point` per ruling lane and a gate window walk of at most `N_s` groups.
- **No per-block gain assumption anywhere.** The render graph tail and strip pool work (#174: `crates/rt/src/task_graph.rs`, `crates/mix/src/strip_pool/`, `crates/mix/src/graph/tail.rs`) must not cache a per-block gain or mute decision; a grep of `crates/mix/src/strip_pool/` and `crates/mix/src/graph/` at the branch base finds none (`graph/tail.rs:1-2` states that gains and mutes never remove a writer). ### 17. Cost, its budgets, and the fallback

Computed from bench medians and call counts, not a measured loop (medians from the portable math bench on the Windows reference machine: `f64` `exp2` 2.86 ns, exact `db_to_gain` 3.90 ns, `pan_gains` 83.64 ns a call; the range is `exp2` alone against the full conversion):

| Load | Conversions a second at 48 kHz | Share of one core, 48 kHz | Share of one core, 192 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 static faders (one conversion a block, 256-sample blocks) | 18,750 | 0.007 % | same order |
| 10 faders moving at once | 480,000 | 0.14 to 0.19 % | 0.55 to 0.75 % |
| 100 faders moving at once | 4,800,000 | 1.37 to 1.87 % | 5.49 to 7.49 % |
| 100 faders and 2 send levels each, all moving | 14,400,000 | 4.12 to 5.62 % | 16.5 to 22.5 % |

The last row is a pathological ceiling, not a session. Not counted: the multiplies into the audio (present today), lane walks and gate counting (small), and pan knots, which are not small. **Pan knot estimates (computed, not measured):** the first revision's 1.2546 % of a core for 100 moving mono pans at 48 kHz counted two pan-law calls per 64-sample grid knot and no breakpoint knots (1,500 calls a second per pan); one call per knot suffices (a knot's matrix ends one interval and starts the next) plus one per block for the knot before the block's first sample: 937.5 calls a second per pan with 256-sample blocks, 0.784 %; with a write pass's breakpoint every 20 ms adding 50 knots a second, 987.5 calls and 0.826 %; at 192 kHz with 1,024-sample blocks, 2.71 %. These use `transit_math`'s `pan_gains` median; the law `build_matrix` calls today (`transit_dsp`, `f32`) was not benchmarked. Stereo sources use the balance law, which calls no transcendental function. Today's `lerp_gain` converts both block ends on every call per sample per channel unless the optimizer hoists them (unverified, audit A14), so today's cost is unknown and may be higher.

**Budgets, landing-blocking, one per slice.** Each slice measures on the reference machine with `render_bench`, release build, median of five runs, the difference between the workload and the same session with every lane, ramp and gate static, as a share of real time, at 48 kHz with 256-sample blocks and at 192 kHz with 1,024-sample blocks. A slice that misses its budget does not land as specified: G1 lands with the fallback below instead (the partition and closed-form tests pass unchanged; the closed-form test's bound is one `f32` ulp either way); G3 is measured with the fade cache disabled and, if it misses, stops and reports, and the qualified promise below the table applies with an uncached budget the maintainer sets; G2 stops and reports, and the maintainer decides. The figures are recorded in each slice's result and in this record.

| Slice | Workload | Budget, 48 kHz / 192 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | 100 stereo tracks of constant input, no inserts, every fader on a volume lane that moves in every block | 2 % / 8 % of one core |
| G2, pans | 100 mono tracks into the stereo main mix, each with a pan lane moving in every block and a breakpoint every 20 ms | 1.5 % / 6 % |
| G2, sends and gates | 100 stereo tracks, two post-fader sends each to two stereo busses, every send level on a lane moving in every block; plus every track's mute lane toggling every 50 ms | 3 % / 12 % |
| G3 | ADR 0056 9.6 g: one stereo clip at 48 kHz with both fades curved and overlapping across the whole clip and a moving envelope (192 kHz: the same clip at that rate), with the fade cache disabled, once per fade shape | 0.5 % / 2 % of one core (104.17 ns per sample at either rate); a clip with no fade and no envelope no slower than the base beyond 5 % |

The G1 computed figures sit at 69 to 94 % of its budget; the G2 pan estimate at 52 to 55 % of its budget at 48 kHz and 45 % at 192 kHz; so the measurements decide. G3 has no automatic fallback: its measurement is uncached (decision 13), and if any shape misses, the qualified promise applies (decided item 9): a bounded cache of `f64` fade factors (built off the audio thread with the same arithmetic, a fixed per-plan byte budget admitted against the plan limit, fades it does not hold evaluated per sample, so no rendered value changes; the benchmark workload with both fades resident in an admitted cache gated at the stated budget and the uncached cost at a budget the maintainer sets, both stated on the claims page).

**The fallback: geometric stepping in `f64`, anchored.** For a level stage over an interval where `D(n)` is affine in `n` (between consecutive anchors, with no Touch return or hand ramp running and `D` inside (−144, cap] at every sample of the interval; otherwise that interval is evaluated per sample as above): anchors are every group time of each ruling lane and every timeline multiple of 64. At an anchor `a`, `y(a) = exp2(D(a) · C)` in `f64`; for `a < n` < the next anchor, `y(n) = y(n − 1) · r` in `f64` with `r = exp2(s · C)`, `s` the interval's slope in dB per sample; the stage applies `y(n)` (times its gate) rounded once. A block that starts inside an interval replays the chain from the interval's anchor (at most 63 multiplies), never starting a fresh one, so the fallback is also block-partition invariant by construction. Cost: one `exp2` per anchor plus one multiply per sample (about 0.5 % of a core for 100 moving stages at 48 kHz, assuming 1 ns a multiply, unmeasured). **What "identical" was shown for:** in the study's model, on #501's case, the anchored chain gave `f32` output identical to the per-sample rule at every one of 4,096 samples at every block size; the `f64` chain's drift over 64 steps is about 3·10⁻¹⁵ relative, nine orders below an `f32` step. That is one case, not a guarantee: a value within the drift of an `f32` rounding midpoint can round differently, so the fallback's output is block-invariant by its anchoring and within one `f32` ulp of the exact value (decision 3's contract), not promised bit-equal to the per-sample rule.

### 18. What changes audibly for existing sessions

Largest level difference between today's render and this record's, the worst over 16 placements of the lane against the blocks (computed):

| Scene | Block 128 | 512 | 1024 | 2048 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slow fade, 6 dB over 2 s | 0.002 dB | 0.008 | 0.016 | 0.032 |
| Fader ride, ±1 dB every 20 ms (a write pass's spacing) | 0.07 | 0.27 | 0.53 | 0.99 |
| Duck, 12 dB over 30 ms | 0.27 | 1.1 | 2.4 | 5.6 |
| Edit fade, 24 dB over 256 samples | 3.6 | 18 | 21 | 22 |
| 60 dB over 10 ms | 5.1 | 38 | 54 | 57 |

Slow fades are unchanged to the ear; recorded rides move by tenths of a dB, mostly from breakpoints inside a block now acting on time; fast ducks and edit fades change clearly, and correctly. Also: mute, solo, fader, pan and send moves stop clicking; a mute lane acts on its breakpoint with a 5 ms ramp instead of up to a block late as a step; send level lanes act inside the block; send mute and send pan lanes start working (sessions that drew them sound different); pan automation is smooth instead of a 64-sample staircase; clip fades follow the closed law per sample and envelopes are exact per sample (decision 13); a phase invert toggle crosses zero over 5 ms; a master stored above +12 dB plays at +12. **Static levels also move,** by however far today's `f32` `powf` conversions (`transit_dsp::db_to_gain`, `crates/dsp/src/lib.rs:890-891`, and the clip's own, `crates/model/src/clip.rs:438-439`) are from the exact conversion. That is not bounded by one rounding step: both divide by 20 in `f32` first, and that rounding alone moves the result; at `f32(−140.1)` dB even a correctly rounded power after today's `f32` division is six `f32` steps from the correctly rounded exact value (9.885528129416343·10⁻⁸ against 9.885523866159929·10⁻⁸; a computed model, not a measurement of any platform's `powf`, which may add its own error). Each slice records the actual old-to-new differences of the values it re-pins (decision 19), separately from the new function's accuracy contract (decision 3), which concerns only the new function against the exact value.

**Alpha rule.** This is a clean break, said loudly in the release notes; there is no option to keep the old rendering. Release-note text: "Levels under automation changed, and are now right. The mixer follows automation sample by sample instead of once per audio buffer, so a mix sounds the same at any buffer size and a bounce matches playback. Fast fades and ducks change the most; slow ones do not change audibly. Mute, solo, fader, pan and send moves no longer click (mutes, solos and phase invert take 5 ms, other moves 20 ms). Send mute and send pan automation now work. Clip fades and gain envelopes changed slightly. Existing sessions render differently from earlier builds; there is no option to keep the old rendering."

## Out of scope

- **Automation timing against latency compensation.** Every lane is read at the uncompensated position: the fader reads at `pos` while the strip's audio is its own insert latency late, and pan and send level read at `pos` while they apply after the edge delay. So on a track behind a latent plug-in the automation leads the audio by the latency accumulated before its stage. It is constant, so it does not break block invariance. Because the evaluator is a pure function of position, the fix is an offset per stage (the strip's latency at that point), and it is its own later slice with its own record or amendment (decided item 6: its own slice, filed as an issue), **scheduled for v0.8.0** (the maintainer, after the convention audit). Until it lands, automation on a track with a high-latency plug-in plays early by the audio-path latency accumulated before the automated stage, including any preceding compensation delay (Consequences; the claims page states it). Conventions: Logic Pro documents a Sample Accurate Automation setting (confirmed on the archived 10.4 help page only) and Plug-in Latency Compensation, but the pages read say nothing about whether automation is compensated; that Ableton Live compensates automation rests on a FAQ that could not be read (HTTP 403, four attempts) and stays unverified.
- **Device-parameter lanes and rack macros** keep today's evaluator (`device_value_at`) and timed delivery (decision 2) and stay block-quantized: built-ins get block-start values (`apply_params`), macros are read at block rate. Sample-accurate device-parameter automation is a later record or issue, which would also decide whether device lanes move to decision 2's evaluator with matching timed delivery. Fixing it means sample-accurate parameters in every built-in or splitting blocks at breakpoints; it is a separate decision (ADR 0055 already names it; decided item 7). The claims page says the block-size claim covers the mixer's gain path only.
- **The External Instrument's return gain** (`process_strip_source`, `strip.rs:122`) stays a per-block value with hand steps; a small follow-up.
- **The monitor path** in `transit-playback` (live only) keeps its own ramp.
- **The clip-effect gain and the click level** (decision 16, classified): not stages of this path.
- **Freeze's double processing** of static trim, phase invert and VCA (decision 15): a defect of today's code, fixed on its own; this record claims no frozen-equals-unfrozen equality.
- **Whether Latch suspends the lane as Touch does** (decision 8).

## Consequences

- A mix renders identically at every buffer size and in a bounce, as far as the mixer's gain path goes, and that becomes a published, tested claim.
- #501's error disappears: every sample under a volume lane is within one rounding step of the closed form, breakpoints exact.
- Hand moves, mutes and solos stop clicking; send mute and send pan lanes work.
- Zero and unity mean the same on every path, so no block boundary can change a bit or a sign of any non-NaN result; a NaN stays a NaN either way, with its payload unspecified.
- Plans take more prepared memory (three `f64` curves per strip, per-send state, group indexes), admitted against the plan limit before allocation.
- The clip editor's gain arithmetic (ADR 0056 decision 9) is decided here once, with the mixer's.
- Moving faders cost a conversion a sample; per-slice budgets bound it and the anchored fallback caps the fader stage.
- Existing sessions render differently once, with no option to keep the old rendering.
- **Automation plays early behind a latent plug-in until v0.8.0.** Every lane is still read at the uncompensated position, so on a track with a high-latency plug-in (a linear-phase EQ or a look-ahead limiter, often tens of milliseconds) a volume dip, a mute, a pan move or a send throw arrives early by the audio-path latency accumulated before the automated stage, including any preceding compensation delay, ahead of the audio it was drawn against. The follow-up that reads lanes at the compensated position is scheduled for v0.8.0 (Out of scope); the claims page states the limit until then.

## Alternatives rejected

- **A fixed grid, linear in gain between grid points** (with every breakpoint in the grid): block-invariant by construction, but a chord in each cell. For 60 dB over 10 ms at 48 kHz the worst error is 0.057, 0.23 and 0.91 dB at grid steps of 16, 32 and 64 samples (computed by the chord formula and by simulation over every phase); 0.068, 0.27 and 1.05 dB at 44.1 kHz. Without the breakpoints in the grid it rises to 0.51, 1.06 and 2.26 dB, the ramp starts up to a step early, and a one-sample dip to −144 dB renders 1.0 instead of 0.0. Kept for pan only (decision 10), where the per-sample law costs 84 ns.
- **Splitting blocks at breakpoints:** block-invariant, but each segment is still a chord unless also evaluated per sample, and every split re-enters every strip and hosted plug-in with a short, irregular block. `render_active` already splits at tempo events and each sub-block runs the whole graph; a write pass leaves a point every 20 ms on each written lane, so many tracks would split most blocks. Handling gain inside the strip costs nothing outside it.
- **Geometric stepping as the default** (#501's suggested direction, and how the original code base fixed the same inherited behavior): needs the anchoring rule of decision 17 to stay block-invariant, and its rounding can differ from the per-sample rule's near an `f32` midpoint, so it is within one `f32` ulp of the exact value like the per-sample rule but not bit-equal to it; it saves about 2.7 times on moving stages only. Kept as the named fallback. In `f32` it drifts 7.7·10⁻⁷ relative after 64 steps and about 1.3·10⁻³ (0.011 dB) after one second at 48 kHz: rejected outright.
- **Smoothing every gain through a filter or slew toward its target:** smooths intended automation steps too and makes the output depend on history, so a bounce started mid-session would differ from the full render.
- **A ramp across the next block for hand moves** (#526's suggested direction): its length would be the block size, so a move would sound different at each buffer size.
- **Multiplying by 0 and by 1 like any other gain:** simpler, but the constant path (a zero fill, a skipped multiply) and the moving path (a multiply) would then differ in sign bits and NaN handling at block boundaries, and a muted strip would pass NaN.

## Decided by the maintainer (2026-11-12)

On 2026-11-12 the maintainer answered the eleven open questions of the proposed record: each is decided as the record recommended, and the alternatives are kept for the history. The record refers to these as decided items 1 to 11.

1. **Mute automation.** *Decided:* a 5 ms ramp anchored at the breakpoint, for lanes and hand alike (click-free, exactly specified and testable; no convention found either way). Alternative: an exact step.
2. **Ramp times.** *Decided:* 20 ms for continuous controls, 5 ms for switches, and a mute/solo gate reversal turning back at the same slope (a phase reversal restarts a full ramp, decision 7); engineering choices, not a cited convention.
3. **Phase invert.** *Decided:* a ramp through zero over 5 ms with the plain linear law, the one law every hand ramp uses (for equal-magnitude opposite endpoints an exact zero sample only at even ramp lengths; at 44.1 kHz the dip bottoms out at ±1/221, −46.9 dB). Alternatives: an instant flip, a flip at the next zero crossing, a zero-forcing law.
4. **Clip gain edits during playback.** *Decided:* they step at the next block, unsmoothed, for now (an edit, not a performance control; a clip has no gain state); revisited if a click is reported.
5. **Bounces and recorded passes.** *Decided:* a bounce renders as if no control were held and nothing were moving (live holds and ramps discarded), and recorded automation is exact on playback but not a sample-for-sample copy of the hand performance; both stated on the claims page.
6. **Automation timing against latency compensation.** *Decided:* its own later slice, filed as an issue, not inside this work (Out of scope). *Scheduled after the convention audit (2026-11-12):* the follow-up is scheduled for v0.8.0, not left unscheduled; until then, automation on a track with a high-latency plug-in plays early by the audio-path latency accumulated before the automated stage, including any preceding compensation delay (Consequences, the claims page).
7. **Device-parameter lanes (built-in and hosted) and rack macros.** *Decided:* they stay block-quantized for now, keeping today's evaluator and timed delivery, including their duplicate-point behavior (an interior duplicate-time group reads its last value, where the mixer's gain lanes read its first); sample-accurate device-parameter automation is a separate issue, stated on the claims page.
8. **The clip fade law.** *Decided:* it follows ADR 0056's question 9, which the maintainer decided on 2026-11-12 as the closed law (decision 13); G3 no longer waits for that answer.
9. **If G3's uncached measurement misses its budget for any fade shape.** *Decided:* the qualified promise with the measured uncached figure stated openly: a 64 MiB per-plan fade cache (about 8.4 million `f64` factors, 175 seconds of fade at 48 kHz), the 0.5 % budget applying only when both fades are resident in an admitted cache, and the uncached cost gated at a budget the maintainer sets and stated on the claims page, which applies otherwise, including when plan admission omits the cache; the cache changes cost, never values. Moot if every shape passes uncached (no cache is built and the promise is unconditional). Alternative: hold G3 until a cheaper exact evaluation exists.
10. **A pan's Touch return.** *Decided:* blended in coefficient space (a hard left-to-right return dips about 3.02 dB below the −3 dB law at its midpoint, like a hand pan ramp), instead of today's blend of pan positions; it keeps the knots a function of position alone and costs one multiply-add per tap.

The zero and unity rule of decision 5 was never a choice: identical results on the constant and moving paths require it.

## Judging round 2

The second judging round accepted round 1's items 6, 8, 9, 11 and 12 and asked for eleven further changes; each of its figures was reproduced in a model (`python -I`, standard library, `f32` by struct rounding, 50-digit `Decimal` references; the platform's `sin`, `cos` and `exp2` standing in for the seam, so every figure is computed, not measured) before the record changed:

- **Summing stages** (round item 1): exact gain unity is told apart from source bits preserved through a sum (`+0 + (−0 · 1)` is `+0`); the promise is bit identity between the constant and moving paths for every non-NaN result, and a NaN result stays a NaN with its payload unspecified (Rust guarantees no payload from arithmetic); a destination sample with no contributing term is not touched, closing the case where `apply_matrix_span`'s `+0.0` accumulator turned a `−0.0` destination into `+0.0`; the accumulation order is today's (decision 5).
- **The clamp equivalence** (item 2): it holds for non-NaN inputs only (`NaN.min(12)` is 12, so today's helper gives 3.981071710586548 where the new function gives 0); the every-input form and explicit NaN tests are stated (decision 3).
