# ADR 0041: One Sampler instrument with three views (Instrument, Pads, Slices)

- **Status:** proposed 2026-11-01 (decided by the maintainer with the coordinator; slices filed)
- **Date:** 2026-11-01
- **Reopen when:** a pad needs its own insert chain or output often enough that "Expand to rack" is the common path rather than the exception; the shaper and matrix cannot serve per-pad or per-zone modulation; streaming long samples ([ADR 0035](0035-disk-backed-audio-sources.md)) cannot meet the voice count on the reference hardware; or a rack-native sampler ([ADR 0034](0034-racks-layering-instruments-through-track-groups.md)) makes the Pads view redundant.

## Context

transit has one small sampler (`crates/dsp/src/plugins/sampler.rs`): one sample across the keyboard, 32 voices, 4-point cubic interpolation, key and velocity ranges, an amp ADSR, a start point, forward or ping-pong loops and gate or one-shot playback. The host hands it the audio through `Plugin::set_sample`. It cannot play a drum kit, slice a loop, layer velocities or hold more than one sample.

Three requests converge on it. A beat slicer in the style of a REX loop player (#267: slices from WAV cue points or detected transients, mapped to keys, with a command that writes the notes that rebuild the loop). Pitched, multisampled sounds (zones, velocity layers, round robins, loops), which also covers the request for a General MIDI SoundFont instrument (#33). And sampling what the computer plays (#268), whose captured clip should become an instrument in one step.

Convention (transit follows what Ableton and Logic agree on, per their current reference manuals). Both split sampling by task and not by product. Ableton's Simpler has Classic, One-Shot and Slicing modes in one device (slices by transient, beat, region or manually, played from a key range, with a Slice to New MIDI Track command), and its Sampler is the multisample instrument. Logic's Quick Sampler has Classic, One Shot and Slice modes in one instrument, and its Sampler is the multisample instrument. For drums both use a rack of samplers: Ableton's Drum Rack is a rack of chains each holding a Simpler or Sampler (with choke groups and per-pad outputs and inserts), and Logic's Drum Machine Designer is a track stack of Quick Samplers (with per-pad outputs, mute and solo, and a choke setting for hi-hat style groups). Both agree that the one-sample instrument has modes in the same panel and that per-pad routing is a rack feature. The maintainer wants the common case light: one picker entry, one panel, per-pad routing only when asked for.

## Decision

1. **One instrument, one picker entry: "Sampler".** It has three views of the same patch: **Instrument**, **Pads** and **Slices**. Switching views changes the panel, never the sound or the mapping: a patch built in Pads plays the same in Slices, and a slice is a pad reached another way. The Macro Oscillator stays the default instrument for a new track ([ADR 0033](0033-built-in-instruments-mutable-instruments-port.md)).
2. **Instrument view** is Ableton's Sampler and Logic's Sampler in one panel: a keyboard map with key zones and velocity zones, each zone one sample with its root key, tune, level and pan; velocity layers; round robins (a group of samples on one zone that alternates in order, or at random); loop points per zone (forward, ping-pong, crossfaded). Zones fade at their edges as the rack's zones do ([ADR 0034](0034-racks-layering-instruments-through-track-groups.md): velocity fades, Ableton's convention), so overlapping layers cross instead of switching.
3. **Pads view** is for drum hits and slices: a grid of pads, each with its own sample (or slice), tune, reverse, amp envelope, a choke group, mute and solo, and a trigger note. One stereo output for the whole instrument. A retrigger restarts the pad's voice (or layers it, per a pad setting); a **choke group** silences the other pads of its group when one of them starts (the hi-hat convention in Drum Rack and Drum Machine Designer), with a short fixed fade to avoid a click.
4. **Slices view** shows one loop's waveform with markers: transient or beat-grid slices, editable (move, add, remove, name), each slice mapped to a pad and to a MIDI note (the first pad plays C3 and slices follow upward, so slice = note - 60). A slice plays its region at the original pitch, with no keyboard transposition, and can be reversed and given attack, decay, declick and note-release fades. Selecting a slice highlights its pad; playing a pad highlights its waveform region.
5. **Slice sources, in order:** the WAV file's own cue points and labels first (they are the author's slices and their names); then transient detection; then beat-grid division (even divisions from the file's tempo metadata or the session tempo). Detection runs on the stereo energy, the power of both channels measured and summed, not a summed signal, so opposite-phase material does not cancel; about 5 ms windows and a 50 ms minimum gap are the starting values, tuned on real drum loops including quiet ghost notes and flams, and the sensitivity is a control. Results are always editable; a detector pass never overwrites edits without being asked.
6. **Two workflows for playing slices.** Perform them and record the MIDI as with any instrument. Or **Slice to MIDI**, one undo step: write the notes that reconstruct the groove onto the track, in two timing modes. *Fit loop length*: slice positions follow their fraction of the loop's length in beats, so the loop re-arranges with the session tempo. *Keep original duration*: positions follow their original seconds at the session tempo, so the loop keeps its feel and its length in time. The command is refused, with a message, when the loop has no slices.
7. **Per-pad output routing is not inside the instrument.** An **Expand to rack** command turns the pads into rack members ([ADR 0034](0034-racks-layering-instruments-through-track-groups.md)), each a Sampler holding that pad's sample, when per-pad inserts or outputs are needed. This is Drum Rack and Drum Machine Designer's model (a rack of samplers) reached from the light case. Expanding is one undo step, replaces the Sampler instrument with the rack, and the notes on the track play the same.
8. **Modulation is the Macro Oscillator's shaper and matrix, reused:** the same filter (multimode SVF, 12 and 24 dB), envelopes, LFOs, 16-row matrix (source, via, destination, amount, curve), panel, help text and automation targets ([ADR 0033](0033-built-in-instruments-mutable-instruments-port.md), implementation note of 2026-10-31, #243), moved to a shared module and not copied. Sampler sources and destinations extend the same lists (sample start, pitch, pad level), and a patch with the section off renders what the instrument renders without it. A pad's envelope is the amp envelope; the shaper acts on the instrument's voices.
9. **Storage and playback.** Samples are read through the sources of the session; a sample longer than the in-memory limit uses the disk-backed sources of [ADR 0035](0035-disk-backed-audio-sources.md) (a streaming thread fills a ring ahead of the voice, and the voice only drains it). Voices, events, pads, zones and slice data are preallocated at their capacities; the render never allocates, locks or does IO ([ADR 0029](0029-strip-job-slots.md) rules). Slice and zone edits reach the audio thread through a fixed-capacity mailbox and retired buffers are freed off the audio thread. Voice stealing is deterministic (oldest in release, then oldest), and the same patch renders the same bits at any host block size, as the Macro Oscillator does.
10. **Formats in:** WAV (with cue points and tempo metadata), AIFF and FLAC. **SFZ** is the open multisample interchange, arriving with the Instrument view (regions with key and velocity ranges, root key, loops and round-robin sequence positions; release triggers are deferred). SoundFont (SF2) import is a later addition and General MIDI banks are not a goal; this absorbs #33's request. No proprietary kit or loop formats: REX (`.rx2`) is out of scope (its SDK carries its own license terms) and the REX workflow is the reference for the feel only (#267), as no Native Instruments format is parsed. Samples are plain files; kits are folders.
11. **Playback is pitched at first:** no per-slice time stretching. The known limit is that slower tempos open gaps between slices and faster ones overlap their tails. The Time Stretcher engine (#199 slice 2) is the candidate for stretching later.
12. **The existing sampler is the seed.** `sampler.rs` keeps its place and its picker entry, grows pads, zones and slices around its voice, and a session saved with it loads unchanged: its single sample becomes a one-zone Instrument patch with the same parameters (ids unchanged, automation lanes and MIDI mappings intact). New parameters are written only when not at their defaults.

## Open questions

- **Pad count and layout:** 16 pads in a 4x4 grid (the MPC and Maschine layout, and what a controller's 16 pads map to) with banks for more? Or one larger grid like Ableton's Drum Rack view?
- **Choke-group count:** 8 groups, or 16? How the groups are labeled (letters, numbers, colors).
- **Dragging slices between pads:** whether a slice can be dragged from one pad to another (re-mapping which note plays it) or only reordered in the Slices view; whether a pad can hold a slice and a loose sample at once.
- **Expand to rack naming and choke:** how members are named (pad name, then slice name, then "Pad 3"); and how choke groups are carried, since racks have no choke groups today. Options: add a rack-level choke group (a small ADR 0034 amendment), or drop choke on expansion with a note telling the user which pads were chained.
- **Velocity to slice start** (a harder hit starts the slice later, past the transient) is a later idea, not decided.

## Consequences

- One picker entry covers pitched, multisampled, drum and loop work; the panel changes by view. No new instrument kind appears in the picker, and the Macro Oscillator stays the default.
- The existing `sampler.rs` becomes the seed; sessions saved with the sampler keep loading and render the same bits (corpus fixtures pin this).
- The panel reuses the shaper and matrix widgets and their automation targets; the help gains a Sampler page (views, slicing, Slice to MIDI, Expand to rack).
- Rack members stay Samplers with ordinary parameters, so Expand to rack needs no new instrument; the cost is that per-pad inserts are a deliberate step.
- Preallocated capacities bound the instrument (pads, zones per patch, voices, slices per loop); the numbers are set in slice 1 and recorded here by amendment.
- Slice data and zone maps are part of the session, so they are saved, copied and undone with it; a missing sample file gives a clear message and silence, the same rule as other sources.
- Slice to MIDI follows the session tempo at the time of the command; later tempo changes do not move the notes.
- The cost is a large instrument. It is delivered in five slices, each testable alone: Pads and Slices, Instrument, streaming and formats, Expand to rack, later ideas.

## Alternatives considered

- **Separate instruments (Sampler, Drum Sampler, Slicer).** Three picker entries and three panels would duplicate voices, envelopes and the matrix, and moving a patch between them would lose the sound. Simpler and Quick Sampler keep modes in one device; so do we.
- **Per-pad outputs inside the instrument.** It turns the Sampler into a mini mixer (multi-output routing in every host path, bus assignment, a pad strip for inserts). Drum Rack and Drum Machine Designer solve it with a rack; Expand to rack does the same on demand and keeps the common case light.
- **A rack of one-sample Samplers as the only drum path.** It is the Ableton and Logic model, but a 16-member rack for a drum loop is heavy to build and to read; the Pads and Slices views are the light path in front of it.
- **Import REX (`.rx2`) files.** Proprietary format with its own SDK license; WAV with cue points is the interchange.
- **Stretch each slice to the session tempo from the start.** It sounds smoother at big tempo changes but needs the stretching engine (#199 slice 2); pitched playback first, stretching as a later option.
- **SoundFont as the multisample format.** SF2 is an old container built around a General MIDI payload; SFZ is open text, maps to zones directly and is what sample libraries ship. SF2 may be added later.
