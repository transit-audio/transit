# ADR 0054: Parametric EQ

- **Status:** accepted 2026-11-12 (proposed 2026-11-11; settled after three judging rounds with edits; the product choices decided by the maintainer on 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-12: the seven design points, the decisions listed in Context, and the recommendations of the earlier open list); amended 2026-11-12, accepted, slice 1 measurements and shared FFT ownership (amendment item 6); amended 2026-11-12, retired devices' lanes are kept (amendment item 8)
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Implementation:** not started. Slices in the Slices section. Issue #233.
- **Reopen when:** the portable math record (#437, #277) lands and changes a helper this device names; the zero-latency magnitude bounds of decision 2.5 are missed in the slice 1 measurement; a user needs a band frequency outside 20 Hz to 20 kHz, more than 24 bands, or a Q on the cuts; the linear-phase low-end resolution of decision 6.3 is reported as a problem even at Maximum, or a steep high cut needs a longer Natural correction than `D`; dynamic bands are designed (their own record); the CPU targets of decision 13 are missed; or the reference workstations' equalizers are found to agree on a behavior this record chose differently.

## Context

Issue #233 asks for a transparent parametric equalizer that competes on workflow and visualization: no "color", a display-first editor with the analyzer behind the curve and band nodes dragged directly, up to 24 bands added and removed dynamically, per-band stereo placement, three phase modes, gain match, and dynamic bands in a later release. The maintainer calls it a must-have for v0.7.1. Effects get a decision record first; this device is transit's own design, not a port ([ADR 0042](0042-stock-effects-airwindows-ports.md) covers ports).

**The panel is designed.** The design hand-off for #233 fixes the window (1120 by 560 points, minimum width 880), the toolbar (analyzer Pre, Post, Both, Off; Freeze; an analyzer settings menu with resolution, decay, peak hold and smoothing; phase Zero latency, Natural, Linear with the reported latency; Gain match with its applied gain; Range ±6, ±12, ±18, ±30 dB), the display well (log frequency 20 Hz to 20 kHz, the analyzer's own scale −90 to 0 dB), the OUT column (a stereo channel meter and an Output knob of ±24 dB), the band list (one chip per band, "+ Band", "6 of 24 bands") and the band strip (Type, Slope, Frequency, Gain, Q, Placement, On, Solo, remove, and a reserved dynamic block). It names nine band types: Bell, Low shelf, High shelf, Low cut, High cut, Notch, Band pass, Tilt shelf, Flat tilt. It shows Slope only for the cuts, Gain not for the cuts, Notch and Band pass, and Q not for the cuts. The hand-off is not on `main` yet; it lands with the panel slice. This record makes the engine deliver exactly what the panel shows and does not redesign the panel. Where the panel needs a fact only the engine can give (ranges, refusal words, the curve, the analyzer data, the latency), this record defines it.

**Decided by the maintainer (2026-11-11, #233; fixed here):**

1. The name is Parametric EQ. Transparent: no saturation, no modeled hardware.
2. Accurate drawing. The EQ curve is the engine's computed magnitude response, one value per physical pixel column joined by straight segments, never an approximated shape; a selected band's own curve is that band's response alone. The analyzer draws the measured spectrum: one point per frequency bin at the bin's center joined by straight segments, the maximum where several bins share a pixel column, no smoothing across frequency unless the user turns it on (1/12, 1/6, 1/3 octave; default Off, shown with a tag). Coarse low-end resolution is fixed in the engine, never in the drawing.
3. Q changes with Ctrl+wheel over a node and with Alt-drag; a plain wheel never changes a control.
4. Per-band placement (stereo, left, right, mid, side) is shown by a letter badge and by solid and dashed channel curves, not by color; color names the band (content palette, fills only).
5. Up to 24 bands.
6. No second bypass: the title bar's power button is the device's bypass. A bypassed device shows no moving meter or analyzer; a static dimmed curve may stay.
7. Project-wide rules apply: no analog dials; VU is a ballistics mode of the meter; follow what the reference workstations agree on unless transit can clearly do better (both put the analyzer behind the curve with direct node dragging; transit adds pre and post blending); alpha, so no migration shims; renders are to be bit-identical across operating systems through portable math (#437, #277), so this device lists exactly which transcendental functions it needs and routes them through that seam.

**Decided by the maintainer on 2026-11-11 during the judging rounds (fixed here):**

8. Default phase mode Zero latency, and the modes framed by use: Zero latency for general mixing and tracking; the higher-latency modes for mastering and other work where nothing is being tracked (decision 6).
9. Linear has a Resolution choice from the start, a device parameter (decision 6.3).
10. EQ 7-Band and EQ 1-Band are removed in the release that ships slice 4, by ADR 0040's rule, with no conversion (decision 16).
11. Shelves and the tilt shelf keep the full Q range, 0.1 to 40, with the continuous factored design of decision 2.2.
12. Gain match is clamped to ±12 dB (decision 7).
13. Gain match is in the minimum cut: applied in slice 2, shown in slice 3 (Slices).
14. The low-end error of Linear's shortest setting is stated plainly in the record and the help (decisions 6.3 and 17).

**How built-in effects and the existing EQs work today** (read on `main` at 6531fba9):

- A built-in is a `Plugin` (`crates/dsp/src/lib.rs`) with a static `PluginInfo` (`id`, `name`, `short_name` of at most 8 characters, `category`, `params`, `pages`) registered in `crates/dsp/src/plugins/mod.rs`. Parameters are static `ParamInfo` tables built with `lin`, `log`, `toggle` and `choice` (`crates/dsp/src/params.rs`); `Params::set` clamps into range and turns NaN into the default. `ParamInfo` has no "not automatable" flag: every parameter can be automated, MIDI-learned and mapped by a rack macro. `Unit` has `None`, `Db`, `Hz`, `Ms`, `Percent`, `Ratio`, `Semitones`, `Cents`, `Seconds`, `Toggle` and `Choice`.
- **How many parameters a built-in may have.** The mix keeps the values last pushed to each plug-in in `Applied` (`crates/mix/src/lib.rs`): up to `APPLIED_INLINE` = 136 values inline in the slot, longer maps in storage the plan compiler prepares off the audio thread (`plan::needs_storage`, `Attach::Applied`). The test `every_builtin_parameter_map_is_tracked_inline` (`crates/mix/src/tests.rs`) requires every built-in to fit inline; the longest today is the Texture Synth's 131. No other limit applies to a parameter count: automation lanes name a parameter by insert slot and id (`AutoParam::Plugin { slot, param: String }`, `crates/model/src/automation.rs`), MIDI mappings and rack macros name it by id (`crates/model/src/midi_map.rs`, `crates/model/src/rack.rs`), built-in presets are JSON maps of id to value (`crates/ui-studio/src/presets.rs`), and a session stores `Insert::params` as a `BTreeMap<String, f32>` (`crates/model/src/mixer.rs`). Factory macro pages hold 8 slots each (`PAGE_SLOTS`, ADR 0047); each slot may name an enable toggle that dims it.
- The mix gives a built-in its parameter values at the block start (`apply_params`, `crates/mix/src/automation.rs`): one `set_param` per changed value, on the audio thread.
- **EQ 7-Band and EQ 1-Band** (`crates/dsp/src/plugins/eq.rs`) run RBJ cookbook biquads (`crates/dsp/src/biquad.rs`): coefficients computed in f64 and rounded to f32, frequency clamped to 1 Hz to 0.49·fs, Q to 0.025 to 100, gain to ±48 dB, run as transposed direct form II in f32 with the states flushed of denormals (`util::flush`) and reset to zero after a non-finite output. Every parameter has a 20 ms one-pole `Smoother` in its own value (frequency smoothed linearly in Hz), and while any smoother settles the sections are redesigned every 32 samples. `eq7_response` and `eq1_response` compute the magnitude response in dB from the same design functions (`Coeffs::magnitude_db`, f64); only tests call them today. Cuts are 6 to 24 dB per octave from first-order and Butterworth sections (`butterworth_qs`).
- **The closed-form evidence the issue cites** is the Filter insert's test (`crates/dsp/src/plugins/filter/tests.rs`, `every_mode_and_slope_matches_the_analytic_transfer_function`): the impulse response's DFT within 0.05 dB of an independently written digital transfer function where it is above −60 dB, else within 1e-4 linear. It is a tolerance test, not a bit-exact one.
- **State-variable sections** live in `transit-instrument-core` (`crates/instrument-core/src/svf.rs`): the trapezoidal-integrator SVF in f32 with `g = tan(π·fc/fs)`, cutoff clamped at `max_cutoff` = 0.49·fs, low, band (`k·v1`), high and notch outputs, states flushed. Its `math` module (`crates/instrument-core/src/math.rs`) routes `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `exp`, `exp2`, `ln`, `log2` and `powf` (f32) and `tan`, `sqrt` and `floor` (f64) to the platform with `std` and to `libm` without; it is the seam a portable implementation will replace.
- **Spectrum.** `crates/dsp/src/spectrum.rs` has `magnitude_db(samples, fft_size)`: a Hann window, `rustfft`, a full-scale sine at a bin center reading about 0 dB, floored at −144 dB. It plans an FFT and allocates on every call. Only tests call it.
- **Sample taps.** An insert whose plug-in returns true from `Plugin::wants_sample_tap` (the Tuner) gets a `SampleTap` (`crates/mix/src/sample_tap.rs`): a preallocated ring of 16,384 frames of the channels' signed sum, written by the render only while the UI has enabled it, with relaxed stores and a release store of the write counter; a reader takes a span ending `READ_MARGIN` (2048) frames behind the newest write. The strip pushes it after `run_insert` (`crates/mix/src/strip.rs`). Nothing allocates or locks on the audio thread.
- **Scope taps.** Every insert instance has a `ScopeTap` (`crates/mix/src/scope.rs`): a ring of the per-frame peak of the insert's output (the largest channel) and a few per-block words, read by the UI through `ScopeHub` by track and insert uid. It holds no per-channel peaks. The title bars' output meters read it.
- **Latency.** `Plugin::latency` reports samples for delay compensation. The strip sums it every block (`strip.rs`). `low_latency::observe_raw` (`crates/mix/src/low_latency.rs`), run every render block, compares each slot's `latency()` with the last value and bumps `raw_latency_revision` on a change; the player then requests the same document again (`crates/playback/src/player/runtime.rs`), and the new plan's compensation includes the new latency. Offline processing compensates `latency()` (`crates/dsp/src/offline.rs`).
- **Bypass.** A bypassed insert is skipped by the strip (`continue` before `run_insert`), so it neither processes nor pushes its taps. **Reset** (`Plugin::reset`) runs on stop on the audio thread (`render_pool::reset_render_state`); `snap_initial_params` runs on the control thread for a fresh instance only.
- **Device window.** `crates/ui-studio/src/device_window.rs`: content-sized and fixed, the design width narrowed toward the panel's minimum on a smaller screen, recomputed every frame (so a note row that appears re-fits the window), no resize handle.
- **Loudness.** `crate::meter::k_weighting` (`crates/dsp/src/meter.rs`, used by `loudness.rs`) builds the ITU-R BS.1770 K-weighting biquads.

(Amended 2026-11-12: the automation lanes are kept as unassigned lanes instead of being dropped; decision 16, amendment item 8.)
- **ADR 0053** (accepted, not implemented) adds `transit_dsp::builtin_check_param` for refusals when set, and accumulate-and-swap meter cells in the `ScopeTap` (`Plugin::band_meters`, `BandMeters { bands, io }`) whose `io` part carries per-channel input and output peaks.

## Decision

### 1. One device, its identity and its home

- **Registry id `parametric_eq`**, name "Parametric EQ", short name "ParamEQ", `Category::Eq`, an effect insert (`is_instrument: false`), `audiosuite: true` (offline processing already compensates latency). No other product's name appears in an id, a parameter, a preset, the help or the panel.
- **The DSP lives in a new Tier B crate, `transit-parametric-eq`** (`crates/parametric-eq`): the band designs, the section realization, the band chain, the placement stages, the smoothing and ramps, the correction and linear-phase kernel builders, the partitioned convolution, the gain-match computation and the response functions the panel draws from. `default = ["std"]`, `#![cfg_attr(not(feature = "std"), no_std)]`, `alloc`, allocation only in constructors and `prepare`; it enters the bare-metal compile job, `xtask`'s `LAYERS` (layer 1) and `STANDALONE` in its first slice (ADR 0037), with a `testing` feature that `transit-dsp`'s `testing` feature forwards. It owns a `math` module naming exactly the functions of decision 11.
- **The `Plugin` wrapper lives in `transit-dsp`** (`crates/dsp/src/plugins/parametric_eq.rs`): parameter table, pages, `update`, `process`, the meter and tap hooks. `transit-dsp` depends on the crate with `std` named explicitly.

### 2. Band types and their filters

**2.1 The analog prototypes.** Every band type is defined by an analog prototype in the variable `s/ω0` (`ω0 = 2π·f`, `f` the band's used frequency), with `A = 10^(G/40)` for a gain `G` in dB (RBJ's convention, so `A²` is the peak or shelf gain):

| Type | Prototype `H(s)`, `s` normalized by `ω0` | Panel fields used |
|---|---|---|
| Bell | `(s² + (A/Q)·s + 1) / (s² + s/(A·Q) + 1)` | Frequency, Gain, Q |
| Low shelf | `A·(s² + (√A/Q)·s + A) / (A·s² + (√A/Q)·s + 1)` | Frequency, Gain, Q |
| High shelf | `A·(A·s² + (√A/Q)·s + 1) / (s² + (√A/Q)·s + A)` | Frequency, Gain, Q |
| Low cut | Butterworth high pass of order `N = slope/6`: `N/2` second-order sections `s²/(s² + s/Qᵢ + 1)` and, for odd `N`, one first-order `s/(s + 1)` | Frequency, Slope |
| High cut | the same as a Butterworth low pass: `1/(s² + s/Qᵢ + 1)` and `1/(s + 1)` | Frequency, Slope |
| Notch | `(s² + 1) / (s² + s/Q + 1)` | Frequency, Q |
| Band pass | `(s/Q) / (s² + s/Q + 1)`, unity at `f` | Frequency, Q |
| Tilt shelf | the high shelf of gain `G` times the constant `10^(−G/40)`: −G/2 dB far below `f`, +G/2 dB far above, 0 dB at `f` | Frequency, Gain, Q |
| Flat tilt | a constant slope through 0 dB at `f`: `G` dB across the ten octaves 20 Hz to 20 kHz, so `G/9.966` dB per octave, built from first-order pairs (2.4) | Frequency, Gain |

The Butterworth Qs are `Qᵢ = 1 / (2·sin(π·(2i − 1)/(2N)))` for `i = 1 … ⌊N/2⌋`, sorted ascending, plus one first-order section when `N` is odd (amended 2026-11-12: the earlier `cos` form was right only for even `N`), computed once as constants for the eight slopes (6, 12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 72, 96 dB per octave; at most eight second-order sections per band). A cut is −3 dB at `f` and has no Q (the panel shows none). The bell's Q is RBJ's: the frequencies where the bell reaches half its gain in dB are `f·(√(1 + 1/(4Q²)) ± 1/(2Q))`, which is what the panel's Q bracket spans. Shelves at Q = 1/√2 have no overshoot; higher Q overshoots, as the prototype does, and the curve shows it.

The prototypes are what "transparent" means for this device: each band does what its analog prototype does, with no added nonlinearity, no modeled hardware and no level change beyond its response. Decision 2.5 makes that measurable.

**2.2 The zero-latency design: magnitude-matched sections.** A plain bilinear (RBJ) biquad maps the whole analog frequency axis into 0 to Nyquist, so every response is squeezed toward Nyquist ("cramping"): a bell or shelf's response at Nyquist is forced to its value at infinite analog frequency. The issue asks for highs that behave like the analog prototype. The choice is a closed-form magnitude match at the native rate:

- **Poles by the exact mapping `z = e^(s·T)`** of the prototype's poles. For a complex pair at frequency `ω_p` (radians per sample) and damping `ζ < 1`: `r = e^(−ζ·ω_p)`, `θ = ω_p·√(1 − ζ²)`; for real poles (`ζ ≥ 1`) the two real roots map separately. Real poles never alias, whatever their frequency; a complex pair is valid while `θ < π`.
- **Which form of a section is designed.** A cut bell, whose poles would lie above its zeros in frequency, is designed as the reciprocal of its mirror (the bell of gain −G) and inverted, so the mapped poles are always the lower-frequency pair and stay below Nyquist for every frequency in range. Shelves and the tilt shelf use the factored design at the end of this decision.
- **Zeros fitted to the prototype's magnitude.** With `φ1(ω) = sin²(ω/2)`, `φ0 = 1 − φ1`, `φ2 = 4·φ0·φ1`, the squared magnitude of a second-order polynomial is `B0·φ0 + B1·φ1 + B2·φ2` with `B0 = (b0 + b1 + b2)²`, `B1 = (b0 − b1 + b2)²`, `B2 = −4·b0·b2` (likewise `A0, A1, A2` for the denominator). Given the poles, `B0`, `B1`, `B2` solve three linear equations so that `|H(e^(jω))|²` equals the prototype's `|H(jω·fs)|²` (the prototype in hertz, unwarped) at `ω = 0`, at `ω = π` and at the section's match frequency `ω_m = ω0` (clamped to 0.49·2π). The zeros are taken minimum-phase (the larger root as `b0`). The fit is computed in the σ domain of 2.3, where it is well conditioned at low frequencies: with the denominator `(c0, c1, c2)` of 2.3, `t = tan(ω_m/2)` (as `sin/cos`) and `|D(t)|² = (c0 − c2·t²)² + (c1·t)²`, the numerator is `n0 = √T(0)·c0`, `n2 = √T(π)·c2` and `n1² = (T(ω_m)·|D(t)|² − (n0 − n2·t²)²)/t²`, `T` the prototype's squared magnitude; minimum phase is `n0, n1, n2 ≥ 0`, and `n1² < 0` is the infeasible case below (equivalent to `W² + B2 < 0`).
- **Exceptions by type.** High-pass sections keep their double zero at DC (`b = g·(1, −2, 1)`) and fit only `g` at Nyquist. The notch keeps its zeros on the unit circle at `ω0` (infinite depth at `f`) and fits its gain at DC. First-order sections map the pole the same way and fit `b0`, `b1` at DC and Nyquist.
- **Feasibility.** The fit needs `W² + B2 ≥ 0` with `W = (√B0 + √B1)/2`. A parameter sweep in the drafting of this record (every type over 30 frequencies from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, gains of ±6, ±18 and ±30 dB, Q from 0.1 to 40, at 44.1, 48, 96 and 192 kHz) found no infeasible point with the forms above. The core clamps `W² + B2` at 0 if it is ever negative (a continuous fallback, no jump), and slice 1 repeats the sweep in the crate with a finer grid and records any clamped point. Feasible is not accurate: the fit places the fitted pair by magnitude at DC, Nyquist and `ω0` only. A bell's two pairs both sit at `ω0`, so bells match at every Q. A shelf's fitted pair sits at `ω0·√A` or `ω0/√A`, away from all three points, and above a shelf Q of about 2 it is a resonance the fit cannot place, at any rate and any frequency: at 48 kHz a ±30 dB shelf at 1 kHz deviates by 0.16 dB at Q 2, 0.63 dB at Q 4, 2.97 dB at Q 10 and 12.2 dB at Q 40, and over the full frequency and gain ranges by 2.3, 6.3 and 13.4 dB for Q up to 2, 4 and 10 (plain bilinear sections are near exact there). Natural phase does not repair it (a +30 dB Q 10 low shelf at 1 kHz stays 1.95 dB and 9 degrees off with `D = 64`). So shelves and the tilt shelf are not designed by the single fit; they use the factored design that follows, which holds a bound over the whole Q range (the maintainer decided on 2026-11-11 that shelves keep the full Q range, 0.1 to 40).

**Shelves and the tilt shelf: two factor sections.** A shelf's prototype is a constant times a pole-pair factor and a zero-pair factor with the same damping `ζ = 1/(2Q)`: with `P(ω_f)(s) = ω_f² / (s² + 2ζ·ω_f·s + ω_f²)` (`s` in radians per sample, unnormalized) and `Z(ω_f) = 1/P(ω_f)`, the low shelf is `A²·P(ω0/√A)·Z(ω0·√A)` and the high shelf `P(ω0·√A)·Z(ω0/√A)`. Each factor becomes its own section, so each pair is placed exactly by mapped poles, which the single fit could not do:

- `P(ω_f)` is a matched low-pass section: poles mapped exactly, numerator fitted (σ-domain form above) to `|P|²` at DC, at Nyquist and at `ω_m = min(ω_f, 0.9π)`.
- `Z(ω_f)` is the matched `P(ω_f)` section inverted (numerator and denominator swapped; valid because the fitted numerator is minimum phase, so the inverse is stable).
- A pair whose mapped angle `θ = ω_f·√(1 − ζ²)` is `0.9π` or more (at or past the top of the band) is not mapped: its `P` is an all-pole section, the inverse of a zeros-only section (denominator 1, so `c = (1, 2, 1)` in σ) whose numerator is fitted to `1/|P|²` at DC, `π/2` and Nyquist. Real pairs (`ζ ≥ 1`) always map. As a hard switch this steps the response the way the rejected design switch does: sweeping the frequency across `θ = 0.9π` jumps it by up to 9.4 dB (a +12 dB Q 10 high shelf at 14.07 kHz, 44.1 kHz), 8.0 dB (+30 dB Q 40 at 9.11 kHz, 48 kHz), 4.2 dB (+3 dB Q 4 at 18.35 kHz, 44.1 kHz) and 1.8 dB even at Q 0.707 (+12 dB at 19.87 kHz, 44.1 kHz), where a 0.1 % frequency step elsewhere moves it by at most 0.34 dB (measured in review). The transition is therefore continuous in the parameters, by construction (round-3 design):

- **A blend weight.** `β = 0` for `θ ≤ 0.9π`, `β = 1` for `θ ≥ 0.99π`, and between them `β = x²·(3 − 2x)` with `x = (θ − 0.9π)/(0.09π)` (a smoothstep, so `β` and its slope are continuous). For real pairs (`ζ ≥ 1`) `β = 0`; as `ζ` rises to 1, `θ` falls to 0, so that edge is continuous too.
- **Only the denominator is blended.** The mapped denominator and the all-pole form's denominator (the fitted numerator of the zeros-only section, before inversion) are each normalized so `c0 + c1 + c2 = 1`, and the section's denominator is `(1 − β)·mapped + β·all-pole`, coefficient by coefficient. Both have all σ coefficients positive, so every blend is stable (a second-order polynomial with positive coefficients is Hurwitz in σ, which the bilinear map takes inside the unit circle).
- **The numerator is refitted, not blended.** It is the σ-domain three-point fit to the true `|P|²` at DC, at Nyquist and at `ω_m = (1 − β)·min(ω_f, 0.9π) + β·π/2`. At `β = 0` this is exactly the mapped section, at `β = 1` exactly the all-pole section (its own fit points), and every input to the fit moves continuously with frequency, gain and Q.

The review's trial blended the whole sections (numerators too), which put the in-between sections far from the target; refitting the numerator to the true target at every `β` keeps them accurate. Measured in the round-3 drafting (the round-2 grid, now with this transition):

| Shelves and tilt shelf, worst deviation | 44.1 kHz | 48 kHz | 96 kHz | 192 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hard switch at `0.9π` (round 2), every gain and Q, `f` to 20 kHz | 5.19 dB | 5.40 dB | 2.33 dB | 0.07 dB |
| Continuous transition, the same, on the round-2 gains (±3, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30 dB) | 3.56 dB | 2.75 dB | 2.33 dB | 0.07 dB |
| Continuous transition, on decision 2.5's 3 dB gain steps (the grid of the Evidence; amended 2026-11-12) | 3.83 dB | 2.76 dB | 2.33 dB | 0.07 dB |
| Continuous transition, `f` at most fs/5 (unchanged: no pair reaches the band there) | 1.58 dB | 1.03 dB | 0.93 dB | 0.07 dB |

Continuity, measured by sweeping each shelf's frequency from 20 Hz to the top of the range (20 kHz or 0.49·fs) in steps of 1 cent, both shelf types, gains ±3, ±12 and ±30 dB, Qs 0.1, 0.707, 2, 10 and 40, the response evaluated at 200 frequencies: the largest change of the response in one step is 0.39 dB at 44.1 kHz, 0.33 dB at 48 kHz, 0.22 dB at 96 kHz and none at 192 kHz (where no pair reaches the transition) within a band around the transition, against 0.23 dB anywhere else in the sweep, which is the prototype's own change when a Q 40 resonance moves by 1 cent. The hard switch steps 20.7 dB at 44.1 kHz (a −3 dB Q 40 low shelf at 18.2 kHz) and 8.1 dB at 48 kHz. The design's change is a slope, not a jump: at the worst case it is 0.39, 0.039 and 0.0039 dB for steps of 1, 0.1 and 0.01 cent (the prototype's 0.23, 0.023 and 0.0023 dB), so it shrinks with the step to nothing; near Nyquist the digital resonance moves about 1.7 times as fast as the prototype's. **Compared honestly:**

- *Plain bilinear biquads* (the existing EQs): exact at DC and at the band frequency after prewarping, cheap, but the cramping above is large at 44.1 and 48 kHz. Rejected as the zero-latency design; they remain the existing EQs' design.

- *Matched sections* (chosen): closed form, native rate, no latency, about the cost of a biquad at control rate; the residual is bounded, and Natural phase reduces it to decision 2.5's Natural bounds for 64 samples of latency.

**2.3 Realization: 64-bit trapezoidal state-variable sections with output mixing.** Any stable digital biquad is exactly a trapezoidal SVF with mixed outputs. With the digital section `(b, a)` written in `σ = (1 − z⁻¹)/(1 + z⁻¹)`:

- denominator `c2·σ² + c1·σ + c0` with `c0 = (1 − r)² + 4r·sin²(θ/2)`, `c2 = (1 + r)² − 4r·sin²(θ/2)`, `c1 = 2·(1 − r²)` (for real poles the same quantities from the two roots), computed in these forms because `1 + a1 + a2` cancels catastrophically at low frequencies;
- numerator `n2·σ² + n1·σ + n0` with `n0 = √B0`, `n2 = √B1`, `n1 = 2·√(W² + B2)`, taken straight from the fit, never from `b0, b1, b2`;
- `g = √(c0/c2)`, `k = c1/√(c0·c2)`, and output `y = m0·v_low + m1·v_band + m2·v_high` with `m0 = n0/c0`, `m1 = n1·g/c0`, `m2 = n2/c2`, where `v_low`, `v_band` (before multiplication by `k`) and `v_high` are the SVF's unit outputs in the notation of `svf.rs`.

The map needs only `sqrt`, which IEEE 754 rounds exactly. First-order sections use a trapezoidal one-pole with the same mixing. The sections hold their states and coefficients in f64 and the whole chain (sections, placement stages, correction filter and convolution) runs in f64 from input to output (one conversion each way). The SVF keeps its states meaningful when coefficients change, which direct forms do not, and its coefficient sensitivity at low frequencies is low; f64 removes the remaining concern at 20 Hz and 192 kHz, where the pole radius is within 1e-3 of 1. The cost is a few more cycles per section than f32, accepted (decision 13). The `Biquad` of `biquad.rs` is not used.

**2.4 Flat tilt.** Fifteen first-order shelving pairs at `f_k = 10·2^k` Hz for `k = −1 … 13` (5 Hz to 81.92 kHz), each `(1 + s/z_k)/(1 + s/p_k)` with `p_k/z_k = 10^(t/20)` and `z_k = f_k·10^(−t/40)` for a slope of `t = G/9.966` dB per octave (swapped for a negative slope), the whole scaled so the response is 0 dB at `f`. The analog cascade lies within 0.07 dB of the straight line from 20 Hz to 20 kHz at ±3 dB per octave for band frequencies from 100 Hz to 5 kHz, and within 0.123 dB with the band frequency anywhere in range (at 20 Hz or 20 kHz the cascade's ripple falls on one side once scaled to 0 dB there; measured in slice 1, amended 2026-11-12). Pairs above Nyquist are real poles and map validly; the digital response near Nyquist is the matched one, and Natural phase corrects the rest. Each pair is designed as a first-order section (pole mapped, `b0` and `b1` fitted at DC and Nyquist, both square roots taken positive, so minimum phase); the fitted pairs are then multiplied two by two into second-order sections (eight sections, the last first-order) whose σ-domain coefficients are the products of the pairs' (no second fit). In Zero latency, the measured maximum deviation from the analog cascade at ±30 dB and 44.1 kHz is 0.780 dB on decision 2.5's grid (table); this is not a bound on the deviation from the straight line (amended 2026-11-12).

### 3. Changing parameters without clicks

**3.1 Smoothing rule: parameters, per sample; designs at control rate; coefficient glides per sample** (heading amended 2026-11-12). Each used band smooths its frequency and Q in the log2 domain and its gain in dB, each a one-pole of 20 ms on the plug-in's own sample clock with the snap of `transit-dsp`'s `Smoother` (to the target within 1e-6 relative). A band whose smoothers moved recomputes its sections every 16 samples of that clock (the Filter's `CONTROL` rule, ADR 0038 and ADR 0053 decision 2.5), plus once in `prepare` and `reset`, and over each 16-sample interval its state-variable coefficients glide linearly per sample from the previous design to the new one, the interval's last sample being exactly the new design (amended 2026-11-12; the amendment gives the measurements). A band at rest runs its plain design, bit-identical to a band that never moved given the same states (amended 2026-11-12), and costs no design or glide work. Output is smoothed as a linear gain over 20 ms.

Cost: one design of one band is a few dozen floating-point operations plus one `exp2`, one `cos` and one `sin` per second-order section; 24 bands all moving at 96 kHz is 144,000 band designs a second, up to 1.15 million section designs with eight sections a band: at the worst case the same order of cost as the section ticks, so slice 2 measures it within decision 13's figures. Coefficients are not smoothed in place of parameters: the parameters are smoothed and designed every 16 samples, and between two designs the state-variable coefficients glide linearly per sample (amended 2026-11-12, replacing this record's earlier rejection of interpolated coefficients). An interpolated set need not be the design of any parameter value, and the stable region is not convex in arbitrary coefficient triples, but at each fixed sample rate, the exact-arithmetic argument below establishes uniform exponential stability for arbitrary glide histories whose endpoint designs lie in a compact set with positive, finite `g` and `k` (amended 2026-11-12, replacing a claim that measurement proved it). In the section's states (`ic1`, `ic2` of the trapezoidal tick), the zero-input update is `M = [[2a1 − 1, −2a2], [2a2, 1 − 2a3]]`, affine in `a1`, `a2`, `a3`, so gliding them glides `M` linearly. For a design, `M = (I − B)⁻¹(I + B)` with `B = g·[[−k, −1], [1, 0]]`; a design with its poles inside the unit circle has `c0`, `c1`, `c2` all positive, so `g, k > 0`, `B + Bᵀ` is negative semidefinite and `M`'s Euclidean norm is exactly 1: no step grows the state. A blend of two designs has norm at most 1 by convexity. For an interior blend, a nonzero vector keeps its length only when both designs share `g` and the vector lies along `(g, 1)`; at a blend endpoint, only the selected design matters. In either case the vector maps along `(−g, 1)`, which no subsequent design or blend with positive `g` preserves in length. Therefore any two consecutive zero-input steps strictly shrink every nonzero state, including two uses of the same design, and every blend has spectral radius below 1. Over a compact design set with `0 < g_min ≤ g ≤ g_max < ∞` and `0 < k_min ≤ k ≤ k_max < ∞`, compactness gives a uniform two-step contraction factor below 1. This argument assumes those endpoint bounds; the feasibility and inverse-stability sweeps establish them only at sampled settings and rates. A first-order section's `G` stays between 0 and 1 under blending. The output mix (`k`, `m0`, `m1`, `m2`) is not in the state update. The floating-point sweeps of the amendment are evidence only at the settings and rates they sample; 1.536 MHz has not been checked.

**3.2 Discrete changes are crossfaded.** Type, Slope and Placement are not continuous. A change of one of them, and On and Used toggling, ramp the band's wet amount `w` (output `x + w·(band(x) − x)`, linear over 10 ms on the sample clock): a change ramps the old configuration out to `w = 0`, switches with cleared states, and ramps the new one in, so the band is briefly absent and never steps. A band at `w = 0` that is off or unused is skipped and its states cleared. In Linear mode no band runs as sections: a discrete change, like any change, reaches the sound through a kernel rebuild and its crossfade (6.4), with no `w` ramp. In Natural mode the ramps apply to the sections and the correction follows by rebuild.

**3.3 Criterion.** As ADR 0053's: a 100 Hz sine at −6 dBFS at 48 kHz, the device with band 1 a bell at 400 Hz, +6 dB, Q 1; after 500 ms of settling one parameter steps at a block start; the output high-passed at 12 kHz (fourth-order Butterworth in the test) holds no sample above −60 dB relative to the larger of the output's steady peaks before and after, in the 50 ms after the step (Evidence lists the steps and their negative controls).

### 4. Placement: a left/right stage, then a mid/side stage

- **Stages.** Stage 1 runs every Stereo, Left and Right band in the left/right domain: a Stereo band on both channels, a Left band on channel 0, a Right band on channel 1. Stage 2 runs every Mid and Side band in the mid/side domain. Stereo bands commute with everything (the same filter on both channels), so placing them in stage 1 changes no response. Left/right and mid/side bands do not commute with each other; the fixed order (stage 1, then stage 2) is part of the device's definition.
- **Encode and decode.** Stage 2 exists only while at least one Mid or Side band is processed (used, and neither skipped as an identity nor at `w = 0`). Then, in f64: `M = L + R`, `S = L − R` before it and `L = (M + S)·0.5`, `R = (M − S)·0.5` after it. Each step is exact in f64 whenever the two input samples are within a factor of 2^29 of each other or either is zero (their f32 sum and difference then fit in 53 bits, and the halving is exact), so encode then decode with nothing between returns both channels exactly: bit for bit, except that a negative-zero sample returns as positive zero (`(1 + −1)·0.5`), equal in value (amended 2026-11-12). Beyond that ratio (about 175 dB, as a full-scale left channel against a right channel at −180 dBFS or a subnormal) the quieter channel comes back changed: `(1, 1e-40)` returns `(1, 0)`. That is why stage 2 is skipped while no Mid or Side band is processed, so decision 2.5's bit-exact pass-through holds with identity Mid and Side bands too. With no Mid or Side band in use the encode and decode are skipped, so a session with only Stereo, Left and Right bands runs exactly the left/right arithmetic.
- **An untouched side.** With Mid bands only, the side signal passes stage 2 untouched in f64; the output's side component, computed in f64 from the f32 outputs, equals the input's within one unit in the last place of the larger of the two outputs (the decode's rounding is relative to the louder channel). It is not bit-identical at the f32 output, because the decode rounds `M' ± S` to f32; the record does not claim more.
- **Cost.** Two additions and two multiplications per frame each way, only while stage 2 exists.
- **Mono** (settled choice 7): Stereo and Mid bands process the one channel; Left, Right and Side bands are inactive (no left, right or side exists), and the panel shows their nodes as disabled. **More than two channels** (settled choice 7): Stereo bands process every channel; Left, Right, Mid and Side bands apply to channels 0 and 1 only.

**5.2 Adding, removing and numbering bands.**

- **A band's number is its slot** and never changes while it lives: the chip, the node, the strip, every readout and every lane name ("Band 3 Gain") use it. Removing band 3 of 6 leaves 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 and "5 of 24 bands"; nothing is renumbered, because renumbering would move every lane, mapping and macro of the higher bands to another band's sound.
- **Add** (+ Band, a double-click or a drag on the empty display) takes the lowest-numbered unused slot that has no automation lane, MIDI mapping or rack macro on any of its parameters, else the lowest unused slot (settled choice 5), and writes `used = on`, type, frequency, gain, Q, the default slope and placement, and the color in one `mix.insert_params` call: one undo step.
- **Remove** writes `used = off` and every other parameter of the slot back to its default in one `mix.insert_params` call: one undo step. Lanes, MIDI mappings and rack macros on that slot are left as they are, as for any parameter (the user removes them); while the slot is unused they drive values nobody hears. Undo restores the band and its values together.
- **At 24 used bands** the panel's + Band chip is gone (hand-off); nothing in the engine needs to refuse a 25th band, because there is no 25th slot.
- **Automation of `used`** is allowed like any parameter (clamped when used: a value at or above 0.5 is used) and ramps (3.2).

**5.3 Checked when set, clamped when used** (ADR 0041's rule). A value typed or sent through `mix.insert_param`, `mix.insert_params` or `mix.insert` outside its range is refused for this device, with the document unchanged and these words, which the panel shows verbatim in its note row: "Frequency must be between 20 Hz and 20 kHz.", "Gain must be between −30 dB and +30 dB.", "Q must be between 0.10 and 40.", "Output must be between −24 dB and +24 dB." The check goes through `transit_dsp::builtin_check_param` of ADR 0053 decision 2.4, which whichever device lands first builds. Automation lanes, rack macros and session files are never refused: `Params::set` clamps them, as for every built-in. Choices and toggles cannot be out of range once rounded.

**5.4 Mixer and editor paths.** Every writer goes through the commands above: the device panel, the generic editor and macro strip (`builtin_editor.rs`), MIDI Learn (a refusal goes to its status line, as for any refused write), preset recall (`presets.rs` through `mix.insert_params`). Each panel gesture is one undo step (hand-off section 5).

### 6. Phase modes

**Modes by use** (decided by the maintainer, 2026-11-11). Zero latency is the default and the mode for general mixing and tracking: it adds no latency, so inserting the device or changing its bands never moves a track's timing. Natural and Linear add latency and are for mastering and other work where nothing is being tracked: Natural for the analog prototype's response, magnitude and phase, within decision 2.5's bounds at about 1.3 ms, Linear for zero phase at a latency chosen by its Resolution (6.3), from 85 ms to 683 ms at 48 kHz. Linear therefore does not have to fit a tracking-friendly latency. The help and the user guide state this framing in these words' substance (decision 17).

**6.1 Zero latency (minimum phase).** The matched sections of decision 2, no latency. Its magnitude is close to the prototype (decision 2.5); its phase is the sections' own minimum phase, equal to the prototype's well below Nyquist and different near it.

**6.2 Natural: the analog prototype's response, magnitude and phase, with a fixed short latency.** Natural targets the prototype composite `H_a(jω)` exactly: its magnitude and its (minimum) phase, the way the analog filter would behave, unwarped, up to min(20 kHz, 0.45·fs). It is realized as the zero-latency sections followed by a short linear correction filter whose target is `C(ω) = H_a(jω)/H_d(e^(jω))·e^(−jωD)`: near 1 at low frequencies (the matched poles agree with the prototype there) and a smooth correction near Nyquist, so its impulse response is short. The kernel is `2D + 1` taps, computed by frequency sampling of `C` on a grid of `8·D` points (512 at 44.1 and 48 kHz; a fixed 512 would be shorter than the 513-tap kernel at 176.4 and 192 kHz), centered and windowed with a 4-term Blackman-Harris window. `C` is computed section by section with the zeros the prototype and the design share divided out first (the double zero at DC of a low cut section, the zero at DC of a band pass, the notch's pair at `ω0`), so it is finite and continuous at every grid point; the plain ratio is 0/0 there, and a correction with `C(0) = 0` puts a 12 dB/oct low cut at 100 Hz 0.8 dB off. Above min(20 kHz, 0.45·fs) the target is `C` held at its magnitude at that frequency with the phase continuing the delay (slice 6 records whether another choice fits better). With bands in both placement domains the correction is a 2×2 matrix of four kernels sharing `D`, run after the decode, as in Linear (6.3); `D` is 64 samples at 44.1 and 48 kHz, 128 at 88.2 and 96 kHz and 256 at 176.4 and 192 kHz (about 1.33 to 1.45 ms; the higher rates keep the same time span, and slice 6 measures them against the bound). Reported latency: `D`. Drafting measurements with `D = 64` at 44.1 kHz (a +12 dB Q 1 bell at 15 kHz, a 15 kHz second-order high cut, a −12 dB high shelf at 16 kHz, a pair of overlapping bells): at most 0.029 dB and 0.10 degree from the prototype; with `D = 32`, 0.125 dB and 0.37 degree. The correction runs as a direct-form FIR per channel (129 multiply-adds per sample at 48 kHz). The magnitude errors of Zero latency shrink to decision 2.5's Natural bounds and the phase comes within them of the analog filter's. Because the IIR sections stay in the path, a parameter drag moves the sound at the sections' smoothing rate in Natural mode too; only the small correction lags (6.4).

**6.3 Linear: zero phase plus a fixed delay, with a Resolution.** An FIR whose magnitude is the prototype composite `|H_a|` and whose phase is a pure delay `L`. The device parameter `linear_res` chooses `L` from the start, so presets and automation ids never change when settings are added: `L = L₁·m` with `L₁` = 4096 (Short), 16,384 (Long) or 32,768 (Maximum) and `m` the rate class of decision 12. The convolution is uniformly partitioned FFT convolution with partition `P = L/16` (FFT size 2P, 31 partitions at every setting), and the kernel is symmetric about tap `L − P`, `2(L − P) + 1` taps long, so the input buffering of one partition plus the kernel's center make exactly `L`. Computed that way, each frame's whole work would fall in the host block in which the frame completes: the products of 31 partitions for four kernels plus two forward and two inverse FFTs of `2P` points, about 0.27 million operations at Short and 48 kHz, 1.1 million at Long, 2.3 million at Maximum and 9.3 million at Maximum and 192 kHz, about 28 Gflop/s inside a 64-sample block there (estimated in review), though only about 1,100 operations a sample averaged over the frame. **So each frame's work is spread over the following frame on the sample clock.** When a frame of input completes, each 16-sample control tick of the next `P` samples does `16/P` of that frame's work (the forward FFTs, the 31 partition products of each kernel, the inverse FFTs and the overlap-add, in a fixed order), and the frame's output is ready when the following frame completes. That adds one partition of buffering, so to keep the latency exactly `L` the kernel is symmetric about tap `L − 2P` and is `2(L − 2P) + 1` taps long: 7,169, 28,673 and 57,345 taps at 48 kHz for Short, Long and Maximum, about 7 % shorter (the realized gains below are for these kernels). Non-uniform partitions (small at the kernel's head, large later, each computed over the samples before its output is due) were the alternative; they were not chosen because their sizes, their deadlines and the schedule that interleaves them are each a further thing to specify and test, while the spread keeps one partition size and one rule. **Per-block cost bound of the steady convolution:** per kernel and frame, `31·(P + 1)` complex multiply-adds (8 operations each), and for the device two forward and two inverse real FFTs of `2P` points (about `2.5·2P·log2(2P)` operations each), so with four kernels about `992 + 20·log2(2P)` operations a sample: 1,172 at Short, 1,212 at Long and 1,232 at Maximum in the 44.1 and 48 kHz class, up to 1,252 and 1,272 for Maximum in the 96 and 192 kHz classes. At every Resolution and rate class that is at most 1,300 operations a sample, 20,800 per 16-sample control tick and 83,200 in a 64-sample block; with one placement domain (two kernels) the products halve. During the one-frame crossfade that ends a rebuild (6.4) both kernel sets run, so for that frame the bound is 2,600 operations a sample (41,600 per tick), on top of the rebuild share. The rebuild's fixed share (6.4) adds its own bound. Kernel design: the target magnitude on a grid of `4·L` points, zero phase, inverse FFT, truncated to the kernel (centered at `L − 2P`) and windowed with a 4-term Blackman-Harris window, then the partition spectra. The shared `transit_math::fft` covers every size this needs (decision 11).

| Resolution | `L` (samples) at 44.1/48, 88.2/96, 176.4/192 kHz | Latency, 44.1 / 48 kHz family | Kernel taps at 48 kHz | Grid at 48 kHz | Ships |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short | 4,096 / 8,192 / 16,384 | 92.9 / 85.3 ms | 7,169 | 16,384 | slice 5 (settled choice 17) |
| Long | 16,384 / 32,768 / 65,536 | 371.5 / 341.3 ms | 28,673 | 65,536 | slice 5 (settled choice 17) |

Every rate class has the same latency in time (and so about the same resolution in hertz) for a setting. The panel shows the reported latency of the chosen setting in the readout the hand-off already draws (`85.3 ms · 4096 smp` for Short at 48 kHz). The Resolution control itself is a one-line design follow-up for the panel: the readout beside the phase segmented control becomes a small dropdown while Linear is selected (settled choice 20). Short is the default and the lowest-latency setting; Long and Maximum are for mastering, where the low end matters more than the latency. Pre-ringing is inherent: a linear-phase filter rings before a transient as much as after it, most for narrow, deep bands at low frequencies; it is the documented trade of this mode. With bands in both placement domains, the composite is a 2×2 matrix of zero-phase responses (left to left, right to left, left to right, right to right), run as four kernels sharing one delay; with one domain only, two kernels (left and right, or mid and side between the encode and decode).

**6.4 Rebuilding kernels on the audio thread, on a fixed schedule.** Correction and linear-phase kernels depend only on parameter values. When a band's targets differ from those the current kernel or the rebuild in flight was built from, a rebuild starts at the next partition frame boundary of the plug-in's sample clock (never at a block start, so the result is the same at every block size), from the targets at that boundary. A rebuild in flight is never restarted: it completes, and if the targets changed meanwhile, the next one starts at the frame boundary after it completes. Its work is spread by samples: each 16-sample control tick does a fixed share of the units (target points, FFT butterflies, windowing, partition spectra), so the work in a host block is proportional to its length. The target is the product of per-band magnitude tables on the kernel grid, each recomputed only when its band changed, so a one-band change at 48 kHz and Short costs about 0.8 million operations for the table plus about 3.5 million for the FFTs, and a steady one-band drag about 1,200 operations a sample. The share per control tick is fixed by the record, not by the number of changed bands (settled choice 22: enough for four band tables plus the FFTs and partition spectra, about 2,500 operations a sample at every Resolution); a rebuild with more band tables to recompute takes longer instead. Finishing 24 changed tables (a preset recall, a common case) within `L` samples would need about 10,600 operations a sample (24 tables of `4·L` points at about 100 operations a point, plus the composite and the FFTs), about 2 Gflop/s at 192 kHz. With this share a full recall completes in about 6·`L` samples (0.5 s at Short, 4.1 s at Maximum, at 48 kHz), the old kernels playing until then (settled choice 22). Slice 5 states the share in operations per tick and measures it. A linear-phase rebuild with up to four changed band tables completes in 16 frames of `P` samples, which is `L` samples (more tables take proportionally longer, as above) (85, 341 and 683 ms at 48 kHz for Short, Long and Maximum); a correction rebuild in at most 4 frames of 128·`m` samples. Because `P = L/16` and the grid is `4·L`, the operations per sample of a rebuild stay about constant across the settings (the FFTs grow by `log2` only): about 1,200 per sample for a steady one-band drag at Short, as above, and of the same order at Long and Maximum; slice 5 states the worst case per setting. When it completes, the output crossfades from the old kernel's convolution to the new one's over one frame (both run during the fade; two sets of partition spectra are preallocated). The rebuild never allocates, locks or waits, and because it advances by samples rather than wall-clock time, a render (live or offline) gives the same output for the same input and the same parameter history. A drag in Linear mode therefore moves the sound in steps of up to one latency (85 ms at Short, 683 ms at Maximum, at 48 kHz) plus a crossfade of one frame: Linear is for settled or slow changes, and the help says so. A thread per instance was rejected: the moment its result lands would depend on scheduling, so renders would not repeat. `snap_initial_params` (control thread, fresh instance) builds the kernels at once, so the first sample of a fresh instance is correct. `reset` (stop, on the audio thread) keeps the current kernels and restarts any rebuild in flight from its first unit.

**Linear-phase resolution.** The kernel's length sets the narrowest feature it can realize: about `fs/(2(L − 2P))` per bin (6.7 Hz at 48 kHz for Short) spread by the window's main lobe (±4 bins for 4-term Blackman-Harris). A narrow or low band comes out wider and shallower than the prototype. **At Short the low-end error is large, and the record and the help say so plainly:** a +12 dB bell at Q 4 reaches only +9.8 dB at 100 Hz and +6.1 dB at 30 Hz. Realized response at the band frequency for the kernels of the spread schedule, measured in the round-3 drafting as the target convolved with the window's spectrum (time aliasing of the grid ignored, so real figures are at least this far off), 48 kHz with 44.1 kHz in parentheses:

**6.5 Latency and mode changes.**

- `Plugin::latency()` returns 0, `D` or `L` for the active mode. The panel's readout prints what the engine reports (`85.3 ms · 4096 smp`, milliseconds to one decimal and samples).
- In Natural and Linear mode, while the composite target is identity (decision 2.5), the device uses a plain delay line of the same length (bit-exact), crossfading to and from the kernel path over one frame.
- **A mode change while playing** (a click, or automation of `phase`, which `ParamInfo` cannot forbid): the device fades its output out over 5 ms; at the end of the fade it switches `latency()` and its path, starts the new mode's kernel rebuild from its first unit (6.4) and feeds the new path from that frame; it outputs exact zeros until the rebuild has completed and the new path's history holds its full latency, then fades in over 5 ms. The kernels of an inactive mode are not kept current. The gap is at most 5 ms + max(rebuild, latency) + 5 ms: at 48 kHz about 95 ms into Linear Short, 351 ms into Long and 693 ms into Maximum (a rebuild takes `L` samples, 6.4), about 21 ms into Natural, 10 ms into Zero latency; a change of `linear_res` while in Linear mode is a mode change by this rule; a test checks it in samples at every rate class. The engine's existing mechanism realigns the track: `observe_raw` sees the new `latency()` in the next block and the player recompiles with the new compensation. Until that plan is adopted the track plays with the old compensation. The change is therefore audible as a short gap, as any latency change is; it is not seamless. Offline renders compensate the latency in force at their start.
- Low Latency mode may bypass the device in Natural or Linear mode on a live path (ADR 0015), like any latent insert; the panel then draws the bypassed state (decision 10).
- `tail_samples` keeps the default (0): built-ins are never suspended.

**6.6 Which modes ship when.** Zero latency in the first slices; Linear (Short and Long) and Natural after the analyzer, Linear Maximum in a later slice (Slices). Until a mode's slice lands, the panel shows its segment unavailable (settled choice 3). The `phase` parameter carries all three choices from slice 2, in this order, so ids and indices never change. Until Natural's or Linear's slice lands, `mix.insert`, `mix.insert_param` and `mix.insert_params` refuse it through `builtin_check_param` ("Natural phase arrives in a later release.", "Linear phase arrives in a later release."), and a lane, macro or session value selecting it is clamped when used: the device runs Zero latency and reports latency 0. `linear_res` carries Short, Long and Maximum from slice 2, in this order; Short and Long are accepted while Linear is unavailable (they change nothing until then), and until Maximum's slice lands, Maximum is refused through `builtin_check_param` ("Maximum linear resolution arrives in a later release.") and clamped to Long when used. `gain_match` is applied from slice 2 and shown from slice 3 (decided by the maintainer, 2026-11-11), so it is never refused.

### 7. Gain match

- **Computed from the curve, not measured from the audio.** The applied gain is `G_match = −10·log10(P)` dB, with `P` the weighted mean power of the composite magnitude response: `P = Σᵢ wᵢ·|H(fᵢ)|² / Σᵢ wᵢ` over 1,914 frequencies at 1/192 octave from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (`fᵢ = 20·2^(i/192)`, i = 0 … 1,913; the last is 19.97 kHz), with weights `wᵢ = |K(fᵢ)|²`: a pink spectrum, whose power per log-spaced point is constant, through the BS.1770 K-weighting as the standard publishes it for 48 kHz (its two biquads' coefficients), evaluated at each `fᵢ`. No `1/fᵢ` factor: on a log-spaced grid it would count the pink slope twice and put 72 % of the weight below 200 Hz (a +6 dB high shelf at 5 kHz would get −0.2 dB instead of about −3.0 dB). The weights are a table of 1,914 f64 literals in the crate, generated once by a script kept beside it and pinned by a test, so they depend on no sample rate and call no platform function (`meter::k_weighting` computes its biquads with the platform's `tan` and `powf` and is not used). The grid is fine enough for the Q range: sweeping a +30 dB Q 40 bell across 1/12 octave moves the match by 0.2 dB at 1/192 octave, against about 5 dB at 1/24 (4.9 to 5.8 dB depending on how finely the sweep is sampled; 1.2 dB at 1/96). Measured in the round-2 drafting with these weights: a +6 dB high shelf at 5 kHz (Q 0.707) gives −2.91 dB, a +6 dB low shelf at 200 Hz −1.23 dB, a 12 dB/oct low cut at 100 Hz +0.29 dB, a composite of +6 dB everywhere exactly −6 dB; a Q 40 band pass at 1 kHz would draw +23.2 dB and a 96 dB/oct high cut at 100 Hz +12.1 dB, which the clamp below limits. `|H|` is the prototype composite (a rational function of frequency, evaluated without a transcendental call), the same in every phase mode, so changing the mode never changes the match. With bands placed, `P` uses the channel curves of decision 8: with bands in one placement domain, the mean of that domain's two curves' powers (left and right, or mid and side); with bands in both domains, the mean of all four (built in slice 1, to be confirmed by the maintainer before slice 2); on mono, the one curve of Stereo and Mid bands (amended 2026-11-12). One gain applies to every channel, so the image does not move. `G_match` is clamped to ±12 dB (decided by the maintainer, 2026-11-11), so gain match never adds more gain than a large boost would take away.
- **When it is computed.** On the plug-in's sample clock, never inside `set_param`: a `set_param` that changes a band's targets marks that band's table stale, and each 16-sample control tick evaluates a fixed number of grid points (120) of the lowest-numbered stale band from its targets, then updates the same points of the composite, the product of the band tables per channel curve, and the weighted sum. So a block's work is proportional to its length whatever automation does, the match is identical at every block size, the share scales with the rate class (120/`m` points per tick), so the work per second and the time to current are the same at every rate: one changed band is current after 256 samples at 48 kHz (5.3 ms), all 24 after 6,144 samples (128 ms, longer than the 50 ms smoothing, so a preset recall's match settles over about 130 ms). A band's refresh, once started, completes from the targets captured at its start; a band changed again meanwhile is marked stale again and refreshed after the other stale bands, taken in band-number order starting after the band just refreshed (round robin), so continuous automation of one band neither restarts a refresh nor starves the other bands. No transcendental call outside `log2` and `exp2` (decision 11). The applied gain is smoothed over 50 ms and multiplies with Output.
- **Why it cannot pump.** It depends only on parameters. It changes when a band changes (that is what gain match means) and never with the program material. It is also exactly repeatable offline, which a loudness measurement over time would not be.
- **Shown and saved.** `gain_match` is a parameter; the applied value is not saved: it is a pure function of the parameters, recomputed identically on load. The panel prints it (`match −2.4 dB` and Output's sub-label `auto −2.4`) by calling the same function on the stored values.

### 8. The published response curve

- **The panel computes the curve with the engine's own design code.** The core crate exposes pure functions over the band set, the sample rate and the phase mode: `response_db(bands, rate, mode, channel, freqs, out)` for the composite and `band_response_db(band, rate, mode, freqs, out)` for one band alone. The panel calls them on the UI thread with the stored parameter values, one frequency per physical pixel column, and joins the values with straight segments. Nothing crosses the audio thread, and the curve is exactly what the engine converges to once smoothing settles (during a drag the sound follows within 20 ms).
- **What each mode's curve is.** Zero latency: `|H_d(e^(jω))|` of the matched sections, evaluated in f64. Natural: the sections times the correction kernel's exact DTFT. Linear: the realized kernel's exact DTFT at each column frequency, not the target, so the resolution limit of 6.4 is visible. The DTFT is summed directly in f64 (no interpolation); if a long kernel's curve does not fit in one UI frame, the panel computes it on a UI worker and keeps the previous drawing until it is done, never an approximation.
- **Channel curves.** `channel` selects one composite: with no placed band, the single composite; with left or right bands, the left curve (Stereo and Left bands) and the right curve (Stereo and Right bands); with mid or side bands, the mid curve (Stereo and Mid bands) and the side curve (Stereo and Side bands). Each is that channel's own path through its stages. With bands in both domains a channel's input-to-output response is a 2×2 matrix, not one curve; the panel then draws the pair of the selected band's domain (Open, hand-off question 6).
- **Bypassed:** the same functions give the static dimmed drawing.

### 9. The analyzer

- **Taps.** A new `Plugin::wants_analyzer_taps` (default false; true for this device) makes the compiler give the instance two `SampleTap`s, pre (pushed by the strip before `run_insert`) and post (after it), registered in the `ScopeHub` beside the existing sample tap. Today's `SampleTap` has a fixed ring (`SAMPLE_FRAMES` = 16,384 with a constant `MASK` and `BYTES`) and stores the channels' sum; slice 4 gives it a constructor taking a power-of-two frame count and a fold (sum, the Tuner's, unchanged; mean for these taps), with its bytes counted per instance in the plan's prepared bytes. Each analyzer tap holds the channels' mean per frame (the sum scaled by `1/channels`; the scaling is exact for 1, 2 and 4 channels), so a centered full-scale sine reads 0 dBFS and opposite-phase channels cancel, as any mono fold does. Their ring holds the largest analysis window at the plan's rate plus the largest latency plus `READ_MARGIN`, rounded up to a power of two (at 48 kHz 65,536 frames, 256 KB per tap, with Long the largest shipped Resolution; 131,072 frames, 512 KB, once Maximum ships; times the rate class), allocated by the compiler. The panel reads the pre ring `latency()` frames further back, so Pre and Post show the same audio in Natural and Linear mode.
- **Cost when the window is closed: none.** The taps write only while the panel has enabled them (the existing `enabled` flag); the push returns at once otherwise. A bypassed insert writes nothing (the strip skips it), so nothing moves.
- **FFT and window.** On the UI thread, on the newest frames each UI frame, with a 4-term Blackman-Harris window (sidelobes below −92 dB, so the −90 dB scale is not filled by leakage) and the coherent gain corrected so a full-scale sine at a bin center reads 0 dB; floored at −144 dB. `rustfft` with a planned, reused buffer (the existing `spectrum::magnitude_db` allocates per call and is not used here). The analyzer is display only, never rendered, so it is outside the portable-math requirement.
- **Low-end resolution: two windows.** A short FFT covers the whole range and a long FFT covers the low end: below `f_x = 12·fs/N_short` (141 Hz at 48 kHz with 4096 points, where the short FFT reaches 12 points per octave) the long FFT's bins are drawn, at and above it the short FFT's. Each bin is one point at its own center, joined with straight segments into one polyline; nothing is interpolated. Resolution in the analyzer menu: **Fast** (4096 only), **Normal** (4096 and 16,384; default), **Fine** (8192 and 32,768), sizes at 44.1 and 48 kHz, doubled at 88.2 and 96 kHz, and at 176.4 and 192 kHz doubled again but capped at 65,536. A single larger FFT was rejected as the only choice: its window is long for the highs too (341 ms at 16,384 points), so the top end responds late.
- **Drawing** (decided by the maintainer): one point per bin at its center, the maximum where several bins share a pixel column, straight segments, the area closed under the same polyline. Smoothing Off by default; 1/12, 1/6 or 1/3 octave averages each bin's power over the bins within half that fraction either side of its center, before the column maximum, and the well shows its tag.
- **Time behavior only.** Decay: Off, Fast (falls 40 dB per second), Medium (20; default), Slow (6); rises at once. Peak hold: Off (default), 2 s, Infinite (until clicked). Freeze keeps the last trace. These run on the panel's frame time; tests drive it with explicit steps.
- **Panel state.** Analyzer mode (default Both), Freeze, Resolution, Decay, Peak hold, Smoothing and Range are per-instance panel state, not parameters and not saved (settled choice 10).

### 10. Meters and bypass

- **OUT column.** The output peak per channel. This device implements ADR 0053's `Plugin::band_meters`, filling `io` (output left and right in `io[1]`, input in `io[0]`) and leaving `bands` at zero; the accumulate-and-swap cells and `ScopeHub::band_meters` of ADR 0053 decision 6.2 are built by whichever of the two devices lands first. Mono: one value in both wells; more than two channels: ADR 0053 decision 6.3's even and odd fold. Ballistics are the panel's (`Ballistics::PEAK_FALLOFF`, clip cells, the hand-off's peak fall-off).
- **Title bar meter.** Unchanged: the insert's `ScopeTap` ring, as for every device.
- **Bypassed** (decided by the maintainer): no meter, no analyzer, nothing animates; the curve, nodes and axis labels may stay as a static dimmed drawing (decision 8). It needs no engine work: the strip skips a bypassed insert and pushes nothing. The mix still delivers parameter values to a bypassed insert (`idle_applies_all_parameters` keeps its default), so the targets stay current, but no rebuild advances while nothing is processed: after un-bypass in Natural or Linear mode the kernels from before the bypass play until the rebuild completes (at most `L` samples, 85 ms at 48 kHz for Short) and crossfade, the same kind of stale state every built-in resumes from. The panel treats Low Latency mode's bypass the same way.

### 11. Determinism and the transcendental functions

Every transcendental call in a rendered path goes through the new crate's `math` module, and only these, all in f64:

- **When a band is designed** (control rate, only for moving bands): `exp2` (the log2-domain frequency and Q back to linear; the pole radius `e^(−ζω)` as `exp2(−ζω·LOG2_E)`, `LOG2_E` an f64 literal; `cosh` for real poles from two `exp2`), `cos` (the pole angle), `sin` (`sin²(ω/2)` at the match frequency and in the cancellation-free forms of 2.3; with `cos`, the `tan(ω_m/2)` of the σ-domain fit as their ratio). `sqrt` is IEEE-exact and needs no seam.
- **When a parameter is set:** `log2` (smoother targets into the log2 domain), `exp2` (dB to linear for Output and the applied match gain as `exp2(dB/LOG2_TO_DB)`), and `log2` for the gain-match power to dB.
- **In `prepare`:** `exp2` for the smoothers' one-pole coefficients (`time_coef`'s formula through `exp2`), `cos` for the Blackman-Harris windows, `cos` and `sin` for the correction filter's bin phasors (`transit_math::fft` fills the caller-owned twiddle table during `prepare`).
- **Per sample:** none. The section ticks, the encode and decode, the FIR and the convolution are arithmetic only.

**The FFT is the shared `transit_math::fft`, not this crate's own** (amended 2026-11-12; ADR 0055 (accepted, landing separately), decision 2.7 and its slice 2b carry the specification; the equalizer carries no FFT of its own for rendered output). The requirements on it are that it is radix-2, real input, with power-of-two sizes from 256 to 524,288 (the linear-phase grid is `4·L` points, 16,384, 65,536 or 131,072 at 48 kHz by Resolution, times the rate class of decision 12, so 524,288 for Maximum at 176.4 and 192 kHz; the partition FFTs are `2P` points, 512 to 16,384), with its twiddles from the seam and a fixed arithmetic order. It must have landed before or with slice 5, the first slice that uses an FFT in the rendered path. `rustfft` is not used in a rendered path: its twiddles come from the platform, outside the seam, and its planner picks SSE, AVX or NEON code at run time (its documentation), so its rounding can differ between machines. The analyzer keeps `rustfft` (display only).

The dsp-wide `db_to_gain` and `gain_to_db` (platform `powf` and `log10`) are not used in the render, so this device adds nothing to #277's list. When the portable helpers exist, the `math` module forwards to them and nothing else changes; until then no cross-platform golden is pinned over a render that calls them (decision 19).

### 12. Block size, sample rate, channels, reset, silence and hostile input

- **Block size.** Everything runs on the plug-in's own sample clock: per sample for the sections, every 16 samples for designs, every `P` samples for the convolution frames and the rebuild schedule (with an internal FIFO). The output is bit-identical whatever the host block size, for the same input and the same parameter values at the same absolute frames (the Filter's rule). Parameter values reach built-ins at block starts, so automation is as block-quantized as for every built-in.
- **Sample rates.** The device takes any rate `prepare` is given (`sane_sr`: 1 kHz to 1.536 MHz). Every rate-dependent size comes from one rate class `m = clamp(2^round(log2(fs/48,000)), 1, 4)`: `D = 64·m`, `L = L₁·m` with `L₁` = 4096, 16,384 or 32,768 by Resolution (6.3), `P = L/16`, the linear grid `4·L` points, the correction grid `8·D`, the analyzer sizes times `m` (the long FFT capped at 65,536). Rates of 32 kHz and below use the 48 kHz sizes (longer in time); rates above 192 kHz use the 192 kHz sizes (shorter in time, coarser linear-phase resolution). The used band frequency is clamped to 0.49·fs (at 32 kHz, 15.68 kHz). The response functions and the analyzer give values only up to 0.49·fs, and the panel draws no curve and no bins above it (a digital response is periodic and would draw a mirror image). A rate change goes through `prepare`, which rebuilds the kernels for the current parameters at the new rate (`reset` alone keeps them). The tests run 44.1, 48, 96 and 192 kHz and one rate of each outer class (32 and 384 kHz).
- **Channels.** The strip's channel count, up to `MAX_CHANNELS` (decision 4 for mono and wider layouts). `prepare` allocates per-channel states, FIFOs, kernels for the largest case (four kernels) and both kernel sets, then calls `reset`.
- **Reset:** section states, FIR and convolution histories and FIFOs to zero; band ramps ended, at `w = 1` for a used and on band and `w = 0` otherwise, with the stored configuration selected (amended 2026-11-12); smoothers snapped to their targets; designs recomputed; kernels kept (6.4); the sample clock to 0.
- **Silence and denormals.** f64 states are flushed to zero below 1e-30 in magnitude (no flush-to-zero mode is set on the audio thread); a silent input gives an exactly silent output once the states have decayed, and from a reset at once.
- **Hostile input.** Non-finite input samples are read as 0 at the device's input, so no state ever holds NaN or infinity and `contain_failure` never takes the device out for its input. Out-of-range parameter values are clamped by `Params::set`.

### 13. CPU cost and the small-target view

- **Targets** (recorded measurements in `plugin_bench`, not CI gates, as the bench is wall-clock): Zero latency, 24 bells, stereo, 96 kHz, block 256: at most 3 % of one core of the implementer's machine; the worst case, 24 Flat tilt or 96 dB/oct cut bands (192 sections a channel), at most 15 %; Natural adds the correction filter (at most 1 % more at 96 kHz); Linear, steady, at most 5 % at 96 kHz at every shipped Resolution, with no 64-sample host block doing more than the per-tick bound of 6.3 and 6.4, and a rebuild fits its schedule without missing a block at 64 samples. A moving band's design cost is counted in the steady figures with all 24 bands smoothing.
- **Unused bands cost nothing** (skipped); a band at rest costs its section ticks only.
- **Memory on the desktop.** `prepare` allocates the Natural and Linear storage of the largest shipped Resolution in every instance, whatever its mode: at 48 kHz about 1 MB of kernel spectra at Short, 4 MB at Long and 8 MB at Maximum (four kernels in two sets), plus the input partition spectra, the grid buffer of `4·L` f64 points and the FFT tables, about 12 MB in all once Maximum ships, and four times that at 176.4 and 192 kHz (about 48 MB). Twenty instances in a 192 kHz session would hold about 1 GB, mostly unused in Zero latency. So the latent modes' storage is allocated only when an instance first selects Natural or Linear (settled choice 21): a re-prepare through the recompile that the latency change already triggers, the device silent until the new plan is adopted (inside the mode-change gap of 6.5). An instance that stays in Zero latency holds none of it.
- **Small targets** (ADR 0037, the adaptable-core principle): the crate is Tier B, so a small target can run it. A small target keeps Zero latency and every band type; it may drop Natural and Linear (their memory, f64 kernel spectra for four kernels in two sets at 48 kHz: about 1 MB at Short, 4 MB at Long and 8 MB at Maximum, times the rate class, plus FIFOs and the rebuild's grid buffer of `4·L` points; allocated when an instance first selects a latent mode, settled choice 21, and then sized for the largest shipped Resolution, so changing `linear_res` afterwards never allocates) and the analyzer (UI only); the device then clamps `phase` to Zero latency when used. Its band count stays 24 in the table; a CPU budget table per target may cap how many are processed (that table's own decision).

### 14. Solo (audition)

- **What is heard:** the device's input (delayed by the reported latency, so the audition is aligned with the normal output), through a filter derived from the soloed band, at unity gain, instead of the device's output: a Bell, Notch or Band pass band plays through a second-order band pass at its frequency and Q (Notch: what the notch removes); a Low shelf or Low cut through a second-order low pass at its frequency (Q 1/√2), so a low cut auditions what it removes; a High shelf or High cut through the matching high pass; a Tilt shelf or Flat tilt through the band's own response alone (settled choice 9). Zero-latency matched sections, designed by decision 2.
- **One band at a time,** latched by a click and momentary while S is held for 300 ms or longer (the panel's rule). Switching in and out crossfades over 10 ms.
- **Never saved, never automated, never in a render.** Solo is not a parameter. The `ScopeTap` gains one `AtomicU32` audition word (a band number, or none), set by the panel through a new `ScopeHub::set_audition(track, uid, band)` and cleared by `ScopeTap::set_enabled(false)` when the panel closes. The strip reads it before `run_insert`, only while the tap is enabled, and passes it to a new `Plugin::set_audition(band: Option<u8>)` (default: ignored). An offline render has no enabled tap, so it never contains an audition; undo, presets and the session never see it.

### 15. Window

The hand-off's 1120 by 560 points with a minimum width of 880 follows the device window rule as is (`device_window.rs`: content-sized, fixed, the design width narrowed toward the minimum, recomputed every frame); the note row adds 24 points while a refusal shows, and the per-frame refit grows the window. 584 points at 150 percent is 876 pixels, within a 1080-pixel screen with its taskbar. No exception to the rule is needed.

### 16. Session format, presets and the existing EQs

- An insert of this device is an ordinary `Insert { plugin: "parametric_eq", params }`. The Parametric EQ insert itself needs no new session field or compatibility marker. Amended 2026-11-12: the stored unassigned-lane representation and its compatibility treatment are separate requirements of #510 and the retirement issue, governed by ADR 0023; they must be settled and tested before retirement ships. No migration shim is introduced. An older build reading such a session plays the slot without the device and keeps the stored parameters (ADR 0053 decision 13's reading, confirmed by a test in slice 2).
- A parameter id once shipped never changes meaning; while alpha a change is a clean break said in the release notes.
- **Presets** are the built-in format (`{"plugin", "version": 1, "params"}`); recalling one sets every parameter, the unlisted ones to their defaults (so unlisted slots are unused). No factory preset in the first slices (Open).
- **EQ 7-Band and EQ 1-Band** retire by ADR 0040 stage 2's rule at the release that ships slice 4 (decided by the maintainer, 2026-11-11): the slot is emptied, the load report written, with no conversion of their sessions to this device (ADR 0040's no-shim rule and the alpha rule). (Amended 2026-11-12: their automation lanes are kept as unassigned lanes, not dropped; see amendment item 8 and ADR 0040's amendment. MIDI mappings and rack macro targets that pointed at them follow the existing rule, dropped with a note, until the maintainer decides otherwise: open point.) The load report names the kept lanes as unassigned instead of saying they were dropped. They stay available until that release. ### 17. Control channel and help

- **No new command.** The device is driven by the existing `mix.insert`, `mix.insert_param` and `mix.insert_params` with the ids of decision 5, and read by `session.inspect`. Refusals carry the words of 5.3. A read-only query of the computed curve is a later option (Open).
- **Help:** `docs/help/parametric-eq.toml` with rows for the device, adding and removing bands, Type, Slope, Placement, Solo, the three phase modes, Linear Resolution, Gain match (computed from the curve), the analyzer and its settings, and the bypassed state. The phase-mode rows carry the framing by use (decision 6): Zero latency is the default and the mode for mixing and tracking, and near the top of the range its shelves can depart from their analog shape by about 3.8 dB at 44.1 kHz and 2.8 dB at 48 kHz, and other types further (steep high cuts most: up to 17.8 and 9.24 dB measured for 96 dB/oct); the curve shows the designed response, and Natural reduces the deviation, within the limits measured for each type (amended 2026-11-12); Natural and Linear add latency and are for mastering and other work where nothing is being tracked. A further sentence covers the sample rate (amendment item 4a): "Accuracy depends on the sample rate. At 32 kHz, bands near the top of the range can depart from their analog shape by several dB, and steep high cuts there by much more. The curve always shows the designed response." (amended 2026-11-12) The Linear rows state each Resolution's latency, pre-ringing, stepped drags, and plainly the low-end error at Short (a +12 dB Q 4 bell at 100 Hz reaches about +9.9 dB and at 30 Hz about +6.3 dB; use Long or Maximum, or another mode, for low bands), and that narrow notches stay shallow in Linear.
- **User guide:** a Parametric EQ section with the same framing by use, low-end statement and sample-rate caveats (amended 2026-11-12), and the README Features line, in the slices that change public behavior.

### 18. Dynamic bands (a later release)

Nothing is reserved in the parameter table now: parameter ids are additive (a session without a new id gets its default), so a later `b{n}_dyn_*` set breaks nothing, and reserving dead parameters now would put them in every lane menu and preset. What the first slices must keep open, and do: each band's gain is already a smoothed per-sample value designed at control rate (decision 3), which a detector can drive the same way; the Zero latency and Natural modes keep the band sections in the signal path (a dynamic gain flows through them unchanged). What would change: about five parameters per band (on, threshold, range, attack, release; their own record decides), `APPLIED_INLINE` again or the prepared-storage path, a detector per dynamic band (with or without a sidechain), per-band gain-change meters (ADR 0053's cells), and a decision for Linear mode, where a moving gain would mean rebuilding kernels continuously.

### 19. Evidence an implementation must carry

All tests are deterministic, use no wall clock and no audio device, and drive time by counting samples. Each negative control says what fails without the rule.

- **Every band type against its closed form** (slice 1). The impulse response of each type and slope, at 44.1, 48, 96 and 192 kHz, over a grid of frequencies, gains and Qs, evaluated by a direct DFT in f64, matches the digital transfer function of the designed coefficients within 0.05 dB above −60 dB, else 1e-4 linear (the Filter test's bounds; proves the realization of 2.3 is exact). Negative control: an SVF map with `m1 = n1/c0` (the factor `g` dropped) fails. (`2·(b0 − b2)` is the same quantity as `n1` and in f64 differs only by rounding, so it cannot serve as a control.)
- **The matching claim** (slice 1). The deviation of the designed magnitude from the analog prototype, over the same grid from 20 Hz to min(20 kHz, 0.45·fs), stays within decision 2.5's bounds, and the same test run on RBJ bilinear sections (the comparison) exceeds them; the measured maxima are recorded in the result note. Negative control: the bilinear sections fail the zero-latency bound at 44.1 kHz.
- **Feasibility** (slice 1). The sweep of 2.2 on a finer grid; any clamped point is recorded.
- **Shelves over the whole Q range** (slice 1). The factored design of 2.2, low shelf, high shelf and tilt shelf, over the sweep of 2.5 (every gain in 3 dB steps, every listed Q from 0.1 to 40) at 44.1, 48, 96 and 192 kHz and the outer rate classes, stays within the factored table's bounds (3 dB gain steps) plus 0.05 dB, at 1 kHz within 0.25 dB at every Q and gain at 44.1, 48, 96, 192 and 384 kHz (amended 2026-11-12: the rates measured, not every rate above 44.1 kHz) and within the pinned test limit of 0.303 dB at 32 kHz (0.252 dB measured, a +30 dB Q 0.1 tilt shelf). Negative control, at 44.1 and 48 kHz: the single three-point fit for shelves misses by more than 10 dB at ±30 dB, Q 40 and 1 kHz (12.7 and 12.1 dB measured; at 96 kHz it misses by 6.9 dB, so the control is not run there; amended 2026-11-12). The factor sections' inverses are stable (all poles inside the unit circle) at every swept point. Continuity across the transition: sweeping each shelf type's frequency from 20 Hz to the top of the range in steps of 1 cent, at Qs 0.1, 0.707, 2, 10 and 40 and gains ±3, ±12 and ±30 dB, at 44.1, 48, 96 and 192 kHz, no step changes the response at any of 200 log-spaced frequencies by more than the prototype's own change in the same step plus 0.2 dB, nor by more than 0.45 dB at all (measured in drafting: 0.39 dB at most, 0.17 dB over the prototype's step); at the worst case the change for a 0.1-cent step is a tenth of the 1-cent change within 5 % (a slope, not a jump). Negative control: the hard switch at `θ = 0.9π` steps by more than 5 dB at 44.1 kHz.
- **Flat tilt** (slice 1). Within 0.07 dB of the line from 20 Hz to 20 kHz in the analog cascade at ±3 dB per octave for band frequencies from 100 Hz to 5 kHz, and within 0.17 dB (0.123 measured plus 0.05) for any band frequency (amended 2026-11-12).
- **Natural** (slice 6). Within decision 2.5's Natural bounds, per type and slope, of the prototype composite after removing `D`, for single bands and sets of up to 24 at every rate class, including 96 dB/oct high cuts from 10 to 20 kHz, the shelves at the top of their Q range, and bands in both placement domains. The correction is finite at its shared zeros: a 12 dB/oct low cut at 100 Hz, a band pass, and a notch at exactly `fs/4` meet the bounds (negative control: the plain ratio with `C(0) = 0` misses the low cut by about 0.8 dB at 20 Hz). Negative control: `D = 16` fails at 44.1 kHz.
- **Linear** (slice 5, each shipped Resolution). The kernel is symmetric about tap `L − 2P` to the bit (it is built symmetric); no host block of 64 samples does more convolution work than 6.3's per-block bound, counted by an instrumented build (negative control: computing each frame in the block where it completes exceeds it at Long); the realized gains of decision 6.3's table are reproduced by impulse DFT within 0.3 dB of the table at every shipped Resolution (the tolerance is confirmed on the implemented kernels when slice 5 is briefed; the result note records the measured values); a change of `linear_res` is a mode change by 6.5; the impulse response of the device peaks at sample `L` for a +6 dB Q 1 bell at 1 kHz with the spread schedule (negative control: a kernel centered at `L − P` with the spread peaks one partition late) (a flat target runs the identity delay line of 6.5 and is checked in the pass-through item); the reported latency equals the measured delay of a click through that kernel at every rate class; the magnitude within 0.1 dB of the target for bells, shelves and band passes of Q at most 4 at 1 kHz and above at 48 kHz (proposed; the slice records the measured region where the bound holds, and below it the resolution-limited deviation is recorded, not bounded). Negative control: reporting `L − 2P` fails the delay check.
- **Bit-exact pass-through** (slices 1 and 2). No used band, or every band an identity: output equals input bit for bit (Zero latency) or the input delayed by exactly the latency (Natural, Linear). Negative control: computing identity bands instead of skipping them breaks bit-exactness.
- **Mid/side exactness** (slice 1). Encode then decode with no band between returns the input values exactly, over random pairs within a factor of 2^29, pairs with a zero, and full-scale extremes: bit for bit except that a negative zero may return as positive zero, as decision 4 states (amended 2026-11-12). Negative control: the f32 encode and decode fails. With Mid bands only, the output's side component equals the input's within one unit in the last place of the larger output. With a Mid band at identity (skipped), every pair, `(1, 1e-40)` included, passes bit for bit (negative control: running the encode and decode for a skipped band loses the quiet channel). With no Mid or Side band, the output is bit-identical to a build with the stage removed.
- **Click-free automation** (slices 1, 2 and 5). Decision 3.3's measure, with a negative control for each (that parameter's smoothing or ramp set to 0): frequency 400 Hz to 4 kHz and back, Gain −30 to +30 dB, Q 0.1 to 40, Type Bell to High cut, Slope 6 to 96 dB per octave, Placement Stereo to Side, On and Used toggled, Output −24 to +24 dB, Gain match toggled with a band at +12 dB; a linear-phase rebuild completing (negative control: switching kernels without the crossfade). A phase-mode change is excluded (decision 6.5); its fade-out and fade-in are checked by their own test.
- **Block-size independence** (slices 1, 2 and 5). The same input at block sizes 1, 7, 64 and 512 gives bit-identical output and end state (`Plugin::fingerprint`), with band frequencies, gains and Qs set to new values after a reset at other values (so smoothers move and designs recompute), and in Linear mode with a rebuild in flight. Negative control: a build that redesigns at block starts instead of on the sample clock differs at block size 7.
- **No allocation on the audio thread** (slice 2, extended in 5 and 6). A counting allocator over `process`, `set_param` of every parameter, `reset`, `set_audition` and `band_meters`, in every mode and through a rebuild; zero allocations. Bare-metal compile of the crate (slice 1).
- **Hostile input** (slice 1). NaN and infinity give finite output and unchanged state (negative control: without the input sanitizing the output is non-finite); a run of 1e-30 values leaves no subnormal state; at 192 kHz, 10 seconds of a full-scale 20 Hz tone through a +12 dB low shelf at 20 Hz and a 12 dB/oct high cut at 40 Hz, then 30 seconds of silence, leave every f64 state exactly zero (negative control: without the 1e-30 flush a subnormal state remains); silence in gives zero out after reset at once.
- **Gain match** (slice 1 for the function, slice 2 for the device). A flat curve gives 0 dB; a composite of +6 dB at every grid frequency gives −6 dB within f64 rounding; a +6 dB high shelf at 5 kHz, Q 0.707, gives a match between −3.3 and −2.7 dB and a +6 dB low shelf at 200 Hz between −1.5 and −1.0 dB (figures computed independently in review, −2.99 and −1.24 dB, and in the round-2 drafting, −2.91 and −1.23 dB; a `1/f` weight on the log grid gives −0.21 and −4.73 and fails both); sweeping a +30 dB Q 40 bell across 1/12 octave at 1 kHz moves the match by at most 0.25 dB (negative control: the 1/24-octave grid moves it by more than 4 dB); a Q 40 band pass gives exactly the +12 dB clamp; the weight table equals its pinned literals; the match is identical at block sizes 1, 7 and 512 with all 24 bands changed in one block; with band 1's gain automated to a new value every 64-sample block, a change of band 24 still reaches the match within 2·24·256 samples (negative control: lowest-numbered-first with restart never updates band 24); two runs with different program material give identical applied gains (it cannot pump). Negative control: a level-follower variant differs between the two runs.
- **The published curve** (slice 3). The panel's curve at each column equals the core's response function at that column's frequency (the same function), and in each mode equals the device's measured response (impulse DFT) within the closed-form bounds. Negative control: a curve drawn from the analog prototype in Zero latency mode differs at 15 kHz at 44.1 kHz.
- **The analyzer against known sines** (slice 4). A sine at a bin center at −6 dBFS reads −6 dB within 0.01 dB; between bins the reading drops by at most the window's scalloping loss (0.83 dB for 4-term Blackman-Harris); a 40 Hz sine is read from the long FFT, its peak within one long bin of 40 Hz; two sines falling in one pixel column draw the larger. Negative control: averaging the bins of a column instead of taking their maximum fails the narrow-peak case. The taps write nothing while disabled and nothing for a bypassed insert.
- **Session round trip** (slice 7). A 12-band session (every type, every placement) saved and loaded in each phase mode gives identical parameters and an output identical sample for sample to the original instance's render (in process).
- **Refusals** (slice 2). Each out-of-range value through `mix.insert_param`, and through `mix.insert` and `mix.insert_params` (refused whole), is refused with its words and the document unchanged; a MIDI Learn write past a range is refused with its status line; a Natural or Linear `phase` before its slice, and Maximum `linear_res` before its slice, are refused (6.6); an automation lane past the range is clamped. Negative control: without the check the value is stored clamped.
- **Older builds** (slice 2). A session with an unknown built-in id round-trips with its parameters unchanged.
- **Audition never renders** (slice 2). With the instance's tap enabled and band 3 soloed in the live engine, an export and a freeze of that track equal the same render with no solo, sample for sample (negative control: a render path that reads the live tap's audition word differs). A soloed Bell plays a band pass at its frequency and Q, aligned with the reported latency, within the closed-form bounds by impulse DFT.
- **Mode change** (slices 5 and 6). A `phase` change, and a `linear_res` change in Linear mode, while playing gives a 5 ms fade-out, exact zeros and a 5 ms fade-in, the gap's length in samples within 6.5's bound at every rate class, and `latency()` changes at the end of the fade-out.
- **Pinned results.** Only IEEE-exact values: the parameter table and pages, the Butterworth Q constants (as literals), the SVF map from given `B` and `A` values (arithmetic and `sqrt`), the mid/side round trip, the bit-exact pass-through, the meter publication. Every render that passes through a design, a kernel or the gain match (they call `exp2`, `log2`, `sin` and `cos`) is proven in process, never by a pinned hash, until the portable math record lands.

## Consequences

- transit gets a transparent equalizer whose zero-latency highs are close to the analog prototype at 44.1 and 48 kHz without oversampling, and within decision 2.5's per-type Natural bounds of it for 1.3 ms of latency.
- The curve the user sees is computed by the code that runs, so it cannot drift from the sound.
- Zero latency is the mode for mixing and tracking; Natural and Linear are for mastering. Linear costs 85 to 683 ms of latency at 48 kHz by its Resolution, pre-ringing and stepped drags, and at its shortest setting it renders low bands visibly short of their gain; Natural is the mode for analog behavior at almost no latency.
- Shelves cost two sections each, so that a resonant shelf follows its prototype at every Q (within 0.2 dB at 1 kHz; near 20 kHz up to about 3.8 dB at 44.1 kHz and 2.8 dB at 48 kHz in Zero latency, amended 2026-11-12, as the curve shows).
- 220 parameters make this the longest built-in table; the fixed slots keep every lane, mapping and macro valid for the device's life, at the cost of long lane menus (grouped by band in the menus is a UI matter) and unused slots stored in every session.
- The one rendered FFT enters the workspace as `transit_math::fft` (ADR 0055 slice 2b), small and ours and shared with every later rendered consumer, so the linear and natural paths can become bit-identical across operating systems with the portable math record.
- A device-to-panel control word (audition) and a pre-insert tap enter the strip; other devices can use them later.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Truly dynamic parameters** (bands created and destroyed as parameters): every reference to a parameter is by id and would be orphaned or remapped on removal; static `PluginInfo` tables cannot express it.
- **Renumbering bands on removal:** moves lanes, mappings and macros to another band's sound.
- **Plain bilinear biquads for Zero latency:** cramping of several dB at 44.1 and 48 kHz (decision 2.2).
- **Oversampling:** comparable to matched sections at the base rate (worse for typical bells, better at the extremes), at about twice the cost, and either latent or not transparent (decision 2.2).
- **The single three-point fit for shelves:** feasible but up to 25 dB off for resonant shelves (decision 2.2).
- **Switching shelf designs by Q or frequency:** the designs differ by up to 26 dB at the switch, so a sweep across it would step the response.
- **One fixed linear-phase length:** a tracking-friendly length cannot realize the low end for mastering, and a mastering length is too long for anything else; a Resolution parameter from the start keeps ids stable as settings are added.
- **Direct-form sections** (the existing `Biquad`): coefficient changes under a running state disturb a direct form more than an SVF, and f32 direct forms lose precision at low frequencies.
- **Interpolating coefficients instead of smoothing parameters:** intermediate sets are not designs. (Amended 2026-11-12: parameters are still smoothed and designed; only between two designs do the coefficients glide, with exact-arithmetic stability justified in decision 3.1 and floating-point behavior checked by the amendment's sweeps.)
- **Natural phase as a fixed phase curve or a mix of modes:** "natural" is defined here as the analog prototype's response, which is measurable; anything else would be a taste.
- **Kernel rebuilds on a worker thread:** renders would depend on scheduling.
- **`rustfft` in the rendered path:** platform twiddles and run-time code selection, outside the seam.
- **A measured gain match** (loudness before and after): follows the program, pumps, and differs between runs.
- **Publishing curve arrays from the audio thread:** more traffic and a copy of what the UI can compute with the same code.
- **One large FFT for the analyzer:** slow highs; two windows give fast highs and a real low end.
- **Solo as a parameter:** it would be saved, automated, undoable and could reach a render.
- **Converting EQ 7-Band and EQ 1-Band sessions:** a shim, against the alpha rule and ADR 0040.

**1. Coefficients glide between designs (decisions 3.1 and 3.3 reconciled).**

- **The contradiction.** With sections redesigned every 16 samples and the coefficients stepped at each design, two of 3.3's steps exceed the −60 dB criterion: Gain −30 to +30 dB by 16.4 dB and Q 0.1 to 40 by 20.5 dB. Each redesign steps the mixed outputs.
- **The decision.** The design still runs every 16 samples of the plug-in's sample clock (3.1). Over each 16-sample interval, each section's state-variable coefficients (`a1`, `a2`, `a3`, `k`, `m0`, `m1` and `m2`, the ones the section tick uses; `g` is not used by the tick and may glide with them or not; `G` and the mixing of a first-order section) glide linearly per sample from the previous design to the new one. Sample `n` of the interval (0 to 15) uses the fraction `(n + 1)/16`, so the last sample is exactly the new design.
  - The glide applies only between designs of the same shape: the same section count, and neither of them the identity.
  - A type or slope change (or a placement change, On or Used) is not glided: the old configuration ramps out to `w = 0` (3.2) and the switch happens at the start of an interval with cleared states, applying the new design at once. The section count never changes within one type and slope, so no stale section runs. Switch-ins and resets also apply the new design at once.
  - A band at rest runs its plain design. Its coefficients equal a never-moved band's exactly, and with the same states its output is bit-identical.
  - Smoothing stays at 20 ms. Block-size independence holds, with the glide state included in the fingerprint.
- **Measured** with 3.3's harness, dB relative to the −60 dB threshold (negative passes):

  | Step | Coefficients stepped | With the glide |
  |---|---|---|
  | Gain −30 → +30 dB | +16.4 | −13.4 |
  | Q 0.1 → 40 | +20.5 | −9.9 |
  | Frequency 400 Hz → 4 kHz / back | −44.3 / −33.6 | −77.9 / −61.5 |
  | Slope 6 → 96, Type Bell → High cut, Placement / On / Used | unchanged (−12.4, −13.0, −23.4) | unchanged |

- **Stability.** Decision 3.1 gives the exact-arithmetic argument; the sweeps check floating-point behavior at the points they sample. A brute-force sweep follows the processor's own smoothing (20 ms one-poles stepped per sample, a design every 16 samples). It runs between every pair of corners of the frequency, gain and Q ranges: 56 trajectories of 0.3 s, every type and slope, at 44.1 and 192 kHz. At each of the 16 interpolated points of every interval it computes the spectral radius of the SVF state matrix `[[2a1 − 1, −2a2], [2a2, 1 − 2a3]]` and checks the one-pole's `0 < G < 1`.
  - Every sampled interpolated set is stable.
  - The largest radius is 0.999998545. That is the radius of a 20 Hz bell's own poles at 192 kHz, not an interpolation effect.
  - A sampled interpolated radius exceeds the larger of its endpoints' radii by at most 2.4e-4 and stays below 1. That does not contradict 3.1's argument, which bounds a blend's norm by 1, not its radius by its endpoints'.
  - The slice 1 code review repeated the sweep at 1, 8, 11.025, 16, 22.05, 32, 88.2, 384 and 768 kHz: all stable, the smallest margin 3.6e-7 at 768 kHz, every worst case a 20 Hz bell's own design radius (the margin falls roughly as 1/fs; 1.536 MHz, the top of `sane_sr`, was not probed).
  - Shelves swept back and forth between 9 and 20 kHz across the 0.9π to 0.99π transition, at ±30 dB and Q 0.707, 10 and 40, at 44.1 and 48 kHz, keep a bounded, finite output.
- **Cost.** 24 operations a sample for each second-order section of a moving band and 9 for a first-order section, computed once per band and shared by its channels; nothing for a band at rest. That is 576 a sample for 24 moving bells and about 4,800 for 24 moving 96 dB/oct cuts or flat tilts. A timing run could not separate it from the redesigns that dominate.
- **Alternatives measured and rejected:**
  - *Redesign every sample* (−15.1 and −12.0 dB): about sixteen times 3.1's design cost while a band moves, for a result no better than the glide's.
  - *Double the Gain and Q smoothing to 40 ms* without the glide: rejected, since the stepped coefficients remain at every interval. With the glide, 40 ms gives −19.2 and −15.7 dB, which the 20 ms glide does not need.
  - *Designs every 8 or 4 samples*: +10.4 / +14.5 and +5.7 / +10.0 dB, still over.
- **Evidence added:** the click steps pass at −60 dB with the glide (negative control: glide off fails by +16.4 / +20.5 dB). The stability sweep is required (negative control: coefficients overshooting fourfold reach radius 3.5). Shelves stay bounded across the transition (negative control: a blend with a negative all-pole weight gives non-finite output). At rest is bit-identical (negative control: a glide that never stops).

**3. Where the record was silent: the rules slice 1 built.**

1. **Reset settles the ramps.** `reset` ends any ramp in progress: each band's wet amount goes to its end value (1 when used and on, else 0) and the configuration switches to the stored one. It does not zero the wet amount, which would fade every band in after a stop and contradict "the first sample of a fresh instance is correct". Decision 12 read "ramps to zero"; it now says "ramps ended".
2. **Discrete changes at the next tick.** Configuration switches, ramp direction, identity and the mid/side stage decision are taken at the 16-sample control ticks, so a setter takes effect up to 15 samples later, on the sample clock.
3. **Slope crossfades only for cuts.** A Slope change ramps (3.2) only when the band's type is a cut; on any other type Slope is stored and unused (5.1), so the band does not dip.
4. **Switch-in snaps.** A band switching in from `w = 0` snaps its smoothers to their targets; the ramp makes the entry click-free, and a newly added band does not glide from old stored values.
5. **Section order.** A shelf runs `P` then `Z`, the constant on `P`. Flat-tilt sections run their pairs in frequency order (2.6's "ascending Q" applies to cuts only; 2.6 now states all three orders).
6. **Flat tilt's 0 dB point** is the analog cascade's value at the band frequency (the prototype's definition); the digital pairs are fitted to their own prototypes.
7. **Gain match details** (the four-curve mean below is the one point the code review listed for the maintainer; built as the rule until the maintainer says otherwise, and listed under "To confirm when slices 2 and 3 are briefed"):
   - The target is recomputed when a band's refresh completes, so it always comes from a consistent set of tables.
   - With bands in both placement domains, `P` is the mean of all four channel curves (left, right, mid and side).
   - On mono, `P` is the one channel's curve (Stereo and Mid bands).
   - `reset` leaves the tables and any refresh in flight unchanged.
   - `prepare` computes all 24 tables at once.
8. **Response functions: the contract.** A frequency above 0.49·fs and every non-finite frequency return NaN (no value there, decision 12); slice 1 evaluates negative infinity at DC (as it does any negative frequency), so slice 2 completes and tests the contract for negative infinity. An evaluated zero magnitude returns −∞ dB, but a notch-center evaluation may stay finite because of coefficient rounding, and Linear returns its realized kernel's finite notch depth. `band_response_db` draws the band's own shape whether or not it is used or on. NaN is the core's contract. **The panel rule is proposed, to confirm before slice 3:** no segment is drawn through a NaN (the curve ends at the last finite column) and no numeric floor is ever drawn as if it were a value; a returned −∞ represents zero magnitude and is drawn to the display floor; a mono instance draws `CurveChannel::Mid` (Stereo and Mid bands), which is exactly what the engine runs on mono (`CurveChannel::Left` would include Left bands the engine skips).
9. **Phase modes in the core.** `PhaseMode` carries only `ZeroLatency` (non-exhaustive); Natural and Linear join with slices 6 and 5.
10. **The `transit-dsp` dependency** exists from slice 1, before its user in slice 2, because the record asks for the `testing` forwarding in the first slice.

**4b. Butterworth sections for odd orders (a correction from the slice 1 code review).** Decision 2.1 gave the section Qs as `1/(2·cos(π·(2i − 1)/(2N)))`. The poles of an `N`-pole Butterworth low pass lie on the unit circle at `s = −sin θᵢ + j·cos θᵢ` with `θᵢ = π·(2i − 1)/(2N)`, `i = 1 … N`; a pair's damping is `ζ = sin θᵢ` and its `Q = 1/(2·sin θᵢ)`, poles `i` and `N + 1 − i` forming one pair, and for odd `N` the middle pole (`θ = π/2`) is the real pole `s = −1`, a first-order section. So the rule is `Qᵢ = 1/(2·sin(π·(2i − 1)/(2N)))` for `i = 1 … ⌊N/2⌋`, sorted ascending, plus a first-order section when `N` is odd. For every even `N` the `sin` and `cos` forms give the same set (checked for `N` = 2, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 16: 0.707107; 0.541196, 1.306563; …; 0.502419 … 5.101149), so only `N = 3`, the 18 dB/oct cuts, was wrong: the `cos` form gave Q 0.5774 where the true pair has Q 1.0. Measured effect: the 18 dB/oct low and high cuts were −7.78 dB at their band frequency instead of −3.01 dB (−7.7827 dB for a high cut at 1 kHz, 96 kHz), and their slope was not Butterworth. Decision 2.1 is corrected in place. **Evidence added:** the prototype of every cut and slope is `−10·log10(2) = −3.010299956639812` dB within 1e-9 dB at its band frequency, and the test checks each designed low and high cut at every slope within 0.1 dB of that value at band frequencies 100 Hz and `fs/40`, at 44.1, 48, 96 and 192 kHz (negative control: the old literal 0.5773502691896257 fails it). The builder re-measured the 18 dB/oct sweep after the fix (maximum deviation in dB, old to new: low cut at 44.1, 48, 96 and 192 kHz 3.958, 3.459, 0.960, 0.243 to 1.837, 1.204, 0.022, 0.000; high cut 2.009, 1.915, 0.555, 0.127 to 1.926, 1.934, 0.555, 0.127; item 4a's 32 and 384 kHz columns are filled below), and the sweep's test pins are the measured values plus 0.05 dB.

**4c. Denormals.** This device's denormal rule is the software flush of every filter state below 1e-30 in magnitude on every section tick, SVF and one-pole alike (decision 12). No flush-to-zero mode is set on the audio thread, consistent with the portable math record (ADR 0055, accepted, landing separately), which keeps rendered arithmetic free of processor modes.

**8. The retired EQs' automation lanes are kept (decision 16; ADR 0040's amendment 2026-11-12).** The maintainer decided on 2026-11-12 that when a built-in device is retired, its automation lanes are not dropped. EQ 7-Band and EQ 1-Band retire at the release that ships slice 4, and their lanes stay on the slot as lanes with no assigned parameter ("unassigned" lanes): nothing plays from them, the curves survive, and they can later be assigned to a parameter of a replacement device. His reason: lanes "should stay because they can be reassigned to a replacement", and "some people spend ENORMOUS amounts of time on automation". This amendment decides built-in retirement. For ordinary plug-in replacement or removal, the maintainer's stated position (2026-11-12) is the same: the lanes stay, which is today's behavior (they are kept and playback skips a lane whose parameter id the slot's plug-in does not have, issue #296). Decided 2026-11-12 (maintainer): those lanes are given the same explicit unassigned state, so that they never bind to a later plug-in by parameter id coincidence; #510 implements it. Skipping unmatched parameter ids alone does not establish that. The record's consequences:

- **Unchanged:** the slot is still emptied; the load report is still written and the status line still counts the removed inserts; there is still no conversion of an old device's settings or lanes into the Parametric EQ (that would be a migration shim, ruled out while alpha). Nothing is assigned automatically, and a lane is never assigned to a Parametric EQ parameter by name.
- **The load report** names the kept lanes as unassigned instead of saying they were dropped (for example `kept 3 automation lane(s) as unassigned`); the exact wording is the removal issue's.
- **Dependency:** the unassigned state must be visible and a lane must be assignable ("Assign to...", issue #510). #510 ships no later than the removal; until the gesture exists a kept lane would be invisible, so the removal does not ship before it.
- **Risk the implementation must handle: a lane binds by slot and parameter id.** A lane names its target as `AutoParam::Plugin { slot, param }` (`crates/model/src/automation.rs`), and the engine reads it by comparing the slot and the id string (`crates/mix/src/automation.rs`). If the retired device's lanes were simply left in place, a later device inserted into that slot with a parameter of the same id (a `gain` or `freq`, say) would silently pick the old lane up and play its curve: a surprise, and the opposite of "nothing plays from them". The implementation must therefore mark a retired lane so that it never binds by id coincidence (an explicit unassigned state recorded with the lane, not the absence of a match). How the mark is stored, and that only Assign to... clears it, is the removal issue's and #510's design; the test is that a lane kept from a retired device stays silent under a device with a same-named parameter.
- **Older readers (amended 2026-11-12).** An older reader must not silently reactivate an unassigned curve. The storage design must specify and test older-reader load and save behavior: safe preservation or refusal, and any warned loss under ADR 0023. An ignored flag beside an otherwise active slot/id target is insufficient.
- **Paths an unassigned lane must survive (amended 2026-11-12).** Unassigned lanes retain their curves and state through session save/reopen, track copy/paste, and track presets or templates wherever those operations carry automation. Freeze/unfreeze preserves them, and freeze and offline rendering never read them. Touch/latch and other parameter-writing modes must neither select nor overwrite them by slot/id coincidence (`Track::lane_mut` finds lanes by parameter equality, so a write to a same-id parameter would otherwise land on the old lane). Assign to... explicitly selects the new target and clears the unassigned state only when assignment succeeds; cancellation or failure preserves it. Tests cover these paths with a replacement exposing the same parameter id, including after serialization. Flatten preserves unassigned lanes with their curves (a test requirement; decided 2026-11-12, maintainer, who wants them kept in case something needs to be tweaked or reused later), while still removing the plug-in lanes it has rendered into audio (`flatten_into`, `crates/engine/src/cmd/freeze.rs`: today it removes every `Plugin` lane, so it must spare unassigned ones).
- **Open, not decided:** (a) whether MIDI mappings and rack macro targets that pointed at a retired device's parameters are kept the same way or dropped with a note (today's rule, which stands until decided: dropped); (b) whether the rule also applies retroactively to devices retired in earlier releases (it cannot recover lanes already dropped from sessions saved since).
- **Judging round 1 edits (amended 2026-11-12):** decision 16's session-format sentence no longer claims no new field (the stored form and older-reader behavior are #510's under ADR 0023); the paths an unassigned lane must survive (save/reopen, copy/paste, presets, freeze, offline render, touch/latch, Assign to...) and the older-reader requirement are added; the sentence about ordinary plug-in replacement records the maintainer's decision (the same unassigned state), and Flatten keeping unassigned lanes is recorded as decided.
