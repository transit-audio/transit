# ADR 0029: Per-strip job slots for the multicore strip render

- **Status:** accepted 2026-10-29 (issue #124; proposed 2026-10-27). Slice 1, the slot protocol, is implemented in `transit-rt` `strip_jobs`; slice 2, the worker set and the deadline policy, in `transit-rt` `worker_set`; slice 3, the deferred-MIDI ring and the continuity test instrument, in `transit-rt` `midi_defer`; slice 4, the wiring into the live render path, in `crates/mix` `strip_pool`. The render workers are the default since 2026-10-29; `TRANSIT_RENDER_POOL=0` renders serially (deviation 18). Evidence: the gate 6 measurement on the reference hardware, 2026-10-28 ([benchmark](../benchmarks/2026-10-28-scarlett-render-workers-gate-6.md)), with #168 and #169 fixed after it, and the #130 endurance run, 2026-10-29 (eight hours at 64 frames with the workers on: the click sample-stable, no drift, no backend errors). Amended 2026-10-29 for #174 (proposed): dependency-aware scheduling, see the amendment at the end; its slice 1 is implemented behind `TRANSIT_RENDER_GRAPH=1` (section 10), keyed plans still serial by default.
- **Date:** 2026-10-27
- **Amends:** [ADR 0012](0012-midi-device-layer.md) gate G1 and [ADR 0013](0013-unsafe-only-in-soundcraft-rt.md) (the lock-free fan-out it names as a `transit-rt` residual)
- **Reopen when:** the slot needs a field written outside its transitions, a second publisher appears, or a later slice cannot keep the callback's bound with this state machine.

## Context

The render pool (`crates/mix/src/render_pool.rs`) is rayon: its injector allocates every ~63 pushes, the callback waits on a latch, and a starved worker makes a late block. Serial was the realtime default on that evidence. #124 replaces it with a fixed worker set owned by `transit-rt`, and froze six correctness gates on 2026-10-27 (issue body, "Correctness gates", and the design notes of 2026-10-26 and 2026-10-27). This ADR records the ownership protocol those gates rest on.

## Decision

Each strip has one `JobSlot`: an `AtomicU8` state and an `AtomicU64` generation (the block it was published for), plus a fault counter written only on a refused transition. A `JobSet` is a `Box<[JobSlot]>` of fixed capacity (the most strips), built once off the audio path; nothing after construction allocates. The callback is the only publisher; workers only claim and complete.

| From | Operation | To | Who | Ordering on success | Refused (counted fault) when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Idle` | `publish(g)` | `Published` | callback | Release (generation and inputs visible to the claimer) | not `Idle`; `g` not newer than the slot's last |
| `Published` | `try_claim()` | `Claimed` + `ClaimToken` | worker | CAS, Acquire | never; a lost race is `None` |
| `Published` | `reclaim()` | `Reclaimed` | callback | CAS, Acquire | never; a lost race is `false` |
| `Claimed` | `complete(token)` | `Done` | the claiming worker | CAS, Release (the worker's writes) | not `Claimed`; a token passed to another slot (its own slot still completes) |
| `Done`, generation `g` | `consume(g)` returns `Ready` | `Idle` | callback | Acquire | `Done` with a generation newer than `g` |
| `Done`, older generation | `consume(g)` returns `Stale` | unchanged | callback | Acquire | |
| `Done`, `Reclaimed` | `retire()` | `Idle` | callback | Acquire (a late worker's writes before buffer reuse) | from `Idle`, `Published` or `Claimed` |

**Gate 1 invariant.** Per publish at most one of claim and reclaim succeeds: both are a CAS from `Published`, and `Published` is re-entered only by a publish, which needs `Idle`, which only the callback reaches, from `Done` or `Reclaimed`. The token is the only way to complete, so a worker that lost the claim cannot.

**Per-block rule** (#124, follow-up 2). At each block the callback reads every slot first: `Idle`, publish; `Done`, retire unread (a late result) and publish; `Claimed`, skip the strip and count an overrun. At the deadline it reclaims the unstarted (within the admission limit) and renders them inline, consumes what is `Done` for this block, and counts what is still `Claimed` as an overrun.

**Policies decided in #124, recorded here.** Overrun, admission and retirement are implemented by slice 2 (`worker_set`); deferred MIDI by slice 3 (`midi_defer`, rules below); the default is slice 4.

| Policy | Rule |
|---|---|
| Overrun | A claimed strip is never canceled, re-run or rendered inline. The callback waits for `Done` no later than its deadline; past it the strip is silence for the block, a counted stream fault, and the slot is republished only after `Done` is observed and retired. Holding the block instead (a late block, no gap) becomes an option only if the bench shows it is ever better. |
| Admission | The callback reclaims only as many unstarted strips as fit in the time left, by each strip's measured cost; the rest are overruns. A starved pool degrades to serial with gaps, never to a late block. **Amended (#168, deviation 16):** the measured cost is the strip's worst case, a strip with no history or a recent overrun is never reclaimed for an inline render. |
| Deferred MIDI | Events of a skipped block carry forward to the next processed block at offset 0, in order, in a fixed-capacity per-strip ring; a collapsed on/off pair gets the off one sample later; on overflow note-ons and ordinary controllers go first, the release class (note-off, sustain-off, All Notes Off, All Sound Off) stays, and past that the queue becomes All Sound Off + All Notes Off. Overflows are counted; recording is unaffected (capture at ingress and device) with an overrun marker in the take journal. |
| Retirement | Engine retirement gives each outstanding slot a bounded wait, then quarantines: the worker thread is abandoned, the instance never freed or reused, the fault logged with the plug-in's name. |
| Default | Serial stays the default unless the bench meets gate 6. **Met (2026-10-29):** the render workers are the default; `TRANSIT_RENDER_POOL=0` renders serially (deviation 18). |

## Deferred MIDI (slice 3, `midi_defer`)

A `DeferSet` holds one `DeferRing` per strip, each a fixed buffer of the caller's capacity (at least 2), allocated in `DeferSet::new`; nothing after it allocates. The event is a 24-byte `Copy` struct: `status`, `data1`, `data2`, `offset` (the sample offset within its block), `block` (the generation it belonged to, stamped on defer and kept on delivery) and `tag` (slice 4: an opaque caller value the ring carries unchanged through defer, the overflow compaction, the end-of-block carry and delivery; `Event::NO_TAG` from the constructors and on every event a silence generates). The mix crate keeps each event's identity (source, sender note id, binding, due frame) in a side table and puts its index in the tag, so a carried note-off still ends the voice its note-on started.

**One producer, one consumer, one thread.** The audio callback defers after a block and drains at a later block; `defer_block`, `deliver` and `drain_into` take `&mut self`, so the ring needs no atomics and no lock. Only the fault counters (`DeferStats`, shared through an `Arc` cloned off the audio path) are atomics, readable from any thread.

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who defers | `should_defer(outcome)` is true only for `Overrun(Busy)` and `Overrun(Unstarted)`; `defer_for` checks it. A `Late` strip's worker consumed its block's events; `Faulted` is replaced; `Mixed`, `Inline`, `NotPublished`, `Pending` have nothing to carry. |
| Landing | An event of block `g` at offset `o` lands in block `now` at `o - (now - g) * block_len`, clamped to `0..=block_len - 1` (`landing`). An event of a skipped block is in the past, so it lands at offset 0. |
| Order | Delivery keeps the original order, across several skipped blocks too; offsets never decrease (an event lands no earlier than the one delivered before it). `deliver` delivers the deferred events and then the block's live events under the same rule. |
| Collapsed pair | A note-off (or note-on with velocity 0) that would land on the sample of the note-on of the same channel and key delivered in the same call moves one sample later. |
| End of block | If that would pass `block_len - 1`, the note-off and every event behind it (live events included, deferred with generation `now`) stay in the ring for the next processed block, where they land at offset 0. No zero-length note, no reordering; counted as `carried`. |
| Overflow | When a block's events do not fit, the oldest events outside the release class are dropped (note-ons, ordinary controllers, pitch bend, pressure, program changes), the new block's own only after every older one; the release class stays: note-off, note-on velocity 0, CC 64 below 64 (sustain off), CC 66 below 64 (sostenuto off), CC 120, CC 123. One pass over the ring and the new events. |
| Silence | If the release class alone does not fit, the ring's content is discarded and the strip is silenced on every channel the discarded events used plus the strip's own (`set_channel`, channel 1 by default): a 16-bit channel mask, no stored events. Events deferred while silenced are dropped and their channels join the mask. The next drain emits, per channel in the mask (lowest first), CC 120 All Sound Off, CC 123 All Notes Off and CC 64 = 0 at offset 0 (CC 120 does not lift a held sustain pedal on a real instrument), then the block's live events, and clears the mask. Nothing allocates. |
| Counters | Per strip and for the set: `deferred`, `dropped` (every event the overflow rule discarded, the silence's included), `silences`, `carried`; `overflow_faults` = dropped + silences; `refused` for a strip out of range. |

The continuity test instrument (`midi_defer::testing::ContinuityInstrument`, feature `testing`) processes blocks given their transport position, records holes (transport time never processed), overlaps, every event with the sample it landed on, out-of-block and unsorted offsets, orphan note-offs, zero-length notes and restrikes, and reports stuck notes and pedals. Its trace and hole lists are preallocated, so it can sit on an allocation-armed path in slice 4.

## Preconditions for callers

The slot enforces its transitions; these rules are the caller's, and the executor's refutation review checks them.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Single publisher | `publish`, `reclaim`, `consume`, `retire` and `JobSet::begin` only from the audio callback; `state`, `counts`, `faults` and `for_each_pending` from anywhere. Workers only `try_claim`/`try_claim_next` and complete. |
| Buffer ownership follows the slot state, by convention | Touch a strip's buffers and instance only while holding its `ClaimToken`, after a successful `reclaim`, or after `consume` returned `Ready` (until the next publish). Never on `state() == Done` alone: a `Done` result is read only through `consume`. |
| Faults are failures | `faults()` counts refused transitions in every build; tests outside `transit-rt` assert `faults() == 0` (inside it, a refusal already fails the test). |
| One `WorkerSet`, owned by the callback | Its block methods (`begin_block`, `publish`, `wake`, `finish`) take `&mut self`, which makes the single publisher a compile-time rule; `stats`, `outcomes` and `jobs` are reads. |
| Inputs only when not busy, after `begin_block` | Write a strip's inputs only after this block's `begin_block`, before its `publish`, and only when `is_busy(strip)` is false: a busy strip's buffers belong to a worker until a later `begin_block` retires its result. |
| Mix only `Mixed` and `Inline`, in the window | After `finish`, a strip is mixed only when its `Outcome` is `Mixed` or `Inline`; `Overrun` is silence; `Faulted` is silence. Read a strip's outputs only between `finish` and that strip's next `publish`, and read the outcomes before the next `begin_block` (which resets them). |
| Never republish a faulted instance | A strip whose runner panicked (a `Faulted` outcome, or a late result that panicked, seen at a later `begin_block`) is suspect: `publish` refuses it (`PublishRefused::Suspect`) until the caller has replaced the instance and called `clear_suspect`. |
| The inline closure must not panic | An unwind through `finish` leaves the block unresolved; the caller catches inside the closure (as `guarded` does in `crates/mix`). |
| Never drop a `WorkerSet` on the audio thread | `Drop` runs `shutdown`, which sleeps while it waits (up to 1 s); retire the set through the garbage path. |
| Defer only what the strip missed; deliver before its next render | Defer with `defer_for` (or `defer_block` after `should_defer`) after `finish`, with the events the block would have delivered; at the strip's next processed block call `deliver` with that block's live events before its `publish` (or `drain_into`, then live events no earlier than `Drained::floor`, and none while events are carried). |
| Never reuse a quarantined instance | A strip named in a `Quarantine` report keeps its instance and buffers alive and unused for the life of the process. |

## Slice 4: the wiring (`crates/mix` `strip_pool`)

An engine has render workers only after `MixEngine::start_render_workers` (control thread; cores minus one, at most `MAX_RENDER_WORKERS`, promoted once at start unless `TRANSIT_RENDER_NO_PRIORITY=1`). Playback starts them unless `TRANSIT_RENDER_POOL=0` set `FORCE_SERIAL` (the opt-out, deviation 18); the per-block path is taken only while `serial_forced()` is off, the plan has no sidechain key and phase 1 has at least `PARALLEL_STRIPS` strips (or a strip is still away). Offline renders never have workers (they keep Rayon's global pool). The rayon render pool and its process-global are removed. No `unsafe`: each strip index has a cell, a `Mutex<Job>` holding a spare strip and the block's inputs, taken only with `try_lock`.

**One block** (`StripPool::render`, after `render_active` cleared the busses and aligned keys):

1. `begin_block(g)`. A strip whose worker has finished (no longer busy) comes home: its late result was retired unread by `begin_block`.
2. Per phase-1 strip: deliver its deferred MIDI (ring, then the live events taken while it was away) into its instrument's queue; a strip that reads the live input or the hardware renders on the callback; a strip still away is published only to be skipped (`Overrun(Busy)`); otherwise it is sent into its cell (the table keeps a spare with the strip's routing state and identity) and published.
3. `wake(published)`; the callback renders its own strips while the workers run.
4. `finish(deadline, inline)`; the inline closure renders the strip from its cell exactly as the serial path does (`guarded(process_strip)`).
5. `Mixed` and `Inline` strips come home (their job must carry this generation, else silence and a counted fault); `Overrun(Unstarted)` comes home unrendered (its queue keeps its events); `Overrun(Late)` stays away; `Faulted` comes home silent and suspect. The mixdown measures and routes only strips that are home and not silent. The live MIDI of `Busy` strips goes to `defer_for`.

**Deadline.** `start + period * share`, where `start` is `Instant::now()` at `render_active`'s entry and the period is `frames / sample rate` of the block. The share is 150% of the period when the device's callback period is 1.5 ms or less (up to 72 frames at 48 kHz, so 64 frames gets 150% and 128 gets 100%) and 100% above it (`transit_mix::default_strip_deadline_percent`, the default share below); `TRANSIT_STRIP_DEADLINE_PERCENT` overrides it at every size for measurement. Below 100 the rest of the period covers the auxes, the masters, the output copy and the driver; at 100 and above they run after the strips' deadline, inside the driver's slack. `MixEngine::set_strip_deadline` overrides it for tests and the bench.

**Deadline share, a measurement knob (#172).** `TRANSIT_STRIP_DEADLINE_PERCENT` sets the strips' share of the period, a whole number from 25 to 300 (anything else is logged once and ignored). It is read once per process where the engine starts its render workers, kept by the pool (no clock or environment read on the callback), logged beside the worker count, and printed in the diagnostics report's `Strip deadline` row. It moves the one deadline: `finish` waits to it and the inline admission measures the time left to it, measured as today (from the device callback's entry when playback passes one with `MixEngine::set_block_deadline`, which it computes with the engine's share, `MixEngine::strip_deadline_percent`, through the same rule, `strip_deadline_share`). It has no effect with the pool off. Above 100 the callback can run past its period while it waits for a slow strip, which is what serial does with the same spike. It exists so the gate 6 runs on the Scarlett can be repeated at other shares (the bench takes it too); set, it overrides the default share below at every buffer size, and the report row and the log say so (`Strip deadline: 75% of the period (TRANSIT_STRIP_DEADLINE_PERCENT)`).

**Default share (#172, decided 2026-10-28).** The maintainer's decision of 2026-10-28: 150% of the period when the block period is at most 1.5 ms, 100% above; 64 and 128 frames at 48 kHz are the measured instances. `TRANSIT_STRIP_DEADLINE_PERCENT` still overrides it for measurement. Without the override the share follows the device's callback period (`default_strip_deadline_percent(frames, rate)`, the 1.5 ms line inclusive). At 48 kHz that is 64 frames (1.33 ms) against 128 (2.67 ms), the two sizes measured; the line itself falls at 72 frames (exactly 1.5 ms), so 65 to 72 frames at 48 kHz also get 150%; 64 frames at 44.1 kHz (1.45 ms) and 128 at 96 kHz (1.33 ms) get 150%. The rule is in time, not frames, because what the strips have to fit in is the period. The pool is built before the device opens (the engine's largest block at its plan's rate stands in), so playback hands the engine each callback's device frames and rate before its blocks (`MixEngine::set_device_block`, from the callback's thread-local entry record, in `plan_bridge::Audio::set_callback_deadline` before the block's deadline is computed, so a changed callback gets the new share in its first block): a new length or rate resolves the share again, the same pair costs one compare, and nothing on the callback reads the environment, a clock or the allocator for it. A reopen at another buffer size builds a new engine and pool, and a driver that changes its buffer without a reopen is followed at its next callback. The share follows each callback's actual length, so a driver that delivers variable lengths around the threshold changes the deadline per callback and nothing else. The report row says the share in effect and where it came from (`Strip deadline: 150% of the period (default for 64 frames at 48 kHz)`), and so does the log at the pool's construction and at the device's open. The measurement (Scarlett, 48 kHz, build `ee038d33`, pool on, ten minutes per run; [benchmark](../benchmarks/2026-10-28-scarlett-render-workers-gate-6.md#share-runs)):

| run | strip overrun blocks at 75 / 100 / 150 | longest callback at 75 / 100 / 150 |
|---|---|---|
| 64 idle | 1,565 / 406 / 73 | 2.5 / 2.0 / 3.1 ms |
| 64 under compile | 2,081 / 594 / 111 | 2.2 / 2.6 / 3.0 ms |
| 128 idle | 47 / 18 / 33 | 2.8 / 26.7 (one unattributed event) / 4.3 ms |
| 128 under compile | 73 / 29 / 18 | 4.1 / 4.1 / 4.3 ms |

At 64 frames the share is the lever: the holes fall about twenty-fold from 75 to 150 while the longest callback stays at 2 to 3 ms, and every run that kept its log had 0 driver overloads and 0 stream errors; at 128 the three shares are within noise of each other. The maintainer's listening pass (64 frames, 150%, about six minutes, the last part under a full workspace compile) was smooth until the compile pegged every core, and the slight break-up then was the audio thread itself preempted (a 4.9 ms wake gap, outside any share; 0 driver overloads, 0 stream errors). Rationale: at small buffers a callback that runs late inside the driver's slack is the trade the hardware already absorbs, while a dropped strip is a guaranteed hole; at larger buffers the holes are already rare and the full period is the principled line.

**Caller preconditions, where each is honored.**

| Precondition | Where |
|---|---|
| Single publisher | `StripPool` owns the `WorkerSet`; only `render_active` (the callback) and `adopt_plan`/`render_workers_settled` (the callback, between blocks) reach it. |
| Buffer ownership follows the slot | A strip's state moves into its cell before `publish` and back only after `finish` resolved it (or, between blocks, when its slot is no longer `Claimed`; see deviation 12); every access takes the cell's lock with `try_lock`. |
| Faults are failures | `StripFaults::conflicts` counts refused borrows, wrong-generation results and refused publisher calls; the tests assert 0. |
| Inputs only when not busy, after `begin_block` | Delivery and `send` happen after `begin_block` and only for a strip that is home (a busy strip is always away). |
| Mix only `Mixed` and `Inline`, in the window | `is_silent`/`is_away` gate the meter and `route_into`; outcomes are read before the next `begin_block`. |
| Never republish a faulted instance | A suspect strip stays silent until its strip's generation changes (a new instance from a plan, ADR 0007's per-plug-in replacement), then `clear_suspect`. |
| The inline closure must not panic | `Job::render` uses `guarded`. |
| Never drop a `WorkerSet` on the audio thread | `into_retired` (control thread) shuts the workers down with a 500 ms bound and brings every strip home; a worker that does not return is quarantined and logged with its track and plug-ins. |
| Defer only what the strip missed; deliver before its next render | `defer_for` after `finish` for every strip with live events (it defers only `Busy` and `Unstarted`); `deliver` after `begin_block`, before that strip's `publish`. |
| Never reuse a quarantined instance | Its strip stays in its cell, leaked with the worker's state; `Retired::instances` leaves it out. |

**While a strip is away** the engine's other entry points cannot reach its instrument. `queue_event` checks what it can without it (epoch, port, binding, transport), tags the event with its identity and parks it in the strip's live buffer (`Queued`); a seek, reset, All Notes Off, transport change or revocation is a pending flag applied when the strip is back; Stop, panic and All Notes Off also drop every parked event (a parked note-on never starts after Stop). A plan is adopted only when every strip is home (`RejectReason::StripsInFlight`; playback defers the publication while `render_workers_settled` is false), and the parked MIDI is delivered into the instances before the moves.

**Stream faults.** `StripFaults` (overruns by cause, late results, inline and callback renders, refused inline renders by reason (deviation 16), faulted renders, suspect blocks, protocol faults, deferred-MIDI events, overflows and silences) reaches `CallbackTelemetry::strip_faults` and the diagnostics report beside the over-budget callbacks (`docs/troubleshooting.md`, "Render worker figures"). `MixEngine::block_overruns` feeds the take journal: while a MIDI pass records, a block with an overrun leaves an `{"overrun":{"frame","strips"}}` line. The take is unaffected: MIDI is captured at admission (`player/audio.rs`, `service_midi`), before any render, and audio by the input callback (`record.rs`).

**What is measured.** `crates/mix/tests/render_headroom.rs` (ignored; release): serial and workers at 64 and 128 frames, idle and under load, late blocks per 2000, render time p50/p95/p99/max, overruns by cause, inline renders, continuity holes and stuck notes; dev-machine numbers in [the 2026-10-27 benchmark](../benchmarks/2026-10-27-ryzen-5900x-render-workers-serial-vs-pool.md). They were not the decision: the default stayed serial until the maintainer's run on the reference hardware (the Scarlett at 64 and 128, ADR 0012 G1) showed the workers at least as reliable on deadlines (gate 6, met 2026-10-28; the default flipped 2026-10-29). Finding: under a full CPU load the workers path had late blocks (3 to 39 per 2000, up to 18 ms) with no overrun counted, because `finish` yielded between sweeps and on Windows `yield_now` hands the core to normal-priority threads for a quantum. Fixed in the slice 4 review (deviation 4 amended: spin bounded by the deadline): the same rows read 0 late blocks. The strips' deadline is now measured from the device callback's entry (`MixEngine::set_block_deadline`), and a wait that ends more than 100 µs past it is counted (`deadline_overshoots`).

### What slice 4 proves (`crates/mix/tests/render_workers.rs`, `crates/mix/src/render_pool.rs` tests)

| Gate | Slice 4 |

| 1 Exclusive execution | **Proven for the real strip processor:** six continuity instruments through a real worker set for 3000 paced blocks with worker stalls of 2 to 10 ms injected; a reentry guard in each instance and the cells' `try_lock` (zero refused borrows). ## Notes for slices 3 and 4 (from the slice 2 review)

- Slice 3 (done): defer MIDI only for `Overrun(Busy)` and `Overrun(Unstarted)`; a `Late` strip's worker consumed its events (`should_defer`).
- Slice 4 (done): the ring's event carries a caller tag; the mix crate keeps each event's identity in a per-strip side table and admits it again into the instrument's queue with it. A silence releases every source the instrument holds (`release_all`), not only the channels the ring saw.

## Consequences

- No `unsafe` in the protocol; slots hold no buffers. Buffer and instance ownership follows the slot state and is enforced by the caller (the later executor), which is where its refutation review must look.
- `loom` was not added (a new dev-dependency in the unsafe crate is the owner's call). The exhaustive tests interleave whole operations, which is complete for this slot because each operation is one atomic read-modify-write on the state; Slice 2 adds parking, wake-ups and the buffer hand-off through the slots; its threaded tests run on x86 only, which cannot catch a weakened ordering on the payload.
- Slice 2 adds no `unsafe` and no dependency. Parking is `std::thread::park`/`unpark` with a per-worker woken flag: only the flag ends a park, so a spurious return or a stray unpark only loops, and a wake before the park is never lost. The shutdown join polls `JoinHandle::is_finished` against the bound.
- Slice 4 adds no `unsafe` either: `crates/mix` shares a strip with a worker through a `Mutex` cell taken only with `try_lock` (a checked borrow that never waits; the slot protocol already gives the strip to one thread, so a refused borrow is a counted protocol fault). The mutex is also what orders a worker's writes before the callback's reads of the strip.
- The WSL gate's starved re-run names `transit-playback`, `transit-plugin-host` and `transit-ui-studio`; the race tests here run starved only once `transit-rt` is added to that list.

## Amendment (#174): dependency-aware scheduling (proposed 2026-10-29)

A plan with a sidechain key renders serially (deviation 18, K88), and the auxes, busses and masters are a serial tail after the strips, so a session with one keyed compressor gets nothing from the workers. This amendment borrows the dependency model of a task-graph scheduler (main inputs, sends and sidechain sources compiled into one execution graph) and keeps every protection above. It is the design #174 requires before code; nothing in it is implemented yet.

### 1. The execution graph

- **Nodes are tasks.** Slice 1: one task per phase-1 strip. Slice 2: one task per aux, bus and master as well (the serial tail becomes graph nodes).
- **Edges** run from every key source to its keyed task (slice 1) and, in slice 2, from every send and bus input to its receiver. `PlanCompiler::describe` compiles them next to the `KeySpec` edges it already builds from `render_rows` (the same cut cycles, the same rule that an inactive track's keys are not honored), into a fixed per-plan table: each task's input count and its dependents. Nothing on the callback allocates for it.
- **Publication.** A task is published only when every input it needs is ready for this block: a per-task counter, reset at `begin_block` to its input count and decremented as each source is consumed. Independent branches run at once; dependent ones keep their order. The callback stays the only publisher: when its `finish` sweep consumes a source, it copies that source's key into each dependent's key entries (`KeyBank::fill_deferred`; the early-key delay is left pending and runs when the strip renders, `KeyBank::apply_pending`, so a strip taken back unrendered leaves its key line untouched), then publishes and wakes each dependent whose counter reached zero. Workers still only claim and complete.
- **Pinned tasks.** A task that reads the live input or the hardware (`reads_input`: monitored audio tracks, External Instruments, hardware inserts) stays pinned to the callback. The callback renders it as soon as its inputs are ready, inside the sweep, and publishes its dependents right after; unrelated tasks are never held back for it.
- **The serial path stays.** `PlanTables::layers` and the per-layer `run_strips` in `render_active` remain the serial render (`TRANSIT_RENDER_POOL=0`, offline renders, and keyed plans until the default flips under section 8).

### 2. Completion identity

A result unlocks downstream work only when three values match: the **graph generation** (stamped by the compiler on the task table, new with every adopted plan), the **task identity** (the task index, its chain identity as `chain_identity` in `strip_pool` computes it, and a hash of its input edges), and the **block generation** `g`. The job records all three when it renders (today's `rendered` stamp, widened). `consume(g)` returning `Ready` is necessary, not sufficient: any mismatch is a stale result, retired unread and counted as a conflict (`StripFaults::conflicts`, as a wrong-generation result is today), and its dependents are skipped for the block as upstream-missed. This is the slot protocol's rule (only `Done` of this generation is read) extended from strip to task.

The #168 reset generalizes as follows: the cost history and the overrun bar belong to the chain (`WorkerSet::reset_history` on a chain change, unchanged); the completion identity also covers the edges, so a changed edge set makes an in-flight result stale without discarding the cost history of an unchanged chain.

**A graph change while a worker owns an old task.** A plan is still adopted only when every task is home (`RejectReason::StripsInFlight`, `render_workers_settled`), and deviation 12 still takes back a task whose slot is no longer `Claimed`. A `Claimed` task therefore keeps its old graph until its worker completes; the next `begin_block` retires that result unread, and the task's first block under the new graph renders afresh. Should a later path ever adopt a graph around an away task, the identity check is what keeps its result out of the new graph.

### 3. The failure rule

A dependent sees each input in one of three states:

| Input | What the dependent does |
|---|---|
| Ready: consumed for this block with a matching identity, or rendered on the callback this block | its key is filled from this block's buffer; once every input is ready, the task is published |
| Late or unavailable: `Overrun(Late)`, `Overrun(Unstarted)`, `Overrun(Busy)`, `Faulted`, suspect, or a stale result | the dependent is not published this block: silence, counted upstream-missed, and the miss propagates to its own dependents; unrelated branches keep rendering |
| Still running: claimed by a worker, the deadline not reached | the dependent waits, unpublished; the worker keeps exclusive ownership of the source's processor and buffers; at the deadline the source is `Late` and the dependent is upstream-missed |

A compressor is never fed last block's key and never runs without its key: a missed key skips the whole keyed task, not just its insert. A dependent that becomes ready but does not fit the time left is admitted or refused by the existing rule (deviation 16) and counted under its own cause. The key alignment stays (`KeyBank::align`): an early key is delayed in its `KeyEntry`, a late key delays the receiver through its `KeyIn`. In slice 1 a tail aux keyed from a phase-1 strip that missed is skipped by the same rule.

### 4. Attributed failure propagation

Per task, by cause: late, busy, unstarted, and upstream-missed (a new `OverrunCause`, recorded with the origin task: the first source in the branch that missed on its own). The #169 per-strip rows keep naming the origin's track; a dependent's row shows its upstream-missed count and the origin it came from. The report and the take journal count the upstream cause once, at its origin: one late key source with three dependents is one overrun at the source plus three upstream-missed skips, not four overruns.

### 5. State continuity on a skip

A dependent skipped while its own processor was free is treated as an overrun:

- **Deferred MIDI.** The `midi_defer` rules (slice 3) apply as to `Overrun(Busy)`: `should_defer` accepts the upstream-missed cause, the block's live events go to the ring (`defer_for`), and they land at offset 0 of the task's next processed block.
- **Held notes.** A skipped instrument's held notes stay held; the ring carries the block's events, note-offs included, under the overflow and silence rules.
- **Delay-line history.** Kept. A skipped task's lines (its path delay lines, its `KeyIn` lines and its `KeyEntry` lines) are neither written nor idled for the block (`KeyLine::idle` zeroes the line before its next write, which is a reset), so the next processed block continues from the history before the skip. The block's output is silence; the state is not reset.
- **A late strip.** Its own state advances: its worker's render completes (its plug-ins, lines and MIDI move on for the block), and only its output is retired unread.
- **Meters and automation.** Both advance as if the block were silent: automation is read at each block's own position (nothing catches up), and the meter reads a silent block.
- **Not retried.** The skipped task is not rendered later in the block, not rendered inline when a late source arrives, and not run twice in the next block to catch up; the missed audio is not recovered; a late source's result is retired unread at the next `begin_block`, as today.

### 6. What is not borrowed

The compared scheduler waits for every job. transit does not: the bounded wait to the strips' deadline (`finish`), the busy-worker skip across callback boundaries (the per-block rule, deviation 7) and the job-slot ownership rules (the transition table, the caller preconditions, gates 1 to 5) stay exactly as stated above. The graph decides only when a task is published.

### 7. Slices

Each slice ships on its own, behind the gates in section 8.

1. **Sidechain-aware strip scheduling.** One task per phase-1 strip, with an edge from every key source. The current routing restrictions stay (no key from a bus or an aux onto a phase-1 track; auxes, busses and masters remain the serial tail). The graph path replaces the `keys.is_empty()` condition in `render_active`, behind a process-wide opt-in read once like `TRANSIT_RENDER_POOL` until section 8's measurement, then by default. At the end of slice 1, K88's "a session with sidechain keys renders serially" is retired, and the matching sentence of deviation 18 with it.
2. **Aux and bus scheduling.** The serial tail becomes graph nodes, with edges from every send and bus input.
3. **Generalized routing.** The bus/aux-to-audio-track key restriction is lifted.
4. **Critical-path prioritization** and splitting at expensive inserts, only if measured to matter. With one task per strip and at least as many workers as strips it does not; dependencies and pinned tasks can make it matter with fewer strips, so measure first.

### 8. Gates

Before each slice lands, and again before the graph path becomes the default for keyed plans:

- **Equivalence.** Serial and graph renders of the corpus sessions (`crates/model/tests/corpus`) and of the keyed sessions in `crates/mix/tests/sidechain.rs` are bit-exact, as gate 2 already requires of the strips. The mixdown's order does not change, so no tolerance is stated; a slice that needs one states it in this ADR first.
- **Sidechain impulse alignment.** An impulse on a key source and on the keyed strip reaches the keyed insert on the same sample under the graph as on the serial path, with an early key, a late key, and plug-in latency on either side.
- **Allocation-free callback.** Zero allocations and frees on the callback over a keyed plan with stalls, upstream-missed skips and inline renders (the allocation-armed helper).
- **Deterministic late workers.** A worker held on a barrier, never a clock: a key source that is late, busy or unstarted gives its dependents upstream-missed and nothing else; a graph change while a worker owns an old task waits until it is home, then the old result is retired unread and no dependent under the new graph reads it; a forced identity mismatch is retired unread and counted; zero stuck notes through the continuity instrument across upstream-missed skips.
- **Scheduling-condition equivalence (#210).** For any stall schedule, not only the clean path: a seeded schedule (which strip is held in which block, for how many blocks; the test's input, replayed exactly) is realized through deterministic seams only (a hold inside the strip's render on a worker, released by the test; a held strip's cost history that refuses an inline render; a deadline reached once only the held strips are unresolved, each claimed by a worker), through two workers (a held strip `Late`, then `Busy`) and through none (`Unstarted`). Per keyed topology of `sidechain.rs` and `render_graph.rs` (the corpus sessions with a key edge included), the continuity session, and a key edge added or removed while a worker holds an old task: the strips that miss a block are exactly the counted skips (busy, late, unstarted, upstream-missed, by strip and block); the graph render equals, on every sample, the serial render with exactly those blocks muted (a skipped strip not rendered, its keys not filled, its lines neither written nor idled, its MIDI held; a late strip rendered and its output dropped); and every home strip's state fingerprint (plug-in state such as the compressor's envelope, the key alignment and delay compensation lines with their write positions, delivered and pending MIDI) is the same in both at every block boundary. A failing seed is shrunk to a minimal schedule, printed as a Rust literal for a named regression test. State no fingerprint reads (the corpus sessions' effects and synths) is covered by a post-fault window stated per case: the render runs past the last hold for the longest tail and latency of the case's plug-ins plus the plan's latency. `crates/mix/tests/sched_equivalence.rs`; the seams behind the `testing` feature of `transit-mix` and `transit-dsp`; the schedule and its shrinker in `transit-rt` `worker_set::schedule`. Its first run (2026-10-29) found one defect, fixed the same day (9c32400e, with its three minimal schedules as regressions): a strip published with an early key and then taken back `Unstarted` had its key entry's line written for the block it did not render, a block ahead of its own signal.
- **E49.** No test asserts a scheduling or real-clock outcome; a test that renders four or more strips calls `MixEngine::force_serial(true)` unless it tests the workers.
- **Measurement.** The default flips only on a measurement on the reference hardware: the gate 6 session with one key edge added (Solid Bus Comp keyed from the Battery 4 strip, saved as its own file and named in the benchmark), serial against the graph, at 64 and 128 frames, idle and under compile, ten minutes per run, at the default share. Compare over-budget callbacks, the longest callback, strip overrun blocks by cause (upstream-missed apart), deadline overshoots and worker wake with the gate 6 record: the pool rows (2 / 45 / 3 / 2 over-budget callbacks at 64 idle, 64 under compile, 128 idle, 128 under compile) and the share runs at the default share (73 and 111 overrun blocks at 64 frames, 18 and 29 at 128).

### 9. Reopen when

A task needs a second publisher (a worker publishing its dependents), an edge cannot be expressed as a buffer ready for this block (feedback, a delay-free cycle), the skip rule loses more audio than serial in a measurement on the reference hardware, or slice 4's critical path is measured to matter.
