# ADR 0063: Clip tempo and Follow Tempo

- **Status:** proposed 2026-11-26 (issue #336, milestone v0.8.0; revised after judging round 1; the open questions at the end are for the maintainer).
- **Date:** 2026-11-26
- **Implementation:** not started. Slices in the Slices section, kept as a checklist in #336.
- **Reopen when:** the quality check of slice 0 finds no acceptable Smooth algorithm under the license rule; renders of ordinary loops miss the render-before-play budget on the reference machine, or a tempo change during playback is heard in the stand-in for more than a moment (the trigger for a real-time stretcher); the render cache's memory budget is exceeded by ordinary sessions; warp markers need a mapping this record fixed differently; or Ableton and Logic are found to agree on a behavior chosen differently here (claims marked unverified in Context).

## Context

The maintainer's case (2026-11-04): a 130 bpm project and a 133 bpm drum loop, and no way to make the loop follow the project tempo. Rulings already made on #336 and binding here: the stretch mode is a property of the clip with Keep Pitch (the default) and Re-Pitch, and Conform to Tempo and Follow Tempo use it without a prompt; Remove Pitch Shift stops being a separate command; the mode lives in the audio clip editor, the clip actions on the right-click menu, with no top-level Clip menu (2026-11-16); no tempo dance: detection happens on import, in the order `acid` tempo, file name, nearest whole-bar count, analysis, and the detected value is shown and can be typed over; the first version users get keeps the pitch; and before the mode is designed, a quality check of the existing pitch-preserving stretch on real material, recorded under `docs/benchmarks`. Standing rules: import makes no audible change the user did not ask for; the arrangement grid is one structure for tempo and meter; no interim surface; no migrations or shims while alpha.

### The code today (read at 5233b087)

- **`Clip::stretch`** (`crates/model/src/clip.rs:176-178`) is documented as "applied by rendered processing" but is a live varispeed: every read goes through `ClipReader::read` at `q = 1 / stretch` (`crates/model/src/disk/read.rs:113-132`), exactly the direct index at a stretch of 1 and the band-limited reader of ADR 0056 decision 8 otherwise, in memory, from the disk ring and offline alike (#604; `crates/mix/src/clips.rs:120-129`). The reader takes rates up to 32 and reads silence above (`crates/resample/src/read.rs:9`, `114-118`). Stretch bounds 1/32 to 32 (`clip.rs:105-110`), a stretch within 1e-9 of 1 stored as 1 (`clip.rs:112-117`). A clip's `length` may exceed its source while it loops (`clip.rs:186-189`), its content wrapping by `content_pos` (`clip.rs:132-137`).
- **The inherited commands.** `clip.conform_to_tempo` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/clip_more.rs:223-260`) sets `stretch = source_bpm / bpm` and rescales the length without rendering (`clip_more.rs:249-253`), keeping its memory in `edit.values` keys `clip.bpm.<id>` and `clip.len0.<id>`; it re-pitches. `clip.elastic_properties` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/clip.rs:141-147`, `214-258`) stores a varispeed without rendering when the track has no Elastic algorithm or has `"varispeed"` (`clip.rs:222-236`), and otherwise renders a pitch-preserving stretch into a new source, clones the clip onto it and resets `stretch` (`clip.rs:239-253`), remembering the original source, offset and length in `elastic.<id>.*` keys, which `clip.remove_warp` (`clip.rs:259-288`) restores. The algorithm is `Track::elastic` (`crates/model/src/track.rs:282-283`, serialized on every track, `null` when unset), set by `track.elastic` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/track.rs:207-219`). The renders go through `render_stretched` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/more_util.rs:143-198`), which sizes its input at up to ten times the output (#544); its callers are `clip.remove_pitch_shift` (`clip_more.rs:262-281`), Elastic on an Elastic track, and `edit.tce_to_timeline` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/edit_more.rs:224`, `749`). None of them is on a menu: the reachability gate lists them as undecided (`crates/ui-studio/tests/command_reachability/exceptions/undecided.rs:307-308`, `353-354`), and #728 was rescoped (2026-11-16) to leave them off until this record. `audiosuite.process` also offers `time_stretch` and `pitch_shift` renders (`crates/engine/src/cmd/audiosuite.rs:103-104`).
- **The stretcher.** `transit_dsp::offline::time_stretch` (`crates/dsp/src/offline.rs:231-327`) is a WSOLA with a 40 ms Hann window, half overlap and a 10 ms search on a mono guide shared by all channels, taking one scalar ratio clamped to 0.1..10. Its window is built with the standard library's `cos` (`offline.rs:245`), outside ADR 0055's portable math. The Time Stretcher built-in (`crates/dsp/src/plugins/time_stretcher.rs:1-40`) is a different thing: a port of a granular module's stretch mode as an insert effect, at 32 kHz on int16 frames; it is not a clip stretcher.
- **The transient detector** `detect_transients` (`offline.rs:382`) is already the one engine for the Sampler's Detect (`crates/engine/src/slice_detect.rs:248`, #267) and the clip transient analysis (`crates/engine/src/io.rs:438`).
- **Embedded tempo.** `transit_audio_io::wav_tempo` (`crates/audio-io/src/wav_cues.rs:337-344`) reads the `acid` chunk's tempo from a WAV's bytes. Its one caller, the Sampler's Slice to MIDI (`crates/engine/src/cmd/sampler_acid.rs:38`), reads the whole file and caches by path (#452). No other format's tempo is read.
- **Tempo and positions.** The tempo map holds step changes only (`crates/time/src/tempo.rs:43-46`); the Linear, Parabolic and S-Curve operations write step series at a resolution (`crates/engine/src/cmd/event_more.rs:34-50`). Clip positions are samples. On a tempo change `retime` works on ticks-timebase tracks only: it moves their clip starts and automation points, an audio clip keeping its length and a MIDI clip musical end to end (edges, fades, envelope, sync point, loop), and refuses a change that squeezes a clip or loop (`crates/engine/src/retime.rs:1-18`, `106-135`, `186-206`). Audio tracks default to the samples timebase (`crates/model/src/track.rs:337`), where neither clips nor automation move.
- **Undo and identity.** Undo swaps the whole document (`crates/engine/src/lib.rs:646-648`), the source pool and the id allocator `next_id` included (`crates/model/src/session.rs:399`, `505-510`), so a source id can be reused by a later import after an undo. The pool holds each source's audio as an `Arc<SourceAudio>` (`crates/model/src/source.rs:79-90`) and records no content identity.
- **Join** in the interface runs `edit.heal` on the range and then, when two or more clips remain, `edit.consolidate`, which renders them into one new clip (`crates/ui-studio/src/edit_tools.rs:190-197`). Heal compares source, offset continuity, loops and clip gain only (`crates/engine/src/edit.rs:455-470`; #578).
- **ADR 0056** (audio clip editor, v0.8.0) owns the clip-to-source mapping (`offset` read at clip time `anchor` at the effective rate `q = r / stretch`, decision 5), reserves a clip key `warp: Option<...>` for this record with its own `needs` entry ("absent means decision 5's fixed-rate mapping"), and forbids new timing state in `edit.values`, any other meaning for `warp`, a source-time position anywhere but `offset` and the warp description, and a transpose that changes `stretch` (decision 12). Its decision 10.2 (accepted 2026-11-12, after the maintainer's #427 ruling of 2026-11-11 that loop cycles start on the tick grid) narrows that ruling: unwarped audio loops keep whole-sample cycles, and "a loop that follows the grid is the warping record's". Its slice 1 (`anchor`, the #554 fix) has not landed (`ClipContent::Audio` is still `{ source, offset }`, `clip.rs:85-87`).

### Conventions

From the issue (the maintainer's survey, not re-verified here): Ableton and Logic agree that a clip carries its original tempo, detected and editable; that a toggle makes it follow the project tempo (Live's Warp; Logic's Follow Tempo); and that markers fix the alignment where detection is off. They differ in algorithms (Live's Beats, Tones, Texture, Re-Pitch, Complex; Logic's Slicing, Rhythmic, Monophonic, Polyphonic, Speed) and in where the control lives (Live's clip panel, Logic's region inspector with Flex on the track). Both have a slicing mode and a speed mode among their algorithms. **Not verified, not relied on:** whether either switches following on by default for imported files and on what condition; where Logic stores the original tempo (file or region); whether either offers the toggle in a top-level Edit menu; what either plays while a stretch is being computed.

## Decision

### 1. Data model

1. **`Clip::tempo: Option<ClipTempo>`**, written only when set, with `ClipTempo { bpm: f64, from: TempoFrom, mode: StretchMode }`. `bpm` is the material's original tempo in quarter notes per minute, 5 to 1000 as the tempo map's (`valid_bpm`), further bounded while following (1.7). `from` is `File`, `Name`, `Bars` or `Typed` (decision 2), shown beside the value so a guess reads as a guess. `mode` is `Beats`, `Smooth` or `RePitch`: Keep Pitch is Beats or Smooth (the two pitch-preserving algorithms), Re-Pitch is speed and pitch together; the ruling's two values are kept, with Keep Pitch carrying an algorithm. The mode lives here, not in `warp`, so it survives Follow being switched off, as the ruling's "property of the clip" requires. A `clip_tempo` need when any clip has one (an older build would drop it on save; ADR 0023).
2. **Per clip, not per source.** A split copies it, so pieces agree; two clips of one file may differ (a trimmed region of a longer file). A per-source tempo would make Set Original Tempo change other clips, which neither convention requires.
3. **`Clip::warp: Option<Warp>`**, the key ADR 0056 decision 12.2 reserved, present exactly while the clip follows the project tempo. `Warp { length: Samples, looping: Option<ClipLoop>, slices: Vec<Samples> }` is **the clip as it is at rate 1**: with the clip's `offset`, `warp.length` and `warp.looping` are exactly the `length` and `looping` the clip has when it does not follow (the length may exceed the source while it loops, as today), in rate-1 clip time; `slices` are the Beats mode's slice points in source frames, ascending, at most 4,096 (empty in the other modes). A `clip_warp` need when any clip has one; an older build plays the clip at its fixed rate and warns, as decision 12.2 states. Warp markers (source and destination anchor pairs) join the structure later.
4. **The arrangement form is derived.** While following, the clip's own `length` and `looping` are written from the rate-1 view by the follow mapping (1.5) on every edit and tempo change, so the playlist rule, the arrangement and the mix keep reading `start + length` as today. Switching Follow on copies `length` and `looping` into `warp` and derives; switching it off writes them back from `warp` and clears it, so the clip returns exactly as it was, looped length included.
5. **The follow mapping.** Arrangement clip time `c` maps to beats `b(c) = (ticks(start + c) − ticks(start)) / 960` through the session tempo map (`samples_to_ticks_f`), and beats to rate-1 clip time `u(c) = b(c) · 60 · R / bpm` with `R` the source's stored rate. Unlooped, the source position is `offset + u(c)`. Looped, with the rate-1 loop `[ls, le)` of cycle `C`, the loop start sits at beat `ls · bpm / (60 R)` and cycle `k` starts at the sample nearest beat `(ls + k·C) · bpm / (60 R)` (computed from the beat directly and rounded once, never by summing cycles: the #427 rule, decision 5), playing rate-1 clip times `ls..le` from there until the next cycle starts. The derived `length` is the first arrangement sample whose `u` reaches `warp.length`. `anchor` stays 0 (decision 1.8). Only integers are stored; the f64 mapping is derived.
6. **Relation to `stretch` and the inherited commands.** `stretch` stays in the model, read and played as ADR 0056 composes it, so a session holding a stretch loads and sounds unchanged; after slice 1 no command writes it. Switching Follow on for a clip with a stretch other than 1 copies `round(length / stretch)` and the loop scaled the same way into `warp` and sets `stretch = 1`, in the same undo step: Follow replaces the varispeed it was. Slice 1 retires `clip.conform_to_tempo`, `clip.remove_pitch_shift`, `clip.elastic_properties`, `clip.remove_warp`, `edit.tce_to_timeline`, `track.elastic` and `Track::elastic`, with `render_stretched` (closing #544) and the `elastic.<id>.*`, `clip.bpm.<id>` and `clip.len0.<id>` keys: Follow Tempo in Re-Pitch is Conform to Tempo that keeps following; switching a clip from Re-Pitch to Keep Pitch is Remove Pitch Shift; a per-track Elastic algorithm is the inherited Pro Tools shape the maintainer declined (2026-11-04). Retiring them with the slice that creates following clips leaves no window in which an inherited render can repoint a following clip's source under its `warp`. Keys already in a session file are ignored (no migration). **What is lost:** a clip that Elastic already rendered keeps playing its rendered source, but the original source, offset and length its `elastic.<id>.*` keys remember can no longer be restored, and Follow off cannot recover them from the rendered file; re-importing the original is the way back. `audiosuite.process`'s `time_stretch` and `pitch_shift` stay, as offline renders that write a new file, refused on a following clip (decision 5).
7. **The tempo domain while following.** For each tempo segment under a following clip, the ratio `ρ = (bpm_segment / bpm) · (R / session rate)` must lie in the mode's domain: Re-Pitch the reader's `[1/32, 32]`; Keep Pitch the renderer's (today's WSOLA 0.1 to 10; slice 0 states the chosen algorithm's). Switching Follow on, setting the tempo or the mode, and any tempo-map edit or retime that would put a following clip outside its domain is refused, naming the clip, as `retime` refuses a squeezed clip.
8. **Transforms.** In the first slices Follow is refused on a clip that is reversed, transposed, fine-tuned or anchored (ADR 0056's transformed clip, decision 4.4), and those transforms are refused on a following clip, each with its reason. Pitch at constant length (ADR 0056's "transpose under warp") is a later slice. Follow is refused on a disk-backed source (ADR 0035) until the cache can stream (slice 6).
9. **No migration, and what saves byte-identical.** A clip without `tempo` and `warp` is today's clip; both fields are skipped when absent, as `Clip::take` is (`clip.rs:194`). Removing `Track::elastic` in slice 1 changes the bytes of every saved session once: each track's `"elastic": null` (or an algorithm name, which no longer does anything) is no longer written. Beyond that one change, a session with neither new field saves as before.

### 2. Original tempo on import

1. **Order** (the maintainer's): (a) the file's `acid` tempo, from a read of the chunk headers only, through one reader the Sampler's Slice to MIDI also moves onto (fixing #452's first point; the cache keyed by size and modification time, its second); (b) a number in the file name from 60 to 200, decimals allowed, beside "bpm" (any case, with or without a space) or alone in brackets or between separators (`_`, `-`, space), as in "Melody[133]" or "Drums_131_"; when several qualify, the one beside "bpm" wins, else none (ambiguous); (c) the whole-bar count: with `L` the file's length in seconds, the project tempo `T` and the quarter notes per bar `n` at the import position, the bar counts `N` in 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, the candidate `60·N·n / L` nearest `T` in ratio, accepted when it lies within 60 to 200 and within a ratio of 1.25 of `T` (outside that, half and double time are as likely as the guess); (d) analysis, a later slice. The first that gives a value sets `tempo` with its `from`; none leaves `tempo` unset. Only `file.import_audio` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/file.rs:55`) detects; recordings and renders get no tempo in these slices.
2. **The maintainer's case.** Four bars of 133 bpm are 7.218 s; at 130 bpm in 4/4 the candidates are 66.5, 133 and 266, so (c) gives 133.0 (`from: Bars`), within 1.25 of 130.
3. **Follow Tempo is off on import** (recommended, question 1). Detection stores a fact and changes nothing audible: an imported clip renders exactly as its file. The alternatives: on by default for files from a loop-aware place (a file with an `acid` tempo or a tempo in its name) changes what an import sounds like without the user asking, which the standing ruling forbids; a per-import choice is a dialog on every import, a prompt the 2026-11-16 ruling avoided. An opt-in preference ("Imported loops follow the project tempo", off by default) is the user asking and can come later. One click (decision 6) makes the clip follow.
4. **Typed.** Set Original Tempo writes `from: Typed`. Follow on a clip without a tempo runs (a) to (c) first and is refused with "set the original tempo first" when none applies.

### 3. Rendering

1. **Re-Pitch is read live** through ADR 0056's reader on the follow mapping: within each tempo segment and cycle the rate is that segment's `ρ` and the position is the mapping's. No render, no cache.
2. **Keep Pitch is rendered offline into a cache**, never on the audio thread. A render job runs off the control thread on a worker, the way Slice Detect does (`slice_detect.rs:1-20`), and its result joins the session's source pool as a rendered entry (an `Arc` of audio beside the sources, never serialized, never in the undo history), which the published snapshot maps from the clip, so the audio thread does one lookup per clip as it does for sources and reads the entry at rate 1 by arrangement clip time. Cycles under one tempo render once and are placed at each cycle start.
3. **The audio's identity.** Each `SourceAudio` gets an `audio_id: u64` from a process-wide counter when its audio is created (decoded, recorded or rendered), never reused within the process and never serialized. Undo and redo restore the same `Arc`, so the same identity; a later import that reuses a source id gets a new identity; re-importing the same file is a cache miss, which is harmless. A content hash was rejected: it reads the whole file to build a key.
4. **The key** is a hash over everything the render reads and nothing else: the `audio_id`, `offset`, `warp.length`, `warp.looping`, `bpm`, `mode`, `slices`, the session rate, the tempo segments under the clip as (offset from the clip start in samples, bpm) pairs, and a renderer version. The clip's position is not in it, so duplicates of a loop at constant tempo share one render, and undo or redo finds an earlier render still cached. Entries are evicted least recently used beyond a memory budget (slice 3 measures and sets it), never while the current snapshot maps them.
5. **Several tempos under one clip**: the renderer takes a monotone piecewise-linear output-to-source position map (one piece per tempo segment), not a scalar ratio; today's `time_stretch` scalar is its one-piece case. The same map serves warp markers later. The tempo map has no continuous ramps, so pieces are exact.
6. **While a render is pending.** A tempo change (any edit to the map, including the step series of a ramp operation), Follow switched on, a mode or tempo change, or an edit of a following clip makes its key new; until the render lands the clip is **pending**, drawn so in the arrangement (the design hand-off gives the state). Two rules keep the common case from ever hearing a stand-in:
   - **Play waits for short renders.** Play and Locate render the pending following clips the playhead reaches within the priming window on the calling thread before starting, as they prime disk-backed rings (ADR 0035 decision 4; `crates/model/src/disk.rs:725`), up to a render-before-play budget that slice 3 measures and sets; a loop's render is expected to fit it.
   - **What plays past the budget, or when a change lands during playback,** is a product choice (question 8). The options: (a) the Re-Pitch reading of the same mapping: timing right at once, pitch off by the tempo ratio until the render arrives (133 to 130 is 0.39 semitone flat; a 10 percent change about 1.8 semitones), then a switch; (b) silence: the part drops out until it is ready, nothing wrong is heard but something is missing; (c) the last finished render at the old tempo: pitch right, timing wrong, drifting off the grid by the tempo difference across the clip (at 133 against 130, about 170 ms by the end of four bars), cut or gapped at the clip's end, and nothing at all for a clip that has just started following. **Recommended: (a)**, the switch to the render a 5 ms linear crossfade starting at a block boundary.
7. **Playback and export.** Offline paths (bounce, export, freeze, consolidate) render any missing entry synchronously first, through the same function, and never use a stand-in. So the live read equals the export bit for bit **when no render is pending**; while one is pending they differ by the stand-in, and the arrangement shows it. Tests drive the worker's pending jobs to completion on the test thread (as they drive `DiskSource::pump`) and compare then.
8. **Determinism.** The Smooth renderer computes its window and every transcendental through `transit-math` (ADR 0055). Beats copies source samples and applies linear fades only, so its renders are IEEE-exact.
9. **Undo** restores clip fields only; the cache is derived state, like peaks.
10. **Rejected for now: a real-time stretcher on the audio thread.** It costs CPU per following clip per block, needs pre-roll on every locate and a latency report, is hard to keep block-partition invariant, and the only permissively licensed candidate seen (Signalsmith Stretch, a header-only C++ library, MIT by its published documentation) would need a new FFI crate under the unsafe policy. Loops are short and their renders fit the play budget. ADR 0035's streaming is the path for long files (slice 6); the trigger is in Reopen when.

### 4. Beats and Smooth

1. **Beats**: `slices` are detected once by `detect_transients` (the Sampler's detector, one engine) when Beats is chosen, and stored, so the render is reproducible on every machine and the slices are the seed of warp markers; Re-detect is an explicit action. Each slice plays at rate 1 from the sample nearest its beat position under the follow mapping. A slice longer than the time to the next slice is trimmed there with a 2 ms linear fade-out; a shorter one ends with the same fade and silence fills the gap (Trim). Filling the gap by looping the slice tail is a later option. The clip's first slice starts at its first source frame whether or not a transient is there, and so does each loop cycle's.
2. **Smooth**: the pitch-preserving stretch slice 0 chooses (today's WSOLA as it is, an improved WSOLA, or another algorithm under the license rule, MIT, Apache, BSD or ISC only), taking the position map of 3.5.
3. **Default mode when `tempo` is first set**: Beats when the detector finds at least two transients per beat on average over the clip, else Smooth (question 4). Until Beats lands, Smooth.
4. **Beats ships in the first user-visible cut.** The maintainer's case is a drum loop, which a WSOLA smears; Beats is the transient-exact answer, IEEE-exact, and simpler than a better Smooth. The surface (decision 6) appears only when Beats, Smooth and Re-Pitch all work.

### 5. Edits on a following clip

Every edit works on the rate-1 view (`offset`, `warp.length`, `warp.looping`, `slices`) with today's rate-1 rules, then re-derives the arrangement form (1.4). An arrangement time `t` is converted to rate-1 clip time `u = round(u(t − start))`.

### 6. Surface

Following the shared shape (a clip-panel control; neither convention relies on a top-level menu for it, as far as the issue's survey shows): the audio clip editor's property column gains a **Tempo** group (Original tempo field with its origin, typed over; Follow Tempo toggle; Mode menu: Beats and Smooth under Keep Pitch, then Re-Pitch; Re-detect beside Beats); the clip's right-click menu gains **Follow Tempo** (checked when on) and **Set Original Tempo...**; a following clip's header in the arrangement shows its original tempo and the pending state. No Edit menu entry and no Clip menu (the 2026-11-16 ruling). The group arrives after ADR 0056 slice 3 (the editor shell) and needs the design hand-off. Nothing is exposed before slice 5; Conform to Tempo never reaches a menu.

## Slices

A checklist in #336, no separate issues. Each lands on its own; slices 1 to 4 are control channel only.

0. **Quality check (docs).** The existing WSOLA on a drum loop, a bass line and a full mix at ±3 % and ±10 to 20 %, with measurements and listening notes under `docs/benchmarks`, and the license-first look at alternatives from their documentation. Decides Smooth's algorithm and its ratio domain. Gate: the record exists and names the choice.

## Consequences

- A loop follows the project tempo with its pitch kept, from one click, and switching Follow off gives the clip back exactly, looped length included.
- Following clips are musical: they move with the grid on a tempo change even on a samples-timebase track, where the track's automation does not.
- After a tempo change during playback, a Keep Pitch clip is heard in the stand-in until its render lands, and the arrangement shows it pending; Play from a stop waits for short renders; exports never use a stand-in.
- The inherited stretch commands and the per-track Elastic algorithm go with the first slice; an Elastic-rendered clip can no longer be restored to its original; `stretch` remains as a read-only legacy until a later record retires it; every saved session drops `"elastic"` once.
- Renders cost memory; long files cannot follow until slice 6.

## Alternatives rejected

- **The tempo on the source:** couples clips that the user may want to differ, and makes a split or a trimmed region inherit a value that may not fit it.
- **A source span alone as the following clip's state** (round 1's `span`): cannot hold a looped clip's length, so Follow off lost the repeats, and its split ignored the loop's wrap.
- **Follow as a fixed-rate `stretch` rewritten on every tempo change** (Conform to Tempo kept running): re-pitches, cannot hold two tempos under one clip, and keeps hidden state in `edit.values`, which ADR 0056 forbids.
- **The render as a new source in the document** (today's `render_stretched`): destructive by bookkeeping, saved files multiply, and toggling off depends on remembered keys.
- **A render keyed on the clip id or the source id:** duplicates re-render, undo loses the render, and a source id reused after an undo would hand one file's render to another.
- **Join falling through to Consolidate on following clips:** silently ends Follow and the way back to the original material.
- **Refusing the inherited commands on following clips until a later retirement:** refusal code written to be deleted; retiring them in slice 1 is simpler.
- **Beats detected afresh on every render:** a render could differ between machines (the detector's floating point), and markers would have nothing to start from.
- **Rubber Band and other GPL or LGPL stretchers:** license.

## Open questions for the maintainer

1. **Follow Tempo on import.** Recommended: off for every import; an opt-in preference later.
2. **The whole-bar rule's bounds** (bar counts 1 to 32 in powers of two, 60 to 200 bpm, within a ratio of 1.25 of the project tempo). Recommended as stated.
3. **Retire `edit.tce_to_timeline` and `track.elastic` too**, beyond the commands the issue names. Recommended: yes; both are the same inherited family, and TCE renders through `render_stretched`. If not, both are refused on following clips from slice 1.
4. **Default mode by detection** (Beats at two or more transients per beat on average, else Smooth). Recommended as stated, the threshold checked on slice 0's material.
5. **AudioSuite and the clip renders on a following clip.** Recommended: refused in the first cut ("switch Follow Tempo off first"); processing the source and keeping Follow is a later option.
6. **If slice 0 finds today's WSOLA not good enough**, ship the surface with Beats and Re-Pitch and add Smooth when its replacement lands, or wait. Recommended: ship; the maintainer's case is Beats.
7. **A following clip's start follows the grid on every track**, overriding a samples timebase (a time-locked clip excepted), knowing that the track's automation on a samples-timebase track stays at its samples and so separates from the clip on a tempo change. Recommended: yes; the grid is one structure, and a clip that follows the tempo but not the bar would drift off it. The alternative is that the track's timebase governs the start too, so a following clip on a samples-timebase track keeps its start sample and its beats drift off the grid.
8. **What a pending Keep Pitch clip plays** past the render-before-play budget or after a change during playback: (a) its Re-Pitch reading, (b) silence, or (c) its last finished render at the old tempo (3.6). Recommended: (a), with a 5 ms crossfade to the render when it lands.
