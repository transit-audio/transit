# ADR 0063: Clip tempo and Follow Tempo

- **Status:** proposed 2026-11-26 (issue #336, milestone v0.8.0; the open questions at the end are for the maintainer).
- **Date:** 2026-11-26
- **Implementation:** not started. Slices in the Slices section, kept as a checklist in #336.
- **Reopen when:** the quality check of slice 0 finds no acceptable Smooth algorithm under the license rule; rendering a following clip takes long enough on the reference machine that a tempo change is heard in Re-Pitch for more than a moment on ordinary loops (the trigger for a real-time stretcher); the render cache's memory budget is exceeded by ordinary sessions; warp markers need a mapping this record fixed differently; or Ableton and Logic are found to agree on a behavior chosen differently here (claims marked unverified in Context).

## Context

The maintainer's case (2026-11-04): a 130 bpm project and a 133 bpm drum loop, and no way to make the loop follow the project tempo. Rulings already made on #336 and binding here: the stretch mode is a property of the clip with Keep Pitch (the default) and Re-Pitch, and Conform to Tempo and Follow Tempo use it without a prompt; Remove Pitch Shift stops being a separate command; the mode lives in the audio clip editor, the clip actions on the right-click menu, with no top-level Clip menu (2026-11-16); no tempo dance: detection happens on import, in the order `acid` tempo, file name, nearest whole-bar count, analysis, and the detected value is shown and can be typed over; the first version users get keeps the pitch; and before the mode is designed, a quality check of the existing pitch-preserving stretch on real material, recorded under `docs/benchmarks`. Standing rules: import makes no audible change the user did not ask for; the arrangement grid is one structure for tempo and meter; no interim surface; no migrations or shims while alpha.

### The code today (read at 5233b087)

- **`Clip::stretch`** (`crates/model/src/clip.rs:176-178`) is documented as "applied by rendered processing" but is a live varispeed: every read goes through `ClipReader::read` at `q = 1 / stretch` (`crates/model/src/disk/read.rs:113-132`), exactly the direct index at a stretch of 1 and the band-limited reader of ADR 0056 decision 8 otherwise, in memory, from the disk ring and offline alike (#604; `crates/mix/src/clips.rs:120-129`). Bounds 1/32 to 32 (`clip.rs:105-110`), a stretch within 1e-9 of 1 stored as 1 (`clip.rs:112-117`). The disk streamer fingerprints it with offset, loop and length (`crates/model/src/disk.rs:141-171`).
- **The inherited commands.** `clip.conform_to_tempo` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/clip_more.rs:223-260`) sets `stretch = source_bpm / bpm` and rescales the length, keeping its memory in `edit.values` keys `clip.bpm.<id>` and `clip.len0.<id>`; it re-pitches. `clip.remove_pitch_shift` (`clip_more.rs:262-281`) renders a stretched clip through WSOLA into a new source. `clip.elastic_properties` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/clip.rs:141-147`, `214-258`) renders a pitch-preserving stretch into a new source only on a track whose `Track::elastic` algorithm is set (`crates/model/src/track.rs:283`, set by `track.elastic`, `crates/engine/src/cmd/track.rs:207-219`), and otherwise stores a varispeed, remembering the original in `elastic.<id>.*` keys; `clip.remove_warp` (`clip.rs:259`) undoes it. `edit.tce_to_timeline` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/edit_more.rs:224`, `749`) is the same render to a selection. All of them go through `render_stretched` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/more_util.rs:143-198`), which sizes its input at up to ten times the output (#544). None of them is on a menu: the reachability gate lists them as undecided (`crates/ui-studio/tests/command_reachability/exceptions/undecided.rs:307-308`, `353-354`), and #728 was rescoped (2026-11-16) to leave them off until this record. `audiosuite.process` also offers `time_stretch` and `pitch_shift` renders (`crates/engine/src/cmd/audiosuite.rs:103-104`).
- **The stretcher.** `transit_dsp::offline::time_stretch` (`crates/dsp/src/offline.rs:231-327`) is a WSOLA with a 40 ms Hann window, half overlap and a 10 ms search on a mono guide shared by all channels, taking one scalar ratio clamped to 0.1..10. Its window is built with the standard library's `cos` (`offline.rs:245`), outside ADR 0055's portable math. The Time Stretcher built-in (`crates/dsp/src/plugins/time_stretcher.rs:1-40`) is a different thing: a port of a granular module's stretch mode as an insert effect, at 32 kHz on int16 frames; it is not a clip stretcher.
- **The transient detector** `detect_transients` (`offline.rs:382`) is already the one engine for the Sampler's Detect (`crates/engine/src/slice_detect.rs:248`, #267) and the clip transient analysis (`crates/engine/src/io.rs:438`).
- **Embedded tempo.** `transit_audio_io::wav_tempo` (`crates/audio-io/src/wav_cues.rs:337-344`) reads the `acid` chunk's tempo from a WAV's bytes. Its one caller, the Sampler's Slice to MIDI (`crates/engine/src/cmd/sampler_acid.rs:38`), reads the whole file and caches by path (#452). No other format's tempo is read.
- **Tempo and positions.** The tempo map holds step changes only (`crates/time/src/tempo.rs:43-46`); the Linear, Parabolic and S-Curve operations write step series at a resolution (`crates/engine/src/cmd/event_more.rs:34-50`). Clip positions are samples; on a tempo change `retime` moves the starts of clips on ticks-timebase tracks only, an audio clip keeping its length, and a MIDI clip musical end to end (edges, fades, envelope, sync point, loop: `crates/engine/src/retime.rs:1-18`, `186-206`). Audio tracks default to the samples timebase (`crates/model/src/track.rs:337`), so today an audio clip does not move at all when the tempo changes.
- **ADR 0056** (audio clip editor, v0.8.0) owns the clip-to-source mapping (`offset` read at clip time `anchor` at the effective rate `q = r / stretch`, decision 5), reserves a clip key `warp: Option<...>` for this record with its own `needs` entry ("absent means decision 5's fixed-rate mapping"), and forbids new timing state in `edit.values`, any other meaning for `warp`, a source-time position anywhere but `offset` and the warp description, and a transpose that changes `stretch` (decision 12). Its slice 1 (`anchor`, the #554 fix) has not landed (`ClipContent::Audio` is still `{ source, offset }`, `clip.rs:85-87`).

### Conventions

From the issue (the maintainer's survey, not re-verified here): Ableton and Logic agree that a clip carries its original tempo, detected and editable; that a toggle makes it follow the project tempo (Live's Warp; Logic's Follow Tempo); and that markers fix the alignment where detection is off. They differ in algorithms (Live's Beats, Tones, Texture, Re-Pitch, Complex; Logic's Slicing, Rhythmic, Monophonic, Polyphonic, Speed) and in where the control lives (Live's clip panel, Logic's region inspector with Flex on the track). Both have a slicing mode and a speed mode among their algorithms. **Not verified, not relied on:** whether either switches following on by default for imported files and on what condition; where Logic stores the original tempo (file or region); whether either offers the toggle in a top-level Edit menu.

## Decision

### 1. Data model

1. **`Clip::tempo: Option<ClipTempo>`**, written only when set, with `ClipTempo { bpm: f64, from: TempoFrom, mode: StretchMode }`. `bpm` is the material's original tempo in quarter notes per minute, in the tempo map's range 5 to 1000 (`tempo.rs`, `valid_bpm`). `from` is `File`, `Name`, `Bars` or `Typed` (decision 2), shown beside the value so a guess reads as a guess. `mode` is `Beats`, `Smooth` or `RePitch`: Keep Pitch is Beats or Smooth (the two pitch-preserving algorithms), Re-Pitch is speed and pitch together; the ruling's two values are kept, with Keep Pitch carrying an algorithm. The mode lives here, not in `warp`, so it survives Follow being switched off, as the ruling's "property of the clip" requires. A `clip_tempo` need when any clip has one (an older build would drop it on save; ADR 0023).
2. **Per clip, not per source.** A split copies it, so pieces agree; two clips of one file may differ (a trimmed region of a longer file). A per-source tempo would make Set Original Tempo change other clips, which neither convention requires.
3. **`Clip::warp: Option<Warp>`**, the key ADR 0056 decision 12.2 reserved, present exactly while the clip follows the project tempo. `Warp { span: Samples, slices: Vec<Samples> }`: `span` is the number of source frames the clip's content covers from `offset` before any loop (what its length would be at rate 1), and `slices` the Beats mode's slice points in source frames, ascending, inside `offset..offset + span`, at most 4,096 (empty in the other modes). A `clip_warp` need when any clip has one; an older build plays the clip at its fixed rate and warns, as decision 12.2 states. The structure is extensible: warp markers (source and destination anchor pairs) join it later.
4. **The follow mapping.** For a following clip, clip time `c` maps to beats `b(c) = (ticks(start + c) − ticks(start)) / 960` through the session tempo map (`samples_to_ticks_f`), and beats to source position `offset + b(c) · 60 · R / bpm` with `R` the source's stored rate. Its length is the timeline span of `span` frames: the first sample at which that position reaches `offset + span`. `offset` keeps its meaning (the source frame at clip time 0); `anchor` stays 0 (decision 1.6). Integer state only in the file; the f64 mapping is derived.
5. **Relation to `stretch` and the inherited commands.** `stretch` stays in the model, read and played as ADR 0056 composes it, so sessions holding a stretch load and sound unchanged; after slice 5 no command writes it. Switching Follow on for a clip with a stretch other than 1 sets `stretch = 1` and `span = round(length / stretch)` in the same undo step: Follow replaces the varispeed it was. `clip.conform_to_tempo`, `clip.remove_pitch_shift`, `clip.elastic_properties`, `clip.remove_warp`, `edit.tce_to_timeline`, `track.elastic` and `Track::elastic` retire in slice 5, with `render_stretched` (which closes #544) and the `elastic.<id>.*`, `clip.bpm.<id>` and `clip.len0.<id>` keys: Follow Tempo in Re-Pitch is Conform to Tempo that keeps following; switching a clip from Re-Pitch to Keep Pitch is Remove Pitch Shift; a per-track Elastic algorithm is the inherited Pro Tools shape the maintainer declined (2026-11-04). Keys already in a session file are left there and ignored (no migration). `audiosuite.process`'s `time_stretch` and `pitch_shift` stay, as offline renders that write a new file.
6. **Transforms.** In the first slices Follow is refused on a clip that is reversed, transposed, fine-tuned or anchored (ADR 0056's transformed clip, decision 4.4), and those transforms are refused on a following clip, each with its reason. Pitch at constant length (ADR 0056's "transpose under warp") is a later slice. Follow is refused on a disk-backed source (ADR 0035) until the cache can stream (slice 7).
7. **No migration.** A clip without `tempo` and `warp` is today's clip, and a session whose clips have neither saves byte-identical.

### 2. Original tempo on import

1. **Order** (the maintainer's): (a) the file's `acid` tempo, from a read of the chunk headers only, through one reader the Sampler's Slice to MIDI also moves onto (fixing #452's first point; the cache keyed by size and modification time, its second); (b) a number in the file name from 60 to 200, decimals allowed, beside "bpm" (any case, with or without a space) or alone in brackets or between separators (`_`, `-`, space), as in "Melody[133]" or "Drums_131_"; when several qualify, the one beside "bpm" wins, else none (ambiguous); (c) the whole-bar count: with `L` the file's length in seconds, the project tempo `T` and the beats per bar `n` at the import position, the bar counts `N` in 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, the candidate `60·N·n / L` nearest `T` in ratio, accepted when it lies within 60 to 200 and within a ratio of 1.25 of `T` (outside that, half and double time are as likely as the guess); (d) analysis, a later slice. The first that gives a value sets `tempo` with its `from`; none leaves `tempo` unset. Only `file.import_audio` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/file.rs:55`) detects; recordings and renders get no tempo in these slices.
2. **The maintainer's case.** Four bars of 133 bpm are 7.218 s; at 130 bpm in 4/4 the candidates are 66.5, 133 and 266, so (c) gives 133.0 (`from: Bars`), within 1.25 of 130.
3. **Follow Tempo is off on import** (recommended, question 1). Detection stores a fact and changes nothing audible: an imported clip renders exactly as its file. The alternatives: on by default for files from a loop-aware place (a file with an `acid` tempo or a tempo in its name) changes what an import sounds like without the user asking, which the standing ruling forbids; a per-import choice is a dialog on every import, a prompt the 2026-11-16 ruling avoided. An opt-in preference ("Imported loops follow the project tempo", off by default) is the user asking and can come later. One click (decision 6) makes the clip follow.
4. **Typed.** Set Original Tempo writes `from: Typed`. Follow on a clip without a tempo runs (a) to (c) first and is refused with "set the original tempo first" when none applies.

### 3. Rendering

1. **Re-Pitch is read live** through ADR 0056's reader on the follow mapping (decision 1.4): within each tempo segment under the clip the rate is the constant `bpm_segment / bpm_clip` (adjusted for the source and session rates, as decision 5.2 is), and the position is the mapping's. No render, no cache.
2. **Keep Pitch is rendered offline into a cache**, never on the audio thread. A render job runs off the control thread on a worker, the way Slice Detect does (`slice_detect.rs:1-20`), and its result joins the session's source pool as a rendered entry (an `Arc` of audio beside the sources, never serialized, never in the undo history), which the published snapshot maps from the clip, so the audio thread does one lookup per clip as it does for sources and reads the entry at rate 1 by clip time.
3. **The key** is a hash over everything the render reads and nothing else: the source id, `offset`, `span`, `bpm`, `mode`, `slices`, the session rate, the tempo segments under the clip as (offset from the clip start in samples, bpm) pairs, and a renderer version. The clip's position is not in it, so duplicates of a loop at constant tempo share one render, and undo or redo finds an earlier render still cached. Entries are evicted least recently used beyond a memory budget (slice 3 measures and sets it), never while the current snapshot maps them.
4. **A tempo change** (any edit to the map, including the step series of a ramp operation) changes the keys of the following clips it touches. Until a new render lands, a clip plays its Re-Pitch reading, so its timing is right at once and its pitch settles when the render arrives; a status line says renders are pending. **Offline paths** (bounce, export, freeze, consolidate) render any missing entry synchronously first, through the same function, and never use the Re-Pitch stand-in, so an export equals the cached live read bit for bit.
5. **Several tempos under one clip**: the renderer takes a monotone piecewise-linear output-to-source position map (one piece per tempo segment), not a scalar ratio; today's `time_stretch` scalar is its one-piece case. The same map serves warp markers later. The tempo map has no continuous ramps, so pieces are exact.
6. **Determinism.** The Smooth renderer computes its window and every transcendental through `transit-math` (ADR 0055). Beats copies source samples and applies linear fades only, so its renders are IEEE-exact.
7. **Undo** restores clip fields only; the cache is derived state, like peaks.
8. **Rejected for now: a real-time stretcher on the audio thread.** It costs CPU per following clip per block, needs pre-roll on every locate and a latency report, is hard to keep block-partition invariant, and the only permissively licensed candidate seen (Signalsmith Stretch, a header-only C++ library, MIT by its published documentation) would need a new FFI crate under the unsafe policy. Loops are short and renders of them are quick, and the Re-Pitch stand-in covers the wait. ADR 0035's streaming is the path for long files (slice 7); the trigger is in Reopen when.

### 4. Beats and Smooth

1. **Beats**: `slices` are detected once by `detect_transients` (the Sampler's detector, one engine) when Beats is chosen, and stored, so the render is reproducible on every machine and the slices are the seed of warp markers; Re-detect is an explicit action. Each slice plays at rate 1 from the sample nearest its beat position under the follow mapping. A slice longer than the time to the next slice is trimmed there with a 2 ms linear fade-out; a shorter one ends with the same fade and silence fills the gap (Trim). Filling the gap by looping the slice tail is a later option. The first slice starts at `offset` whether or not a transient is there.
2. **Smooth**: the pitch-preserving stretch slice 0 chooses (today's WSOLA as it is, an improved WSOLA, or another algorithm under the license rule, MIT, Apache, BSD or ISC only), taking the position map of 3.5.
3. **Default mode when `tempo` is first set**: Beats when the detector finds at least two transients per beat on average over the clip, else Smooth (question 4). Until Beats lands, Smooth.
4. **Beats ships in the first user-visible cut.** The maintainer's case is a drum loop, which a WSOLA smears; Beats is the transient-exact answer, IEEE-exact, and simpler than a better Smooth. The surface (decision 6) appears only when Beats, Smooth and Re-Pitch all work.

### 5. Edits on a following clip

All follow from decision 1: material is an integer source span, positions are musical.

| Edit | Rule | Issues |
|---|---|---|
| Tempo change | A following clip is musical end to end, as a MIDI clip is in `retime` (`retime.rs:186-206`): start, length, fades, gain envelope, sync point and loop edges re-placed by ticks on any track, whatever its timebase; a time-locked clip keeps its start | question 7 |
| Split, start trim | The split point's source frame `f = offset + round(b(t)·60·R/bpm)`; left `span = f − offset`, right `offset = f`, `span` the rest, slices partitioned; the right piece starts at the split and its end is derived from its `span` (within one sample of the whole's end). The source is partitioned exactly; each piece renders on its own, so a Smooth seam is continuous in material and time but not bit-identical to the whole, and a Beats slice straddling the seam is cut there. Re-Pitch pieces read the same positions as the whole to within the mapping's rounding | #554 left to ADR 0056 slice 1: following clips use neither `stretch` nor `anchor` |
| End trim | Changes `span` | |
| Heal, Join | Pieces merge only when same source, same `tempo` (all three fields), right `offset` equal to left `offset + span`, slices compatible, and both following or neither; then `span` adds | fixes #578's stretch case for following clips; its mute and envelope cases stay open |
| Loop | Loop edges stay in clip time (ADR 0019) and are re-placed by ticks on a tempo change; each cycle starts at the sample nearest its tick, as the maintainer ruled for #427, and plays the render of the loop's range from its start | fixes #427 for following clips; unwarped loops stay #427's own amendment to ADR 0019 |
| Fades, gain envelope | Stay in clip time (ADR 0056 decision 12.3); re-placed musically on a tempo change; switching Follow on or off moves no point and clamps fades to the new length; envelope points past the end are kept, silent | |
| Follow off | `length = span`, `offset` kept, `warp` cleared: the file's length and position come back exactly | the issue's gate |
| Bounce, freeze, consolidate, DAWproject stems | Render through the mix, which reads the cache; the new clips have neither field | |
| AudioSuite, clip gain render, clip effects render | Refused on a following clip with "switch Follow Tempo off first" (question 5) | |
| Split to Mono Tracks (#767) | The new clips keep `tempo` and `warp` on their mono sources; Beats keeps the stored slices on every channel, Smooth renders each channel alone (its segment choices no longer shared, said in the help) | |
| Stretch render (#544) | `render_stretched` retires in slice 5 | fixes #544 |

### 6. Surface

Following the shared shape (a clip-panel control; neither convention relies on a top-level menu for it, as far as the issue's survey shows): the audio clip editor's property column gains a **Tempo** group (Original tempo field with its origin, typed over; Follow Tempo toggle; Mode menu: Beats and Smooth under Keep Pitch, then Re-Pitch; Re-detect beside Beats); the clip's right-click menu gains **Follow Tempo** (checked when on) and **Set Original Tempo...**; a following clip's header in the arrangement shows its original tempo. No Edit menu entry and no Clip menu (the 2026-11-16 ruling). The group arrives after ADR 0056 slice 3 (the editor shell) and needs the design hand-off. Nothing is exposed before slice 6; Conform to Tempo never reaches a menu.

## Slices

A checklist in #336, no separate issues. Each lands on its own; slices 1 to 5 are control channel only.

0. **Quality check (docs).** The existing WSOLA on a drum loop, a bass line and a full mix at ±3 % and ±10 to 20 %, with measurements and listening notes under `docs/benchmarks`, and the license-first look at alternatives from their documentation. Decides Smooth's algorithm. Gate: the record exists and names the choice.
1. **Model and Re-Pitch (M, engine).** `ClipTempo`, `Warp { span }`, both needs, `clip.set_tempo {clips, bpm}`, `clip.follow_tempo {clips, on}`, `clip.tempo_mode {clips, mode}`, the follow mapping, the live Re-Pitch read in memory, musical retime of following clips, split, trims, heal and Follow off on `span`, the refusals of 1.6 and 5. Gates: a four-bar 133 bpm file of synthesized clicks, Follow on in Re-Pitch in a 130 bpm session, plays exactly four bars at 130 with each click's peak within one sample of its beat; a tempo change re-places it; Follow off restores `offset` and `length` exactly; a session without the fields saves byte-identical and loads unchanged; split then heal restores the clip's fields.
2. **Detection on import (S, engine and audio-io).** The header-only `acid` read shared with the Sampler (#452), the name rule, the whole-bar rule, `from`. Gates: table tests for the name rule (including names it must refuse); an `acid` fixture; the 133-in-130 case gives 133 from `Bars`; an imported clip renders bit-identical to its file (no audible change).
3. **Cache and Beats (L, engine and mix).** The worker, the rendered entries in the pool, the key, eviction and its budget, the Re-Pitch stand-in, offline renders waiting for entries, Beats with stored slices and Re-detect. Gates: Beats keeps every click sample-exact on its beat in the 133-in-130 case (pinned hash allowed: copies and linear fades only, proven on Linux before review); a tempo change makes a new entry and undo reuses the old one; an export equals the live read bit for bit; the no-allocation callback test with a following clip playing; two tempos under one clip place every click on its beat.
4. **Smooth (M, dsp).** The algorithm slice 0 chose, on the position map, through `transit-math`. Gates: in-process comparisons only (no pinned hash: the window is transcendental); each click of the 133-in-130 case within the stretcher's stated tolerance of its beat; the one-piece map equals the scalar stretch it replaces.
5. **Retirement (S, engine).** The commands, field and keys of decision 1.5, `render_stretched` (#544 closed), their reachability exceptions. Gate: the catalog no longer lists them; a session holding a `stretch` or the old keys loads and plays as before.
6. **Surface (M, UI; after ADR 0056 slice 3 and the hand-off).** Decision 6, help entries, user guide, the README features. Gate: UI tests for the group and both menu entries; the reachability gate passes without exceptions for the new commands.
7. **Later.** Warp markers; tempo analysis (detection step d); pitch at constant length on a following clip; Follow on disk-backed sources through the streamer; a real-time stretcher if the trigger fires; the opt-in import preference; recordings carrying the session tempo.

## Consequences

- A loop follows the project tempo with its pitch kept, from one click, and switching Follow off gives the file back exactly.
- Following clips are musical: they move with the grid on a tempo change even on a samples-timebase track.
- After a tempo change a Keep Pitch clip is briefly heard re-pitched while its render runs; exports never are.
- The inherited stretch commands and the per-track Elastic algorithm go; `stretch` remains as a read-only legacy until a later record retires it.
- Renders cost memory; long files cannot follow until slice 7.

## Alternatives rejected

- **The tempo on the source:** couples clips that the user may want to differ, and makes a split or a trimmed region inherit a value that may not fit it.
- **Follow as a fixed-rate `stretch` rewritten on every tempo change** (Conform to Tempo kept running): re-pitches, cannot hold two tempos under one clip, and keeps hidden state in `edit.values`, which ADR 0056 forbids.
- **The render as a new source in the document** (today's `render_stretched`): destructive by bookkeeping, saved files multiply, and toggling off depends on remembered keys.
- **A render keyed on the clip id:** duplicates re-render and undo loses the render.
- **Beats detected afresh on every render:** a render could differ between machines (the detector's floating point), and markers would have nothing to start from.
- **Rubber Band and other GPL or LGPL stretchers:** license.

## Open questions for the maintainer

1. **Follow Tempo on import.** Recommended: off for every import; an opt-in preference later.
2. **The whole-bar rule's bounds** (bar counts 1 to 32 in powers of two, 60 to 200 bpm, within a ratio of 1.25 of the project tempo). Recommended as stated.
3. **Retire `edit.tce_to_timeline` and `track.elastic` too**, beyond the commands the issue names. Recommended: yes; both are the same inherited family and render through `render_stretched`.
4. **Default mode by detection** (Beats at two or more transients per beat on average, else Smooth). Recommended as stated, the threshold checked on slice 0's material.
5. **AudioSuite and the clip renders on a following clip.** Recommended: refused in the first cut ("switch Follow Tempo off first"); processing the source span and keeping Follow is a later option.
6. **If slice 0 finds today's WSOLA not good enough**, ship the surface with Beats and Re-Pitch and add Smooth when its replacement lands, or wait. Recommended: ship; the maintainer's case is Beats.
7. **A following clip's start follows the grid on every track**, overriding a samples timebase (a time-locked clip excepted). Recommended: yes; the grid is one structure, and a clip that follows the tempo but not the bar would drift off it.
