# ADR 0057: judging rounds of the first draft

The round notes of [ADR 0057](../0057-midi-devices-chord-arpeggiator-humanize.md) as written after each judging round on 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13, moved out of the record on 2026-11-26 so that the record reads as a contract. Section numbers, slice names and open question numbers are those of the draft at the time; the open questions were decided by the maintainer on 2026-11-13 (the record's "Decided by the maintainer" section), and the revision of 2026-11-26 changed some of the text these notes describe (the record's "Changed since the first draft").

The draft's open questions, as they stood when the maintainer answered them:

1. **The chord device's name.** Recommendation: Chord (Ableton's name; the modes say the rest). Alternatives: Chord Hold, Chorder.
2. **Interval slots and the default.** Recommendation: six slots (Ableton's number), defaulting to a major triad (+4, +7) so a new Chord is heard at once.
3. **Chord memory in the first slice?** Recommendation: no; Intervals and Hold first (C1), Single and Multi Trigger with Learn as C2, so the three builders start together.
4. **Humanize early notes.** Recommendation: late-only now (no latency path exists); centered timing with reported latency as H2 when you want it.
5. **The Arpeggiator while stopped.** Recommendation: it runs from live input without a button, because finding ideas happens with the transport stopped; Logic stops unless its own Play button is pressed, Ableton runs (from memory).
6. **The first note while playing.** Waiting for the next grid step delays the first note by up to one step (125 ms at 1/16 and 120 BPM); sounding at once and joining the grid at the next step is the alternative. Neither workstation's behavior was verified. Recommendation: wait for the grid (a pattern that starts on the grid stays on it, and a bounce matches playback); revisit with a snap setting like Logic's Input Snap if it feels late.
7. **Humanize on loop passes.** Recommendation: the same draw every pass (a transit choice, 6.5), re-roll to change; a "new each pass" toggle later if wanted.
8. **Cards in the chain or device windows.** Settled for the first cut by the maintainer (one card per effect at the shared height, 14.1). Recommendation: device windows for MIDI effects as their own later step, if a card proves too small.
9. **Which exploration feature next** from Where this goes. Recommendation: Capture MIDI Effect Output, since a found melody is only useful once it can be kept and edited; then the scale-aware slices.
10. **Stored chord size (C2).** Recommendation: up to 8 keys per stored chord (the played note plus Ableton's six shifts, with one to spare).
11. **Sustain pedal and Hold in the Arpeggiator.** Recommendation: Hold wins (with Hold on a release latches whatever the pedal does); with Hold off the pedal keeps released keys in the pattern until it lifts; turning Hold off with the pedal down hands latched keys to the pedal.
12. **Humanize defaults.** Recommendation: Timing 10 ms, Velocity ±10, Length ±10 %, audible but small.
13. **Seeds and presets.** Recommendation: a preset recall keeps the current seed (a preset is a feel); a fresh seed on every add from the "+" menu.
14. **The MIDI effect host under overload (slice P3b, every chain, not only these devices).** Approve final stage admission with a 384 non-release quota, 512 total slots, and host refusal accounting for every MIDI effect chain? Recommendation: yes; today a full stage buffer evicts an accepted note-on to fit a release, and overload is rare (more than 384 note-ons in one block).
15. **Every chain edit ends the track's sounding notes (slice P3a, every chain, 2.13).** Today adding a later effect sends note-offs for the chain's notes but no pedal-up (the pedal-ups and the other safety controllers go out only after an overflow, `midi_fx.rs:626`), so with the sustain pedal down those notes keep sounding until it lifts, and a note held only by the pedal is not touched; adding the first effect sends nothing at all. Under P3a every edit, the first add included, ends every sounding voice of the track, then releases every pedal the instrument holds so the voices they hold end too, and presses no pedal again; pitch bend, pressure and the wheels are kept. The one consequence you will notice: hold the sustain pedal, edit the chain, keep holding, and the notes you play afterward are not sustained until you lift the pedal and press it again. Recommendation: yes; one rule for every edit, the cut at the moment of an edit you made, and, with an instrument that accepts its releases, nothing left sounding that no later event could end (restoring the held pedal was tried in round 4 and fails three ways, 2.13; under refusal the limits of 2.13 apply, question 19).
16. **A new device when the lane is full (panel design hand-off, open point 2).** Should a device added while the open cards already fill the lane open folded? Recommendation: no; it opens unfolded and is scrolled into view, because the user has just asked for it and a folded card hides what they came to set; folding the others stays the user's choice.
17. **Fold whole cards instead of sections (panel design hand-off).** The hand-off folds any card to a 28-point strip and drops the vertical section collapse inside the MIDI card, which 14.1 had kept as a column toggle. Recommendation: yes; one fold model for every card in the chain, which also answers width at small windows; the section toggles and their view state are retired in K2a.
18. **Stock cards at 200 points (panel design hand-off, open point 1).** The 168-point stock instrument and insert cards gain empty height. Recommendation: keep their content as it is for K2a (top-aligned); filling the height with more rows is its own later design step.
19. **Make ADR 0059 R1 a prerequisite of all three visibility slices, shipping with the remaining §2.13 limits while R2 follows?** Recommendation: yes. (The split of judging round 6, gated in round 7.) The devices' host work is specified for an instrument that accepts what it is given; 2.13 states the refusal and adapter limits (a release past 64 retries lost, no order of a retried release, a lost note-off outliving Stop at a VST3 instrument, an Audio Unit adapter that drops events silently and discards Stop's releases before the format's reset; #600, #602), and ADR 0059 (MIDI event delivery under refusal, proposed) owns the guarantee. Its adapter slice R1 (truthful admission, per-voice release ownership, releases kept through reset, the VST3 controller boundary) touches only the adapters and needs nothing, so waiting for it costs the devices no host work; R2 (the host's release obligations) builds on P3a and P3b and follows. R1 alone does not resolve host releases lost beyond the retry buffer: those remain stated limits until R2. Refusal is not confined to third-party instruments: under this record's rules a built-in instrument can be made to refuse in the block of an edit or a barrier, or right after a locate, near the chain's capacity (2.13, the two loads); the limits are today's behavior for every chain already, and holding three accepted devices for R2 as well would tie them to the part of the concern that failed three rounds in a row.

## Round 1

- **Judging round 1 edits.** One canonical conversion from musical position to frame, absolute and closed-form from the tempo map, replaces the block-relative formula; the wrong citations are corrected (the Random Sequencer's margin is 1e-6, tempo splitting is in `tempo_split.rs`) and the Random Sequencer's own block dependence is recorded as a latent defect. Two clocks replace the single discontinuity test, so stopped blocks no longer look like jumps. Parameters: a host slice puts MIDI effect parameters on a 64-frame timeline grid before the devices are visible, and the promise until then is stated. Note ownership is an explicit state table with the invariant's argument; rejected and flushed notes have their later note-offs consumed. The ledger is the devices' own, bounded, with identity-directed removal and stated saturation. Chord stores the sounding identity per key; the order at one frame is causal; Humanize keeps the release order of a key and adds no overlap its input lacked; its velocity and length draws are symmetric; its full-schedule fallback rejects instead of writing undelayed. The Arpeggiator's octave repetitions follow Logic's documented definition, Offset applies to every style, Converge is Logic's Outside-In, and empty steps, rate changes and the stopped anchor are defined. Pedal, Hold and latch reasons are separate. The prerequisites are honest: #292 is needed and not sufficient; #555's first item, #556, #559's third item (slice P2) and three new host slices (P0, the tempo segment the conversion needs; P1; P3) come before the devices are visible. Chord memory gets its own `needs` marker and a typed, validated field with preset support; Re-roll always changes the seed and is ghosted under automation. The slices are re-cut (host slices, a maintainer-owned integration slice, control-channel-only engines and panels, per-device visibility), and panel acceptance is defined at 1.0 and 1.5 pixels per point.
- **Where the judge was corrected.** The host's `given` lookup cannot mis-pair today, since every note the mix sends carries a fresh voice id; slice P3 covers it with the `out` table, which can. Velocity's rewriting of a velocity-0 note-on is unreachable today, since ingress normalizes it. Block-partition equality is not made unconditional by host streaming: it is promised under a stated capacity bound, with safety above it.
- **Where it shows.** Context (what reaches the chain, time, held notes, parameters, known defects), decision 2, 3.4 to 3.7, decision 4, 5.2 to 5.10, 6.2 to 6.7, decisions 8, 11, 12, 13 and 14.6, the Slices section, the conventions table and open questions 5, 6, 10 to 13.

## Round 2

- **Judging round 2 edits.** Deadlines: the device no longer waits for a block of a deadline's tempo segment (a segment can have none when two changes round to one frame); slice P0 now prepares the whole tempo table with the plan and passes a read-only view with every call, so any position converts at once, bit for bit with the clip conversion; one placement rule (the effective frame, `max(frame(P), cause frame)`, in whichever block contains it) replaces the clamp at a new segment's first frame, so a step and a clip note on a tempo change's tick share a frame; no deadline is past when created, by construction, and a tempo edit while playing is the one stated exception. Tempo ramps: the engine stores none (imports become steps), stated as a limit with its reopen condition. Parameters: the 64-frame grid is anchored at timeline frame 0, lanes are frozen at the stopped position while stopped, stopped locates re-read without flushing, hand moves take effect at the next block start, and split sub-calls are chronological and rebased. Delivery: slice P3 makes a stage buffer's acceptance final and has the host account for every note-on it does not deliver by consuming its release, so a device's tables cannot diverge from what was delivered; the capacity bound now counts non-release events and outstanding notes. Unmatched releases are consumed, never passed; every note-on a device lets through is owned; an identified note-off closes only its own entry; anonymous rejections are recorded in oldest-first order, with fail-closed saturation and recovery at reset. Rate and Swing changes re-anchor at or after the change and after the last step, playing and stopped. Wording: the user guide is shared by section; bounce claims need a matching input history, order and start; a stopped locate does not flush ownership or restart the render clock.
- **Where the judge was followed with a choice.** At or after the change, not strictly after it: a change on a point of the new grid plays that point. The refusal contract chosen is final acceptance plus host accounting, rather than device-side reservations of downstream space, which a stage cannot make for the stages after it.
- **Where it shows.** Context (capacity, time), 2.1, 2.3, 2.4, the table of 2.8 and its argument, decision 4, 5.4, 5.10, 6.5, 6.7, decision 8, the ledger and kit of decision 11, decision 13 (new Arpeggiator rows, ledger cases, tests, host slice evidence, chain test), the Slices table and order, Consequences, open question 14.

## Round 3 (historical; superseded by round 6)

- **Judging round 3 edits.** First insertion: with no chain the mix gives notes to the instrument directly, so the host's tables do not hold them; before the first chain becomes active the mix now ends those voices through its own note table, a refused note-off retried directly and never through the chain; removing the last effect was checked and is sound. Admission: separate limits, a non-release admitted while fewer than 384 non-release events and fewer than 512 events are in the buffer, a release while fewer than 512, so the order of releases and note-ons in a window no longer matters; the capacity bound and the partition argument are re-derived from them. Reset: one barrier for the whole chain, finishing the block as the existing overflow path does, with no note-on delivered after the trigger and no older batch resumed after the reset. Release obligations: a note-off the host owes keeps its delivery-table entry until the instrument accepts it, is given directly and never filtered, with capacity by construction (256 notes plus a 16 × 7 controller table), replacing the 64-entry retry buffer. A Swing change while stopped re-anchors like a Rate change; every other Arpeggiator parameter was checked, playing and stopped, and stated not to move a step. Bar divisions follow meter changes by the `SyncDivision` rule (points on the bar lines of bars 0, `m`, `2m`, counted through meter changes; step length to the next point), with the meter map in P0's table. Found on the way: the Arpeggiator now passes a pedal-up, and the delivery filter applies to note-offs only. Ownership is worded per hop; open question 14 is reworded and open question 15 added. Recorded with this revision, a maintainer decision of 2026-11-12 after the first panel designs: each MIDI effect is its own card in the device chain at one fixed card height shared by every device card (a design-language token, value open), controls in columns, the existing MIDI effects moved onto the same row in K2; Chord and the Sampler's Instrument view share one keyboard display component (held or sounding keys in signal blue).
- **Where the judge was followed with a choice.** Meter changes were defined rather than deferred, because the existing `SyncDivision` rule gives a clean answer with no rounding (every point a bar line, a whole tick). The barrier finishes the block rather than discarding its remainder, because that is the existing overflow path and lets the block's releases end their notes in order; note-ons after the trigger are dropped so that nothing starts which the reset would cut at once. The stopped re-anchor keeps its accepted form (the anchor restarts swing pairs), so a Swing change can move which steps are swung.
- **Where it shows.** Context (capacity; stop, locate, edit, bypass), 2.4, 2.10, 2.12, the table of 2.8 and its argument, decision 4 (P0's meter rows, tempo and meter edits, grids, capacity), 5.4, 5.5, 5.9, decision 13 (two Arpeggiator rows, tests, P3 evidence), decision 14 and Context's panel paragraph (the card row and the keyboard display), the Slices table (P3, K2, panels) and order, Consequences, Alternatives rejected, open questions 8, 14 and 15.

## Round 4 (historical; superseded by round 6)

- **Judging round 4 edits.** Chain edits (new 2.13): every edit, the first add included, ends every voice from before, lifts every pedal the instrument holds (a host table of the instrument's pedal values, kept in `give`), re-presents the physical pedal state to the rebuilt chain at offset 0, and keeps pitch bend, pressure, the wheels and the program; owed controller releases hold a later value of their controller behind them; Context now states that today an edit sends no pedal-up unless it overflowed, and open question 15 is corrected. The host's `out` table has explicit states, Open, Owed and Free: a note-off claims only an Open entry, owed note-offs of one channel and key are given in sequence order and a refused one holds back the later ones, and a same-key note-on is refused behind an owed note-off rather than overtaking it; the record says plainly that retention is bounded but a release is audible only once the instrument accepts it, and that Stop and panic end such a note by resetting the instrument. The two-ledger reset case is now reachable with the specified devices (Humanize at zero amounts into Chord +4 +7, 171 disjoint inputs); the earlier figure of 55 notes, which came from a simplified model that the specified devices cannot reach, is removed. The card row's scope is assigned in full: K2 is split into K2a (the row model, the fixed height, every existing card type adapted, horizontal scrolling, before any new device) and K2b (the three devices' integration and panel hooks), with the dependency graph updated, the relation to the resizable-panes issue stated, and the Sampler's move onto the shared keyboard display assigned to whichever slice extracts it (new 14.7). P3 is re-sized to L. With this revision, input from the panel design hand-off (a proposal the maintainer has not signed off) is recorded as proposed, to be confirmed with the panel images, and K2a is scoped to build to it: one 200-point card height for every card and a 216-point pane as a `deviceChain` token block, widths per device, horizontal folding of every card to a 28-point strip in place of the MIDI card's section collapse, the MIDI FX spine and "+" slot, the card anatomy, and per-device columns and display wells (14.1, 14.2, 14.7; open questions 16 to 18). The capacity bullet now says no event exceeds either admission limit (a buffer may hold exactly 384 or 512).
- **Where the judge was followed with a choice.** A same-key note-on behind an owed note-off is refused and accounted, not held back, because waiting needs storage across blocks and moves the note late, and the mix already drops a re-strike behind a retried release. Owed note-offs of one key are ordered across identified and anonymous entries alike, not anonymous ones only, for instruments that pair by key. At an edit no CC 120, 121 or 123 is sent: note-offs and pedal-ups end every voice from before, and CC 121 would reset the expression the edit keeps. The External Instrument card reflows into columns and the rack's member list scrolls, because the first has a fixed set of rows and the second an unbounded one. Neither workstation's behavior at the insertion of a MIDI effect while notes sound could be cited; the edit rule is stated as an engineering choice.
- **Where it shows.** Context (stop, locate, edit, bypass), 2.4, the table of 2.8, 2.13, 4 (capacity), 5.9, decision 13 (P3 evidence: the `out` states, slot reuse, the reachable two-ledger case, controller state at edits), 14.1 to 14.4, 14.6, 14.7, Where this goes (the step display), the Slices table (P3, K2a, K2b, the engines and panels) and Order, Minimum cut, Consequences, open questions 15 to 18.

## Round 5 (historical; superseded by round 6)

- **Judging round 5 edits.** Chain edits no longer press a pedal again: round 4 re-presented a held pedal to the rebuilt chain and the instrument in the block of the release, and the judge showed three ways that fails (the VST3 adapter keeps one controller point per offset, so up-then-down arrives as down; a refused note-off accepted after the restored pedal leaves the voice sustained, or captured again by sostenuto; a queued restoration survives into the next edit's chain). Now every edit releases every pedal the instrument holds and presses none again: the mix's record of what it sent goes to up and its holders are marked stale, so a pedal held through the edit has no effect until it is released and pressed again (2.13, open question 15). Pedal-ups are obligations like note-offs, offered only after the edit's owed note-offs of their channel, the mix's direct retries included; a later value of a controller whose release is owed, or was accepted in that block, waits as one pending value and is delivered in a later block, never at the same offset; another edit keeps the owed releases and drops the pending values; new notes during the wait are stated. Stop and panic (new 2.14): every instrument adapter's reset now guarantees that the instrument forgets every voice or holds a note-off for each in the adapter's own queue (the CLAP and VST3 hosts clear a key's held flag only once its note-off is admitted, the VST3 host keeps a due mark for note-offs that do not fit, the Audio Unit host keeps its releases through reset), so the host may clear its obligations after that reset; the adapters' part is P3c. The remnants of the section collapse now read as unfolded and folded cards, conditional on open question 17; 2.13 says the Arpeggiator consumes CC 64-down; the keyboard display's two descriptions agree (the played key a reference mark in `text`, sounding and added keys filled in the value color). With this revision, the panel design hand-off's revision 3 is recorded, still as proposed: narrower cards (Chord 680, 512 and 622 by mode, the Arpeggiator 578, Humanize 336), the well rule, Chord's 60-point three-octave keyboard block with Mode, Hold and Range under it, Hold and Seed as fifth rows in the Arpeggiator, Seed in Humanize's Amounts, the shared keyboard component and the revised `deviceChain` tokens; the hand-off does not yet specify the horizontal scroll's behavior, which K2a needs before it is built.
- **Where the judge was followed with a choice.** Restoration was replaced rather than repaired: releasing without pressing again needs no restoration state (only the stale marks and the instrument's pedal table) and removes every failure the judge found, at the cost of a re-press after an edit. A later value waits for the next block rather than the next frame, which is the same at one-frame blocks and also holds for instruments that read a controller once per block. At Stop the adapters were fixed rather than the host keeping its obligations across Stop, because each change is local and a kept obligation would refuse a new strike of its key in the next playback.
- **Where it shows.** 2.4, the table of 2.8, 2.13, 2.14 (new), 5.9, decision 13 (P3 evidence), 14.1 to 14.3, 14.6, 14.7, the Slices table (P3, K2a) and Order, Consequences, open question 15.

## Round 6

- **Judging round 6 edits (the split).** The judge accepted the devices again, the original three round-5 replays (when each adapter block really processes), the stale-holder semantics, the built-in reset paths and every open question, and found five gaps, all in what the host guarantees when an instrument refuses events or an adapter does not process a block: the VST3 adapter can skip a processing call, so a pedal-up and a later press meet at one offset; a second edit must extend a retained pedal-up's prerequisites; pedal-ups the host still owes cannot be cleared at Stop; the VST3 and Audio Unit adapters cannot own two voices of one key losslessly, and the Audio Unit host drops events silently; and clip pedal events delayed by the deferral made renders differ across block partitions. That concern covers every MIDI effect chain and every instrument format, not the three devices, and three rounds of fixes had each added machinery the next round broke. So it moves to its own record, ADR 0059 (MIDI event delivery under refusal, proposed), as the clip gain arithmetic moved from the audio clip editor record to ADR 0058; the five items are stated there as its unresolved work, with what the code does today. This record keeps the host work its devices need, each stated for an instrument that accepts what it is given: the two admission limits with every refusal reported to the device within the call or accounted by the host; the unmatched note-off filter; first insertion ending the voices the mix gave directly; and the chain-edit rule in its simple form (note-offs, then a pedal-up for every pedal the instrument holds, nothing pressed again, stale holders, all at offset 0 of the edit block). Removed with the move: the `out` table's Owed state and its ordering, the refusal of a note-on behind an owed note-off, the owed controller table with its pending later value and the delivery of that value in a later block, another edit's ordering rule, clearing the host's obligations at Stop, the adapters' reset changes (2.14 as written, and the adapter sub-slice), and the claims round 6 refuted with them. Under refusal this record now states today's behavior as known limits (the retry buffer of 64, given directly; no order of a retried release; what ends a lost release's voice per adapter; the VST3 adapter's one point per offset), reproduced in a reference model, with #600 and #602. P3 is re-cut into P3a (chain edits) and P3b (the delivery contract), replacing round 5's P3a, P3b and P3c, and the devices' visibility depended on no slice of ADR 0059 (changed in round 7: every V now waits for ADR 0059's adapter slice R1). Round 6's fifth item no longer arises here: with nothing delayed to a later block, an edit is partition invariant given the same effective edit frame (2.13). New open question 19 (ship with the limits stated: recommended yes). Optional items applied: the instrument sees every pedal as up once it has accepted the pedal-ups; the sentence that an up waits only with an instrument that refuses it went with the waiting itself; "no new state" now names the state (the stale marks and the instrument's pedal table, 2.13 and the round 5 note).
- **Where the judge was followed with a choice.** The record was split rather than repaired a seventh time: every remaining finding concerned refusal, which belongs to every chain and format, and the devices' acceptance should not wait for it. The retry buffer stays as it is (only routed directly, so P3b's delivery filter cannot consume a retried release) rather than being half-replaced, so that today's behavior under refusal stays what #600 and #602 describe until ADR 0059 changes it knowingly. Reproduced before writing (reference model, round 6): the round-5 cases at an accepting instrument, plain and VST3, at first insertion and at a later edit, leave nothing sounding; the two-holders case needs the stale marks; 70 notes with every release refused at an edit lose seven releases past the buffer, and the voices end at Stop at a built-in instrument but not at the VST3 model; a re-press before a retried note-off is accepted sustains the old voice until the next lift; a new press on the edit block's first frame at the VST3 adapter sustains the old voices until the next lift.
- **Where it shows.** The index title, Reopen when, Context's first-insertion sentence, 2.4, the table of 2.8 and its argument, 2.12, 2.13, 2.14, decision 4 (capacity), 5.9, decisions 8 and 11 (slice names), decision 13 (host slice evidence, the chain property test), the Slices table (P3a, P3b, X, V), Order, Why #292, Minimum cut, Consequences, Alternatives rejected, open questions 14, 15 and 19, Related.

## Round 7

- **Judging round 7 edits.** The judge accepted the split and everything else and asked for two corrections of fact and the dependency gate. The adapters are described as they are, separately (2.13, 2.14): the VST3 adapter rebuilds note-offs at reset from a held flag cleared before admission, as demonstrated; the Audio Unit adapter rebuilds nothing: its event push drops at capacity while the trait's default `event` reports success, Stop queues note-offs and a CC 123 and the adapter's `reset` then discards the whole queue and calls the format's reset, so what sounds after Stop depends on the unit's own reset; the unconditional "every voice at every adapter" is removed, and the Audio Unit guarantee is left to ADR 0059's adapter slice R1. The event bound is stated per buffer: each chain buffer holds at most 512 events, and edit releases, retries, safety releases and the clip releases of a locate are additional instrument traffic; counted against the built-in queues (1,024, 257 reserved for releases), an edit, the chain and a barrier sum to 1,200, and two loads make a built-in instrument refuse (corrected in round 8 through the instruments' event conversion: an edit block, 832 events offered, 48 unsupported pedal-ups ignored, 767 queued and 17 CC1 events refused; a locate followed by a barrier block, 1,134 offered, 48 unsupported safety controllers ignored, 1,024 queued and 62 safety releases refused, all 62 retained by an initially empty retry buffer, none lost), so the inference that refusal needs a third-party instrument is removed from 2.13 and open question 19. The gate: ADR 0059's adapter slice R1 is a prerequisite of all three visibility slices (the V row, Order, Minimum cut, Consequences, 2.13); the graph stays acyclic, since R1 needs nothing and R2, which builds on P3a and P3b, gates no V; R1 alone does not resolve host releases lost beyond the retry buffer, which remain stated limits until R2. Open question 19 is reworded to that gate. Optional items applied: the partition property carries the explicit same effective edit frame condition (2.13, the chain property test, the round 6 note); rounds 3 to 5 are marked historical, superseded by round 6; the limits are labeled refusal and adapter limits.
- **Where the judge was followed with a choice.** The bound the judge asked for was computed rather than assumed, and it does not hold: the record now says plainly that a built-in instrument can be made to refuse, and under which loads, instead of narrowing the claim to a case that happens to fit. Reproduced before writing (reference model, round 7): the judge's count, 256 edit note-offs then 384 non-release and 128 release events, is 768 events and refuses one non-release event when the releases come first (767 queued); with 64 pedal-ups added, 832 are offered, 48 CC66/67/69 pedal-ups are ignored, 767 queued and 17 non-release events refused; an edit block whose chain triggers the barrier offers 944 and refuses nothing; the locate case offers 1,134, ignores 48 safety controllers, queues 1,024 and refuses 62 safety releases, all retained by an initially empty retry buffer, none lost (figures corrected in round 8: round 7's model queued the controllers the built-ins ignore).
- **Where it shows.** The index title, Reopen when, 2.13 (the limits' heading and introduction, the per-adapter bullet, the event bound, the partition condition), 2.14, decision 13 (the chain property test), the Slices table (V) and Order, Minimum cut, Consequences, open question 19, the round 3 to 6 notes, Related.

## Round 8

- **Judging round 8 edits.** The judge accepted everything else and asked for three changes. Stop is stated per adapter (2.14): built-in resets end every voice; CLAP requests its format reset before the next processing call; the VST3 adapter keeps admitted releases through reset but resets nothing in the processor, so acceptance at Stop cannot recover a note-off lost earlier whose held flag was cleared, and ADR 0059's adapter slice R1 owns that repair; the unconditional "Stop and panic end every note" is removed. The event-count examples go through the built-in instruments' event conversion (2.13): only CC 1, 64, 120, 121 and 123 become queue events, and CC 66, 67 and 69 are ignored and reported as accepted, so the edit load refuses 17 CC1 events (not 65) and the locate/barrier load refuses 62 safety releases, all retained by an initially empty retry buffer (not 110 with 46 lost); the examples establish built-in refusal, not release loss beyond the retry buffer; the 1,200 sum is stated as independent path maxima, not a jointly reachable load. Chain edits clear P3b's delivery refused table before new-chain events are admitted, with the device/carry reset and the epoch change; old `given` entries stay as epoch tombstones, both release-retry paths are kept, and a plan adoption with an unchanged chain keeps the tables (2.13, P3a, P3b and its evidence). Round 7 had left open whether an edit clears that table; it is decided here, as part of this record's ownership contract rather than ADR 0059's.
- **Where the judge was followed with a choice.** The judge's replacement for example (b) refers to "the stated locate/barrier sequence", which the old example (b) had stated; the sequence is kept as one sentence ahead of the examples, with the conversion rule and its code references, so the replacement still points at something. Reproduced before writing (reference model, round 8): 768 offered, 767 queued, one CC1 refused; 832 offered, 48 ignored, 767 queued, 17 refused; 944 offered, nothing refused; 1,134 offered, 48 ignored, 1,024 queued, 62 safety releases refused, 62 retained, none lost; the anonymous-key replay both ways: with the refused table kept across the edit the new key 72 keeps sounding, with it cleared nothing sounds.

The revision that brought the record to `main` e3ffc964 and wrote in the maintainer's answers was judged NOT SETTLED with six items, and revised the same day:
