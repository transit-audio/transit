# ADR 0059: MIDI event delivery under refusal: one ownership rule, Stop against panic, and the adapters' contract

- **Status:** proposed 2026-11-12 (revised after judging rounds 1 to 5 and settled in round 6 on 2026-11-13; the maintainer answered its eight questions as recommended on 2026-11-13; references brought up to date on 2026-11-26, Changed since the first draft)
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Implementation:** not started. Issues #557, #600 and #602 track the user-visible parts of today's behavior; their mapping to slices is in Choices.
- **Reopen when:** (proposed record) a stuck or over-sustained note is reported with a hosted instrument; a plug-in format is added whose adapter cannot keep the ownership rule; a supported VST3 instrument is found to read only the last parameter point of a processing call, or to need another order at one frame than 3b gives it (Decision 3b); a plug-in's processing errors are reported in ordinary use (the containment silences the instrument until the next Stop, Decision 2); the ownership ledger (Decision 1) is measured refusing starts in ordinary use; a VST3 instrument is measured falling behind, its separated events waiting more than a few frames (Decision 3b); a third-party Audio Unit is found outside the fence's two assumptions (Decision 2); or a source is reported whose same-key anonymous notes overlap across Stop (Decision 2).

## Context

**Where this record comes from.** ADR 0057 (MIDI devices Chord, Arpeggiator, Humanize, proposed) adds three MIDI effects and the host work they need. Its rounds 4 to 6 kept failing on one concern, and each fix added machinery the next round broke: what the host guarantees when an instrument **refuses** events or an adapter loses them. That concern is about every MIDI effect chain and every instrument format, so it was split out here (ADR 0057, round 6), the way the clip gain arithmetic was split from the audio clip editor record into ADR 0058. The first draft of this record carried rounds 3 to 6's machinery (an Owed state with ordering, an owed controller table, pending later values, refreshed prerequisites at a second edit, adapter repairs by Boolean reconstruction). Judging round 1 named the invariant to test first; tested against every replay of the earlier rounds and this one, it removes most of that machinery, and this record is built on it. Round 2's fixes each added a mechanism again (pending pedal points, a same-frame collapse, a recovery protocol, a split safety-controller rule), and round 3 broke every one; round 3 replaced each with a rule the code or the invariant already has (Judging round 3). Round 4 kept those simplifications and found statements stronger than their evidence and one real interaction, between implied releases, VST3 separations and capacity; this revision answers them with one counting rule (Judging round 4).

**Three stages, not one.** An event reaches a plug-in through three stages, and each can fail differently:

1. **Adapter admission:** the plug-in adapter's `Plugin::event` takes the event into its queue or refuses it (returns false "only when the plug-in's own queue refused it", `crates/dsp/src/lib.rs:629` to `633`).
2. **Native delivery:** at an actual processing call the adapter hands the event to the plug-in through the format's API, and the call may be skipped, may fail, or may merge events.
3. **Successful recovery:** at Stop the adapter's `reset` promises an end to every voice; whether it keeps that promise depends on what the format's reset does and on what the adapter kept.

**What the host does with a refusal today.** A release refused inside the MIDI effect host goes to a retry buffer of 64 (`MIDI_FX_RETRY`, `crates/mix/src/midi_fx.rs:63`; `hold_for_retry`, `:545` to `553`), given again at offset 0 of the next block ahead of the chain's events (`:431` to `438`); past 64 it is lost (`retry_overflow`), and nothing orders it against a later note-on of its key (#600). The mix's own table already keeps the rule this record generalizes: "Each open note ... and pedal ... holds one release slot from its admission until its release is *delivered*" (`crates/mix/src/events.rs:10` to `17`); a refused release keeps its entry for a retry (`deliver_off`, `:848` to `868`; `pedal_set`, its retry branch at `:898` to `907`; `retry_releases`, `:931` to `942`).

**What the mix does with a failing instrument today.** An instrument whose output is not finite is taken out until the next engine reset: its block is zeroed, it is marked `failed`, its queue is voided and every note and pedal it held is released at once (`contain_failure`, `crates/mix/src/strip.rs:505` to `517`; the instrument branch, `:224` to `230`; `release_all` and `void`, `events.rs:1090` to `1126`). `release_all` also ends the MIDI effect host's notes, forgets its carries and resets its devices (`panic`, `midi_fx.rs:421`; ADR 0057), and no event reaches a failed instrument (`instrument_in`, `crates/mix/src/events/engine.rs:60` to `72`) until Stop's reset clears the flag (`reset_render_state`, `render_pool.rs:256` to `273`). One gap: `void` returns at once when the mix's own source tables and queue are empty (`busy()`, `events.rs:1119` to `1121`), and `busy()` sees of the MIDI effect host only its retries and carries (`fx.pending()`, `midi_fx.rs:266`), so a device that still owns output (Chord's Hold, an Arpeggiator's scheduled note) is not reset at a containment until Stop. A processing error a plug-in *reports* is only counted and shown: its host puts the failed audio out as silence and it stays in the signal path (`PluginFailures`, `crates/mix/src/lib.rs:301` to `333`; only the CLAP host reports one, `Plugin::process_errors`, `crates/dsp/src/lib.rs:523` to `528`), and the status line says so: "... reported a processing error, so its output is silent while the error lasts" (`plugin_error_status`, `crates/ui-studio/src/host.rs:201` to `206`, written after the failure message, `host/transport.rs:344` to `355`).

**Stop is not the All MIDI Notes Off action.** The player's Stop calls `live.reset()` and its All MIDI Notes Off action (the panic) calls `live.all_notes_off()` (`crates/playback/src/player/audio.rs:538` to `556`). `MixEngine::reset` reaches `reset_render_state`, which runs `release_all`, the instrument's `all_notes_off` and its `reset` (`crates/mix/src/render_pool.rs:256` to `273`); `MixEngine::all_notes_off` reaches `release_instrument`, which runs `release_all` and `all_notes_off` without `reset` (`render_pool.rs:276` to `283`), on purpose: "Unlike `reset`, this leaves plugin state ... alone, so queued releases still sound out" (`crates/mix/src/lib.rs:1093` to `1096`; pinned by `all_notes_off_releases_every_held_note_without_resetting`, `crates/mix/src/tests.rs:373`). The render workers keep the distinction (`PENDING_RESET` against `PENDING_NOTES_OFF`, `crates/mix/src/strip_pool.rs:1180` to `1184`). Three doc comments call `reset` "Stop or panic" (`lib.rs:1063`, `render_pool.rs:254`; also `events.rs:37`), which is where the earlier draft's "Stop and panic ... `all_notes_off` and `reset`" came from. Today, after a CLAP note-off is refused (its held flag already cleared) and the host's retry overflows, the panic rebuilds nothing and requests no reset, and the voice keeps sounding (reproduced, Replays).

**What ADR 0057 decides, and leaves here.** For an instrument that accepts what it is given, ADR 0057's chain-edit rule ends the track's notes at every edit, gives a pedal-up for every pedal (CC 64, 66, 67, 69) the instrument holds, presses none again, and marks the holders stale; every chain edit clears P3b's delivery refused table before new-chain events are admitted, keeps the old `given` entries as epoch tombstones, preserves both release-retry paths, and a plan adoption with an unchanged chain keeps the tables (ADR 0057, P3a and P3b). Under refusal it states today's limits with a pointer here. The devices' visibility waits for this record's slice R1 (ADR 0057, round 7).

## Decision (proposed)

### 1. One invariant: a start reserves its release

**Accepting a note-on or a pedal press reserves durable ownership of its eventual release until native delivery or a proven reset. New starts are refused when ownership storage is exhausted.**

It holds at every layer that can lose an event, from the mix down to the native call, and above all at **every instrument's admission** (built-in queues, the CLAP, VST3 and Audio Unit adapters, the hosted wrapper), which is where today's losses are. The mix's event queue already keeps it (F10, `events.rs:10` to `17`); this record extends the same rule downward instead of adding a second mechanism beside it.

- **One ledger, counted in entries outstanding.** Each instrument instance keeps one fixed ledger of **704 entries**, and admission has one rule: a start is admitted only while fewer than 704 entries are outstanding. An entry is one reservation: a note (channel, key, voice id or none, its native id) or a pedal press (channel, pedal kind), with a sequence number and its stage (start queued, sounding, release queued, release owed after a fault). It stores the start event and, once admitted, the release event, so retained notes and pedal edges need no queue of their own. An entry is held from its start's admission until the end of the processing call that delivers its release and succeeds; nothing else frees it, so explicit releases, the releases a safety controller implies, events a VST3 separation keeps waiting (3b) and events a skipped call keeps (3a) are all the same case: entries still outstanding. **Why 704:** the proof takes ADR 0057's P3a as its premise. While a chain is active, live and clip notes reach the instrument only through it (`send` to `take`, `events.rs:811` to `813`; `midi_fx.rs:261`), so no direct-note population sits beside the chain's 256-note table, and at a chain's first insertion P3a ends the voices the mix gave directly, whose reservations stay until that call is processed: at most 224 notes and 32 pedals, so that block holds at most 256 + 384 = 640. 704 is therefore not an unconditional guarantee of R1 alone on today's base, where no P3a exists. Given P3a: when no event waits past its processing call, the entries alive as a call's block starts are the notes and pedals the instrument still holds, at most 256 notes (ADR 0057's `MIDI_FX_NOTES`) and 64 pedals (sixteen channels by four kinds), 320 in all; every start is a non-release event, so one block adds at most 384 (ADR 0057's non-release limit); a release, explicit or implied (CC 121 releases a channel's four pedals in one event, CC 123 its notes), adds no entry and frees none before the call. So at most 320 + 384 = 704 entries are outstanding in any call at any block partition, and no start inside ADR 0057's bound is refused there. The judge's round-4 stream reaches it exactly: 256 notes and 64 pedals held, then 96 times CC 121 on one channel followed by presses of its four pedals, one event a frame, needs 704 entries at block size 512 (round 3's 576 refused 128 presses there) and 320, 325 and 371 at 1, 7 and 64. The figure was also searched for a counterexample in the reference model: 1,600 stream and partition pairs, random and greedy (a greedy stream starts whenever the window allows and otherwise releases with CC 121 or CC 123), kept inside ADR 0057's bound by a sliding 512-frame window (at most 512 events and 384 non-release events) and at most 256 notes held, at block sizes 1, 7, 64 and 512: the most entries seen was 701 and nothing was refused at 704. **Where the bound does not hold:** at a VST3 instrument whose separated events wait past a call (3b), and after skipped calls (3a), entries are still outstanding as the next block starts, so a start inside ADR 0057's bound can be refused there, at a frame that can differ between partitions; ADR 0057's capacity and partition paragraphs state that exception. The mix's queued direct path (224 notes and 32 pedals open, 256 non-release events and 512 events a queue, `crates/mix/src/events.rs:375` to `381`) keeps at most 256 + 256 = 512 outstanding by the same counting, a **restricted bound**: clip notes reach the instrument through the mix's table at once (`clip_on` and `clip_off`, `events.rs:1144` to `1220`) without the queue's non-release quota, so on that path the ledger itself is the limit, and clips that would keep more than 704 entries outstanding in one call have the excess starts refused (counted), at the block sizes where they share a call (Replays: 481 clip note cycles in one block are all admitted; 800, two a frame, refuse 96 at block size 512 and none at block size 1; both exceed ADR 0057's event bound).
- **Storage for everything admitted, skipped calls included.** A skipped call (3a) keeps every admitted event, and no rule cancels a note whose on and off both wait (a zero-length note can be a drum hit), so the native event list holds the whole ledger at two events per entry, plus the ordinary allowance (today's 1,024-event queues, kept) and the 48 safety slots below: 2 × 704 + 1,024 + 48 = **2,480 events**, which is also the most an instance can keep waiting. Memory per hosted instance, estimated at 48 bytes per entry and per native event (an estimate from the formats' published layouts, not measured in this repository: a VST3 `Event` about 48 bytes, a CLAP note event 40, a MIDI event 24): the ledger 704 × 48 B = 33,792 B and the native list 2,480 × 48 B = 119,040 B, about **149 KiB**; a built-in instrument keeps the ledger and the slots, with no native list. R1 accounts the storage at implementation time (a test states the sizes of the entry and of each native list, and the record's figure is corrected to them). Work: a call hands at most 2,480 events. Inside ADR 0057's bound, a block carries at most 512 host events; implied releases can expand the native delivery beyond 512. The native-event upper bound remains 2,480.
- **Admission of notes.** A note-on is admitted only when an entry is free; otherwise it is refused: `event` returns false at once (the refusal stays synchronous), ADR 0057 P3b's instrument-refusal accounting counts it and consumes its note-off, and nothing else changes. A note-off is matched to an owned entry: **an anonymous one matches the oldest anonymous owned entry of its channel and key not already releasing; an identified release matches only its own identity** (ADR 0057's identity matching). A matched release is **always** admitted into the entry its start reserved, so it can never be refused. A release that matches nothing owned is accepted as nothing: the plug-in never received its start, or its release is already owed. The native translation keeps the matching: the CLAP and VST3 adapters give every entry its own native note id and send its release with that id, so a plug-in that pairs anonymous offs in its own order cannot end the wrong voice. Formats that carry MIDI bytes (Audio Unit, CLAP's MIDI dialect) carry no id, so for two overlapping voices of one channel and key the plug-in chooses which one a note-off ends: the count is kept (two offs for two ons), the order is the plug-in's. A user can hear a different tail on such an overlap, never a hang.
- **Pedals.** For one channel and pedal kind (CC 64, 66, 67, 69), the *latest state* is the class (down at 64 or above, up below) of the last value admitted. A value that crosses into down while the latest state is up is a **press**, a start: it takes a ledger entry or is refused (counted, `event` false). A value that crosses into up while the latest state is down is that press's **release**: always admitted, into the entry its press reserved. A value of the latest state's class is a **value change** (a half-pedal depth): an ordinary event, admitted while the ordinary allowance lasts and owning nothing; refusing one never changes the state. No press cycle is combined with another: several cycles before one call, or through skipped calls, are each an entry, delivered in arrival order (3b). A delivered press never lacks its release: the entry reserves it, and a press cancelled before delivery (the panic, Stop, a unit that is not ready) is cancelled together with its release.
- **Safety controllers.** CC 120 (all sound off) and CC 123 (all notes off) are accepted as a release of every owned voice of their channel, and CC 121 (reset all controllers) as a release of every owned pedal of its channel. Those releases go into their entries, so they are never refused, and `event` returns true. The controller itself is forwarded from a **reserved slot** per channel and safety controller (16 × 3 = 48 slots, fixed), so the first of each kind per channel before a processing call is never refused (admission, not order: at VST3 the controller and its implied pedal releases are ordered against the frame's other events by 3b's rule): CC 121 resets bend, modulation and the channel's other controllers, and CC 120 silences at once, even at a full ordinary allowance. A further one of the same kind and channel before that call is forwarded from the ordinary allowance; inside ADR 0057's bound a call carries at most 512 events against an allowance of 1,024, so that forwarding can be refused only above the bound or after skipped calls. That case is **partial acceptance**: the implied releases are guaranteed and the rest of the controller's effect is best effort; `event` still returns true, the forwarding refusal is counted per instance, and what a user can hear is that one controller's own effect missing (bend and modulation not reset by that CC 121; voices playing their release tails instead of being silenced by that CC 120). `true` for a safety controller means its implied releases are stored, nothing more: no host table records bend or modulation as reset because of it (none does today, and R2 adds none). Today `is_release()` (`crates/dsp/src/event.rs:82` to `90`) counts them as releases, and the MIDI effect host sends a refused one to its 64-entry retry buffer; under this rule none is refused as an obligation.
- **Ordinary events.** Everything else (other controllers, bend, pressure, program changes, pedal value changes) is admitted while the ordinary allowance lasts and refused otherwise, and it owns nothing.
- **Freeing.** An entry is freed only at the end of a processing call that delivered its release and succeeded (Decision 2), at a proven reset (3d), or at the instance's retirement. A start cancelled before native delivery (the panic, Stop, a unit that is not ready) is cancelled together with its release, never the release alone.

What the invariant **must preserve:**

- A note-on reaches the plug-in before its note-off. The entry holds both; its release is never emitted ahead of its own start, whatever offsets the host gave them.
- Time order. Reserved storage gives a release room, not priority: a release is delivered at its own time among the other events, and nothing moves releases ahead of non-releases (a note-off at offset 300 and a CC 1 at offset 10 arrive as the CC 1, then the note-off).
- A release is not silence. Native delivery of a note-off frees its entry; the voice's release tail then sounds as the instrument shapes it, and nothing in this record shortens it except Stop's reset where that reset is proven.

What the invariant **does not solve,** each given its own rule below, as small as possible: the order of events at one frame where a format cannot express it (3b), the timing of events retained through a skipped call (3a), processing calls that report an error (Decision 2's containment), and plug-in reset behavior that its format does not document (3d).

### 2. The three stages and what each guarantees

- **Adapter admission** guarantees the invariant: an owned release is admitted, a start is admitted only with storage for its release, and refusal is reported truthfully (`true` means stored). The Audio Unit adapter overrides `event` so that it reports refusal, and its push no longer drops. The hosted wrapper with no plug-in loaded accepts releases as nothing and refuses starts.
- **Native delivery** guarantees that every admitted release is handed to the plug-in at an actual processing call, in time order, after its start. A skipped call keeps everything owned (3a). What the native API reports is part of delivery:
  - **A processing error is the existing failure containment.** A processing call that reports an error may have applied any part of its events, and the adapter cannot know which: a VST3 `process` that returns an error; a CLAP `process` that returns `CLAP_PROCESS_ERROR`; an Audio Unit `MusicDeviceMIDIEvent` that fails, or a `render` that fails (the two are separate: a start scheduled at offset 10 stays pending in the unit until a render consumes it). Replaying individual events cannot be made safe (round 2: retrying only the releases undoes a fresh press, or ends the wrong voice), and round 2's per-adapter recovery protocol failed as well: it freed the adapter's ownership while the producers upstream still owed their releases, so an old anonymous note-off later ended a new voice of the same key, and at the Audio Unit it sent its note-offs ahead of a start still scheduled (Replays). The mix already has the boundary that discharges the producers, the one it applies to non-finite output (Context), and R1 puts an instrument's reported processing error into it:
    1. The adapter counts the error (`Plugin::process_errors`; R1 adds the count to the VST3 and Audio Unit adapters), zeroes the call's output, and runs its own `all_notes_off`: every entry whose start it handed, in an earlier call or in the failed one (for the Audio Unit: submitted, whatever the submission returned), owes its release; every entry whose start it never handed is cancelled with its release; every pedal whose press it handed owes its release.
    2. The mix, which already reads that count after `process` (`note_errors`, `crates/mix/src/lib.rs:324` to `332`), contains the instrument as for non-finite output: `failed` until the next engine reset (Stop), its queue voided, and `release_all` run **whether or not the mix's own tables and queue hold anything**. That is a change R1 makes for both containment causes: a new `contain` in `events.rs` counts the voided events and always runs `release_all`, so the MIDI effect host ends its `out` notes, forgets its carries, resets every device and moves its `given` entries to the old epoch (`panic` and `end_notes_at`, `midi_fx.rs:421` and `:614` to `653`) at the error, not at Stop: a note Chord's Hold keeps, a note a device generated and a note an Arpeggiator has scheduled are all ended or forgotten then. (`void` keeps its early return for inactivation, which this record does not change.) The producers' releases reach the adapter and match nothing (it already owes them), and until Stop no event reaches the instrument (a failed strip the audio thread holds answers `NoInstrument` at once; a strip a render worker holds can first answer `Queued` and then `NoInstrument` when it is readmitted; either way nothing reaches the failed adapter). What this discharges is everything the chain and its devices own and every release the mix can tell apart: one from another source, one that carries its own identity, a device's note. Through an active MIDI effect chain the old notes' later releases are swallowed by ADR 0057's epoch rule (a release of an old-epoch `given` entry is consumed); that path exists only once ADR 0057's P3b lands, so the claim is conditional on P3b and its test is R2's. **The one limit left is the mix's existing one:** a source's anonymous notes are matched by source, channel and key (`NoteEntry::matches`, `events.rs:457` to `458`; `:662` to `676`), and the match follows the incoming release's identity (an anonymous release compares source, channel and key only, `events.rs:457` to `458`), so if that source's note A was open at the error and, after Stop, the same source sends B on the same key before A's late anonymous note-off arrives, that note-off ends B early, **even when B carries a sender id**, and B's own note-off then matches nothing. Nothing is left sounding; B is cut short. A keyboard cannot produce this (a key is released before it is struck again); a software source that overlaps same-key anonymous notes across Stop can, and the same happens today at every Stop and panic. Considered and rejected: a per-source marker set at Stop or containment so that a release from before it matches nothing. A note-off carries no evidence of which note-on it answers, so the marker must guess that the old note-off will still come; when it does not (it arrived while the instrument was failed and was answered `NoInstrument` before any table saw it, or its port went away), the marker consumes the next note's note-off and that note hangs, which is worse than a shortened note. The limit has a reopen trigger.
    3. At Stop, `reset_render_state` gives `all_notes_off` and `reset` as for every Stop; the first call after it delivers the owed releases, with their native ids (for CLAP after the format's reset has run, which kills all voices), and the adapter frees those entries when that call succeeds. An Audio Unit first gets a **fence**: after the reset it renders, with nothing submitted and its own output zeroed, at least as many frames as the call in which the fault happened (the adapter keeps that frame count), over as many calls as that takes when the calls after Stop are shorter; its queue waits through them as through skipped calls (3a). Keep the outstanding fence horizon across failures and Stop. Credit only successful fence renders against it; a failed render never reduces it. A subsequent fault must not replace an unfinished horizon with a smaller call length. Deliver recovery releases only after that horizon reaches zero. Then the owed note-offs and the channel's CC 123 (from its reserved slot) go to the next call, and recovery counts only when both their submission and that render succeed. The fence rests on two **assumptions**, which Apple documents neither of (`MusicDeviceMIDIEvent`; `AUAudioUnit.reset()`): (A1) every event the adapter submits has an offset inside the render it is submitted for (the adapter clamps offsets to its call and submits per render slice, `au-host/src/plugin.rs:146` to `165`); (A2) a successful render of n frames executes the scheduled events with offsets below n and keeps nothing scheduled from before it beyond what it has not yet reached. Under A1 and A2 a fence of the faulted call's length leaves nothing from before the fault pending, whether or not `AudioUnitReset` drops scheduled events (Replays: a one-frame or seven-frame fence after a 16-frame faulted call left a start pending in round 3's rule, in 12 of 60 combinations; the fence of that length leaves none). R1 checks both assumptions with the fake unit and against Apple's DLS music device (Decision 6). A third-party unit outside them is a stated limit, not a proven recovery: a voice it scheduled before the fault can sound after recovery, and only removing the unit is certain.
    4. A failure in the fence or in that call is contained again the same way, until the next Stop, with the fence horizon kept as above (Replays: a one-frame fence call that fails after a 16-frame faulted call, under round 4's text, set a one-frame fence and left the start sounding). There is no recovery state, no refusal state in the adapter and no storage beyond the ledger: the owed releases are its entries (at most 704 releases and 16 CC 123), within the native list.

    **What a user hears:** at the error the instrument goes silent (its output zeroed from that call), every note and pedal it held is released, and it stays silent until the next Stop, as an instrument that puts out non-finite audio does today; the status line says what happened and what to do: R1 gives an instrument its own message, "<plug-in> on <track> reported a processing error. It stays silent until you press Stop.", in `plugin_error_status` (#284, `crates/ui-studio/src/host.rs:201`), and keeps today's message for an effect insert, whose output does return when the error ends. In the first block after Stop a VST3 or Audio Unit instrument may sound the release tails of the notes it held at the error (CLAP's reset kills them). Today a CLAP instrument that reports an error is silent only for that call; R1 changes that for instruments, not for effect inserts. Silence until Stop is a product choice (open question 4): resuming at the next call needs a recovery that does not trust what the plug-in applied, and round 3 broke the per-adapter one round 2 wrote; a documented destructive reset, such as CLAP's, could be the basis of a later resume rule.
- **Successful recovery** at Stop: the adapter hands every owned release (Decision 4) and then calls the format's reset where one exists. A reset frees ownership only where it is proven (3d); elsewhere the owned releases are delivered after the reset at the next actual call (an Audio Unit after a fault, after the fence). Every native reset's result is checked and a failure counted; a failed or unproven reset is never treated as proof.

### 3. The rules the invariant does not cover

- **3a. Skipped calls (#557).** On every path where an adapter does not call the plug-in (main thread holds the instance, parked, inactive, zero frames), the events it keeps move to offset 0 in arrival order, as parameters already do; the next actual call delivers them first, in arrival order. This fixes timing, not order (order is the entry's, Decision 1): without it the replay of #557 delivers the on and its off at offset 500 in the right order but a call late.
- **3b. Order at one frame: arrival order.** Every adapter delivers each frame's events in arrival order. Round 2's same-frame collapse is withdrawn: with the pedal up, CC 64 = 127, note-off A, CC 64 = 100 at one frame sustains A, and the collapse to note-off A, CC 64 = 100 released it (Replays; the same with CC 66). CLAP (one time-sorted event list, controllers as MIDI events) and the Audio Unit (`MusicDeviceMIDIEvent` in submission order) express that order natively and combine nothing; the supported plug-in behavior is to apply events at one offset in the order given.

  **VST3 is the one format that cannot.** Controllers, the safety controllers among them, reach the plug-in as parameter points (`IParamValueQueue`; the adapter's `control`, `vst3-host/src/plugin.rs:761` to `772`, and `fill`, `ffi/events.rs:153` to `180`), in queues apart from the note events, and the format does not say whether a plug-in applies a frame's points before or after that frame's events. Its one rule: **at VST3, any two events of one channel at one frame whose order changes voice state go on successive frames, served in arrival order.** By definition: a **controller-borne voice event** is a pedal edge of any pedal (a value of CC 64, 66, 67 or 69 that crosses 64: a press or a release), CC 120, 121 or 123, or a pedal release that CC 121 implies (delivered at the controller's position). At one frame of one channel, a controller-borne voice event is separated, in arrival order, from every other voice-affecting event of that channel that is not the same controller: a note event, another pedal's edge, a safety controller, an implied release. No pair of controller-borne voice events is assumed to commute: the MIDI specification lets CC 69 implement other hold functions (freezing envelopes, for one), so a soft-pedal change and a Hold 2 press, or a damper release and a soft-pedal release, can leave different voices in either order, and VST3 gives no order between two parameter queues. Note events among themselves keep their order in the event list, as today, and the values of one controller keep the rule above (a value that does not cross 64 moves nothing). **Service is bounded:** an event that must wait is retained in arrival order and never overtaken by a later arrival, every rendered frame serves at least the first waiting event of each channel, and past a call's end the wait continues at offset 0 of the next call; a waiting event keeps its entry (Decision 1). VST3's own two exceptions to arrival order change no voice: a pedal value that does not cross 64 becomes or replaces its pedal's point at that frame (one point per parameter and offset), and other parameter points (bend, CC 1, ...) keep the format's last value at one offset. The rule depends only on the events and their frames, so it gives the same frames at every block partition while nothing is refused. **Its cost is timing, and under a sustained stream, admission:** an event is one frame late for each separation it waits behind; ordinary streams wait a few frames (Replays: random streams of notes, CC 64 and 66 values and CC 120, 121 and 123 at block sizes 1, 7, 64 and 512 keep the final voice state exact under both reading orders, with the largest delay 6 frames in sparse streams and 40 in dense ones; ADR 0057's own capacity example, 96 times CC 121 with four presses, now waits up to 384 frames at VST3, because each CC 121 is separated from its four implied releases and they from each other, and it still refuses nothing at block sizes 1, 7, 64 and 512). A stream with a separation at every frame falls behind by a frame every frame: the waiting events hold their entries, so the ledger fills and new starts are refused even inside ADR 0057's bound, at frames that can differ between partitions, and the delay is bounded by what an instance can keep waiting, 2,480 events: at most **2,480 rendered frames** (about 52 ms at 48 kHz). The judge's stream of a pedal edge and a note event at every frame needs 604 entries by frame 1,204 at block size 1 (refused nothing at 704 there, 64 starts at block size 512); kept up for 4,000 frames it refuses 1,298 starts at block size 1 and 1,504 at 512, with the largest delay 1,406 frames. A VST3 plug-in that reads only the last point of a call (block rate) sees one value per call and can still miss an edge; that is its stated limit, with a reopen trigger. Rejected: distinct actual processing calls, which split native calls at every such point and still cannot order two things inside one frame. ADR 0057's partition and capacity paragraphs (2.13) state this rule and its exception, amended in its own commit before any device is visible.
- **3c. Processing errors:** the containment of Decision 2.
- **3d. Proven reset.** A reset frees ownership only where it is proven: built-in instruments (their power-on reset, proven by R1's tests, the Texture Synth's restored-state branch included: it clears its queue and held state in that branch); the CLAP reset once it has actually run before a processing call (its documented contract kills all voices); and retirement of the instance (nothing renders it again). Not proven: the Audio Unit reset (Apple documents clearing rendering state, not ending voices) and the VST3 adapter's reset (it calls nothing in the processor). Where the reset is not proven, the owned releases are delivered after it.

### 4. Stop and the All MIDI Notes Off action

The two actions stay distinct, and the simplest correct contract keeps both as the code already routes them, so no caller changes:

- **All MIDI Notes Off (panic)** releases: the host and the mix give their releases (`release_all`), and each instrument's `all_notes_off` gives a note-off for every owned sounding voice and a pedal release for every owned pedal, cancels starts still queued together with their releases, and calls no reset. Under the invariant that is complete for everything the instrument accepted, including a release the host lost: the adapter still owns it. Release tails sound out, as Logic's MIDI panic sends note-offs rather than silencing; no adapter's reset is involved.
- **Stop** does what the panic does, then calls each instrument's `reset`: a proven reset (3d) ends the voices and their tails at once; an unproven one is followed by the owned releases at the next actual call. Stop also ends a containment (Decision 2).

The reset guarantee is Stop's alone. A plug-in that holds a voice the ledger does not own (its own internal fault) is recovered only by Stop's reset, and only where that reset is proven. The doc comments that call `reset` "Stop or panic" are corrected (R2). Tests: R1, per adapter, panic with owned voices and pedals at a full ordinary allowance: every owned note-off and pedal release reaches the fake plug-in, no reset is called, and nothing remains held or sustained; Stop the same, then the reset (CLAP, built-ins) or the releases delivered after it (VST3, Audio Unit). R2, through the mix: the round-1 replay (a refused CLAP note-off, the host's retry overflowing, then the panic) ends the voice.

**The correction to ADR 0057** (applied there in its own commit, one sentence of 2.14 and the words "or panic" in 2.13): "At Stop, the host gives its `out` note-offs, the mix releases its sources (...), then the instrument gets `all_notes_off` and `Plugin::reset` (...), and every device is reset (2.12); the All MIDI Notes Off action gives the same releases and `all_notes_off` without `Plugin::reset` (`release_instrument`, `render_pool.rs:276`), so release tails sound out and no adapter's reset is involved (ADR 0059, 4)."

### 7. What the host keeps, and what R2 may delete

**The mix's retry of refused releases stays.** It is not dead code under R1. With several holders of one pedal, releasing one sends the remaining holders' maximum, which is a press-class value and can be refused; `pedal_set` then keeps the released holder with its retry (`events.rs:875` to `927`). Deleting that retry leaves the holder stale, and the pedal stays down after every physical release (Replays: holders at 127 and 64). Under R1 the direct path (`fx.give`) never refuses an owned note-off or a pedal release (both go into reserved entries), and refuses a pedal value while that pedal is down only at a full ordinary allowance, since that value is now an ordinary value change (Decision 1). Through an active MIDI effect chain (`fx.take`) the events meet ADR 0057 P3b's input admission instead, and that path can refuse them: judging round 3 filled P3b's 512-event chain input with 128 clip note-ons, 128 clip note-offs and 256 CC 1 before a live holder's release, because clip traffic reaches the chain without the mix's queue quotas (a re-strike is charged twice, and a live CC 121 expands into reserved pedal releases plus one charged reset, but clips bypass those quotas), so the remaining holder's press value is refused there and only the retry recovers the holder. That is the reason the retry stays. The alternative (remove a holder at its release whatever is delivered, and owe only the aggregate value per channel and kind, sent again at the next block) is equally correct in the model, but it changes working code for no audible gain. **Deletion criterion:** the retry may be deleted only when tests show that no caller of `send` can refuse (i) an owned note-off, (ii) a pedal release or (iii) a pedal value while that pedal is down. R1's adapter tests must cover `fx.give`. P3b admission tests must cover `fx.take` with the mix's queue full (224 notes and 32 pedals open, the non-release charge used up, re-strikes included) and with clip traffic filling the chain input. Until both exist and pass, the retry stays. R2 adds the holder regression first (holders at 127 and 64, the press value refused, both released: the pedal ends released and no holder remains), before it changes anything nearby.

**The MIDI effect host's 64-entry retry buffer** (`MIDI_FX_RETRY`) retries releases the instrument refused, and nothing else. Under R1 the instrument accepts every release class: an owned note-off or pedal release into its reserved entry, an unowned one as nothing, and the safety controllers as their implied releases (Decision 1). R2 deletes the buffer and its overflow counter only when R1's tests show, for every adapter and for the hosted wrapper with no plug-in, at a full ordinary allowance, that `event` returns true for an owned note-off, an owned pedal release, an unowned release, and CC 120, 121 and 123, and that the owned voices and pedals end. If any of those tests is missing or fails, the buffer stays.

Deleted from this record's first draft, as unnecessary under the invariant: the `out` table's Owed state and its ordering, the refusal of a note-on behind an owed note-off, the owed controller table, the pending later value and its delivery in a later block, the refreshed prerequisites at a second edit, the clearing of host obligations at Stop, and the adapter repairs by Boolean reconstruction. Deleted from round 2's revision: the four pending points per pedal cell, the collapse of one pedal's values at one frame, the per-adapter recovery protocol after a processing error, and the split rule that forwarded safety controllers as ordinary events. Corrected in round 4: the 576-entry ledger (to 704 counted in entries outstanding), the unbounded VST3 separation (to bounded service), and the containment's early return when the mix's tables are empty. What the host keeps is ADR 0057's: the `out` and `given` tables, the delivery refused table (a start the instrument refused has its note-off consumed), the admission limits and the reset barrier, plus the mix's retry above.

## Choices for the maintainer, with their audible costs

Frequency measurements can set priority; they cannot set the correctness contract, which is chosen here.

1. **Full ownership (recommended): R1 and R2.** Fixed storage per instrument and admission limits: above ADR 0057's capacity bound (dense clips, skipped calls), and at a VST3 instrument that falls behind under a sustained stream of separations, a new note or pedal press can be refused (it does not sound or sustain; counted), a release can be delayed by a skipped call, and an instrument that reports a processing error is silent until the next Stop, but no obligation is forgotten. Most of the earlier machinery goes away instead of being added.
2. **R1 only, for now.** The adapters keep the invariant; the MIDI effect host's retry buffer and the legacy API stay, unreachable for releases once R1's tests show it, but still code to maintain. In the narrower R1 the judge assessed (adapter repairs without the release reservation at every admission), host retry overflow and release overtaking remain audible: "Under heavy load, notes can still hang after an edit, and a delayed release can cut off a fresh strike." Under this revision's R1, what a musician can still hit is narrower: a refused new note or pedal press above the capacity bound, refused ordinary controller values, at VST3 an event a frame late for each separation it waits behind (up to 2,480 frames, with refused starts, under a sustained stream of them), a release one processing call late after a skipped call, an instrument silent until Stop after a processing error, and a plug-in whose own reset or voice handling misbehaves holding a note until Stop (or past it where Stop's reset is unproven).
3. **Limits only.** Today's failures remain (a hung note at VST3 and Audio Unit instruments past Stop, lost releases past 64, overtaking); it requires changing ADR 0057's gate (its visibility waits for R1) and a repair deadline.

**Acceptance conditions of this record:** the release that carries R2 is the release that makes the first MIDI device of ADR 0057 visible (decided by the maintainer, 2026-11-13, question 3). **Issue mapping:** #600's first item (chain edits with the pedal down, for an accepting instrument) is ADR 0057's P3a; its second and third items (releases past 64, a note-on overtaking a refused release) are R2's regressions, made unreachable by R1. #602's items (the Audio Unit reset discarding Stop's releases, held flags cleared before admission, the VST3 points at one offset) and #557 are R1's. Each issue closes only when every regression mapped to it passes, #602 including the macOS job's run.

## Questions decided by the maintainer (2026-11-13)

Each was answerable in one line; the cost is what a user can hear. The maintainer answered all eight as recommended on 2026-11-13; the answers are part of the decision.

1. **Scope: full ownership, R1 and R2?** Recommended: yes. Cost: beyond ADR 0057's capacity bound a new note or pedal press can be refused (silent, counted); nothing is forgotten. **Decided: yes.**
2. **The All MIDI Notes Off action stays a release, and the reset is Stop's alone?** Recommended: yes. Cost: a voice the plug-in itself stuck, which the ledger does not own, needs Stop. **Decided: yes.**
3. **R2 ships in the release that makes the first MIDI device visible?** Recommended: yes. Cost of later: none audible; the retry buffer and the legacy API linger as code. **Decided: yes.**
4. **An instrument that reports a processing error is contained until the next Stop, like one that puts out non-finite audio, and the status line says so?** Recommended: yes. Cost: one error silences that instrument until you press Stop (today a CLAP error silences one block). **Decided: yes.**
5. **One ledger of 704 entries outstanding per instrument (about 149 KiB per hosted instance, estimated)?** Recommended: yes. 6. **At VST3, two events at one frame whose order changes voice state go on successive frames, served in arrival order?** Recommended: yes. ## Consequences

- One rule, already kept by the mix's queue, now holds down to the native call: no instrument refuses an owned release, so the MIDI effect host's retry buffer can go once R1's tests show it, and the host needs no ordering of retried releases and no obligations to clear at Stop. The mix's own retry stays until its deletion criterion is met.
- New starts can be refused at one fixed ledger counted in entries outstanding, sized so that ADR 0057's bound holds at every partition whatever releases a block gives, except at a VST3 instrument that falls behind and after skipped calls; that refusal is reported and counted, and it costs a note or a press, never a hang.
- Every event reaches the instrument in arrival order; VST3 alone pays a frame per separation where it cannot express an order, with bounded service.
- A processing error takes the instrument out until Stop through the containment that non-finite output already uses, instead of a recovery protocol.
- The panic and Stop stay distinct: the panic releases with tails, Stop adds the reset, and only proven resets free ownership.
- R1 is larger than the first draft's (the built-ins' shared queue and the hosted wrapper join the adapters), it touches three mix files and one studio file, so it takes its turn in those crates, and it is the only slice the MIDI devices wait for; R2 adds regressions and deletes only what its criteria show unreachable (the legacy API at once).

## Alternatives rejected

- **Keeping the concern inside ADR 0057.** Three rounds in a row added machinery for refusal that the next round broke.
- **Host obligations with ordering (this record's first draft).** Each refused release needed a state, an order against later strikes, a controller table, pending values and a refreshed barrier at the next edit, and still could not recover what an adapter dropped. Reserving the release at the start makes refusal of a release impossible instead of managing it.
- **Boolean held flags with reconstruction at reset.** One flag per key cannot own two voices of one key, and a dropped event cannot be rebuilt.
- **A reset at every plan replacement.** It would cut unchanged voices; unchanged slots move with their ledger instead.
- **Distinct processing calls for controller separation.** Splits native calls for a gain limited to block-rate plug-ins, and cannot order two things inside one frame (3b).
- **Making the panic reset.** It would change an action that is deliberately a release (tested today) and cut tails, while the ledger already makes the release complete.
- **A per-adapter recovery protocol after a processing error (round 2).** It freed ownership while the producers still owed releases, so an old anonymous note-off ended a new voice, and at the Audio Unit it sent note-offs ahead of a start still scheduled. The existing containment discharges the producers and needs no protocol.
- **Pending pedal points per cell with a same-frame collapse (round 2).** The four points put a partition-dependent limit inside ADR 0057's bound, continuous values used them up, and the collapse released a note that a press before its note-off should have sustained. Ledger entries and arrival order replace both.
- **Forwarding safety controllers as ordinary events (round 2).** A full allowance left bend and modulation unreset and a CC 120 unsilenced while `event` reported success; a reserved slot per channel and controller forwards the first of each kind per call.
- **Resuming an instrument at the next call after a processing error.** It keeps a CLAP error to one silent block. Round 2's version reconstructed what the plug-in might have applied, and round 3 broke it; a version built on a documented destructive reset (CLAP's kills all voices) may be possible but is not designed here. Silence until Stop is the product choice for now (open question 4), with a reopen trigger.
- **Separating VST3 events with a fixed delay limit and the plug-in's own order past it.** It would refuse no start inside the bound, but past the limit the order of a note against a pedal edge would silently become the plug-in's; bounded service keeps the order and makes the cost visible (counted refusals). Round 4 also considered a second, smaller limit on waiting events; one counting rule was preferred.
- **A per-source marker for releases from before Stop or a containment.** It would remove the same-source anonymous limit, but a note-off carries no evidence of which note-on it answers, and a marker whose old note-off never comes eats the next note's note-off and hangs it (Decision 2).
