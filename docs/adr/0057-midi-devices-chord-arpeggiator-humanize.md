# ADR 0057: MIDI devices: Chord, Arpeggiator, Humanize

- **Status:** proposed 2026-11-12 (eight judging rounds on 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13; every open question decided by the maintainer on 2026-11-13; revised on 2026-11-26 to today's `main` and to those decisions, Changed since the first draft)
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Implementation:** not started. Slice cards in the Slices section; a contributor can take any slice whose needs have landed.
- **Reopen when:** a session or track key exists (#303, a chord track, or a session key), which the scale-aware later slices follow; hosted MIDI effects arrive and share the slot (#671; decision 16 states what they inherit); the rack stage of ADR 0036 lands (stateful devices then run once per rack, not once per member); a MIDI track's external output gains the effect chain; the tempo map gains true ramps (the prepared tempo table of decision 4 then needs ramp segments with their own closed form); or a reference workstation is found to agree on a behavior this record chose differently; or ADR 0059 (MIDI event delivery under refusal, proposed) is accepted (the refusal and adapter limits of 2.13 then follow it).

## Context

**Why these devices exist.** The maintainer, 2026-11-12: "i want tools that can help artists experiment and find new progressions/melodies/etc...". He asked for more MIDI devices for the MIDI effect slot, "a chord holder -> arpeggiator -> humanizer", to be built by parallel builders. Three consequences shape the record wherever they cost little: every random choice comes from a seed the user can see, keep and re-roll, so an idea found by ear is repeatable and a bounce matches playback; what the chain plays must be something that can be kept and edited (the capture question, decision 9); and the devices that pick pitches should know a key and a scale (decision 10, a later slice with a stated dependency).

**Decided by the maintainer (2026-11-12, fixed here):**

1. The chord device carries the features of both reference workstations: Ableton Live's Chord (an interval stack, each added note with its own velocity), Logic Pro's Chord Trigger (a stored chord assigned to a key, single-chord and multi-chord modes, learned from held keys), and a hold so a chord keeps sounding after release until the next one. It is one device, with modes.
2. Panel process: builders design each panel from the design language ([docs/design/language](../design/language/README.md)) out of existing components, render it through the headless test rig to check the result, and show the maintainer images before the panel lands; a cleanup pass over the views may follow.

**What exists (read on `main` at e3ffc964, 2026-11-26; the first draft read a5fc6898).**

- **The slot and its host** ([ADR 0036](0036-midi-effect-slot.md)). `Track::midi_effects` holds at most eight `MidiEffectSlot { plugin, params, bypass, state, pages }` (and an in-memory `uid`) ahead of the instrument (`crates/model/src/midi_effect.rs:19`). The host (`crates/mix/src/midi_fx.rs`) collects a block's events, then runs them through the effects in chain order right before the instrument processes (`flush`, `midi_fx.rs:409`; `run`, `midi_fx.rs:430`). Each effect gets its input sorted by offset, stably (`sort_by_offset`, `midi_fx.rs:210`); its output is sorted stably again before the next stage (`midi_fx.rs:526`), so an effect may write in any order and only ties keep write order.
- **The trait** (`crates/dsp/src/midi_fx.rs:146`): `info`, `reset` ("forgets every sounding note and any state"), `set_param`, `param`, `set_transport` and `set_sample_rate` (given before every `process`), and `process(input, out, frames)`; three test-only state record methods with defaults (`fingerprint`, `print_complete`, `restore_print`, under the `testing` feature, #742) that a new device may leave at their defaults. There is no latency method and no tempo map view. Events are `TimedEvent { offset: u32, event: PluginEvent { channel, note_id, message } }` (`midi_fx.rs:38`; `crates/dsp/src/event.rs:146`). The contract (`midi_fx.rs:12`): pair every note-on it lets through, pass an unknown release unchanged, never write a note-on of velocity 0. `MidiOut::push` returns whether the event was taken (`midi_fx.rs:74`).
- **What reaches the chain.** Every note the mix gives an instrument carries a fresh per-instance voice id (`deliver_on`, `crates/mix/src/events.rs:824`), and a re-strike from one source arrives as the old voice's note-off then the new voice's note-on (`events.rs:829` to `841`); since #292 the mix counts a source's open note-ons of one key and pairs a live note-off with the oldest (`events.rs:662` to `676`), clip notes keeping their first-off rule. So the chain's input overlaps a key with itself only when two sources play it (two notes with distinct ids), and anonymous notes (`note_id = PluginEvent::NO_ID`) appear only inside the chain, from a generator (the Random Sequencer) or from these devices. Ingress turns a note-on of velocity 0 into a note-off (`crates/playback/src/player/ingress.rs:1226`, `crates/mix/src/events.rs:1302`) and clip velocities are clamped to 1..=127 (`played_note`, `crates/mix/src/midi.rs:264`), so no velocity-0 note-on reaches the chain from the mix.
- **Capacity.** A block takes at most 512 events into the chain and each effect writes at most as many (`MIDI_FX_BLOCK`, `crates/mix/src/midi_fx.rs:56`); an event written at or past `frames` waits in that stage's carry of 64 (`MIDI_FX_CARRY`, `midi_fx.rs:58`); the host tracks 256 sounding notes (`MIDI_FX_NOTES`). The host's output sink accepts every release: when full it evicts the newest pending note-on, and with only releases left it coalesces them into channel safety releases, which end in a host reset (`crates/mix/src/midi_fx/output.rs:101` to `160`; `end_notes_at` with `overflow`, `midi_fx.rs:614`); so `MidiOut::push` refuses a release only in a fixed buffer (tests, a future hosted effect). Two refusals are never reported to the effect that wrote the note: the sink can accept a note-on (`push` returns true) and evict it later in the block for a release, keeping the note's later note-off; and `deliver` refuses a note-on when its 256-note `out` table is full (`midi_fx.rs:575` to `582`) but forwards that note's note-off later. A device that counts the note as sounding then owns a note-off for a note the instrument never received. A release the instrument refuses waits in a retry buffer of 64 (`MIDI_FX_RETRY`, `midi_fx.rs:63`) and is given again through `deliver` at the next block (`midi_fx.rs:431` to `438`); past 64 it is lost and counted (`retry_overflow`); and `end_notes_at` clears a note's `out` entry before it gives that note's note-off (`midi_fx.rs:614` to `624`). The built-in instruments take 1,024 events a block (`crates/dsp/src/plugins/instrument_queue.rs:8`) and the CLAP host keeps entries reserved for releases, so a refused release is rare today. The Random Sequencer keeps its own schedule and writes only events due in the block, so it never uses the carry (ADR 0036, note of 2026-10-31); these devices do the same.
- **Time.** `Transport { playing, recording, tempo, numerator, denominator, samples, seconds, quarters, bar_start, bar }` at the block start (`crates/dsp/src/lib.rs:255`; `samples` is an `i64`), built by `transport_at` from the tempo map (`crates/mix/src/automation.rs:222`). Since the pre-count zone ([ADR 0052](0052-pre-count-zone.md)) positions before bar 1 are negative samples and ticks, so a playing block can have a negative `samples` and `quarters`; the tempo map extends its first tempo and its first meter backward, and a record count-in renders with the transport parked at the record start, not playing (`CountInClick`, `crates/mix/src/lib.rs:877` to `887`). The host renders a block in sub-blocks split at every tempo and meter change (`render_active`, `crates/mix/src/tempo_split.rs:13`, through `TempoMap::render_span`, `crates/time/src/tempo.rs:83`), so the tempo is constant within the block an effect sees. The tempo map holds discrete changes only (`TempoEvent { tick, bpm }`, `tempo.rs:10`): it stores no ramps, and an imported tempo ramp becomes steps at its points (`crates/engine/src/io/dawproject.rs:287`). Changes that round to one frame coalesce into one split, so a tempo segment can have no sub-block of its own (at 8 kHz, changes at ticks 2 and 3 of 800 and 1000 BPM both take effect at frame 2); clip notes are placed by one absolute conversion of their tick (`TempoMap::tick_to_samples`, rounded to nearest, `tempo.rs:232`; used at `crates/mix/src/midi.rs:271` to `272`). A loop wrap ends a block at the loop end and the next starts at the loop start (`crates/playback/src/player/audio.rs:1052` to `1071`). While stopped the render position does not advance (`audio.rs:1175` to `1178`), so every stopped block reports the same `samples`. The Random Sequencer detects a jump by comparing `samples` with where the last block ended (`crates/random-sequencer/src/sequencer.rs:415`) and turns beats into offsets relative to the block, `ceil((beat - quarters) / beats_per_sample - 1e-6)` (`SAMPLE_EPSILON`, `sequencer.rs:230`; formula at `:423`). That form depends on the block's start: the margin of 1e-6 hides the dependence on every grid tested, but a stored tempo of 119.99999999997999 BPM puts beat 250 at frame 6,000,000 with 512-frame blocks and 6,000,001 with 1-, 7-, 64- and 4,096-frame blocks (a latent defect, to be filed; with a margin of 1e-9 the dependence shows at 120 BPM, beat 308.5: frame 7,404,001 with 64-frame blocks, 7,404,000 with others). `SyncDivision` and the fourteen synced divisions (`DIVISIONS`, 4 bars to 1/16 D, each a whole number of ticks at 960 per quarter) are shared in `crates/instrument-core/src/lfo.rs:33` and `:58`.
- **Held notes today.** Transpose remembers each sounding note's shift by voice id, else by channel and key (`crates/dsp/src/midi_fx/transpose.rs:60`), and a re-strike of a remembered note replaces its entry (`transpose.rs:108`): with no voice ids, on 60, on 60, off, off through Transpose sends the first off transposed and the second untransposed, and the transposed note hangs (#555, first item). The #292 fix (merged) makes the built-in instruments pair a note-off with the oldest open note-on of its key and channel, the convention Ableton and Logic agree on, through a shared `NotePairing` table (`crates/instrument-core/src/pairing.rs`), and the mix's table pair a live source's note-offs oldest-first. It does not settle pairing for the whole engine: the mix's own clip notes keep their first-off rule, Transpose and the MIDI effect host are unchanged (#555's first item is open), and `NotePairing` counts with `u8` per key, drops the oldest open note at 255, forgets older notes when told the key is no longer held, and closes oldest-first only, with no removal by identity. The host's own note tables match the first used slot, not the oldest: `given` (`crates/mix/src/midi_fx.rs:358`, exact by voice id for every event the mix sends, so it cannot mis-pair today) and `out` (`midi_fx.rs:600`, where an anonymous note-off matches whichever same-key slot comes first, identified or not).
- **Latency.** `Plugin::latency` exists for instruments and inserts (`crates/dsp/src/lib.rs:500`); the `MidiEffect` trait has no latency and the host reports none. Nothing can play a note before the time it reaches the slot, so today a device can move notes later, never earlier; slice P4 adds the latency path centered Humanize needs (decision 7).
- **Parameters, presets, automation, MIDI Learn, help.** Parameters are `ParamInfo` values (`crates/dsp/src/lib.rs:89`) stored as an id-to-value map in the slot. The host reads each effect's parameters once per block, at its start, from an automation lane when the track reads one (`MidiFxHost::begin`, `crates/mix/src/midi_fx.rs:329` to `340`; `param_value`, `:222`); lanes use slots 240..247 (`MIDI_EFFECT_SLOT`, `crates/model/src/midi_effect.rs:15`). Pan automation, by contrast, is read on a grid of 64 timeline samples so that playback and bounces agree (`PAN_STEP`, `crates/mix/src/automation.rs:270`, with floor division so negative positions work). Every MIDI effect control can be automated and mapped to a MIDI controller (user guide, Random Sequencer section). Factory presets are recalled from the card's row menu (`apply_midi_preset`, `crates/ui-studio/src/midi_fx_panel.rs:164`, the menu at `:320`; `presets::factory_for`, `crates/ui-studio/src/presets.rs:89`), a recall writing every parameter from the preset or its default (`presets.rs:107`); a `Preset` holds only `plugin`, `version` and numeric `params` (`presets.rs:27`). Factory presets are JSON files under `assets/presets/<device id>/`, listed in `FACTORY` (`presets.rs:40`) and compiled in, so they are immutable; user presets are separate files in the user's configuration folder, listed after the factory ones, and saving over a factory preset makes a user preset. Help entries live in `docs/help/*.toml`, each file listed in `FILES` (`crates/ui-studio/src/help/catalog.rs:14`), with ids in `crates/ui-studio/src/help/ids.rs` (the MIDI effect ids at `:126` to `130`). Commands: `engine.midi_effects`, `mix.midi_effect`, `_remove`, `_move` (within one track), `_bypass`, `_param`, `_params` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/midi_effect.rs:11`). The menu lists `REGISTRY` (`crates/dsp/src/midi_fx.rs`), and an add is accepted for an id `info()` finds there.
- **The panel today.** MIDI effects do not open device windows: the device editor's `Dev` is an `Insert` (`crates/ui-studio/src/device_editor/mod.rs:19`). They are rows in the MIDI Effects card of the device chain, 184 points wide (`CARD_W`, `midi_fx_panel.rs:25`): a row per effect (power for bypass, the name, remove) and a row per parameter, grouped parameters in sections (the first open initially, each toggling independently, `midi_fx_panel.rs:75` to `101`), choices as buttons that list their values, toggles as buttons that flip, the rest as value fields; the Random Sequencer adds a credit line (`crates/ui-studio/src/random_sequencer_panel.rs`). Every effect of the track is stacked in that one card, whose height is the header plus the rows of every effect (`card_height`, `midi_fx_panel.rs:115`), and the Devices pane grows to its tallest card (`pane_height`, `crates/ui-studio/src/devices.rs:200`, folding the instrument, MIDI effects, rack and insert cards over `STOCK_H`, 116, `devices.rs:31`, derived from `PANE_H`, 132, `devices.rs:27`; the Devices pane is sized from that figure at `crates/ui-studio/src/mixer/strip.rs:16`, before the Mixer pane at `:22`), so three devices with several sections each on one track would make a pane well over 1,000 points tall. Insert cards, by contrast, sit side by side in the chain, one per device. The Sampler's Instrument view draws a keyboard under its key map with held keys lit in signal blue (`keys`, `crates/ui-studio/src/sampler_panel/instrument/key_map.rs:648`; design language, Sampler views). The design language has no entry for the MIDI Effects card yet.
- **Recording, freeze, audition.** A MIDI recording captures live input from the ingress path (`crates/engine/src/midi_take.rs`), before the mix and so before the MIDI effects; since ADR 0052 a take keeps what was played during the count-in. Freeze and bounce render the strip offline, so they include the chain (`crates/engine/src/cmd/freeze.rs`). The piano roll's audition sends a note through the player's admitted sources (`Player::note`, `crates/playback/src/player/device.rs:537` to `560`), the same path as live input, so it plays through the chain. Inserts that read notes (the Filter, [ADR 0038](0038-inserts-read-the-tracks-note-stream.md)) see what the instrument accepted, after the chain: an arpeggiated note triggers the Filter's envelope.
- **Stop, locate, edit, bypass.** Stop and panic make the host end every note it started on the instrument, forget carries and call every effect's `reset`; the releases the mix then sends for the chain's input are swallowed (`panic`, `midi_fx.rs:421`; `end_notes_at`, `midi_fx.rs:614`). Adding, removing, moving or bypassing any effect, or a plan whose chain differs, does the same at the next block and makes every stage afresh (`midi_fx.rs:294` to `327`; ADR 0036, "Edits end the chain's notes"). The host can end only the notes that passed it: while a track has no effects, the mix gives its notes to the instrument directly (`InstrumentEvents::send`, `crates/mix/src/events.rs:811`, through `give`, `midi_fx.rs:558`), which fills neither `out` nor `given`, so when the first effect is added the notes then sounding are not ended and their releases go through the new chain. Today's effects pass a release they do not know, so those notes still end; an owning device (2.4) would consume it and leave the note sounding, and so would slice P3b's delivery filter for every effect, which is why P3a ends those notes first. Removing the last effect is the mirror and is sound: the edit ends the notes in `out`, and the host stays active while `given` holds entries, so the later releases are still swallowed. Pedals at an edit: the host sends note-offs only; the pedal-ups and the other safety controllers go out only after an overflow (`end_notes_at`, the `overflow` branch at `midi_fx.rs:626` to `637`). So with the sustain pedal down, an edit today leaves every note it ended sustaining at the instrument until the pedal lifts, and a key released before the edit, held only by the pedal, is not touched at all (no table holds it). The mix tracks pedals per source and gives the instrument the maximum over the holders (`holders` and `sent`, `crates/mix/src/events.rs:510` and `:512`); `sent` is what entered the chain, not what the instrument holds, and the two differ whenever a device consumes a pedal-down (the Arpeggiator, 5.9). A locate or a loop wrap does not reset effects: the mix releases its clip sources (`release_clips`, `crates/mix/src/active.rs:415` to `423`) and keeps live notes sounding (`crates/mix/src/events.rs:41` to `44`). Events sent between blocks (those releases, a pedal-up at Stop) run through the chain at once with `frames = 1` and the previous block's transport (`midi_fx.rs:377` to `381`), which can make a generator start a stray note (#559, third item).
- **Known defects on the note path** (audit of 2026-11-12, issues #556 to #565, plus #555; #556, a clip note left sounding when an edit removed its note-off, is fixed on `main`): a skipped plug-in block can put a note-off before its note-on at a hosted instrument (#557); controllers are delivered after a note-on at one sample (#559, first item); a held-back live note-off can land on its note-on's sample (#559, second item); events between blocks use the previous transport (#559, third item); a rack member can be released by another strike's note-off (#555 follow-up). The devices must not add to these classes; decision 2.9 and the Slices section say which fixes they need.
- **External MIDI out.** A MIDI track's external output and an External Instrument's MIDI out do not go through the chain (ADR 0036, Model and Consequences; [ADR 0022](0022-external-midi-output.md)). These devices cannot drive hardware synths until that changes (Where this goes).
- **Key and scale.** `transit_midi::scale::KeyScale { root, scale }` with 23 scales in Ableton Live 12 and Logic Pro order (`crates/midi/src/scale.rs:101`) exists as a per-clip editing aid, "never applied to MIDI playback or live input" (`ScaleMode`, `scale.rs:109`, stored on the clip, `crates/model/src/clip.rs:151`). There is no session or track key. The Random Sequencer keeps its own Root and Scale parameters.
- **Seeds.** The Random Sequencer's seed is a parameter (0 to 9999, default 1) with a latch that restarts it at every start, locate and loop (`crates/dsp/src/midi_fx/random_sequencer.rs:127`). The Sampler's round robin draws from the patch seed through xorshift32 (`crates/sampler-core/src/selection.rs:18`). `Lfo::draw` is xorshift32 too (`crates/instrument-core/src/lfo.rs:124`).
- **Capture.** Slice to MIDI writes notes as a clip in one undo step (`crates/engine/src/cmd/slice_to_midi.rs`); retrospective capture of live input is issue #330. Nothing captures what the MIDI effect chain plays.

What a humanize is expected to do in a piano roll: move each note's start, velocity and length by a bounded random amount set separately, with small defaults (Logic's Transform preset; its users report ±10 ticks as the useful range, not a source this record relies on).

## Changed since the first draft (revision of 2026-11-26)

The first draft was judged in eight rounds on 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13 and its nineteen questions were answered by the maintainer on 2026-11-13, but it waited on a branch while `main` moved through v0.7.3, v0.7.4 and v0.7.5. This revision brings it to e3ffc964. What changed in the design, and why, is listed here; the decisions themselves are written into the sections named.

**From the maintainer's answers (2026-11-13; the list is under Decided by the maintainer):**

- Chord's six slots start empty (3.2); one Chance per slot in C3 (3.8); Learn in both workstations' ways, in C2 (3.7).
- The Arpeggiator gains a Play control: while stopped it passes notes through unless Play is on (5.4, 5.11); playing a latched key again removes it (5.7).
- Stop stops everything: Stop and the end of a play range reset the devices, and slice P2 brings a record count-in entered while playing under the same reset (2.14, decision 4, 5.11).
- Humanize timing is centered and reported as latency (6.1, 6.3, decision 7); the worked timing values are recomputed (6.4; velocity and length draws unchanged); Timing is not automatable in the first cut. New host slice P4, which keeps the chain's latency out of the note readers' alignment; the first draft's H2 is retired.
- Capture MIDI Effect Output is designed and is a condition of visibility (decision 9, slice CAP), with tick fidelity: positions to the nearest tick in heard time, the clip renderer's order at one tick and its re-strike for same-key overlaps, stated to the user; sample-exact capture is left to its own record.
- Re-roll's undo is stated (6.6).
- ADR 0059's R1 runs in series with this record's mix and studio slices, not beside them; R2 ships in the release of the first visible device (Order).

**From what `main` built since:**

- **#292 merged:** the built-ins pair oldest-first through `NotePairing`, and the mix pairs a live source's note-offs oldest-first. It is no longer a prerequisite; the record's own ledger still does not use `NotePairing` (decision 11).
- **#556 merged:** an edited clip note is released. No longer a prerequisite of the Arpeggiator.

## Decision

### 1. Three devices, their names and identities

| Device | Registry id | Name | Short name | Group in the menu |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chord | `midi_chord` | Chord | Chord | after Velocity |
| Arpeggiator | `midi_arpeggiator` | Arpeggiator | Arp | after Chord |
| Humanize | `midi_humanize` | Humanize | Humanize | after Arpeggiator |

The ids follow `midi_transpose` and `midi_velocity`; the names are the plain names both workstations use (Ableton's Chord and Arpeggiator, Logic's Arpeggiator; Humanize is Logic's Transform preset's name). All three are `transit_dsp::midi_fx::Builtin` values made in place on the audio thread, like the existing built-ins, each sized for its fixed tables.

### 2. The shared note rules (every device, every mode)

1. **The invariant.** Every note-on a device emits is followed by exactly one note-off of the same channel and key, carrying the voice id the note-on carried, written in the same or a later block, at the same offset or later and after it in write order; or the host ends the note for it (Stop, panic, an edit, below). A device never emits a note-off for a note it did not start or pass through: a release that matches nothing it owns is consumed (2.4).
2. **Normalized ingress.** A note-on of velocity 0 is read as a note-off of that key, whatever reaches the slot, and is written as a note-off if passed. None reaches the chain from the mix today (Context); the rule covers upstream effects.
3. **Ownership.** Every input note-on a device receives becomes an entry of its input ledger (decision 11) with an explicit state, or a rejection the ledger records; there is no third way. That includes a note the device passes through unmodified (a key outside Chord's trigger range in C2, the played key with all shifts 0, Humanize at zero amounts): passing a note-on is owning it. Every note the device emits or schedules is an output note with an explicit state. The states and every transition are the table of 2.8; the ledger closes an identified note-off's entry by its voice id only, an anonymous one the oldest anonymous entry of its channel and key (decision 11).
4. **Releases that match nothing are consumed.** A note-off that closes no entry and no rejection record is consumed and counted (`unmatched_releases`), never passed. Such a release cannot belong to a note this device let through, because every note-on it let through has an entry (2.3); the notes that began before the device existed were ended by the host when it was added (an add is an edit, 2.8): those the chain had delivered by the edit's reset, whose releases the host's epoch swallows, and, when the device is the track's first effect, those the mix had given the instrument directly, which slice P3a ends through the mix's own note table before the chain becomes active, so that their later releases send nothing; at every edit the voices a pedal holds end too, since every pedal is released and none is pressed again (2.13); what remains is a release whose note-on never reached this device (refused upstream or at the chain's input) or a stray from a non-conforming upstream effect. Passing it could end a different note downstream: with the Arpeggiator holding key 60 at Octaves 2, a generated 72 sounding from 12000 to its gate at 15000 and a stray note-off of 72 at 13000 would reach the instrument as on, off, off. Consumed, the instrument gets on 72 at 12000 and off at 15000. This departs from the trait's general contract ("pass an unknown release unchanged", `midi_fx.rs:12`), which K2b amends to allow it for effects that own every note-on they let through; the existing effects keep passing.
5. **Velocities written are 1 to 127.** Every scaling rounds half away from zero and clamps to 1..=127.
6. **Keys out of range are dropped, not folded.** A computed key below 0 or above 127 is not played, and nothing is emitted for its release (as Transpose does). Folding by octaves would change a voicing without telling the user.
7. **Identity of what is written.** A note a device adds (a chord's added notes, every arpeggiated note) has `note_id = NO_ID` and the channel of the input note it came from. A note that is the input note moved (Humanize; Chord's played key) keeps its voice id. Where two contributions share one output key (Chord, 3.4), the device stores, per channel and key, the identity of the note that currently sounds, and its re-strike off and final off carry that identity.
8. **The ownership table.** States of an input entry: **Open** (its note-off has not arrived), **Held** (its note-off arrived, and the device keeps its effect alive for a reason of its own: Chord's or the Arpeggiator's Hold, the Arpeggiator's pedal, Humanize's pending release), **Orphaned** (the device ended or cancelled everything it owed the entry before its note-off arrived, so that note-off is consumed) and free. States of an output note: **Pending** (scheduled, not emitted), **Sounding** (its note-on emitted, its note-off not yet), **Ended** (its note-off emitted) and **Cancelled** (dropped while Pending: nothing was ever emitted).

| Event | Chord | Arpeggiator | Humanize |

Why the invariant holds. Each Sounding output note leaves that state by exactly one transition that writes its note-off once and removes it from the device's tables (counting to zero, a scheduled release, a discontinuity, a Hold turn-off, a channel controller), or by a host reset, where the host writes the note-off from its own table of what it delivered and the device forgets the note. Cancelled notes never had a note-on. An Orphaned entry's later note-off is consumed, so no transition can release its outputs a second time; no input note-off is ever passed unless it closes an entry whose note-on was passed, so none can end someone else's note downstream. Ownership is per hop. A note-on counts as emitted only when the device's own output accepted it, and under slice P3b that acceptance is final. A device cannot know what sounded after a later stage transformed, withheld or refused its note, and does not try to: each later device owns what it accepted, by its own ledger, and after the last stage the host owns delivery, accounting for every note-on it does not deliver by consuming that note's release, and giving every note-off it owes, retrying one the instrument refuses (slice P3b). So at every hop the tables equal what that hop's output accepted, and an instrument that accepts the releases it is given gets exactly one note-off for every note-on it accepted. An instrument that refuses releases is outside this guarantee: the limits are stated in 2.13, and ADR 0059 (MIDI event delivery under refusal, proposed) owns the guarantee. Non-note events pass at their offsets (2.10).

9. **The defect classes of Context.** The devices add none of them: they never act between blocks once #559's third item lands (they then see a "no time passes" call, decision 4), never create a same-key overlap their input did not have (Chord and the Arpeggiator never overlap a key with themselves; Humanize keeps only the overlaps its input had, 6.4), never use the host's carry, and pair by their own ledger (decision 11), not by `NotePairing`. They do inherit their input: a note that is never released is a key held forever, which the Arpeggiator would turn into an endless pattern; the one known source of such clip notes (#556, an edit removing a sounding note's note-off) is fixed on `main`. Chord and Humanize with such a note hold it as the instrument would, no worse.
10. **Everything else passes.** Controllers, pitch bend, channel and poly pressure, program changes and the pedals pass at their own offsets, unchanged, except where a decision below says otherwise (the Arpeggiator's sustain pedal-down, 5.9; a pedal-up always passes). Rule 2.4 is about note-offs only: a pedal-up or a channel-mode controller is never consumed for matching nothing. MPE in this engine is a note per channel (there is no per-note expression message other than poly pressure), so per-channel data stays with its channel's notes; poly pressure follows the key the device mapped the note to, as Transpose does. The devices keep the order they are given at one offset, so a clip's controllers still arrive after its note-on at one tick until #559's first item lands.
11. **The order of what a device writes at one frame `f`.** (0) The releases caused by a discontinuity (at offset 0 of its block) or by Hold turned off (at the frame the change takes effect: a block start until slice P1, its grid point after); (1) events scheduled before `f` and due at `f`, in the order they were scheduled; (2) the input events at `f`, in input order, each followed at once by its own consequences, a consequence's note-offs before its note-ons; (3) the Arpeggiator's step at `f`. A note-off is never written ahead of its own note-on: its note-on was written in an earlier frame, or earlier at `f` in (2) or (3). Two same-key inputs at one offset through Chord therefore give on, off, on (the second input's re-strike), never off, on, on. The host's stable sort keeps this order.
12. **Reset.** `reset` forgets the ledger, the rejected counts, every held, latched and scheduled note, and restarts every counter and clock. It is called after the host has already ended every note the chain started (Stop, panic, an edit, a bypass, the safety reset), so a reset never emits anything and no note is ended twice. Every device of the chain is reset at the same barrier (slice P3b); none resumes an older batch afterwards.
13. **Chain edits: voices end, every pedal is released once and none is pressed again, expression is kept (slice P3a).** This applies to every chain edit: the track's first effect, a later add, a removal (the last effect included), a move, a bypass, an un-bypass, and a plan whose chain differs. Everything in this decision is stated for an instrument that accepts what it is given; what happens when it refuses, or an adapter loses an event, is stated at the end, as the refusal and adapter limits. In the block where the new chain is installed, before any of that block's events, the host does four things in this order, all at offset 0.
    1. **End every voice from before.** A note-off for every note in the host's `out` table, and at first insertion for every voice the mix gave directly (through the mix's own note table, slice P3a).
    2. **Release every pedal the instrument holds, after the note-offs.** For each channel and pedal kind (CC 64 sustain, 66 sostenuto, 67 soft, 69 hold 2) whose value at the instrument is 64 or above, a value 0. The host knows those values from a 16 × 4 table it updates in `give` for every pedal controller the instrument accepts (every event to the instrument passes `give`, with or without a chain, `midi_fx.rs:558`; an accepted CC 121 clears its channel). Given after step 1's note-offs, the pedal-ups end every voice a pedal keeps: a key released before the edit whose voice the sustain pedal keeps, and the voices step 1 has just released. Nothing else is sent: no CC 120 (it cuts release tails short), no CC 121 (it would reset the expression kept in step 4), no CC 123 (every voice from before has its own note-off, since slice P3b delivers no untracked note).
    3. **Press nothing again.** The mix's record of the pedal state it sent (`sent`, the maximum over its holders, `events.rs:512`) is set to up for every pedal kind of the track, and every pedal holder of the track is marked stale: a stale holder no longer counts toward the maximum, the values it sends while it stays at 64 or above change nothing, and its own release sends nothing (the pedal is already up); a new press from any source counts as usual and clears that source's stale mark. After the edit the instrument, once it has accepted the pedal-ups, and the rebuilt chain see every pedal as up until the player's next physical pedal movement: a pedal still physically down during the edit has no effect until it is released and pressed again. The rebuilt devices start from `reset`, so every pedal is up for them too: the Arpeggiator treats a released key as not pedal-held until a new press, consistent with the instrument.
    4. **Keep expression.** Pitch bend, channel pressure, the mod wheel (CC 1), every other continuous controller and the program: nothing is sent. They hold no voice, the devices pass them unchanged (2.10), so the instrument already has the position the player's hand holds, and resetting them would make the next note play at a pitch or timbre the wheel is not at. Poly pressure belonged to the ended voices and needs nothing.

    **The delivery tables at an edit.** Every chain edit clears P3b's delivery refused table before new-chain events are admitted, together with the device/carry reset and epoch change. The old `given` entries are kept as epoch tombstones to swallow their later releases, and both release-retry paths (the retry buffer and the mix's direct per-entry retry) are preserved. A plan adoption with an unchanged chain preserves these tables. Without the clear, a stale refusal consumes a new voice's note-off, with an instrument that accepts every event offered: a full `out` table records an anonymous generated key 72 as refused; the edit resets its device, so that refusal's note-off never comes; the rebuilt chain delivers a new anonymous key 72 and its off; oldest-first matching consumes the new off against the stale refusal, and the new voice keeps sounding. With the clear, the new off ends the new voice and nothing is left sounding.

    **After the edit.** Notes played after the edit are not sustained, since the pedals are up; a new press from any source sustains as usual, and another edit applies the same four steps again. Nothing of an edit waits for a later block: its events are all in the edit block at offset 0, and every later event is delivered at its own frame.

    **Block partitions.** An edit is a live action: it takes effect at the start of the block in which it is applied, like a hand-moved parameter or a tempo edit (decision 4), so the frame at which it takes effect depends on the partition. Given that frame, the rule depends only on the state at that frame and delays nothing to a later block, so two partitions that apply an edit at the same frame give the same events at every frame, clip pedal events included (they reach the mix's holders like live pedals, `clip_ctrl`, `events.rs:1222` to `1238`): a clip pedal-down one frame after the edit reaches the instrument on that frame at block sizes 1, 7, 64 and 512. The condition is the same effective edit frame: two partitions that apply the edit at different frames (the starts of different blocks) can leave different sustained state beyond either edit block, as every live edit can. Once ADR 0059's slice R1 lands, every event reaches the instrument at its frame in arrival order; a VST3 instrument, which cannot order a parameter point against a note event at one frame, gets any two events of one channel at one frame whose order changes voice state (note events, pedal edges, CC 120, 121 and 123, as ADR 0059 defines the pairs) on successive frames, served in arrival order, so such an event can arrive one frame late for each separation it waits behind, the same at every partition including one-frame blocks while nothing is refused (ADR 0059, 3b); this property holds with that exception, and with one more: under a sustained stream with such a separation at every frame, a VST3 instrument falls behind by up to 2,480 rendered frames and refuses new starts once its 704 entries are outstanding, at frames that can differ between partitions.

    **Refusal and adapter limits.** ADR 0059 (MIDI event delivery under refusal, proposed) owns what the host guarantees when an instrument refuses events or an adapter loses them. Until its slices land this record keeps today's retry and states what it cannot guarantee. The devices' visibility waits for ADR 0059's adapter slice R1 (Slices); R1 alone does not resolve host releases lost beyond the retry buffer, which remain stated limits here until ADR 0059's host slice R2.
    - *A refused note-off or pedal-up*, at an edit or anywhere in the chain's delivery, goes to the existing retry buffer of 64 (`MIDI_FX_RETRY`, `midi_fx.rs:63`) and is given again at offset 0 of the next block, directly: it passes no device and not the delivery filter (slice P3b), so the filter never consumes a retried release. Past 64 a release is lost and counted (`retry_overflow`): 70 notes and a held sustain pedal at an edit, every release refused in that block, keep 64 and lose the other six note-offs and the pedal-up (#600).
    - *Order is not guaranteed under refusal.* A pedal-up can reach the instrument before a refused note-off of its channel: if the player presses the pedal again before that note-off is accepted, the old voice is sustained until the next lift (#600). A same-key note-on can be delivered ahead of a refused note-off of its key (#600, third item).
    - *A refused direct note-off at first insertion* keeps the mix's own per-entry retry, which is bounded by the mix's entries and so not lost, given to the instrument directly, never through the chain.
    - *What then ends a voice, per adapter.* At built-in and CLAP instruments the voices of a lost note-off or a lost pedal-up end at Stop (their reset ends every voice). At a VST3 instrument a lost pedal-up's voices end at the player's next press and release of that pedal (a stale holder's release sends nothing, Stop sends no further pedal-up, and the adapter's reset keeps only release controllers already admitted and resets nothing in the processor, `crates/vst3-host/src/plugin.rs:1028` to `1034`), and a lost note-off's voice can outlive Stop: the adapter rebuilds note-offs at reset from a held flag per channel and key, which is cleared before the note-off is admitted (`plugin.rs:1188` to `1191`, `:1323` to `1336`), so a refused note-off is not rebuilt (#602). The Audio Unit adapter is different and rebuilds nothing. It never reports a refusal: its event push drops an event silently when its queue is full (`push_event`, `crates/au-host/src/plugin.rs:74` to `79`), the adapter uses the trait's default `event`, which reports success whatever happened (`crates/dsp/src/lib.rs:634` to `646`), and `note_off` clears the key's held flag whether or not the event fit (`plugin.rs:226` to `235`); so the host's retry never sees an Audio Unit refusal, a dropped release is never retried, and the instrument's pedal table records a dropped pedal-up as up. At Stop the adapter queues a note-off for every key still flagged held and a CC 123 (`all_notes_off`, `:244` to `252`), and its `reset` then discards the whole queue, those events and every release the host and the mix gave just before included, clears the held flags and calls the format's reset (`AudioUnitReset`, `:108` to `112`; `crates/au-host/src/ffi.rs:740` to `743`). Whether a voice ends at Stop is therefore what the unit's own reset of its rendering state does, which this record does not assume; short of that reset, a voice whose pedal-up was dropped sounds until the player's next press and release of that pedal, and a voice whose note-off was dropped gets no further note-off from the adapter. The Audio Unit guarantee (truthful admission, releases kept through reset) is the evidence ADR 0059's adapter slice R1 owes (#602).
    - *The VST3 adapter keeps one point per controller and offset* (`crates/vst3-host/src/ffi/events.rs:158` to `193`). A new press that reaches the same VST3 processing call at the same offset as the edit's pedal-up (a press on the edit block's first frame, or after the adapter skipped the edit block's call) arrives as the press alone, and the old voices sustain until the next lift (#602). This happens without any refusal and to every up and down of one controller that meet at one offset, edits or not; ADR 0059 carries it with the processing boundary its fix needs.

    Each chain buffer holds at most 512 events; edit releases, retries and safety releases are additional instrument traffic. Built-in queues hold 1,024 events and reserve 257 for releases (`crates/dsp/src/plugins/instrument_queue.rs:8` and `:11`: a release is admitted while the queue holds fewer than 1,024, any other event while it holds fewer than 767). Under this record's rules no bound keeps a built-in instrument's traffic for one processing call inside its queue. The paths and their maxima: an edit's note-offs (256) and pedal-ups (64: CC 64, 66, 67 and 69 on 16 channels); the chain's delivery (512, at most 384 of them non-release); a reset barrier's note-offs (256) and safety releases (112: CC 64, 66, 67, 69, 120, 121 and 123 on 16 channels); the retry buffer (64); and the clip releases a locate or loop wrap sends between blocks (up to 256 note-offs into the same queue, through `frames = 1` calls). Those independent path maxima sum to 1,200; this is not a demonstrated jointly reachable load. Two loads make a built-in instrument refuse. The locate/barrier sequence: a locate or loop wrap releases 256 chain notes from clips between blocks while 256 refused notes of held live keys fill the refused table; the next block's chain delivers 256 new note-ons, a 257th note-on that finds both tables full and triggers the barrier, 126 CC1 events and 128 CC121 releases; the barrier then gives 256 note-offs and 112 safety controllers. Only CC 1, 64, 120, 121 and 123 become queue events at the built-in instruments; any other controller is ignored and reported as accepted (`crates/dsp/src/plugins/macro_osc.rs:202` to `211` and `:713` to `714`, the same mapping in Modal Voice, Resonator, Texture Synth and the Sampler, `sampler/events.rs:37` to `47`). (a) An edit offers 256 note-offs and 64 pedal-ups, followed by 128 CC121 releases and 384 CC1 events. The current built-ins ignore the 48 CC66/67/69 pedal-ups without queueing them; 767 events are queued and 17 CC1 events are refused. Without the edit's pedal-ups, the 768-event example refuses one CC1 event. (b) The stated locate/barrier sequence offers 1,134 events. The current built-ins ignore 48 of the barrier's safety controllers, queue 1,024 events and refuse 62 safety releases. With an initially empty retry buffer, all 62 are retained for retry and none is lost. These examples establish built-in refusal, but do not establish release loss beyond the retry buffer. So refusal does not need a third-party instrument; it needs a load near the chain's capacity in the block of an edit or a barrier, or right after a locate. The devices ship with these limits stated (decided by the maintainer, item 19).

    **What the user hears.** At an edit every note of the track goes to its release: a key still held down (it stays silent until struck again; its later release sends nothing), a note a pedal holds, a generated note. A pedal held through the edit stops working until it is pressed again: hold the sustain pedal, edit the chain, keep holding, and the notes played afterward are not sustained until you lift the pedal and press it again. The wheels keep their positions. Neither workstation documents what happens to sounding notes or held pedals when a MIDI effect is inserted (no documentation was found), so this is an engineering choice: the simplest rule that, with an instrument that accepts its releases, leaves nothing sounding which no later event could end. Re-presenting the held pedal to the rebuilt chain fails three ways (the VST3 adapter's one point per offset turns up-then-down into down; a refused note-off accepted after the restored pedal leaves the voice sustained, and a restored sostenuto captures the old voice again; a queued restoration survives into the next edit's chain); releasing without restoring removes all three, at the cost of one re-press and two small pieces of state (the instrument's pedal table and the stale marks). Expected results, at an accepting instrument at a plain adapter and at the VST3 adapter's one point per offset (controller points applied before and after the note events of their offset), each at first insertion and at a later edit: CC 64 down, 60 held, an edit, 60 released: nothing sounds from the edit on and the instrument's pedal is up; a note released before the edit and held only by the pedal ends at the edit; with CC 64 and separately CC 66, a lift and a new press after the edit, then the old key released: the old voice is neither sustained nor captured again, and a key struck under the new CC 64 press is sustained until the lift; consecutive edits (an owning pass-through effect added, then a generator holding a key in the next block, a key held into it): no generated voice sounds past its gate, a physical pedal-up during the second edit sends nothing, and after a new press, which the Arpeggiator consumes, the generated notes still end at their gates; two sources holding the pedal through an edit: neither release sends anything and the next press sustains, where setting only the mix's record to up, without stale marks, lets the first release send the second source's down and sustain a note played after it.
14. **Stop and panic: unchanged by this record.** Stop stops everything (decided by the maintainer, D1): after Stop nothing a device generated or holds is sounding, latched and held sets are cleared by `reset` (2.12), and nothing sounds again until a new input note; 5.11 lists the paths that end playing and the one (a count-in entered while playing) that P2 brings under this reset. At Stop, the host gives its `out` note-offs, the mix releases its sources (`release_all`, `events.rs:1092`), then the instrument gets `all_notes_off` and `Plugin::reset` (`reset_render_state`, `crates/mix/src/render_pool.rs:256` to `273`, from `MixEngine::reset`, `crates/mix/src/lib.rs:1068`), and every device is reset (2.12); the All MIDI Notes Off action gives the same releases and `all_notes_off` without `Plugin::reset` (`release_instrument`, `render_pool.rs:276`), so release tails sound out and no adapter's reset is involved (ADR 0059, 4). Built-in resets end every voice; CLAP requests its format reset before the next actual processing call. VST3 preserves admitted releases through reset but resets nothing in the processor: ending every voice requires every necessary note-off and pedal-up to have been retained and delivered. Acceptance of the releases offered at Stop alone is insufficient; a previously lost note-off whose held flag was cleared is not reconstructed. ADR 0059's adapter slice R1 owns that repair. At an Audio Unit instrument nothing on the host's side guarantees it: the adapter's `reset` discards every release queued at Stop, the host's, the mix's and its own CC 123, and calls the format's reset, so the result is whatever the unit's reset does (2.13, "What then ends a voice, per adapter"). What the adapters guarantee at reset, for releases refused (VST3: held flags cleared before admission, no rebuild for a refused note-off) or silently dropped and discarded (Audio Unit), is filed as #602 and belongs to ADR 0059 (MIDI event delivery under refusal, proposed), whose adapter slice R1 comes before the devices are visible (Slices).


### 3. Chord

**3.1 Modes.** `Mode` is Intervals (Ableton's Chord) or, from slice C2, Single Trigger and Multi Trigger (Logic's Chord Trigger). Hold (3.5) applies in every mode.

**3.2 Parameters (slice C1).** All are automatable and MIDI-learnable, like every MIDI effect parameter.

| Id | Name | Range | Default | Unit, taper | Group |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `shift_1` … `shift_6` | Shift 1 … 6 | −36 to +36, whole | 0 | semitones, linear; 0 means off | Intervals |
| `velocity_1` … `velocity_6` | Velocity 1 … 6 | 1 to 200 | 100 | percent of the played note's velocity, linear | Intervals |
| `hold` | Hold | off, on | off | toggle | Hold |

Six slots because Ableton has six and Logic's stored chords are larger only by the played note. Every slot starts empty (decided by the maintainer, item 2): a newly added Chord passes its input through by the counting rule (3.4) and changes nothing until a shift is set; the chords are in the factory presets (3.9).

**3.3 Note rule, Intervals.** For an input note-on (channel `c`, key `k`, velocity `v`), the output set is the played key `k` at `v` (with its voice id) plus, for each slot `i` in order whose shift `s_i` is not 0, key `k + s_i` at `round(v × velocity_i / 100)` clamped to 1..=127, with `NO_ID`. A key out of 0..=127 is dropped (2.6). A key already in this note's set (two slots with one shift) is played once, at the first slot's velocity (Ableton: the same pitch cannot be assigned twice). The entry records its output keys, so its release ends exactly those keys, whatever the shifts are by then.

**3.4 Overlapping outputs.** Per channel and output key the device keeps a count of the entries whose set holds the key and the identity of the note that sounds:

- count 0 to 1: a note-on with the entry's identity for that key (the played key's voice id, else `NO_ID`), stored as the sounding identity;
- count n to n + 1 (the key already sounds from another entry): a note-off with the stored identity, then a note-on with the new entry's identity, which is stored: a re-strike;
- count n + 1 to n (n ≥ 1): nothing (the release is absorbed, the re-struck note sounds on);
- count 1 to 0: a note-off with the stored identity.

So holding C and then playing E with shifts +4 and +7 re-strikes E once, and E keeps sounding until both C and E are released. With shift +4 alone, C (no id) adds an anonymous E; a played E with voice id 42 re-strikes it as off E (anonymous), on E (id 42); releasing E is absorbed; releasing C last writes off E with id 42, the identity that sounds. The invariant holds because each emitted note-on has its note-off either at a re-strike or at the last release. Chord's output never overlaps a key with itself.

**3.5 Hold.** With Hold on, an input note-off makes its entry Held and releases nothing. A note-on that arrives while no input key is down first releases every Held entry (their note-offs at that offset, by the counting rule, as that input's consequences), then plays its own set; a note-on while another key is down adds its set to what sounds. This is the replace rule both arpeggiators agree on (Ableton's Hold: the pattern repeats "until another key is pressed"; Logic's Reset latch: "The first key played clears the currently latched notes"). Turning Hold off releases every Held entry at the frame the change takes effect (2.11). A Stop, an edit or a bypass ends held chords through the host (2.8). Chord does not read the sustain pedal; it passes it.

**3.6 Hold in a chain.** Chord's Hold withholds note-offs, so every device after it sees the held chord as keys still down; no flag passes between devices and neither reads the other's setting. Chained Chord (Hold on) into Arpeggiator, the Arpeggiator keeps arpeggiating the held chord; when the next chord arrives, Chord writes the old chord's note-offs and the new note-ons at one offset as consequences of one input (2.11), so the Arpeggiator sees the old keys released and the new set pressed at that frame, and replaces its pattern by its own rule. The Arpeggiator's own Hold changes nothing while Chord holds. The panel's help says to use Hold on one device.

**3.7 Trigger modes (slice C2).** `mode` becomes a choice (Intervals, Single Trigger, Multi Trigger) and `range_low`, `range_high` (note choices with ADR 0051 labels, defaults C1 and C6 in the C3 = 60 numbering) join; notes outside the range pass unchanged, as Logic's do.

- **Single Trigger:** one stored chord (up to 8 keys, decided by the maintainer, item 10) with its trigger key `t`; an input key `k` in range plays the stored keys each moved by `k − t`, at the input velocity. A stored chord that does not contain the trigger key does not play the trigger key.
- **Multi Trigger:** each key in range may hold its own stored chord, played as stored at the input velocity; a key without a chord is silent (Logic). Up to 128 stored chords of up to 8 keys.
- **Storage.** Chord memory is a typed optional field of the slot, `MidiEffectSlot::chords: Option<ChordMemory>` (absent when empty; the `state` field stays reserved for hosted effects). `ChordMemory` holds up to 128 `{ trigger: u8, keys: [u8] }` with 1 to 8 distinct keys in 0..=127 per chord and distinct triggers; a file whose chord fails these checks loads without that chord and the load reports it (alpha: no repair, no migration). The plan carries the memory by value; the audio side copies it into the device's fixed table (128 × 8 keys) at a block start only when its content hash, computed off the audio thread when the plan is built, differs from the one the device holds, so undo and redo, which restore the slot, are picked up like any edit; nothing is allocated on the audio thread.
- **Learn, two flows (decided by the maintainer, D4).**
  - **What Learn observes.** The device's own input: the note-ons and note-offs that reach its slot, from live input and from playing clips alike (as a MIDI effect's learn sees what reaches it). Learn does not use the recording capture or any per-track journal.
  - **How it is read.** The device records what it learns on the audio thread, in a fixed table of its state (the root or trigger, up to 8 keys, and whether the learn is complete). The panel reads that table the way the display reads the device's sounding keys (14.2). The panel, not the audio thread, writes the result with a command.
  - **Arming.** `learn` is a parameter of Chord: off or on, not automatable (K2b's flag, decision 7), never saved in the session or a preset (it reads off when a session loads), and not an undo step. The Learn button sets it as a hand move, so it takes effect at a block start (decision 4).
  - **At the block where Learn is armed,** before that block's input:
    - Every Held entry is released by counting, as when Hold is turned off.
    - Every pending strum note (C3) is Cancelled.
    - Every Open entry's added notes end: their note-offs are written by counting, so a key another entry still sounds keeps sounding.
    - The Open entry itself becomes a pass-through entry: its played key keeps sounding and ends by its own note-off.
    - So from that block on, only keys still physically down sound, once each, and an old held chord never sounds with the keys being learned.
  - **While armed,** every new note-on makes a pass-through entry, whose output set is its played key alone under the counting rule of 3.4: it is written unchanged, Hold does not apply to it, and its note-off is written unchanged. The keys being learned therefore sound once each.
  - **Pass-through entries keep their kind** through completion, cancellation, a mode change and the parameter writes that end Learn, until their own note-offs. A key still down when Learn ends is not re-chorded; the next note-on after Learn ends gets the device's new behavior.
  - *Intervals mode, Ableton's Learn:*
    - The first key pressed is the root.
    - Each later key pressed while any key is still down fills the next shift with its distance from the root, in the order pressed.
    - A key whose distance is 0, more than 36 semitones, or already assigned is ignored; a seventh shift is ignored with no error.
    - When every key is up, the device marks the learn complete. The panel then writes the shifts and `learn` off together with `mix.midi_effect_params` (unfilled slots set to 0, velocities kept), one undo step for the shifts.
    - A learn completed with only the root pressed writes `learn` off and nothing else.
  - *Single and Multi Trigger, Logic's note-by-note Learn:*
    - Each key pressed while armed is added to the chord being learned: in Single, for the trigger key the user picked, else the first key pressed; in Multi, for the key picked in the panel's key list.
    - Pressing a key already in the chord removes it. A chord holds at most 8 keys, and a ninth is ignored with no error.
    - Learn ends only when the user presses the Learn button again. The panel then writes the chord with `mix.midi_chord_learn {track?, index, trigger, keys[]}` and `learn` off, one undo step; an empty chord clears that trigger.
    - Keys still down at that moment remain pass-through entries, as above.
    - `mix.midi_chord_clear {track?, index, trigger?}` clears one or all.
  - **Ending without writing.** Leaving the mode, removing or bypassing the device, Stop, panic, or loading a session while armed disarms Learn and writes nothing. Removal, bypass, Stop and panic are host resets, which end every note (2.8). A mode change keeps the pass-through entries as above.
- **Presets.** `Preset` gains an optional typed `chords` field (preset version raised); a user preset saved from a Chord slot stores its memory and a recall writes it with the parameters in one undo step. Factory presets carry none.
- **Compatibility.** A session whose Chord slot has chord memory lists `midi_chord_memory` in `needs` (decision 12), distinct from `midi_chord`, because a C1 build knows `midi_chord` and would drop the memory without a warning.

**3.8 Strum and chance (slice C3).** `strum` (−400 to +400 ms, 0: off; negative plays the set from the top down), `chance_1` … `chance_6` (0 to 100 %, default 100, one per interval slot, decided by the maintainer, D3), `seed` (decision 6.6 rules). The played note always sounds; slot `i`'s added note sounds when `c_i × 100 < chance_i × 2^24`, with `c_i = sm(h ^ (6 + i)) >> 40` from the hash `h` of 6.3 built on Chord's own seed and the input note-on (its timeline frame while playing, its render-clock frame while stopped, channel, key, ordinal): 100 % always plays, 0 % never, and a note gets the same draws on every loop pass (6.5). A slot whose note does not sound adds nothing to the counting rule of 3.4 for that entry, and the entry's release ends only the keys it played. A strummed note is a Pending output on the render clock (decision 4), late only (Chord reports no latency), with its note-off at least one sample after its note-on.

**3.9 Presets.** Major (+4, +7), Minor (+3, +7), Sus2 (+2, +7), Sus4 (+5, +7), Major 7 (+4, +7, +11), Minor 7 (+3, +7, +10), Power (+7, +12), Octaves (+12, −12), Stacked Fifths (+7, +14). Factory presets ship with the application, compiled in, and are never written over; saving over one makes a user preset in the user folder (Context, presets).

### 4. Time: one conversion and two clocks (Arpeggiator, Humanize, Chord's strum)

- **Musical positions** are integers in centiticks, a hundredth of a tick (96,000 per quarter). Every grid point is one (each beat division is a whole number of ticks, a bar division's points are bar lines, whole ticks), and so are a gate end (`gate × len_g` centiticks for a step of `len_g` ticks) and a swing shift (`2 × (swing − 50) × len_g` centiticks), since Gate and Swing are whole percents.
- **The canonical conversion.** A position `P` becomes an absolute frame `frame(P) = to_samples(S(t) + f × spt(t) / 100)`, with `t = floor(P / 100)` and `f = P mod 100` taken as floor division and its non-negative remainder (`div_euclid`, `rem_euclid`), so a position before bar 1 (negative, ADR 0052) converts like any other, `S(t)` the tempo map's closed-form `tick_to_samples_f(t)` (`crates/time/src/tempo.rs:236`), `spt(t)` the samples per tick of the tempo in force from `t` to `t + 1`, and `to_samples` the map's rounding to nearest (`crates/time/src/lib.rs:73`). For `f = 0` this is the clip conversion `tick_to_samples` exactly. Nothing in it depends on where a block starts, so every partition of the timeline gives the same frames; its only operations are IEEE additions, multiplications and divisions, so every platform gives the same frames. The regression case: 120 BPM, 48 kHz, quarter 308.5 is frame 7,404,000 at block sizes 1, 7, 64 and 512 (the block-relative form with a 1e-9 margin gives 7,404,001 at 64).
- **One placement rule.** An event at position `P`, created by a cause at frame `c` (the step whose gate it ends, the input note-on whose first step it is, the parameter change that re-anchored the pattern), is due at its **effective frame** `E = max(frame(P), c)`, and sounds in the block that contains `E`, at offset `E − transport.samples`, whatever tempo segment that block belongs to. There is no exception at tempo changes: at 48 kHz and 127 BPM with a change at tick 240, a clip note and an arpeggio step on tick 240 both sound at frame 5669, although the new tempo's sub-block starts at 5670 (`render_span` splits on the first frame at or after the change); the step sounds in the earlier sub-block, as the clip note does. `E` is a function of the map and the cause's frame only, so it is the same at every partition. The `max` is a guard: `frame` is nondecreasing in exact arithmetic, and no decrease was found in 776,400 adjacent positions around every change of 40 random maps, but it is a sum of rounded `f64` terms.
- **No deadline is in the past when it becomes known, by construction.** A device creates an event in the block containing its cause frame `c`, and `E ≥ c`, so `E` lies in that block or a later one; while the timeline continues, every frame lies in exactly one block, so every event is emitted exactly at `E`. This needs the device to convert a position at the moment it creates the event, which the next bullet gives it. The causes are chosen to keep `c` honest: the first step after a note-on is the first grid point whose frame is at or after the note-on's frame (5.4), a re-anchored step the first at or after the change (5.4), a gate end is caused by its step. The one way a deadline can fall behind is a change of the tempo map itself while playing (below).
- **What the device is given for it (slice P0).** A device sees a `Transport`, not the tempo map, and the tempo segment of its block is not enough: a deadline can fall in a segment no block exposes (Context: two changes that round to one frame), and a deadline can be owed before any block of its segment arrives (at 8 kHz with tempos 800 BPM at tick 0, 1000 at tick 2 and 500 at tick 3, a step at tick 0 with Rate 1/32 and Gate 2 % owes its release at tick 2.4, frame 1, and no block ever exposes the segment from tick 2 to 3). So slice P0 gives the device the whole map in prepared form. When the host builds a plan (off the audio thread), it prepares the session's **tempo table**: for each tempo event `i` its tick `tick_i`, `acc_i` (the `f64` that `tick_to_samples_f` accumulates up to that event, computed by the same loop in the same order) and `spt_i` (the map's `samples_per_tick` expression), at the session's sample rate. The host passes the device a read-only view of it, a `Timeline<'_>` (a borrowed slice and the table's generation), with every `process` call through a new trait method `process_in(input, out, frames, timeline)` whose default calls `process`, so the existing effects are unchanged. `Timeline::frame(P)` finds the last `i` with `tick_i ≤ t` by binary search (for `t` below the first row, which is tick 0, it uses row 0, the first tempo extended backward, exactly as `tick_to_samples_f` does) and returns `to_samples(acc_i + (t − tick_i) × spt_i + (f × spt_i) / 100)`: the same operations in the same order as `tick_to_samples_f`, so the same bits (checked over 57,880 probes on 300 random maps, including dense steps). The table also holds the meter map: for each meter event its first bar number, its tick and its ticks per bar, so `Timeline::bar_tick(b)` gives the tick of bar `b`'s line by binary search, the same integer as `TempoMap::bar_start_tick(b)` (`tempo.rs:274`), bars before bar 1 extending the first meter backward as that function does, short bars from a meter that does not fall on a bar line included (8,600 probes on 200 random meter maps, no difference). No allocation, no lock, no waiting; any position, at any time. A pending event is stored as its position and cause frame, and converted when created.
- **A tempo or meter edit while playing.** A plan whose table has another generation than the previous block's re-converts every Pending event and the next step: a Pending event whose new `E` lies before the block start is emitted at offset 0 of that block, in schedule order, and counted (`late_after_tempo_edit`); the Arpeggiator's next step becomes the first grid point of the new table at or after the block start and after the last step's position (a pending note-off keeps its position, so a gate already scheduled keeps the length it was given). A tempo or meter edit is a live action, taken at a block start like a hand-moved parameter, so this is the one place a block start shows in timeline results; it is stated in the help as a limit.
- **Tempo ramps.** The engine has none: an imported ramp is stored as steps at its points, and the devices follow those steps exactly as clip notes do (each step is a row of the table). A true ramp (a closed form between two points) is not supported by this arithmetic; if the map gains them, the table gains ramp rows with their own closed form and the clip conversion changes with it (Reopen when). Until then there is nothing for a device to approximate.
- **Grids, and meter changes.** A beat division (1/2 to 1/16 D) has its points at whole multiples of its length `d` in ticks from quarter 0, negative multiples included (the pre-count zone), whatever the meter; the first point at or after a position is found with floor division. A bar division of `m` bars (4 bars, 2 bars, 1 bar) has its points on the bar lines of the zero-based bars 0, `m`, `2m`, …: point `j` (any integer, negative before bar 1) is at tick `bar_tick(1 + j × m)`, the bars counted through every meter change without restarting. This is the `SyncDivision` rule (`lfo.rs:58`: its phase counts bars from the song start in the meter in force, so its zeros are exactly these bar lines; checked on 200 random meter maps, no disagreement), so a bar division needs no origin of its own and no rounding at a boundary: every point is a whole tick, converted like a clip note on that tick. A step's length `len_g` is the distance from its point to the next, `point(g + 1) − point(g)` in ticks (`d` for a beat division); its gate and its swing are fractions of it (5.5), so a bar step that spans a meter change is as long as the bars it spans. The device reads the meter of a future bar from the table when it takes the step, so no length is cached from the old meter. Case: 120 BPM, Rate 2 bars, a step at tick 0, 4/4 changing to 3/4 at tick 3840: the points are 0, 6720, 12480 (frames 0, 168000, 312000), and the first step's 50 % gate ends at tick 3360 (frame 84000); adding the old meter's 7680 ticks would have put the next step at frame 192000. A meter change in the map is known in advance and needs nothing more; a meter edit while playing is the table change of the bullet above. While stopped a bar division's length uses the stopped meter (`transport.numerator`, `denominator`).
- **The timeline clock, while playing.** A block continues the timeline when the device processed a block since its reset, that block was playing, and `transport.samples` equals the previous block's `samples + frames`. Otherwise (play start, a locate, a loop wrap) the block is a **discontinuity**, handled per 2.8 before anything else in it. Stop and the end of a play range reset the devices first, and a record count-in entered while playing does too once slice P2 lands (5.11 lists the paths); until then that count-in reaches the devices as a playing block followed by a stopped one without a reset, which is a discontinuity like the others.
- **The render clock, while stopped and for delays.** Each device counts the frames of every block it processes since its reset. While stopped, `transport.samples` is not compared at all: a stopped locate does not flush ownership or restart the render clock (the parameters and the tempo it implies are read again at the next block start, below), the Arpeggiator's free-running steps (with Play on) and every millisecond delay (Humanize, the strum) are due on this clock, and the stopped tempo is `transport.tempo`. The render clock is the same for every partition of the same render.
- **Events between blocks.** Once #559's third item lands, the host runs an event sent between blocks through a call that says no time passes; a device then applies the event's ownership transition (2.8), writes its consequences at offset 0 of that call, and moves neither clock, starts no step and checks no discontinuity. Until then the devices are not user-visible (Slices).
- **Times in milliseconds** become whole samples once, `round(ms × sample_rate / 1000)`.
- **Parameters.** Until slice P1, the host gives a device its parameter values once per block, at the block start (`midi_fx.rs:329` to `340`): results are the same at every block size for fixed parameters, and an automated device parameter takes effect at block starts (a known limit while the devices are invisible). Slice P1 makes them exact, with one grid anchored at timeline frame 0: grid points are the timeline frames `64 × n` for every integer `n`, negative ones included, and `64 × floor(x / 64)` is floor division (as `PAN_STEP`'s grid already is).
  - *While playing*, the host gives each stage, at the block start, the lane's value at the grid point at or before the block's timeline start, and wherever a lane value changes at a grid point inside the block, splits that stage's call there. The sub-calls run in chronological order; each gets the events at or after its first frame and before the next split, with offsets rebased to its start (`offset − split`), a transport for its start (`samples` + split, playing) and `frames` up to the next split; what it writes is rebased back (`+ split`) and appended in call order, so at a split frame the earlier call's output precedes the later's, and events at one frame keep their order (the host's stable sort). Each stage splits independently, at its own lanes' changes; since every device gives the same output for any partition (and its render clock advances by each sub-call's frames, so the total is the block's), this is compatible with the order of 2.11. The value in force for an event at timeline frame `x` is then the lane's value at `64 × floor(x / 64)`, at every block size.
  - *While stopped*, there is no advancing timeline, so no lane advances either: every stopped block reads each lane at the grid point at or before the stopped position, `64 × floor(stopped_position / 64)`, and no stopped call is split. The render clock keeps advancing by the frames processed; lanes do not move with it. (Splitting literally would make the value depend on the block size: with the transport stopped at 0 and a lane step at timeline frame 64, an event at render frame 110 would read 1 in a 512-frame block, which crosses timeline offset 64, and 0 in 1-, 7- and 64-frame blocks, each of which starts at timeline 0 again.) A stopped locate moves the stopped position: the lanes and the tempo are read again at the next block start, and nothing is flushed (the render clock keeps counting).
  - *Hand moves.* A value set from a panel, a controller or MIDI Learn, playing or stopped, is a live action and takes effect at the next block start, as today; while the track reads a lane for that parameter, the lane wins, as for every automated parameter. Live actions are the stated exception to block-size independence.
- **Capacity and partitions.** The host's buffers are per block (512 events in, 512 per stage), so a dense enough stream overflows them at one block size and not at another (a hundred seven-note chords in 200 frames fill one 512-frame block, not 200 one-frame blocks). Equality across partitions is therefore promised for streams under the capacity bound: for the largest block size compared, no window of that many frames carries more than 384 note-ons and other non-release events, or more than 512 events in all, into the chain or out of any stage (carried events counted in the window they are due), and no more than 256 of the chain's notes are outstanding at the instrument at once (the host's delivery table, `MIDI_FX_NOTES`). Why that is enough: slice P3b admits a non-release event to a buffer while it holds fewer than 384 non-release events and fewer than 512 events in all, and a release while it holds fewer than 512; every block of any partition lies inside one such window, so no event in any block exceeds either admission limit (a buffer may hold exactly 384 non-release events, or 512 in all), nothing is refused or reset at any partition, and the outstanding notes never fill the delivery table. Once ADR 0059's slice R1 lands, each instrument also keeps a note's release reserved until the end of the processing call that delivers it, so a released note still holds its reservation for the rest of its block; a pedal press holds one the same way until its release is delivered, and the instrument's one ledger of 704 entries for notes and pedal presses is the 320 notes and pedals it can hold as a block starts plus the 384 starts one block can carry, whatever releases the block gives, explicit or implied by CC 120, 121 or 123 (ADR 0059, 1), so the instrument refuses none of them at any partition either, pedal presses included (with 256 entries, the 200 releases followed by 200 note-ons below would lose 144 of the note-ons at block size 512), except at a VST3 instrument whose separated events wait past a processing call (ADR 0059, 3b) and after skipped calls: there a start inside this bound can be refused, at a frame that can differ between partitions, and a separated event can be up to 2,480 rendered frames late. Releases admitted earlier in a block do not use the non-release quota, so the order of releases and note-ons within a window does not matter: 200 releases followed by 200 note-ons, one event per frame over 400 frames, are admitted whole at block sizes 1, 7, 64 and 512. 385 non-release events at one frame refuse exactly one at every block size, so the bound is tight. The devices' own tables fill the same way at every partition (they follow the timeline, not the block). Above the bound, the invariant and the counters are promised, and so is per-hop agreement (2.8, slice P3b): a refused note-on is reported to the device that wrote it within the call, or accounted by the host, which then consumes its release; a note-off the host owes is given, and retried while the instrument refuses it, within the limits stated in 2.13.

### 5. Arpeggiator

**5.1 Parameters (slice A1).** All automatable and MIDI-learnable.

| Id | Name | Range | Default | Unit | Group |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `style` | Style | Up, Down, Up-Down, Up & Down, Down-Up, Converge, Diverge, As Played, Random, Random Other, Chord | Up | choice | Pattern |
| `rate` | Rate | the fourteen `DIVISIONS` (4 bars … 1/16 D) | 1/16 | choice | Pattern |
| `gate` | Gate | 1 to 200, whole | 50 | percent of a step, linear | Pattern |
| `octaves` | Octaves | 1 to 4, whole | 1 | octaves | Pattern |
| `offset` | Offset | 0 to 15, whole | 0 | steps | Pattern |
| `swing` | Swing | 50 to 75, whole | 50 | percent, linear (50 straight) | Feel |
| `retrigger` | Retrigger | Off, Note | Off | choice | Feel |
| `velocity_mode` | Velocity | As Played, Fixed | As Played | choice | Feel |
| `velocity` | Fixed Velocity | 1 to 127, whole | 100 | velocity | Feel |
| `hold` | Hold | off, on | off | toggle | Hold |
| `play` | Play | off, on | off | toggle; acts only while the transport is stopped (5.11) | Hold |
| `seed` | Seed | 1 to 9999, whole | 1 | number | Hold |

**5.2 The held set.** Apart from the pass-through entries of 5.11, the Arpeggiator swallows its input note-ons and the note-offs that close their entries, and keeps up to **32 held keys** (channel, key, velocity, arrival number), each with its reasons: down (an Open entry), latched (5.7), pedal-held (5.9). A key with no reason leaves the set. A note-on for a 33rd key is rejected (its entry Orphaned, 2.8, so its note-off is consumed and cannot end a generated note of that key downstream). A note-on for a key already in the set adds an entry, keeps the key's arrival number and takes its velocity.

**5.3 Note orders.** The **base pattern** is built over the held keys: ascending by key (ties by channel, then arrival) for every style but As Played, which uses arrival order. Octaves `o` repeats it: repetition `r` (0 to `o − 1`) is the base pattern moved up `12 r` semitones (Logic: "The first repetition is transposed by one octave"), so one cycle is `o` repetitions; a key above 127 in a repetition makes that step silent (it still counts). With `n` held keys and the base pattern's period `p`, the cycle length is `P = p × o`; step `t = s + offset` (every style) plays base index `t mod p` of repetition `floor((t mod P) / p)`.

| Style | Period `p` of the base pattern over ascending `l0 … l(n−1)` |
|---|---|
| Up | n: l0, l1, …, l(n−1) |
| Down | n: l(n−1), …, l0 |
| Up-Down | 2n − 2 (n ≥ 2): l0 … l(n−1), then l(n−2) … l1 (end points once) |
| Up & Down | 2n: l0 … l(n−1), then l(n−1) … l0 (end points repeat, Logic's Up/Down) |
| Down-Up | 2n − 2 (n ≥ 2): l(n−1) … l0, then l1 … l(n−2) |
| Converge | n: l(n−1), l0, l(n−2), l1, … (outside in, highest first: Logic's Outside-In, the one documented definition) |
| Diverge | n: Converge reversed (inside out) |
| As Played | n: arrival order |
| Random | the expanded list `E` (the held keys ascending, then each repetition's keys appended, keys above 127 dropped, duplicates kept; length `m`): `E[draw(seed, t) mod m]`, a note may repeat; `draw(seed, a) = sm(sm(seed) ^ a) >> 40` with SplitMix64 `sm` of 6.3 |
| Random Other | `E[perm_c[t mod m]]`: `perm_c` a Fisher-Yates shuffle of 0..m with draws `draw(seed, c, i) = sm(sm(sm(seed) ^ c) ^ i) >> 40` (for `i` from m − 1 down to 1, swap `i` with `draw mod (i + 1)`), `c = floor(t / m)`: each entry of `E` once per cycle (Ableton's Random Other) |
| Chord | every held key moved up `12 × (t mod o)` at once (Ableton's Chord Trigger style) |

With one held key every style plays it. Pinky, Thumb, Con & Diverge, Random Once, Inversions and Ableton's Distance in scale degrees are slice A2.

**The step counter `s`.** `s` is 0 at the first step after a discontinuity and at the first step after the held set was empty; it rises by one at every grid step taken while the held set is not empty (a silent step of a repetition above 127 counts; a step with an empty held set does not exist). Retrigger Note sets `s` to 0 at every input note-on (not at a release). When the held set changes otherwise, `s` keeps counting and the next step reads the new pattern.

**5.4 The clock.**

- **Playing: locked to the song grid.** Step `g` sits on grid point `g` of decision 4 (a beat division at `g × d` ticks from quarter 0; a bar division of `m` bars on the line of zero-based bar `g × m`, through meter changes), converted by decision 4. A key pressed between grid steps sounds at the next grid step: the first grid point whose (swung) frame is at or after the note-on's frame, so a key at a step's exact frame is in that step (2.11) and the step is never in the past (decision 4). Waiting for the grid is a transit choice: neither workstation's first-note behavior was verified for this record (Logic has an Input Snap setting for it, which was not read for this record). At the default 1/16 and 120 BPM it delays the first note by up to 125 ms (decided by the maintainer, item 6). A discontinuity ends every sounding step note at offset 0 and restarts `s` on the new grid with the held set as it is, so every pass of a loop and every playback from one position plays the same notes whenever the held set at the start is the same (always, for input from clips with Hold off; a key latched or held across a loop wrap carries into the next pass).
- **Stopped, Play off (the default): pass-through.** While the transport is stopped and Play is off, the Arpeggiator passes its input notes through unchanged, each owned as a pass-through entry (2.3), and generates nothing; Hold and the pedal do not latch anything while stopped with Play off. Logic's convention: "When Logic Pro is stopped: Arpeggio playback stops", incoming notes pass through (decided by the maintainer, item 5).
- **Stopped, Play on: free-running on the render clock.** With Play on (Logic's Play button), the Arpeggiator runs from its input while the transport is stopped. The first step sounds at the render frame of the note-on that made the held set non-empty (input events at that frame first); that frame is the anchor, and step `g` is due at `anchor + to_samples(g × d_q × 60 × sample_rate / tempo)`, `d_q` the division in quarters (a bar division in the stopped meter) and `tempo` the stopped tempo, computed from the anchor and the integer step index (never by adding rounded steps). A change while stopped that can move the next step (Rate, Swing, the stopped tempo, and for a bar division the stopped meter; whether from a hand move or from a lane read again after a stopped locate) re-anchors, never retroactively: with `L` the render frame of the last step taken, `c` the render frame at which the change takes effect (a block start, 4) and `len` the step length in exact samples after the change (`d_q × 60 × sample_rate / tempo`), the next step sounds at `L + to_samples(k × len)` for the smallest `k ≥ 1` that puts it at or after `c`; it becomes the anchor and `g` restarts at 0 (swing parity counts from it, so that step is unswung). Last step at render frame 0, Rate changed from 1/4 to 1/32 at render frame 10240: the next step is at 12000 (`k` = 4), not 3000. Swing: Rate 1/16 with Swing 75 % from anchor 0 (the step at 0 taken, the swung step pending at 9000), Swing changed to 50 % at render frame 8000: recomputing the pending point would give 6000, already past, and keeping 9000 would ignore the change; the rule gives 12000 (`k` = 2). A Swing change before the pending point re-anchors too (75 % to 60 % at 2000: next step 6000, unswung, then 13200). Note-offs already scheduled keep their frames. Starting the transport is a discontinuity (2.8): the stopped step notes end at offset 0 and the pattern restarts on the grid (5.11).
- **Rate changes while playing.** The next step after a change of Rate or Swing is the first point of the new grid whose swung frame is at or after the frame at which the change takes effect (a block start until slice P1, its grid point after) and strictly after the last step taken since the discontinuity, if any. Swung starts rise strictly with `g` (an odd point moves by at most half a step), so "first" is well defined. `s` keeps counting; swing parity is that grid point's index `g`; note-offs already scheduled keep their frames. At or after, not strictly after, the change: a change that takes effect exactly on a point of the new grid plays that point (the step and the change are at one frame, and the change is in force there); a step of the old grid at that frame is not taken. Cases (120 BPM): a 1/4 step at frame 0, Rate changed to 1/32 at 10240: the next step is 12000 (`g` = 4), not 3000, which was already past; the 1/4 note's gate end at 12000 keeps its frame. A 1/4 step at 0, Rate 1/16 with Swing 75 % from frame 7000: 9000 (`g` = 1, swung); from 9000: 9000; from 9500: 12000 (`g` = 2).
- **The other parameters, playing and stopped.** Only Rate and Swing (and the tempo and meter, above and in decision 4) move a step point; each re-anchors by the rule of its transport state. No other parameter moves one: Gate applies to every step taken at or after the frame its change takes effect (a step on that frame included) and leaves note-offs already scheduled at their frames; Style, Octaves, Offset, Seed, Velocity and Fixed Velocity change which key or velocity the next step plays, not when (`s` keeps counting); Retrigger acts only at an input note-on; Hold acts on the held set (5.7), and when turning it off empties the set the pending step is dropped and the next note-on starts afresh (playing: the next grid point; stopped: a new anchor at that note-on). A1 has no Steps parameter (Ableton's Distance and Steps are slice A2).

**5.5 Gate and swing.** A step at grid index `g` starts at grid point `g`, plus `2 × (swing − 50) × len_g` centiticks when `g` is odd (`len_g` the step's own length, decision 4: `d` for a beat division; the shift is at most half of it, so a swung start stays before the next point; pairs counted from the grid, not from the pattern's start, so swing stays on the bar; while stopped, from the anchor). Its note-off is at its start plus `gate × len_g` centiticks (while stopped: plus `to_samples(gate / 100 × d_q × 60 × sample_rate / tempo)`), at least one frame after the start. With Gate above 100 % notes overlap (legato, Ableton). If a step's key (same channel and key) is still sounding from an earlier step, its pending note-off is cancelled and a note-off is written at the new step's frame before the new note-on: the Arpeggiator never overlaps a key with itself, as the Random Sequencer does not.

**5.6 Velocity.** As Played: each note at its held key's velocity. Fixed: every note at `velocity`. Ableton's velocity decay and target are slice A2.

**5.7 Hold.** Off: a key whose last entry closes leaves the held set at once unless pedal-held; when the set is empty no further step sounds, and a sounding note ends at its gate. On: a key whose last entry closes stays, latched. **Playing a latched key again removes it** (decided by the maintainer, D2; Ableton's Hold: "To remove notes, play them a second time"): a note-on for a key that is in the set as latched and not down removes that key from the set, plays nothing, and its entry is Orphaned (2.8), so its note-off is consumed; this comes before the replace rule that follows, and the removing note's velocity is discarded. Removing the last key ends the pattern: no further step sounds and a sounding step note ends at its gate. Otherwise, a note-on while no input key is down first removes every key that is not down (latched and pedal-held alike), then adds its own; a note-on while some key is down adds to the set (the rule of 3.5; Logic's Add, Through and Transpose latch modes are slice A2). Turning Hold off removes every latched key at the frame the change takes effect (2.11), except that a latched key on a channel whose pedal is down becomes pedal-held.

**5.8 Re-strikes.** A re-strike of a key that is down adds a ledger entry and takes its velocity; the key's down reason ends only when its last entry closes (2.3).

**5.9 Sustain pedal.** The Arpeggiator reads CC 64 on each channel and does not pass a pedal-down: passing it on would make the instrument sustain every arpeggiated note into a wash. A pedal-up (below 64) is acted on and then passed, so the instrument's pedal is right whatever reached it before (under slice P3a the edit that added the device has already released the instrument's pedals, 2.13), and a pedal-up there with no sustain active changes nothing. A pedal held across the edit that adds or rebuilds the device is not shown to it again: after the edit the device, like the instrument, treats the pedal as up until the player presses it again (2.13). With Hold off, a key whose last entry closes while that channel's pedal is down (64 or above) becomes pedal-held; pedal up (below 64), CC 121 (Reset All Controllers) or CC 120/123 on that channel removes the channel's pedal-held keys. With Hold on, a release latches whatever the pedal does. A pianist's expectation (Ableton's behavior, from memory); Hold wins over the pedal (decided by the maintainer, item 11). While stopped with Play off the pedal passes like every event (5.11). Other pedals and controllers pass.

**5.10 Capacity.** 32 held keys; a schedule of 128 pending note-offs (the Chord style at 32 keys with a 200 % gate needs 96). A step emits a note only after reserving its note-off's slot, held until that note-off is written; a step whose notes cannot all be reserved emits only those that can and counts the rest as refused note-ons (`MidiOut::add_refused_ons`): an on is never emitted without its off in hand. A note-on its own output refuses is Cancelled and its reservation freed (2.8); the verdict is final (slice P3b).

**5.11 Play, Stop and the transport (decided by the maintainer, item 5 and D1).**

- **Two kinds of entry, fixed at the note-on.** Every Arpeggiator ledger entry (2.3) is one of two kinds, chosen when its note-on arrives and never changed afterwards:
  - A **pattern entry**: its key joins the held set and is arpeggiated. This is every note-on while playing, and every note-on while stopped with Play on.
  - A **pass-through entry**: its note-on was written unchanged. This is every note-on while stopped with Play off. Its note-off is written unchanged when it arrives, whatever has happened to Play or the transport in between. It is never latched, never pedal-held and never Orphaned by a Play or transport change. Only Stop, panic, an edit and the safety reset end it, through the host (2.8).
- **Same key, both kinds.** A generated step note on a channel and key where a pass-through voice is sounding is not written: that step is silent for that key and still counts in `s` (5.3). So the device never overlaps a key with itself, and the pass-through voice ends only by its own note-off. A new note-on for a key whose pass-through voice is sounding makes a pattern entry, as any note-on does, and its steps on that key stay silent while the pass-through voice sounds. An anonymous note-off of that key closes the oldest entry first, the pass-through one (decision 11); an identified one closes its own.
- **Stop ends everything.** Stop resets the device (2.12, 2.14): every step note and every pass-through note it started ends (the host writes their note-offs), the ledger and the held set (down, latched and pedal-held keys) are cleared, and nothing sounds again until a new note-on reaches it. Play is a parameter, and `reset` clears ownership without writing any parameter. So with Play on, the next key played while stopped arpeggiates from a new anchor. The All MIDI Notes Off action resets the devices the same way (the host's `panic`), but does not call the instrument's `Plugin::reset` (2.14): the two have different reset scopes at the instrument and the same one at the devices.
- **Where playing ends.**
  - The Stop command and the end of a play range (`crates/playback/src/player/audio.rs:835` to `846`, the same mix reset) both take Stop's reset.
  - A runtime fault stops rendering altogether, so no device runs after it.
  - A locate while stopped is not a stop and resets nothing (decision 4).
  - **A record count-in** parks the transport without resetting the mix (`Transport::count_in`, `audio.rs:380` onward, `change(Some(from), false, None)`), and the audio-side Record dispatch does not check whether the transport was playing. A count-in entered from a playing transport would therefore reach the devices as playing followed by stopped with no reset. Slice P2 closes it: when a count-in is entered while the transport is playing, the player calls the same mix reset Stop calls, before the parked blocks, so D1 holds there too. A count-in entered while stopped changes nothing. P2's test enters a count-in from playing with an Arpeggiator pattern and a Chord Hold sounding: nothing sounds after the reset.
- **Play turned on while stopped.** Takes effect at a block start (decision 4). Pass-through entries keep their kind and sound until their own note-offs. The pattern starts with the next note-on, anchored at its render frame. A key still down from a pass-through entry is not added to the held set: it is arpeggiated once struck again.
- **Play turned off while stopped.** At the block start the change takes effect, every sounding step note ends and the held set is cleared. Only pattern entries are Orphaned (2.8): their note-offs are consumed, and their keys stay silent until struck again. Pass-through entries are untouched: a note passed while stopped, then Play turned on and off before its release, is still a pass-through entry, and its note-off is written. From then on new input passes through.
- **The transport starts.** A discontinuity (2.8). Pattern entries: with Play on, the stopped step notes end at offset 0 and the pattern restarts on the grid with the held set as it is. Pass-through entries keep sounding until their note-offs; their keys join the held set only when struck again.
- **During a record count-in** the transport is parked at the record start while the count-in clicks (`CountInClick`, `crates/mix/src/lib.rs:877` to `887`), so the device sees a stopped transport. With Play off, notes played during the count-in are pass-through entries, and the record start is the start of playing above. Recording captures the played notes before the chain in either case (decision 9).
- **Automating Play.** An automation lane for Play moves the visible control as every lane does. While stopped it is read at the stopped position (P1, frozen), so it changes only at a stopped locate or a hand move. Its changes follow the same transitions as a hand move. Bounce, freeze and capture render with a playing transport from the range start, so Play has no effect on them; play and export match.
- **Hold and the pedal while stopped with Play off** latch nothing: a pass-through note is released by its own note-off, and a pedal-down passes to the instrument like every other event.

**5.12 Presets.** Up 1/16, Up & Down 1/8, Converge 1/16 Two Octaves, Random 1/16 Swing, Chord Stabs 1/8 (Chord style, gate 25 %), Trance Gate 1/16 (gate 35 %).

### 6. Humanize

**6.1 Parameters (slice H1).**

| Id | Name | Range | Default | Unit, taper | Group |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `timing` | Timing | 0 to 100 | 10 | ± ms, linear; sets the reported latency, so not automatable (decision 7) | Amounts |
| `velocity` | Velocity | 0 to 64, whole | 10 | ± velocity, linear | Amounts |
| `length` | Length | 0 to 50, whole | 10 | ± percent of the note's length, linear | Amounts |
| `seed` | Seed | 1 to 9999, whole | 1 | number | Seed |

The defaults were decided by the maintainer (item 12). With centered timing the average shift is zero.

**6.2 Exactness.** With all three amounts at 0 the device is transparent: the same events, offsets and order as its input, event for event (tested; a professional can null this), velocity-0 note-ons excepted (2.2; none reach the chain from the mix), and a release that matches no note it received is consumed (2.4).

**6.3 The draws.** For each input note-on, a 64-bit hash `h` is built with SplitMix64 (`sm(x)`: add `0x9E3779B97F4A7C15`, then `z ^= z >> 30; z *= 0xBF58476D1CE4E5B9; z ^= z >> 27; z *= 0x94D049BB133111EB; z ^= z >> 31`, wrapping):
`h = sm(sm(sm(seed) ^ position) ^ (channel << 16 | key << 8 | ordinal))`, where `position` is the note-on's frame as a two's-complement `u64`: its timeline frame (`transport.samples + offset`) while playing, its render-clock frame while stopped; `ordinal` counts the earlier note-ons of the same channel and key at the same frame, in input order. Six 24-bit uniforms `u_t = sm(h ^ t) >> 40` for `t` = 1 to 6, and `W = 2^24 − 1`. All arithmetic below is integer (products in `i128`), so the result is the same on every platform and at every block size.

- **Timing (centered):** `shift = trunc(T × (u1 + u2 − W) / W)` samples, `T = round(timing × sample_rate / 1000)`, an integer in −T..=T whose distribution is symmetric about 0 and approximately triangular, peak at 0 (the same construction as the velocity draw below). The device delays the note by `delay = T + shift`, in 0..=2T, and reports a latency of `T` samples (decision 7), so after delay compensation the note is heard `shift` samples from where it was played: earlier or later by at most `T`, zero on average (measured over 29,000 draws at `T` = 480, seed 1, channel 0, key 60, ordinal 0, at positions 0, 7, 14, … up to 202,993: mean shift −1.5, delays from 5 to 954). Most notes stay near where they were played.
- **Velocity:** `dv = trunc(V × (u3 + u4 − W) / W)`, an integer in −V..=V whose distribution is symmetric about 0 (the sum of two uniforms on 0..=W is symmetric about W; truncation is toward zero both ways), approximately triangular; added and clamped to 1..=127.
- **Length:** `dl = trunc(D × length × (u5 + u6 − W) / (100 × W))`, symmetric in the same way, where `D` is the note's input length in samples, measured when its note-off arrives.

**6.4 Placement and order.** Frames here are render-clock frames (decision 4; within one continuous playback they differ from timeline frames by a constant). Per channel and key:

- **Note-on:** `on' = max(input frame + delay, previous on' of that key)`, so note-ons of one key never reorder.
- **Note-off:** `off' = max(on' + D + dl, frame of the input note-off, previous off' of that key)`, written after the earlier releases of that key: releases of one key leave in the order they arrived, so oldest-first pairing downstream closes the right note. A note can be lengthened freely and shortened only as far as its own delay allows (the device cannot see the future; the help says so). `off' ≥ on'` always, and a note with a nonzero input length keeps a nonzero length (`D + dl ≥ 1` when `D ≥ 1`) unless the next rule cuts it at a later note-on of its key that the order rule placed on the same frame.
- **No overlap the input did not have.** When a moved note-on of a key becomes due while an earlier note of that key is still sounding, and the earlier note's input note-off arrived before the later note's input note-on (they did not overlap in the input), the earlier note's note-off is written at that frame, just before the new note-on. Notes that overlapped in the input may overlap in the output, releases in arrival order.
- Non-note events are not moved. The input voice id is kept. Delays are counted on the render clock, so a loop wrap neither moves nor drops the pending notes of keys still held (2.8).

Worked values (seed 1, 48 kHz, Timing 10 ms so `T` = 480 and the reported latency is 480, Velocity 10, Length 10 %, notes at timeline frame 0 on channel 0 with velocity 100 and their note-offs at frame 6000; frames are the device's output frames, heard 480 earlier after delay compensation; computed by a reference script from the formulas above, independent of any implementation):

| Key | shift | delay | dv | dl | Output (heard) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | −39 | 441 | −5 | −161 | on 60 v95 at 441, off at 6280 (heard −39 to 5800) |
| 64 | −225 | 255 | 0 | −7 | on 64 v100 at 255, off at 6248 (heard −225 to 5768) |
| 67 | +150 | 630 | −2 | +74 | on 67 v98 at 630, off at 6704 (heard 150 to 6224) |

The same note at timeline frame 12000 (key 60) gets shift −2 (delay 478), dv −2, dl −246; with seed 2 the frame-0 key 60 gets shift −125 (delay 355), dv +2, dl +194. Release order: seed 2, Timing 0, Length 50 %, key 60 note-ons at frames 0 and 1 with note-offs at 1000 and 1001 draw releases at 1162 and 1001; the second is written at 1162, after the first. No new overlap: with the defaults and seed 2, key 60 from 0 to 6000 and again from 6000 to 12000: the first note sounds at 355 (v102) with its release drawn at 6549, the second at 6173 (v92); the first note's note-off is written at 6173 before the second's note-on, and the second ends at 12000, its own input note-off (a note-off is never written before the device receives it). The velocity and length draws are unchanged from the first draft; only the timing draw is new.

**6.5 Same draw every pass.** Because the position is the timeline frame, a note gets the same draws on every loop pass, and a bounce matches playback given a matching input history, order and start; re-rolling the seed gives a new humanization. This is a **transit choice**, not an agreement of the workstations: Logic's Transform writes its change into the notes once (a different mechanism), and Ableton's Velocity Random draws anew per event. The maintainer decided the same draw every pass (item 7); a "new each pass" option is not planned. What is promised: the same input stream (the same events, in the same order) from the same start gives the same output. A note's draws depend only on the seed, its frame, channel, key and ordinal; its placement can also depend on the earlier notes of its key (6.4), which a render from a later start may not include; distinguishable notes of one key arriving at one frame in another order swap their draws; while stopped the draws follow the render clock and are not reproducible.

**6.6 Seeds, for every device that draws.** The seed is a visible parameter (1 to 9999), stored in the slot like every parameter, automatable and learnable. The panel puts a **Re-roll** button beside it, which writes a new seed through `mix.midi_effect_param`, one undo step (undo restores the previous seed; decided by the maintainer, item 7): `new = 1 + (old + r) mod 9999` with `r` drawn on the UI thread uniformly from 0..=9997, so the new seed always differs from the old. While the track reads an automation lane for the seed, Re-roll is ghosted and its help says the seed follows its lane (writing the stored value would change nothing audible). Adding the device from the card's "+" writes a fresh seed with the add (`mix.midi_effect` with `params: {seed}`), so two instances do not move in lockstep; the control protocol's add without a seed gets the default, 1. A preset recall keeps the current seed (a preset is a feel, not a draw; decided by the maintainer, item 13): the recall writes every parameter except `seed`. The fresh seed, the re-roll and the seed-keeping recall are one shared piece of the card's code (slice K2b), not three panels' copies.

**6.7 Capacity.** A schedule of 512 entries. A note-on is admitted only with two free entries, one reserved for its note-off until that note-off is written. Without room the note-on is rejected: not played, counted, its entry Orphaned so its note-off is consumed (2.8). A moved note-on that its own output refuses when it falls due is Cancelled with its release and the entry Orphaned (2.8). It is never written undelayed: that could overtake an earlier delayed note-on of its key.

### 7. Early notes and latency (slice P4)

A MIDI effect cannot report latency today: the `MidiEffect` trait has no latency, and the strip's head latency counts only a frozen track, the instrument and an External Instrument (`head_latency`, `crates/mix/src/strip.rs:523` to `527`). So nothing can play a note earlier than it reached the slot. Centered Humanize timing (decided by the maintainer, item 4) needs a latency path, and slice P4 adds it for every MIDI effect.

**Two figures, kept apart.** The MIDI effect chain sits before the instrument, and the note stream that note-reading inserts see is what the instrument accepted, after the chain (ADR 0038). So P4 distinguishes:

- **The track's compensation latency:** `L_midi + head + inserts`, where `L_midi` is the sum of the active, unbypassed MIDI effect stages' `latency()`. This is what delay compensation and the Low Latency limit see.
- **The note readers' alignment:** the delay `NoteDelay::deliver` gives the accepted-note view so that it meets a reader's input audio. It is measured from the accepted-note tap, which is after the chain. It is the head latency (instrument, External Instrument, frozen chain) plus the inserts before the reader, exactly as today (`process_strip_inserts`, `strip.rs:249` onward), and never includes `L_midi`. Example: Humanize writes a note at frame 441 and a zero-latency instrument sounds it there. A note-reading Filter after it receives that note at 441, not at 441 + 480.

**Where `L_midi` enters, all in P4:**

- **The trait.** `MidiEffect::latency(&self) -> usize`, in samples at the sample rate last given, defaulting to 0, so every existing effect is unchanged. A device's latency is a function of its parameters and the sample rate. It is defined as soon as both are set, before the first `process`.
- **Initial planning and storage sizing.** The plan build that gathers raw slot latencies and sizes compensation (`crates/mix/src/compile/build.rs:33` onward, `latency_of` and the `raw` map) gains one entry per track for its MIDI chain. For a moved chain it is read from the running stages. For fresh instances it is read after the slot's stored parameters and the sample rate have been applied, so a new Humanize reports its real `T` in the first plan and the compensation storage is sized for it. Before any key alignment, a parameter change is applied to the instance first and its latency read after.
- **Runtime.** The same entry is observed at runtime with the instrument's (`observe_raw`, `crates/mix/src/low_latency.rs`). A change, growth included, bumps `raw_latency_revision`, and compensation is re-planned through the existing path a hosted plug-in's latency change takes.
- **Bypass and edits.** A bypassed or removed stage reports nothing. A chain edit is an edit (2.13) and changes the entry at the block it takes effect.
- **Freeze.** Freezing renders the chain with the instrument, so the latency a frozen track records (`frozen_latency`) includes `L_midi` at freeze time, and a frozen track's MIDI effects report nothing while frozen.
- **Offline render.** The offline render reads the same entry the same way.

**Low Latency mode.** Today it counts an instrument's latency on a track's path but only ever bypasses software audio inserts (`low_latency_bypass`, `crates/mix/src/low_latency.rs:36` onward). P4 keeps that rule for the MIDI chain:

- `L_midi` counts toward the track's path.
- No MIDI effect is ever bypassed by Low Latency.
- So with Humanize on a monitored track, Low Latency may bypass that track's latent audio inserts to meet its limit while Humanize stays active. The help says so, and that the remedy for tight live playing is to disable Humanize.

**When play equals export.** Playback and an offline render (bounce, freeze, capture) place the same events on the same frames when the effective chains and compensation are the same. The offline render does not inherit the live Low Latency bypass selection, so with Low Latency bypassing anything the two differ, as they do today for any latent insert. Live input is not part of an export.

**Humanize** reports `T` samples, its largest early shift (6.3).

- **Timing is not automatable.** Timing is the only parameter that changes the latency, so in the first cut it cannot be automated: an automated Timing would move the whole track's compensation as the lane moved.
  - Today `ParamInfo` has no automatable flag, so slice K2b adds one (default automatable).
  - It is enforced in three places: the automation menus never offer a lane for such a parameter; the control protocol refuses to create one; a session that carries one loads without it and the load says so.
- Timing is set by hand (panel, MIDI Learn, a preset). A change takes effect at the next block start like every hand move. The compensation follows from that block, so notes already scheduled keep their frames and the track's alignment steps once.
- This is the one exception to "every MIDI effect control can be automated", stated in its help as a first-cut restriction.

**Live input.** Nothing can be played before it is played. A live note through Humanize is delayed by `T + shift`, so with the default 10 ms it is heard on average 10 ms late, up to 20 ms. The help says so in those words and gives the remedy: record with the device in place, then capture the result (decision 9), or disable the device for tight live playing.

There is no late-only mode and no separate later slice for early timing: the first draft's slice H2 is folded into P4 and H1, and Humanize is not visible before P4 lands (Slices).

### 8. Composition

What a device assumes of its input: offsets sorted and below `frames` (the host's sort); any mix of identified and anonymous notes; same-key overlaps with or without voice ids; releases that close nothing; velocity-0 note-ons only from an upstream effect (normalized, 2.2). What it guarantees of its output: the invariant (2.1) for the notes it emitted; no note-off it did not owe (a release that matches nothing it owns or passed through is consumed, 2.4); its tables equal to what its output accepted (2.8, slice P3b); velocities 1..=127, offsets below `frames`, the order of 2.11, and transparency only where a decision states it (6.2; Chord in 3.3 and decision 13). Chord and the Arpeggiator never overlap a key with themselves; Humanize overlaps a key only where its input did (6.4).

That is not a proof that any chain composes: the chain's result also depends on the existing devices and the host. Known today: the Random Sequencer with Thru off swallows note-ons but forwards their note-offs, which can end a generated note of the same key early downstream, and with Thru on its forwarded and generated notes can overlap one key (`crates/dsp/src/midi_fx/random_sequencer.rs:271` to `276`; to be filed); Transpose loses a re-struck anonymous note (#555); the host's `out` table can match an anonymous note-off to an identified note (Context; slice P3b); Velocity rewrites a velocity-0 note-on into a sounding one (`crates/dsp/src/midi_fx/velocity.rs:95`), unreachable today because none reaches the chain; the test-only Delay can reorder a note-on and its note-off when its delay changes, with no user path. The evidence for composition is therefore the chain property test of decision 13 (slice X), which runs chains of the three devices with the three public existing effects (Transpose, Velocity, the Random Sequencer; the test-only Delay is left out), under the capacity bound and above it, through the host's lifecycle operations, after the prerequisites it names. Random permutations are evidence, not a proof.

### 9. Capturing what the chain plays (slice CAP)

Recording keeps capturing the input before the MIDI effects (both workstations record the played notes, from memory), so a take stays editable at the source. Keeping what the chain plays is a separate command, **Capture MIDI Effect Output**. It ships with the devices: it is a condition of the first device's visibility (decided by the maintainer, item 9).

**The promise is tick fidelity, not sample identity.** A MIDI clip stores notes and controllers at whole ticks (960 per quarter, `transit_midi::Sequence`, `crates/midi/src/sequence.rs`). Notes and controllers are kept in separate lists, and the clip renderer imposes its own order at one tick and its own pairing of same-key notes. So a captured clip cannot reproduce every event stream a chain can write, and this record does not claim it can. What the first cut promises, and what the help and the command's result line say:

- **Positions to the nearest tick.** Every event is placed at the tick nearest its heard frame, so on replay it sounds within half a tick of where the chain played it. That is 12.5 samples (about 0.26 ms) at 120 BPM and 48 kHz, and proportionally more at slower tempos. An event already on a tick is exact; Arpeggiator steps and gate ends at whole-tick lengths are such events.
- **Order at one tick is the clip renderer's.** Events that land on one tick replay in the clip renderer's order (today notes, then controllers; #559's first item may change it), not necessarily the order the instrument accepted.
- **Same-key overlaps replay as the clip renderer pairs them.** Two overlapping notes of one channel and key in the capture replay as a re-strike: the earlier note ends at the later note-on. Chord and the Arpeggiator never overlap a key with themselves, so their output has no such overlap. Humanize keeps only the overlaps its input had (6.4).
- **The user is told** in the result line ("notes placed to the nearest tick; overlapping notes of one key play as re-strikes"), and in the help.

Exact sample positions and full event order would need clips that store sample offsets and a shared event order. That is a representation change for every MIDI clip, and it is left to its own record, not done here (Where this goes).

**The time domain.** The capture records what the instrument accepted, in heard time:

- An accepted event at device output frame `x` is placed at `x − L_midi`, `L_midi` being the chain's reported latency (decision 7). So a note Humanize wrote at frame 441 with `T` = 480 is placed at −39, where the listener heard it. The new track has no MIDI effects, so replaying it needs no compensation for them.
- The render starts at the range start `a` with a playing transport, exactly as a bounce of that range starts. It runs to `b + L_midi`, so every event heard before the range end `b` is rendered.
- Kept: events heard in `[a, b)`.
- Dropped: an event heard before `a` (a note shifted earlier than the range start).
- Not captured: an event whose input lies before `a`. The render does not see it, as a bounce of the range does not.
- A note still sounding at `b` ends at `b`.

**What it records and writes.**

- **Range:** the time selection, else the extent of the track's clips on its active playlist.
- **Render:** the track is rendered offline with fresh instances through the same renderer as a bounce (`crates/mix/src/offline.rs`).
- **Recorded:** every event the instrument accepted (the accepted-note view of ADR 0038, plus the controllers, pitch bend, pressure and program changes it accepted).
- **Written:** one MIDI clip spanning the range. Notes and `CtrlEvent`s go at ticks relative to the clip start, each converted once through the tempo map from its heard frame and rounded to the nearest tick.

**Where it goes.**

- **A new track directly below the source.** It is made by Duplicate Track's logic (`track.duplicate`, `crates/engine/src/cmd/track.rs`, playlists off), extracted into a helper the capture command calls. Its MIDI effects and their automation lanes (slots 240 to 247) are removed.
- So the new track plays the captured clip through the same instrument and inserts.
- The source track is not changed: nothing is muted, bypassed or replaced (no processing the user did not ask for).
- This follows Ableton's way of keeping post-effect MIDI (another track recording the first track's output, from memory); Logic's way was not verified.

**Refusals.** Capture refuses whole, before any change:

- a track that is frozen (its duplicate would keep the frozen audio, not play the clip);
- a track with no MIDI effects;
- an empty range;
- a track that is not an instrument track.

**One undo step,** named "Capture MIDI Effect Output".

- The command runs inside `Engine::execute_scoped` (`crates/engine/src/lib.rs:836`), which snapshots the document and rolls back a failed command's changes.
- The offline render and every refusal check run before the first document change. The duplication helper and the clip placement follow inside the same scope.
- `preview` answers the same and changes nothing (Slice to MIDI's convention, `crates/engine/src/cmd/slice_to_midi.rs`).

**Determinism.**

- The capture is a function of the session and the range. The seeds, the canonical conversion and the timeline clocks make it the same at every block size (decision 4).
- The new track, played back, places each note within half a tick of where the source played it, under the overlap and order rules above.
- Live input is not captured (it is not in the session); record it first.

**Surface.** A command in the track's context menu and in the MIDI effect card's `⋯` menu, plus the control protocol's `mix.capture_midi_effect_output {track?, start?, end?, preview?}`. No other entry is added.

### 10. Key and scale

No session or track key exists; `KeyScale` is a per-clip editing aid that never touches playback. Scale-aware behavior (Chord shifts in scale degrees with diatonic qualities per degree, Arpeggiator distance in degrees) is a later slice that gives each device its own Root and Scale parameters, as the Random Sequencer has, with a Follow option once a session key or the chord track (#303) exists. The dependency is named here so the first cut does not invent a key model.

### 11. The shared kit

`crates/dsp/src/midi_fx/kit/` (slice K1), built on nothing from #292:

- `ledger.rs`: the input ledger of 2.3 and 2.8. **Capacity** 256 entries `{ state, channel, key, voice, seq: u64, payload }` (the payload fixed-size and per device: Chord's output keys, the Arpeggiator's held-key link, Humanize's output note). **Insertion** in the first free entry with the next sequence number; every note-on the device receives is inserted or rejected (2.3), pass-through notes included. **Closing.** A note-off with a voice id closes the Open or Orphaned entry of its channel with that id, and nothing else: there is no fallback to an anonymous entry (a conforming producer writes a note's note-off with its note-on's identity, 2.1, so an identified note-off that finds no entry belongs to a rejected note or to no note this device saw); it is consumed. A note-off without an id closes the oldest of the anonymous Open or Orphaned entries of its channel and key and the anonymous rejection records of its channel and key, by sequence number (oldest-first, the convention downstream pairs by); with none, it is consumed. **Rejection.** With 256 entries in use a note-on is rejected (not played, counted). An identified rejection needs no record: its note-off will find no entry with its id and be consumed. An anonymous rejection is recorded `{ channel, key, seq }` in a table of 256 rejection records, taking the next sequence number, so its note-off is matched in its oldest-first place among the entries of its key: older accepted notes close first, a note of that key admitted later closes after it, on time. **Saturation, fail-closed.** With the record table full, a further anonymous rejection is only counted (`rejected_unrecorded`, saturating); its note-off then closes the oldest anonymous entry of its key if one is open (that note ends early, by at most the rejected note's lifetime) and the entry's own note-off later finds nothing and is consumed; with no entry, the note-off is consumed. In no case is an unmatched note-off passed, ended late, or written twice, so saturation can only shorten a note. **Recovery:** `reset` (every host reset: Stop, panic, an edit, the safety reset of slice P3b) clears the entries, the records and the count; the host's epoch swallows the input note-offs of notes from before the reset, and any that reach the device find nothing and are consumed. `NotePairing` is not used: it counts in `u8`, drops the oldest open note at 255 and cannot remove by identity.
- `schedule.rs`: fixed queues of Pending events, due by absolute timeline frame (from decision 4's conversion) or by render-clock frame, with reservation (an on is admitted only with its off's entry reserved), `due(frame)` in the order of 2.11, cancellation, and the discontinuity handling of 2.8.
- `clock.rs`: the effective frame of decision 4 (through P0's view), the timeline continuity test, the tempo-edit re-conversion, the render clock, the stopped anchor, and `SyncDivision` grid steps with swing in centiticks.
- `draw.rs`: SplitMix64, the hash of 6.3, the uniforms and the Fisher-Yates shuffle of 5.3.
- `emit.rs`: writing through `MidiOut` with the order of 2.11 and refusal handling, relying on slice P3b's contract that `push` returns the final verdict (an accepted event is never evicted later): a refused note-on makes its output note Cancelled and frees its reservation; a refused note-off stays reserved and is written again at offset 0 of the next block, ahead of any later note-on of its key (the host's sink never refuses a release; a fixed buffer can).- Test support: a runner that feeds a timeline of input events at any block partition (including the host's between-block call once P2 lands) through a device or a chain and returns the events with their timeline frames; the invariant checker (every emitted note-on has exactly one note-off at or after it, matched oldest-first by channel and key, with its identity); an allocation probe in the style of `crates/dsp/tests/macro_osc_release_alloc.rs`.

### 16. What a hosted MIDI plug-in in the chain inherits (for #671)

Issue #671 asks to host third-party VST3 and CLAP plug-ins that write notes in the same MIDI effect chain; it needs its own record, written after this one, and nothing here designs it (how note output is read from each format, which plug-ins qualify for the slot, including an instrument-category plug-in whose real output is notes, their audio buses, editors, state and missing-plug-in behavior, and routing notes to another track). What this record fixes about the chain is stated here so that record can build on it. Each rule is enforced by the host, not by a well-behaved effect, unless it says otherwise, so a hosted plug-in is held to it without being trusted, within the limits each rule states:

1. **Final acceptance per hop.** A stage's output buffer accepts or refuses each event once, and an accepted event is never evicted afterwards; a non-release is admitted while the buffer holds fewer than 384 non-release events and fewer than 512 in all, a release while it holds fewer than 512 (P3b). A hosted stage's output is admitted by the same rule and its refusals are counted.
2. **The host owns delivery to the instrument.** After the last stage the host records every note it delivers (`out`), accounts for every note-on it does not deliver by consuming that note's note-off, consumes a note-off that matches nothing it delivered (note-offs only: pedal-ups and channel-mode controllers always pass), and matches oldest-first by channel and key, an identified release by its id only (P3b). What this guarantees: no note-off reaches the instrument for a channel and key with nothing outstanding from the chain, every note-on the host delivers gets at most one note-off from the host's table, and every note the chain delivered is ended at a reset. What it does not guarantee: that a note-off belongs to the note it ends. The host matches by identity and oldest-first, which proves an entry exists, not that the release answers it. A hosted stage that writes an anonymous C on, its off, another anonymous C on and then a duplicate of the first off ends the second C early. A guarantee against that needs provenance per note (a release tied to the note-on it answers), which this record does not specify; the #671 record decides whether a hosted stage gets it, for example through a host-side ledger around the stage.
3. **Every chain edit, Stop, panic and the safety reset end the chain's notes from the host's own table,** and an edit releases every pedal the instrument holds and presses none again (2.13, 2.14, P3a, P3b). A plug-in that writes a note-on and never its note-off is ended there; a hosted stage is reset (its format's reset or an equivalent the #671 record names) at the same barrier as every other stage, and none resumes an older batch.
4. **Ownership inside a stage is the stage's own.** The built-in devices own every note they let through and consume releases that match nothing they own (2.3, 2.4); that is an amendment of the trait contract for effects that declare it (K2b), and the existing effects keep passing unknown releases. A hosted plug-in cannot be shown to own its notes, so it is treated as the existing effects are, and the host rules 1 to 3 are its guarantee; whether the #671 record adds a ledger around a hosted stage is its decision.
5. **Time.** Each stage gets its events sorted by offset with offsets below `frames`; an event written at or past `frames` waits in that stage's carry of 64. With P0 a stage may read the prepared tempo table (`Timeline`, `process_in`); with P2 events between blocks reach every stage through a call in which no time passes; with P1 parameters are read on the 64-frame timeline grid. A hosted plug-in gets the format's own transport and tempo information from the same transport the built-ins see.
6. **Latency.** With P4 every stage may report latency (`MidiEffect::latency`), summed over the active, unbypassed stages and added to the instrument's for delay compensation; a hosted plug-in's reported latency enters the same sum.
7. **Capacity.** The bound of decision 4 (384 non-release and 512 events per window, 256 notes outstanding at the instrument) is the chain's, whoever writes the events; above it the invariant and the counters hold and partitions may differ.
8. **Refusal by the instrument** is ADR 0059's for every chain, hosted stages included.
9. **Capture MIDI Effect Output** records what the instrument accepted, so it covers a hosted stage with no change, with the same tick fidelity, same-tick order and same-key overlap limits (decision 9).

- **CP, AP, HP, the panels.** *Start from:* 14.2 to 14.6 and K2b's panel hook; the design language. *Done when:* 14.6's assertions at 1.0 and 1.5 pixels per point for the controls of the device's first slice (C1, A1 with the Play toggle, H1); the factory presets load; the images have been shown to the maintainer. *Out of scope:* device windows; a live step display; Chord's Learn controls (C2), its mode and range controls (C2) and its strum, chance and seed controls (C3), which those slices add to the chord panel with the same assertions.
- **CAP, Capture MIDI Effect Output.** *Start from:* decision 9; `track.duplicate` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/track.rs`, its duplication extracted into a helper), `Engine::execute_scoped` (`crates/engine/src/lib.rs:836`), Slice to MIDI's command shape (`crates/engine/src/cmd/slice_to_midi.rs`), the offline render. *Done when* (with the fixture effects of decision 13, one of them reporting a latency of 480): the captured clip holds, at the nearest tick to each heard frame (`x − L_midi`), every event the instrument accepted in a bounce of the range, at block sizes 1, 7, 64 and 512; **the captured clip is replayed** on the new track and every event its instrument accepts lies within half a tick of the source bounce's heard frame, exactly on it where that frame is a tick, with the same velocity and controller value; an event heard before the range start is absent and a note sounding at the range end ends there; a same-key overlap replays as a re-strike; the new track sits below the source with no MIDI effects and no MIDI effect lanes; the source is unchanged; one undo step restores the session exactly; a frozen track and the other refusals refuse before any change and `preview` changes nothing. *Out of scope:* capturing live input; capturing onto the same track; sample-exact capture (Where this goes).
- **X, the chain property test.** *Done when:* decision 13's chain property test runs in CI with its listed combinations; combinations failing on a filed defect of an existing device are listed in the test with their issue.
- **V, visibility.** *Done when:* the row's needs have landed; the id moves to `REGISTRY`; the menu lists the device.

## Where this goes (not decided; each line is for the maintainer to pick, as its own record or slice)

- A Scale device: force or fold notes into a key, and the session key it would read (decision 10).
- Scale-aware Chord and Arpeggiator: shifts and distance in scale degrees, diatonic chord qualities per degree.
- A chord progression generator: a sequence of chords per bar from a key, with a seed, feeding Chord's trigger memory or the chord track (#303).
- A melody generator: notes in a key from a rhythm and a contour, seeded and re-rollable (the Random Sequencer is one already; a scale-first one is another).
- Note probability: a per-note chance device, and per-step skip and chance in the Arpeggiator.
- Sample-exact capture: MIDI clips that store sample offsets and one shared event order, so Capture MIDI Effect Output can reproduce a chain's output exactly (decision 9 states the first cut's tick fidelity).
- A live step display for the Arpeggiator that follows the playing step (the proposed well shows the pattern, not the position).
- A note-repeat or ratchet device (#313).
- Arpeggiating hardware: the chain on a MIDI track's external output.
- MIDI effects in device windows with the full design-language layout.

## Consequences

- An instrument track gains the three tools most asked of a MIDI effect slot, each deterministic for a stated input: a bounce plays the same notes as playback given a matching input history, order and start, and an idea found by re-rolling is kept by keeping its seed.
- Every new device owns its input notes through explicit states in a ledger of its own, pairing oldest-first by channel and key and by identity where it is given.
- The host gains four things every MIDI effect benefits from: a tempo table any deadline converts through, parameters exact on a timeline grid (frozen while stopped), a clean between-blocks call, and note ownership in which every refused note-on is reported or accounted for, and every chain edit ends the track's notes and releases the pedals the instrument holds.
- Humanize moves notes earlier and later; the track's compensation grows by its Timing, and live playing through it is late by that amount on average, which the help states.
- MIDI effects can report latency (P4); every existing effect reports none.
- Stop ends everything a device holds; the Arpeggiator runs while stopped only with its Play control on.
- What the chain plays can be kept as an editable clip (Capture MIDI Effect Output) from the first visible device on.
- A new Chord does nothing until a shift is set; the chords are in the factory presets.
- The Arpeggiator does not drive external MIDI until the chain reaches a MIDI track's output.
- MIDI effects become one card each in the device chain at the shared card height, the existing three included, so adding a device widens the chain and never grows the pane; sections are side-by-side columns, and a card folds whole to a strip.
- The device chain scrolls horizontally when it is wider than the pane; the rack card's member list scrolls inside the card; the External Instrument card takes the shared height; as the panel design hand-off proposes, any card folds to a narrow strip.
- A chain edit is audible as a cut: every note of the track goes to its release, pedal-held ones included, and a pedal held through the edit has no effect until it is pressed again.
- With an instrument that refuses events the host keeps today's bounded retry, with the refusal and adapter limits stated in 2.13 (#600, #602); the guarantee is ADR 0059's (MIDI event delivery under refusal, proposed), and the devices become visible only after its adapter slice R1.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Separate Chord, Chord Trigger and Hold devices.** Three devices for one idea, and Hold would sit between them with its own replacement rule; the maintainer asked for one device.
- **A hold inside every device with its own rules, or a "held upstream" flag between devices.** Withheld note-offs already tell every later device that a key is down (3.6).
- **Folding out-of-range chord notes by octaves.** Changes the voicing silently; dropping is Transpose's rule.
- **Restarting the Arpeggiator's pattern on every key press by default.** Neither workstation does (Ableton's Retrigger defaults to Off).
- **Beat-to-offset conversion relative to the block start (the Random Sequencer's form).** Its result depends on where a block starts (Context); one absolute conversion cannot.
- **Building the ledger on `NotePairing`.** It cannot remove by identity, counts in `u8` and drops the oldest at 255.
- **Comparing timeline positions while stopped.** The stopped position does not advance, so every block would look like a jump.
- **Humanize from a running random stream.** A stream advances with every note, so a loop pass, a locate or a bounce from another start would draw differently; a hash of seed and position is the same every time.
- **Writing a note undelayed when Humanize's schedule is full.** It could overtake an earlier delayed note of its key.
- **Normal (Gaussian) timing.** Unbounded without clipping, and needs transcendental functions (ADR 0055); a triangular draw is bounded and integer.
- **Humanize early timing by delaying the whole chain without reporting latency.** The track would drift late against the others.
- **Late-only Humanize (the first draft).** Moves a whole part later on average; the maintainer chose centered timing with reported latency (item 4).
- **An Arpeggiator that runs while stopped with no control (the first draft).** Notes keep coming after Stop with nothing on screen saying why; Logic's Play control was chosen (item 5).
- **A major triad as Chord's default (the first draft).** Adding a device would change the sound before the user asked for anything (item 2).
- **An automatable Humanize Timing.** Its lane would move the track's whole delay compensation as it moved (decision 7).
- **Capturing the chain's output onto the source track.** The captured notes would play through the chain a second time, or the chain would have to be bypassed for the user (decision 9).
- **Stacking every MIDI effect as rows of one card (today's MIDI Effects card).** The pane grows to the tallest card, so three devices with several sections each would make it over 1,000 points tall; the maintainer chose one card per effect at one height (14.1).
- **Using the host's carry for scheduled notes.** 64 entries shared by a stage; the Random Sequencer's own-schedule pattern keeps the carry free.
- **Settling delivery under refusal in this record.** Rounds 3 to 6 each added host machinery for instruments that refuse events (owed note-offs in order, owed pedal-ups behind them, a pending later value delivered in a later block, reset changes in the adapters), and each next round broke it while the devices were accepted; the concern belongs to every chain and every instrument format, so it is ADR 0059's (MIDI event delivery under refusal, proposed).

## Decided by the maintainer (2026-11-13)

The first draft ended with nineteen open questions. The maintainer answered all of them on 2026-11-13, and added four device behaviors the draft had not listed (D1 to D4). Each answer is written into the decision it changes; this list is the one place that records them. Where an answer rests on what Ableton Live or Logic Pro do, the source is named; "from memory" marks a claim not checked against the manual for this record. The project rule is to follow what the two agree on unless transit can do clearly better.

1. **The chord device is called Chord.** Ableton's name; the modes say the rest (1).
2. **Six interval slots, all empty when the device is added.** Every shift is 0 (off), so adding Chord changes nothing until a shift is set: no processing the user did not ask for. The draft's recommendation, a major triad, is not taken. The factory presets carry the chords (3.2, 3.9).
3. **Chord memory is the second chord slice (C2), not the first.** Intervals and Hold come first (C1).
4. **Humanize timing is centered: notes move earlier and later.** The device reports a latency equal to its largest early shift, and delay compensation aligns the track by it; live input through Humanize is late by that latency on average, which the help says plainly, with the advice to capture the notes and disable the device for tight live playing (decision 7). This needs MIDI effects to report latency (slice P4), and Humanize is not visible before it lands. Neither workstation has a centered real-time humanize to follow (Ableton has none; Logic's Transform Humanize edits the notes once); this is a transit choice.
5. **The Arpeggiator does not run while the transport is stopped unless its Play control is on.** Off by default: while stopped, played notes pass through unarpeggiated. With Play on, the Arpeggiator runs from live input on the free clock while stopped. This is Logic's convention ("When Logic Pro is stopped: Arpeggio playback stops", and its Play button starts the arpeggio while stopped; Apple's latch and sync page). Ableton's arpeggiator is believed to run from live input while stopped (from memory, not verified for this record); the two disagree, and Logic's is chosen because a device that keeps generating notes after Stop with no control showing why would surprise the user (5.4, 5.11).
6. **While playing, the first arpeggiated note waits for the next grid step.** Neither workstation's first-note behavior was verified for this record (Logic's Input Snap setting governs it; its page was not read); this is a transit choice, revisited with a snap setting if it feels late (5.4).
7. **Humanize gives the same result on every loop pass.** Re-roll changes it. Re-roll is one undo step, and undo restores the previous seed (6.5, 6.6).
8. **MIDI effects stay cards in the device chain for the first cut.** Device windows for MIDI effects are a later step, if a card proves too small (14.1).
9. **Capture MIDI Effect Output ships with the devices.** It is a condition of the first device becoming visible, not a later feature: a found idea is only useful once it can be kept and edited (decision 9, slice CAP).
10. **A stored chord holds up to 8 keys** (C2).
11. **In the Arpeggiator, Hold wins over the sustain pedal** (5.9).
12. **Humanize defaults:** Timing 10 ms, Velocity ±10, Length ±10 % (6.1). With centered timing the average shift is zero.
13. **A preset recall keeps the current seed, and an add from the "+" menu writes a fresh seed** (6.6).
14. **The MIDI effect host under overload (every chain):** final stage admission with a 384 non-release quota and 512 slots, and host accounting for every refused note-on (slice P3b).
15. **Every chain edit ends the track's sounding notes, releases every pedal the instrument holds and presses none again** (2.13, slice P3a).
16. **A device added while the lane is full opens unfolded** and is scrolled into view (14.7).
17. **Cards fold whole to a strip; the section toggles inside the MIDI card are retired** (14.1, K2a).
18. **The stock cards keep their content top-aligned at the shared height** for K2a (14.7).
19. **ADR 0059's adapter slice R1 is a prerequisite of every visibility slice**, and the devices ship with the remaining limits of 2.13 stated. The maintainer also decided ADR 0059's own questions on 2026-11-13: full ownership (R1 and R2), with R2 in the release that makes the first device visible (Slices, Order).
- **D1. Stop stops everything.** After Stop nothing a MIDI device generated or holds is sounding, and nothing sounds again until the user plays something. Latched and held sets are cleared at Stop. The maintainer: "It would be surprising if you hit stop on transport ... and notes continued sounding." Logic stops its arpeggio at Stop (2.14, 5.11).
- **D2. With Hold on, playing a latched note again removes it from the pattern** (Ableton's Hold: "To remove notes, play them a second time") (5.7).
- **D3. One Chance per interval slot** in slice C3, six values; the played note always sounds (3.8).
- **D4. Learn works both ways:** in Intervals mode as Ableton's Learn (the first key is the root, later keys fill the shifts), in the Trigger modes as Logic's note-by-note Learn (3.7).

## Judging rounds

The first draft (2026-11-12) went through eight judging rounds on 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13, and ADR 0059's five rounds corrected three of its sentences. Their decisions are in the text above; what each round changed, and where the judge was followed with a choice, is kept in [0057/judging-rounds.md](0057/judging-rounds.md). The main turns: one canonical, block-independent conversion from musical position to frame and a prepared tempo table (rounds 1 and 2); explicit note ownership with consumed unmatched releases and per-hop final acceptance (rounds 1 to 3); the chain-edit rule that releases every pedal and presses none again (rounds 4 and 5); and the split of delivery under refusal into ADR 0059 (round 6), with ADR 0059's adapter slice R1 gating visibility (round 7). The revision of 2026-11-26 had one judging round of its own, which asked for: the chain's latency kept out of the note readers' alignment, with planning, freeze and Low Latency covered (decision 7); Capture's fidelity, time domain and boundaries stated honestly, with a replay test (decision 9); ownership of pass-through notes across the Arpeggiator's Play changes and a count-in entered while playing (5.11); Learn's entry and exit ownership and what it observes (3.7); a narrower claim of what the host guarantees for a hosted stage (decision 16); and early slices tested with fixture effects instead of devices that do not exist yet (decision 13, Slices).
