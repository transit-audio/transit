# ADR 0056: Audio clip editor

- **Status:** accepted 2026-11-12 (proposed 2026-11-12; settled after six judging rounds with edits, the revisions listed under Judging rounds 1 to 6, then three requirements brought in line with ADR 0058's later revisions (After judging, below); the product choices decided by the maintainer on 2026-11-12: the recommendations of the fourteen open questions, Decided by the maintainer, with the clip fade law the closed law, which ADR 0058 renders; after acceptance, the same day, a convention audit checked every decided question against what Ableton Live and Logic Pro document, and the maintainer decided four changes from its findings: the tape-style transpose labeled as such, the macOS slip chord, the file's own format in the header, and corrected convention statements (After acceptance: the convention audit, below))
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Implementation:** not started. Slices in the Slices section. Issue #515 (this decision); it absorbs the Reverse and Normalize rows of #181 and decides #427's question for audio that is not warped; the fix tracked in #517 (with #501), this record's slice 0 until round 3, is now the clip slice of ADR 0058; slice 1 is the fix for #554 (P2, v0.7.2), which lands ahead of the editor; it depends on #224 for the arrangement's range gesture, #477 for a source shared with a Sampler, and #506 for the peaks job of a disk-backed source.
- **Reopen when:** the warping record needs a clip field or a mapping this record fixed differently (decision 12); the reader misses its acceptance bounds or its cost budget (decision 8); an amendment to ADR 0058 changes where a fade or an envelope point sits in clip time or what an edit does to it (decision 9); the maintainer wants several audio clips edited together, or comping, in the editor; a user reports that a transposed clip changing its length surprised them, or that a transpose there and back did not give the clip back; 44.1 kHz sessions are reported dull at the top (the 48-tap kernel of decision 8.7 is the upgrade, to be measured densely first); or the reference workstations are found to agree on a behavior this record chose differently (the claims marked unverified in Context).

## Context

Issue #515, decided by the maintainer on 2026-11-12 and not reopened here: transit gets an audio clip editor. Double-click an audio clip and it opens in the bottom pane, the way a MIDI clip opens in the piano roll. Editing there is non-destructive: the audio file is never changed. "Similar to Ableton / Logic." The editor comes first; warping (stretch markers, follow tempo) comes after as its own record and release and is out of scope, except that the stored form of a clip must leave room for it without a later format break. It is the probable flagship of v0.8.0.

### What a clip is today (read at 474e5850)

- **One `Clip` for audio and MIDI** (`crates/model/src/clip.rs:125-189`). Positions are `Samples` (`i64`, `crates/time/src/lib.rs:20`) at the session rate, bounded by `MAX_POSITION` = 2^40 (`crates/time/src/lib.rs:24`, `clip.rs:375-405`). An audio clip's content is `ClipContent::Audio { source: SourceId, offset: Samples }` (`clip.rs:83-90`); `start` and `length` place it; `offset` is where in the source its first sample is. The source record holds path, channels, frames, rate and format (`crates/model/src/source.rs:10-30`) and stays in the session when its file is missing; the decoded audio is the unsaved pool (`source.rs:32-103`).
- **Fields an editor would show already exist:** `gain_db` (static clip gain, dB), `gain_env` (dB breakpoints from the clip start, linear in dB, added to `gain_db`: `clip.rs:139-143`, `env_value` at `clip.rs:420-436`, a linear scan), `fade_in` and `fade_out` (`Fade { len, shape, curvature }`, shapes Linear, EqualPower (default), SCurve, Exponential, Logarithmic: `clip.rs:7-81`), `muted`, `name`, `color`, `sync_point`, `looping: Option<ClipLoop>` (ADR 0019, clip time, `clip.rs:92-123`), `take` (ADR 0039) and `whole_fades`. A crossfade is two overlapping clips with fades (`crates/engine/src/edit.rs:811-864`).
- **No reverse and no pitch.** The doc comment "Reversed playback (AudioSuite Reverse renders into a new source; this is for previews)" sits on the `group` field (`clip.rs:165-167`), a clip-group id; no reverse field has ever existed. `stretch: f64` (`clip.rs:162-164`, documented as "applied by rendered processing") is in fact applied live as a varispeed with two-point linear interpolation and no anti-aliasing (`StreamMap::sample`, `crates/model/src/disk.rs:164-180`): the clip reads source position `offset + rel / stretch`. Only the inherited control-channel commands `clip.conform_to_tempo` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/clip_more.rs:223-258`) and `clip.elastic_properties` on a track without Elastic Audio (`crates/engine/src/cmd/clip.rs:231-235`) set it, clamped to 0.05..20; `sanitize` accepts any finite positive value (`clip.rs:402-403`). Warp, tempo and pitch bookkeeping live in `edit.values` string keys (`elastic.<id>.*`, `clip.bpm.<id>`, `clip.len0.<id>`, `clip.pitch.<id>`: `clip.rs:223-246`, `clip_more.rs:239-267`), and so do clip effects (`clip_fx.<clip>.*`, `crates/mix/src/clips.rs:89-104`).
- **Edits assume rate 1.** A start trim and a split add the clip-time phase straight to `offset` (`Clip::trim_start_to`, `clip.rs:313-327`; `split_clip`, `edit.rs:36-66`); `heal` joins pieces when `offset + length` meets the next offset (`edit.rs:441-466`); the trim bound, `material_len` and the crossfade handles count clip samples as file frames (`edit.rs:775-834`); so do `trim_to_file` and `trim_to_fill` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/edit.rs:725-760`) and the arrangement's waveform origin (`crates/ui-studio/src/waveform.rs:29,58`). Only the loop range check of `clip.set_loop` divides by the stretch (`cmd/clip.rs:461-469`). A clip with `stretch ≠ 1` therefore jumps when split: with stretch 2 and offset 100, the unsplit clip reads source positions 150, 150.5, 151 ... at clip times 100, 101, 102, and today's right piece reads 200, 200.5, 201 (computed; filed as #554, P2, milestone v0.7.2, to be fixed ahead of the editor: slice 1).
- **The session format** is serde JSON; new fields take `#[serde(default)]`, unknown fields are ignored and dropped on save (`docs/contributing/knowledge/session-format.md`), `format_version` is 1 (`crates/model/src/session.rs:392`) and a feature an older build would lose goes in `needs` ([ADR 0023](0023-session-compatibility-markers.md); `KNOWN_NEEDS` at `session.rs:404-405`, computed over every playlist of every track, so inactive playlists and stored takes count).

### How a clip renders

- **One path for playback and bounce.** `render_audio_clips` (`crates/mix/src/clips.rs:33-56`) calls `render_clip` (`clips.rs:112-136`), which reads the source through `StreamMap::sample` (in memory, from the disk ring on the audio thread, or synchronously offline) and sums with `mix_clip` (`clips.rs:140-175`). Bounce compiles the same plan (`crates/mix/src/offline.rs:48-261`). The loop wrap is integer math before the source read (`content_pos`, `clip.rs:118-123`).
- **Gain and fades** are one function, `Clip::gain_at` (`clip.rs:289-310`): `db_to_gain(gain_db + env)` times the fade gains. Fade shapes use `sin`, `cos` and `exp2` through the platform (`clip.rs:22-31`, `61-69`).
- **A defect of the #501 class, now #517.** `mix_clip` evaluates `gain_at` every 32 samples and interpolates linearly between the first and last sample of each 32-sample segment, and the segments start at each block's first frame (`clips.rs:144-170`). A curved fade or a gain envelope therefore renders differently at different block sizes (computed for a 300-sample S-curve fade-in: the gains rendered at block sizes 97 and 512 differ by up to 6.2·10⁻³). The interpolation also flattens short fades: a 32-sample equal-power crossfade renders as a linear one, a 3.0 dB dip at its center (0.67 dB at 64 samples, 0.27 dB at 100; computed). No test covers fades or envelopes across block sizes (the loop test `audio_wraps_at_the_seam_sample_accurately_and_in_any_block_size`, `crates/mix/src/clip_loop_tests.rs:23-53`, uses neither).
- **A fade-out's last sample is not silent.** `from_end = length - rel` is at least 1 inside the clip (`clip.rs:305-307`), so the last sample of a fade-out is `gain(1/len)` (0.01 for a 100-sample linear fade), while a fade-in's first sample is `gain(0)` = 0 and its last sample is `gain((len - 1)/len)`, not 1. This half-open law is exactly complementary in a crossfade (computed in `f32` for every length up to 4096: every pair of linear arguments sums to exactly 1.0).
- **Sample rates.** Timeline sources are resampled to the session rate when they enter the pool, by a Kaiser-windowed sinc (32 zero crossings, `crates/dsp/src/offline.rs:112-223`), at import and again at every open (`crates/engine/src/io.rs:236-299`, `crates/engine/src/io/session.rs:286-290`). The resampled buffer's rate replaces the file's (`io.rs:272-282`) and `add_source` stores that rate and frame count (`io.rs:189-192`): **a timeline source's `sample_rate` is the session rate it is stored at, and the file's own rate is not kept.** Only Sampler sources keep their native rate (ADR 0041). A disk-backed source must be at the session rate (`crates/engine/src/io/disk.rs:47-63`).
- **Memory.** Sources are decoded into RAM; long takes are disk-backed ([ADR 0035](0035-disk-backed-audio-sources.md)), read by a streamer into rings already mapped through loop and stretch. Its file cache refills from four frames behind a miss (`disk.rs:337-350`), which suits forward reads only. A missing file leaves the source out of the pool and the clip silent (`clips.rs:117`), with a "missing media" load note (`io/session.rs:286-295`).
- **Paths that render a clip into a new file and repoint it** keep different subsets of the clip's fields. AudioSuite (`crates/engine/src/cmd/audiosuite.rs:69-132`) renders the clip as it plays, loop and stretch included, then repoints it and drops its loop, **but keeps its `stretch`**, so a stretched clip's render plays stretched a second time (an existing defect, also #554). Gain rendering resets the stretch (`io.rs:558-591`). The full audit is decision 4.

### The arrangement and the bottom pane

- **Gestures** (`crates/ui-studio/src/arrange.rs:80-124`): move, copy-drag, trim edges, the loop handle, fade corners and curve bends; one command at release (`arrange.rs:668-682`). Split is Ctrl+E. A body drag with Ctrl or Alt (Cmd or Option on macOS) copies (`drag_copies`, `arrange.rs:548-550`) and Shift bypasses the snap (`arrange.rs:554-556`). **There is no slip** (moving content under a fixed clip window): no gesture and no command changes `offset` in place. "Slip" in the code is the inherited edit mode (`EditMode::Slip`, `session.rs:48`). **No clip gain handle**: the engine has `clip.gain`, `clip.gain_render` and the gain-envelope setters (`crates/engine/src/cmd/clip.rs:90-113`), but nothing in the UI issues them; the arrangement draws the waveform scaled by `gain_at` (`crates/ui-studio/src/waveform.rs:30,59`).
- **Double-click** (`arrange.rs:700-737`): on a fade, reset it; on a clip's name strip, rename; on a MIDI clip's body, open the piano roll (several selected MIDI clips open together, #329); **on an audio clip's body, nothing** (`open_keys` refuses non-MIDI content, `crates/ui-studio/src/keys.rs:370-372`).
- **The bottom pane.** Tabs Piano Roll, Mixer and Devices (`crates/ui-studio/src/keys/view.rs:266-317`). The piano roll, when shown, replaces the lower panes (`crates/ui-studio/src/frame_logic.rs:460-464`); Mixer and Devices are independent and may both be open (`crates/ui-studio/src/menus.rs:979-989`, `docs/user-guide.md`). The piano roll opens by double-click or Ctrl+Alt+K (the `TogglePianoRoll` row, `crates/ui-studio/src/shortcuts.rs:401-408`) and does not follow a single-click selection (`keys.rs:564-581`; `click_clip` never touches it). A **clip inspector** strip shows when exactly one audio clip is selected and the roll is closed (`crates/ui-studio/src/clip_inspector.rs:89-92,172-184`): Fade In, Fade Out and a **Crossfade** field that sets both partners of an overlap to one length, shows a custom marker when the two ends differ and refuses when the clip overlaps nothing or the length is not above zero (`clip_inspector.rs:122-166,195-205`), all typed in the main counter's format.
- **Time-range selection** (#224) is not built: the model and engine have `edit.selection` and `edit.select {tracks, start, end, exact?}` (`crates/engine/src/cmd/edit.rs:59`); without `exact` it widens the tracks to every active edit group's members (`cmd/edit.rs:480-493`); Crop, Separate and Fade to Start/End act on tracks and ranges, so on every clip they cross (`cmd/edit.rs:99-142`); no gesture sets a range apart from a clip (`crates/ui-studio/src/edit_tools.rs:18-19`).
- **Commands** are namespaced string ids in `specs()` tables (`crates/engine/src/cmd/mod.rs:102-220`); undo is a whole-document snapshot per command (`crates/engine/src/lib.rs:164-188,738-740`) with merge keys for runs (`lib.rs:799-872`). A command that changes a frozen track's material is refused by one check after it runs (`edits_frozen`, `crates/engine/src/cmd/freeze.rs:47-49`, comparing serialized track inputs), so new clip fields are covered without new code. UI-only commands are stubs in `crates/engine/src/cmd/view.rs` handled in `StudioApp::run`. Help entries are `docs/help/*.toml` with an `Id` per entry (`crates/ui-studio/src/help/ids.rs`). Files near the 1500-line budget: `keys.rs` 1323, `cmd/edit.rs` 1294, `cmd/edit_more.rs` 1203.

### Parts to reuse

- **Waveform data.** In-memory sources carry an exact min/max/RMS mipmap (`Peaks`, level 0 of 32 samples, fan-out 4, `crates/audio-io/src/peaks.rs:3-63`) whose `spans` stay connected at any zoom (`peaks.rs:126-140`). A disk-backed source has a single-level sidecar of 1024-frame buckets (`crates/model/src/disk/peaks.rs:14`). The arrangement draws one column per physical pixel and a polyline at one sample per column or less (`waveform.rs:60-156`).
- **The Sampler's views** draw their waveform inline (`crates/ui-studio/src/sampler_panel/slices/bench.rs:547-572`, `instrument/zone_wave.rs:190-238`) through `slices::wave::columns` (`slices/wave.rs:12`), with a viewport of zoom and center (`slices/viewport.rs`), a time ruler (`bench.rs:931-954`), the status tag, the missing-media card and the drop target (`bench.rs:436-488`). There is **no waveform, marker, handle or ruler widget** in `crates/ui-studio/src/widgets/`.
- **The transient detector** `detect_transients(ch, rate, sensitivity)` (`crates/dsp/src/offline.rs:382`; spectral flux on 24 bands an octave, 30 ms minimum gap, `delta = 2.5·2^((0.5 - s)·4)`), run off the UI thread by the Slices view's worker (`crates/engine/src/slice_detect.rs`) and on rendered clips by `io::transients_in` (`io.rs:421-427`). **Zero-crossing snap** exists for slice markers: nearest crossing within ±5 ms in any channel (`crates/model/src/slicing.rs:452-458`).
- **The playhead from voices** reaches the UI through atomics in the scope tap (`crates/mix/src/scope.rs:193-219`); the transport playhead is the `cursor` token.
- **A reversing, rate-stepping reader** exists in the Sampler: four-point cubic, reverse entry at `end - 1`, one step for pitch and rate (`crates/sampler-core/src/playhead.rs:1-30`). #474 records its aliasing when pitched up.
- **Offline tools** (`crates/dsp/src/offline.rs`: normalize, gain, invert, reverse, fade, resample, WSOLA time stretch and pitch shift) run through `audiosuite.process`. No UI calls them.
- **The portable math seam** `transit-math` (ADR 0055) is on main: `transit_math::f32` and `f64` (`sin`, `cos`, `exp2`, `log10`, `sqrt`, ...) and `transit_math::gain::db_to_gain` (f64 inside, rounded once; 0 dB is exactly 1.0).

**The common core** (both): a double-click opens a docked editor at the bottom of the main window; the clip or region is a window onto a file and its start, end, gain, pitch and fades are settings on it; fades and crossfades are editable curves; changing the file is a separate, explicit step that reaches every clip on that file; a stretch layer (Warp, Flex) is its own mode. **The differences:** Ableton's reverse writes a new file, Logic's is a playback flag; Ableton's editor shows the sample, Logic's shows the track's timeline; Ableton has clip envelopes, Logic region automation; Logic has a built-in destructive editor, Ableton hands off to another application. **Not a consensus:** a sampler-style transpose that changes a clip's length. It differs from Logic Pro, whose region Transpose is a pitch control its documentation never ties to a length change, and Ableton's manual does not document what transposing an unwarped clip does to its length.

## Decision

### 1. Where it opens

1. **One Editor tab for both kinds** (decided by the maintainer, question 1). The bottom pane's Piano Roll tab becomes **Editor**: it shows the piano roll for a MIDI clip and the audio editor for an audio clip, as Ableton's one Clip View and Logic's editor area both choose the editor by the clip's content. The pane keeps the piano roll's place in the frame (it replaces the lower panes while shown, `frame_logic.rs:460-464`); Mixer and Devices keep their independence (`menus.rs:979-989`). Ctrl+Alt+K keeps its key: the existing `TogglePianoRoll` row (`shortcuts.rs:401-408`) becomes `ToggleEditor` with the same chord, and its action toggles the Editor for the selected clip, MIDI or audio; no second binding is added.
2. **Double-click an audio clip's body opens it** (the branch at `arrange.rs:727-735` opens the audio editor where it now does nothing). Double-click on the name strip still renames and on a fade still resets it. Before this record the gesture did nothing on an audio body, so nothing is taken away.
3. **One audio clip at a time; MIDI as today.** A double-click inside a selection of several clips opens the double-clicked clip. Several selected MIDI clips still open together in the piano roll by double-click or Ctrl+Alt+K (#329, `keys.rs:564-581`), unchanged; several audio clips do not (decision 11). A clip in a clip group opens alone (groups are not drawn by the UI today). A take stack opens its active take's clip; an inactive take's lane keeps its click-to-choose behavior.
4. **The Editor follows a single-clip selection while it is open**, for MIDI too (decided by the maintainer, question 2), as Ableton's does ("the Clip View always shows the currently selected clip", Clip View chapter; Logic's editors following the selection is known only from search-index snippets, not confirmed from the vendor's current guide): a click that selects exactly one clip shows it, switching between the piano roll and the audio editor by kind. A selection of several clips, an empty selection, or a track-only selection (tracks picked by their headers with no clip selected) leaves the editor where it is. While several MIDI clips are open together, a click that selects one of them keeps the group open with that clip in front (a choice of this record, so following never closes a #329 group by surprise); a click on any other single clip shows that clip alone. This changes the piano roll, which today does not follow.
5. **The clip inspector strip stays until the editor has its parity**, then retires in its own slice (decided by the maintainer, "only after inspector parity"; question 5). Parity means the editor's property column has, with the same behavior: Fade In and Fade Out lengths; the **Crossfade** length field that sets both partners of an overlap, shows the custom marker when they differ, and refuses when the clip overlaps nothing or the length is not above zero (`clip_inspector.rs:122-166,195-205`); entry in the main counter's format; Tab, Enter and Escape as the strip handles them; commit on Enter or focus loss. One thing the strip does that the editor will not: it shows on a plain selection without opening anything. After retirement those fields are one double-click (or Ctrl+Alt+K) away; that is the trade the maintainer accepted.
6. **Closing** the Editor restores the panes that were shown, as closing the piano roll does (`keys.rs:584-594`), and returns keyboard focus to the arrangement with the edited clip still selected. A deleted or undone clip closes the audio editor (the piano roll hands over to the next open clip, `keys/view.rs:8-26`; the audio editor shows one clip, so it closes).

### 2. What the editor shows

The audio editor is a property column at the left and a waveform display at the right, both in the design language (decision 16).

1. **The axis is the timeline.** The display's horizontal axis is session time, and the source is drawn where the clip plays it, through the clip's mapping (decision 5): each source frame is drawn at the clip time where the mapping reads it, extended past the region as if the region covered the whole file. The region (the part that sounds) is drawn bright; the rest of the source, before and after, is drawn dim (`ink.ghost`, "off, still editable", never the hatch). A reversed clip draws the source reversed and a transposed clip draws it scaled, so what is seen is what plays. Logic's editor shows the timeline; Ableton's unwarped sample editor maps the sample to time. Showing the source beyond the region, on the timeline, is where transit does better than a file-only view: a trim or slip is seen against the bars it lands on.
2. **Two rulers.** Bars and beats from the session's tempo map above the display (the arrangement's grid, its lines through the display); the source position below it, in Min:Secs from the file's start, computed from source frames at the source's stored rate (`Source::sample_rate`, which for a timeline source is the session rate it was resampled to; durations are the file's, since resampling keeps them, so the ruler needs no file rate). The header shows the file's own format (2.9). The grid follows the arrangement's grid setting.
3. **Zoom and scroll** are the editor's own view state (not saved, not undoable), with the arrangement's gestures and keys for zoom and scroll. The view opens with the region fitted and 10 percent margin each side. Zoom runs from the whole source in the width to 8 physical pixels per sample; at one sample per column or less the waveform is a polyline through the samples (`waveform.rs:60-70`); on a transposed clip that polyline is drawn through the reader's output at the clip's own samples, not the raw source. Z zooms to the time range and X returns (Ableton), editor-scoped keys checked against the shortcut table in slice 3. A vertical waveform zoom is display only.
4. **Channels.** One lane per source channel up to 8, labeled (L and R for stereo); a wider source draws one overlaid lane with a `12 ch` status tag. Channels are never summed for drawing (`slices/wave.rs:8`).
5. **Playhead and following.** The transport playhead (`cursor`) when it is in view; the view follows it while playing, page by page, and stops following while a gesture is held (a choice of this record; whether Ableton pauses Follow while editing is not verified). A muted clip, an inactive track or a stopped transport draws no moving playhead and the view does not follow (the bypassed-shows-nothing-moving rule applies to both).
6. **Fades and gain on the wave.** The waveform is drawn scaled by the clip's gain and fades (until ADR 0058's clip slice lands, today's nominal `gain_at`, as the arrangement draws, with no claim of sample-exact agreement with playback; after it, the rendered gain of ADR 0058's clip gain path, the same function the render calls; decision 9.4), the fade curves as lines over it with the arrangement's handles, and a gain line across the region at the clip gain. A gain envelope, when the clip has one (inherited sessions or the control channel), is drawn as a read-only line until its editing slice (decision 3.6).
7. **Transients** as short ticks along the display's top edge (Ableton's gray markers), from `detect_transients` on the source, computed off the UI thread when the editor opens a source and kept in memory per source and sensitivity, never saved. A sensitivity control sits in the editor's snap menu (0 to 1, default 0.5, the Slices view's scale). While detection runs the status tag reads `Analyzing…`.
8. **Cue points** from a WAV file's cue chunk, read-only flags (the Slices view's Auto flag style), usable as snap targets (slice 9).
9. **Header.** The clip's name and color (editable in place), the source's file name, **the file's own format** (sample rate, bit depth, channels), its length, and whether it is disk-backed, as Ableton Live's Sample Editor header shows the sample's "sample rate, bit depth, and channel count" (Clip View chapter, section 8.10). When the file's rate differs from the session's, a short note says the file is converted for playback: `44.1 kHz · 24 bit · stereo, played at 48 kHz`; a file at the session rate shows no note (`48 kHz · 24 bit · stereo`). A lossy file shows its codec in place of a bit depth (`Opus · 48 kHz · stereo`). The pool holds a timeline source at the session rate and the import does not keep the file's rate (Context), so the editor reads the format from the file itself when it opens a source, off the UI thread: `transit_audio_io::probe_metadata` (header only, no decode) for WAV, AIFF and FLAC, `transit_audio_io::probe` for other formats, with slice 3 extending the format-probe result to preserve the exact declared bit depth and codec identity where available. `AudioInfo::sample_format` alone is insufficient: its integer variants bucket bit depths. An unavailable bit depth is omitted, never inferred from that bucket; the result is kept in memory per source and never saved, and no session field is added. Until the read returns, or when it cannot (a missing file), the header shows the stored record's channels and length with no rate or bit depth; a recording not yet written to disk is at the session rate by construction and shows the stored rate and channels with `not saved yet`. Before the convention audit this header showed the stored session rate, labeled "stored" (question 13). A clip whose file is missing hatches the display with the Slices view's card (`<file> is missing`); the fields that need no audio stay editable (decision 15 lists which commands refuse).

None of the new properties is automatable in this record: no automation lane, clip envelope or modulation target reaches `reverse`, `transpose` or `fine`. A transposition envelope is a later record's.

1. **Slip.** `clip.slip {clip, by}` moves the content `by` clip samples later under the fixed window (`anchor + by`, decision 5.4) and changes nothing else: `start`, `length`, the fades, the gain envelope, the sync point and the loop range stay, because they are clip time and follow the clip's window (Logic's and Ableton's fades sit on the clip's edges). `clip.slip {clip, frame}` sets the source frame read at the clip's first sample (`offset = frame`, `anchor = 0`). Both are bounded by the material bounds of decision 5.5 (from `Source::frames`, known even when the file is missing); the command clamps the requested distance to them and reports the clamp (a gesture limit, as a trim stops at the file's end today; the stored anchor is never clamped after the fact). One undo step per gesture; a run of nudges merges. In the editor the axis is the timeline, so a slip moves the source drawing under the fixed region, as it moves the audio under the clip. **In the arrangement**, slip is a modifier drag on the clip body with **Ableton Live's documented chords on both systems: Ctrl+Shift drag on Windows, Shift+Option drag on macOS** (Arrangement View chapter, Moving and Resizing Clips). Both were decided by the maintainer, separately: a modifier-drag slip at all (question 4), and the chord (question 6; the macOS chord changed from Cmd+Shift after the convention audit, 2026-11-12), which changes an existing gesture on each system. Neither chord is free in transit today: Ctrl or Alt (Cmd or Option on macOS) copies a dragged clip and Shift drops the snap, so Ctrl+Shift drag on Windows and Shift+Option drag on macOS are unsnapped copies. With this record they slip instead, and an unsnapped copy stays available: **Alt+Shift drag on Windows** (Alt also copies) and **Cmd+Shift drag on macOS** (Cmd also copies; transit's existing chord there, unchanged). The slip snaps its distance to the grid like a move; adding the other copy modifier makes it free: **Ctrl+Alt+Shift on Windows, Cmd+Option+Shift on macOS**, which are Ableton's documented unsnapped slides (Shift cannot free it, being part of the chord; decided, question 11). The body-drag chords, with the snap on:

   | Body drag | Windows | macOS |
   |---|---|---|
   | Move | drag | drag |
   | Move, unsnapped | Shift | Shift |
   | Copy | Ctrl or Alt | Cmd or Option |
   | Copy, unsnapped | Alt+Shift | Cmd+Shift |
   | Slip | Ctrl+Shift | Shift+Option |
   | Slip, unsnapped | Ctrl+Alt+Shift | Cmd+Option+Shift |

   **Why Cmd+Shift for the unsnapped copy on macOS** (the record's choice, still open for the maintainer's confirmation, Still open): it follows transit's own rule that a copy modifier plus Shift is an unsnapped copy, the rule that gives Alt+Shift on Windows; it is the chord that already makes an unsnapped copy on macOS today, so no macOS user loses it; and the literal mirror of Windows, Option+Shift, is the slip. Ableton's and Logic's copy-drag modifiers on macOS were not read in the convention audit or for this amendment, so the choice rests on transit's model, not on a convention. The chord test is per system: `drag_copies` and the snap read (`arrange.rs:548-556`) take the system as a parameter, so both columns are tested on any host. Ctrl+Alt without Shift on Windows, and Cmd+Option without Shift on macOS, stay copies.
2. **Trims** in the editor use the arrangement's trim (`edit.trim_clip`, `crates/engine/src/edit.rs:758`), with the bounds of decision 5.5 (today's `material_len` bound, `edit.rs:804`, assumes rate 1), snapping and one undo step, **but never ripple.** Today the command passes the session's Shuffle mode (`cmd/edit.rs:1018-1019`), and in Shuffle a trim keeps the start, slides the material, ripples every later clip on the track and follows automation (`edit.rs:761-764,784-786,796-799`). `edit.trim_clip` gains an optional `shuffle: bool` parameter (absent means the session's mode, as today, so every existing caller is unchanged); the editor's region-strip trims and Crop to Range always pass `shuffle: false`, so an editor action changes only the open clip, whatever the arrangement's edit mode (decided by the maintainer, question 14). Outside Shuffle mode an editor trim and an arrangement trim are the same command with the same result.
3. **Fades** render through ADR 0058's clip gain path (decision 9). The editor adds no fade law and no gain arithmetic of its own.
4. **Clip gain** keeps today's edit range: `clip.gain` clamps to −144..+36 dB (`crates/engine/src/cmd/clip.rs:525`), −144 or below is silence. Its conversion is ADR 0058's, whose exact fast path makes 0 dB exactly 1.0, so a clip at 0 dB with no fades and no envelope applies exactly unity and, alone on its strip, renders its source bit for bit, except that a −0.0 source sample renders +0.0 and a NaN stays a NaN with its payload unspecified (9.6 f).
5. **Normalize** (#181's row) sets the clip's static gain from a measured peak and writes no audio, as Logic's Normalize Region Gain does.
   - **What is measured:** the sample peak, `P = max |y_c(p)|` over every source channel `c` and every clip sample `p` in `0..length`, where `y` is what the mapping and the reader return for the clip (decisions 5 and 8: loop wrap, rate and reverse included), before the clip's gain, envelope and fades and before the track's channel fold. It is a sample peak, not a true peak (no oversampling); the help says so.
   - **The gain:** `gain_db = target_db − 20·log10(P)`, computed in `f64` through `transit_math::f64::log10` and rounded once to `f32`. It replaces the static gain; the envelope, if any, still adds on top (so an envelope above 0 dB can exceed the target, said in the help).
   - **Target:** default −0.1 dBFS; a finite value in −60..0 dB, else refused.
   - **Refused:** a peak below the smallest normal `f32` (2^−126), zero or not finite (silent or damaged; #502's NaN is not repeated); a missing file or a Sampler-shared source; an edit-locked clip; non-audio. **Above +36 dB:** the needed gain is clamped to +36 dB and the result reports the level reached (`raised by the maximum 36 dB; peak now −14.2 dBFS`) (decided by the maintainer with what is measured, question 10).
   - **Measured off the UI thread.** The editor's button starts a peak job (the Slices view's worker pattern) that returns `P` with the clip's mapping fingerprint (source and its frame count, offset, anchor, reverse, transpose, fine, stretch, loop, length); the result is applied by `clip.normalize {clips, target_db?, peaks}`, which refuses a peak whose fingerprint no longer matches the clip (`the clip changed while it was measured`). Without `peaks` (the control channel), the command measures synchronously and refuses a region longer than the render limit, as the offline commands do (`io.rs:550-557`). One undo step for the whole batch; a refusal of any clip refuses the batch.
6. **The gain envelope stays out of the minimum cut.** The two references do not agree on its form (Ableton's clip envelope, relative to clip gain, with its own loop; Logic's region automation of track parameters), the model already renders one, and an editing gesture for breakpoints is a design of its own. It is drawn read-only and edited in a later slice, with a decision on Ableton's form or Logic's recorded there.

### 4. What non-destructive promises, and the paths that bake

1. **The editor never writes audio.** Every property in decision 3 is a field of the clip; the source file, its pool audio and its peaks are never changed, and another clip on the same source is unaffected. Undo is the snapshot it is today.
2. **Saving writes no audio for an editor edit.** A session saved after any editor edit references the same files.
3. **The offline tools stay commands that render a new file**, repoint the clip and keep the original. They are not in the minimum cut. A later slice may list them in the editor's menu under **Render** (Logic's processing, Ableton's Consolidate), each saying it writes a new file, one undo step. `audiosuite.process` with `reverse` stays on the control channel; the editor's Reverse is the flag (decision 6), which supersedes #181's planned `clip.reverse` render command.
4. **Every path that renders, copies or transfers a clip keeps or clears each new field by one rule:** a path that renders the clip's sound into a new source clears exactly the transforms that render baked; a path that copies or cuts a clip keeps every field and moves the mapping through decision 5; a path that rescales positions between sample rates rescales `anchor` with `offset`; an inherited path that cannot read through the mapping is refused on any clip whose mapping is not the plain one (a **transformed** clip: reversed, transposed or fine-tuned, or with a nonzero `anchor`). ### 5. One clip-to-source mapping

Rendering, reverse, slip, trims, splits, heals, take joins, crossfade handles, the waveform drawing and Normalize all use one mapping from clip time to source position, at any effective rate.

1. **Stored fields.**
   - `ClipContent::Audio { source, offset, anchor }`: `offset: Samples` (exists), the source frame read at clip time `anchor`; `anchor: Samples` (new, `#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "is_zero")]`), the clip time at which `offset` is read. Their numeric domain is decision 5.5. Making `anchor` a field of the variant makes the compiler list every site that builds or destructures it, which is the caller audit of 5.6.
   - `Clip::reverse: bool` (decision 6), `Clip::transpose: i8` and `Clip::fine: i8` (decision 7), each written only when not default; `Clip::stretch: f64` (exists).
2. **The effective rate.** `r = exp2((100·transpose + fine) / 1200)` in `f64` through `transit_math::f64::exp2`; `q = r / stretch` (one `f64` division; `stretch > 1` slows, as today); the signed rate is `s = −q` when reversed, else `q`. **Bounds: q is in [1/32, 32].** `transpose` and `fine` alone give 0.0607..16.47; a command that changes `transpose`, `fine` or `stretch` refuses a result outside the bounds; `sanitize` repairs a file outside them (only a hand-edited one can be: the commands clamp `stretch` to 0.05..20) by resetting `transpose` and `fine` to 0 and then clamping `stretch` into the bounds, with a load note.
3. **The mapping.** For clip time `p` in `0..length`: `c = content_pos(p)` (ADR 0019's integer wrap, unchanged), then `x(c) = offset + s·(c − anchor)`, evaluated in this order: `d = c − anchor` in `i64` (exact; |d| < 2^47 in the domain of 5.5, so `d as f64` is exact too), then `(d as f64) · s` (one rounding), then `(offset as f64) + that` (one rounding); no fused multiply-add. This evaluation is **the declared mapping arithmetic**: every bound in this record that speaks of a computed position (the material invariant, the far-end rule, the trim and slip limits) evaluates it this way. **Precision, under the material invariant only** (5.5): for a clip that satisfies it, every addressed `c` has |s·d| ≤ 2^41 (5.5), so the product's rounding is at most 2^−12 and the sum's, the result lying in `[0, 2^40)`, at most 2^−13: the computed position is within 2^−11 frame of the exact one, and within 2^−20 frame when the offset and the position are below 2^31 (12.4 hours at 48 kHz). The numeric bounds alone do not give this: offset 1, anchor 2^46, c = 2^40 − 1 and q the `f64` just below 32 satisfy every numeric bound, and the computed position is 0.0039 frame from the exact one (computed, round 3); that clip addresses positions about 2^51 frames outside any file, which the invariant excludes. A position whose reader taps fall outside `0..F` reads zeros there (decision 8.5).
   - **The fast path is q = 1.** Then `s = ±1` and `x` is an integer by construction (offset and anchor are integers), and the read is today's direct index, bit for bit. Every other rate goes through the reader, including q = 2 (an octave up needs the anti-alias filter); at q < 1 the reader returns the source sample exactly at integer positions, because its table holds exactly 1 at 0 and 0 at every other integer.
   - **Normal form and canonical form.** When q = 1, every command that writes the clip stores it in **normal form**: `offset ← x(0) = offset − s·anchor`, `anchor ← 0` (the positions are identical, and `x(0)` is an integer). A clip that is neither transposed nor stretched therefore writes no `anchor`, and a session whose clips all have `anchor` 0 after the normal form (every clip at q = 1, and stretched clips never split or start-trimmed) saves byte-identical to before. A split or start-trimmed **stretched** clip necessarily gains a nonzero `anchor` and the `clip_anchor` need (5.7), even without transpose or reverse: that is #554's correction, and byte identity does not cover it. The **canonical form** of a clip is its normal form at q = 1 and its stored fields at q ≠ 1. Every comparison of two clips' mappings (heal, take joins, the tests) compares canonical forms, never raw fields.
4. **Operations, all integer, all exact at any rate.**
   - **Split at clip time `t`, a start trim by `t`, the copy of a time range, a take cut:** the later piece's clip time starts `d` later, where `d = t` for an unlooped clip and `d = content_pos(t)` for a looped one (the phase `trim_start_to` computes today, with the loop shifted by it as today); its `anchor` becomes `anchor − d`, then the normal form applies. Today these add `d` to `offset`, which is right only at q = 1 forward.
   - **An end trim** changes nothing in the mapping.
   - **Slip by `by`:** `anchor + by`.
   - **Reverse:** `offset` kept, `anchor' = (ls + le − 1) − anchor` with `[ls, le)` the loop range or `[0, length)` unlooped, and `s` changes sign (decision 6; refused in the one case of 6.4).
   - **Heal and take joins:** two touching pieces merge only when splitting the merged clip at the seam gives both back in canonical form (the take-join rule, `takes_join.rs:73-110`). At q = 1 that means the same source, `reverse` and `stretch` and the right piece's offset equal to the left's `x(d)`, with `d` the seam's phase in the left piece; at q ≠ 1, the same source, `offset`, `reverse`, `transpose`, `fine` and `stretch` and the right piece's `anchor` equal to the left's minus `d`. Example (round 2): `(offset 100, anchor 0)` at q = 1 split at 20 stores the right piece as `(120, 0)`; splitting the merged clip at 20 gives `(100, −20)`, whose normal form is `(120, 0)`: equal, so the pieces heal. At q ≠ 1 the test is conservative: two pieces whose stored fields differ but describe the same positions (possible only when q is an exact ratio such as 2, with `(100, 0)` and `(102, 1)`) are not healed; a heal never merges pieces that do not play continuously.
   - **Transpose** rebases at the clip start (decision 7.3).
5. **The domain, closed under every edit.**
   - **Addressed positions.** A clip's addressed clip times are `[0, length − 1]` unlooped and `[min(0, ls), le − 1]` looped (`content_pos` returns no other); its addressed centers are `x` over them. With `F = Source::frames` (the stored record, present even when the file is missing) and `Fa = min(F, 2^40)` (frames from 2^40 on, 254 days at 48 kHz, are not addressable, as no `offset` could reach them before), the **material invariant** is: every addressed center, computed with the declared mapping arithmetic (5.3), lies in `[0, Fa − 1]`, inclusive. The computed `x` is monotone in `c` (each rounding is monotone), so the two ends decide. These bounds are what trims, slips and the crossfade handles stop at (`edit.fades_create` extends a clip into a neighbor only while its addressed centers stay in the file). A looped clip's end is not bounded by the file, as today. The kernel's taps may reach past the addressed centers (decision 8.5).
   - **Numeric bounds.** `start` in `[0, MAX_POSITION]`, `length` in `[1, MAX_POSITION]` and loop edges within ±`MAX_POSITION` (as today); `offset` within ±`MAX_POSITION` (as today); **`anchor` within ±2^46**; q in `[1/32, 32]` (5.2); a transpose that lengthens a clip keeps `start + length ≤ MAX_POSITION` (7.3).
   - **Every edit keeps the invariant.** (a) A split, an inward start trim, the copy of a time range and a take cut give pieces whose addressed centers are a subset of the clip's (a looped right piece addresses `[min(d, ls), le)` of the original's clip time, inside `[min(0, ls), le)`). (b) An end trim leaves a looped clip's addressed centers unchanged and keeps an unlooped clip's last center in the file (today's file bound, through the mapping). (c) Outward start trims, slips and the crossfade handles are bounded by the invariant: the requested distance is clamped to it and the clamp reported, as a trim stops at the file today; on a clip that already violates it they may only move its centers toward the file. (d) Reverse addresses the same centers when the clip is unlooped or `ls ≤ 0`, and is refused in the one case that would address new ones (6.4). (e) `clip.set_loop` checks the loop's addressed centers (its range check moves onto the mapping, 5.6). (f) A transpose writes `anchor = 0` and `offset = round(x(0))`, inside `[0, Fa − 1]` for a clip that keeps the invariant (a clip starting outside its file is refused, 7.3's precondition), and its far-end rule (7.3) keeps the last center in the file; for a looped clip, the loop's intersection with the clip time in the file (7.3) keeps every addressed center in the file, or the transpose is refused. (g) The normal form writes `offset = x(0)`, inside `[0, Fa − 1]`.
   - **The invariant bounds the anchor.** Clip time 0 is always addressed, so the computed `x(0)` lies in `[0, Fa − 1]`. If |anchor| exceeded 2^46, the exact product |s·anchor| would exceed 2^41, its rounding (monotone, and 2^41 is representable) would be at least 2^41 in magnitude, and with |offset| ≤ 2^40 the computed sum would lie at least 2^40 from 0, outside the file; so |anchor| ≤ 2^46. Then, for an addressed `c`, `|c − anchor| ≤ 2^40 + 2^46 < 2^47`, and since the computed `x(c)` is in `[0, 2^40)` the rounded product lies within ±2^41, so the exact one does too, to within one rounding (rounding cannot cross the representable 2^41): `|s·(c − anchor)| ≤ 2^41`, the figure 5.3's precision statement relies on. **The round's counterexample:** `offset 0`, `anchor −2^40`, `length 2^40`, q = 1/32, on a source of at least 2^36 + 1 frames, addresses 2^35 to 2^36 − 1/32, inside the file; reversing it gives `anchor 2^41 − 1` and a one-sample start trim gives `−2^40 − 1`; both are inside ±2^46 and keep the invariant (computed). The first revision's bound, ±`MAX_POSITION` for the anchor, was not closed under these edits. Over 4,000 random clips (files of 1,000 to 2^41 frames, q across its bounds, 40 percent looped) and 110,039 random splits, trims, slips, reverses and transposes, the invariant and every bound held; the largest anchor reached was about 2^44 (computed).
   - **Checked refusal, never a clamp.** Every command computes `offset`, `anchor`, `length` and the loop edges in checked `i64` and refuses (with a reason, the whole batch) a result outside the bounds above. **Valid inputs may produce refused requests; accepted results satisfy all numeric bounds.** Example (round 3): loop `[−2^40, 64)`, length 100, q = 1/32, offset 2^35, anchor 0 satisfies the invariant (its addressed centers run from 0 to 2^35 + 63/32); splitting it at 63 gives a right piece whose loop starts at −2^40 − 63, below −`MAX_POSITION`, so the split is refused (computed). The invariant is about positions in the file; the numeric bounds are about stored fields, and an edit that keeps the first can still leave the second. A clip can violate the invariant without any edit of this record (an inherited session whose `offset` lies past its file, a source relinked to a file of another length, a clip-group import's rounding, decision 4.4); it still plays (positions outside the file read zeros), its stored anchor is still within ±2^46 (it was when written), and the bounds are still checked. A file whose anchor lies outside ±2^46, possible only by hand editing, is repaired at load by resetting that clip's `anchor` to 0 with a load note naming the clip and saying that its audio now starts from `offset`; no command clamps a stored anchor.
   - Proven by command, save and load tests at these limits (Evidence).
6. **One implementation.** `Clip::source_pos(c) -> f64` and its inverse `Clip::clip_span_in_file(F) -> Range` (the clip-time interval whose positions lie in the file) serve every caller; no caller does offset arithmetic. Today's unit-rate callers that move onto them in slice 1: `Clip::trim_start_to` (`clip.rs:313-327`), `split_clip` (`edit.rs:36`), `heal` (`edit.rs:441`), `trim_clip`'s file bound and `material_len` (`edit.rs:775-807`), the handles in `create_fades` (`edit.rs:819,834`), `trim_to_file` and `trim_to_fill` (`cmd/edit.rs:725-760`), the range check of `clip.set_loop` (`cmd/clip.rs:461-469`), the arrangement waveform origin (`waveform.rs:29,58`), the loop ghosts and cycle segments (`arrange/clips.rs:106,314`), the health check's offset test (`cmd/health.rs:132-143`), the clip-group import's rescale (`clip_group_file.rs:207-217`), and `StreamMap` (`disk.rs:131-180`) with its fingerprint. Slice 1 thereby fixes today's split, heal and bound defects for clips with `stretch ≠ 1` (#554).
7. **Older builds.** A `clip_anchor` need when any clip has a nonzero `anchor` (it can only arise at q ≠ 1: a split or start-trimmed piece of a stretched or transposed clip). A build without it reads `offset` at the clip's start: the wrong part of the file. Builds since the marker warn.

### 6. Reverse

1. **A playback flag, not a file.** `Clip::reverse` (`#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "Not::not")]`) is the sign of the mapping's rate: the clip reads its source backward. Logic's region Reverse is a flag; Ableton's renders a new file. The flag is clearly better here: no new file in the session folder, instant, undoable, and it keeps the rule that editing never produces audio.
2. **The toggle.** `clip.reverse {clips, on?}` (toggle when `on` is absent; `on: true` on a reversed clip and `on: false` on a forward one change nothing for that clip) keeps `offset`, sets `anchor' = (ls + le − 1) − anchor` and flips the sign. Reverse twice restores every field exactly, at any rate (integer arithmetic; at q = 1 through the normal form both times).
3. **An unlooped clip plays exactly the frames it played, in reverse order.** With F = 1000, offset 100, length 100 and q = 2, the forward clip reads 100, 102, ..., 298, and the reversed clip reads 298, 296, ..., 100 (computed positions; the first draft's `offset' = F − offset − length` read 199 ... 1). These are the mapped positions; at q = 2 the full `f32` reader's output at position 298 of a ramp source is 297.99399, not 298, because its response to a constant is close to 1, not exactly 1 (at q = 2 a constant 1.0 comes out at most 3.13·10⁻⁵ low over 32,768 positions; computed, decision 8.4). It needs nothing of the source, so a clip whose file is missing can be reversed.
4. **A looped clip: the cycle plays the same frames backward** (rule (i); decided by the maintainer, question 8). The loop range stays in clip time and reverse mirrors the mapping around the loop. With `ls ≤ 0` (no intro, the common case) the reversed clip addresses exactly the frames the forward clip did. With an intro (`ls > 0`) the intro plays the material that follows the loop, mirrored.
   - **Illustration (abstract: the model refuses a cycle shorter than `MIN_CYCLE` = 64, `clip.rs:101-114`):** F = 100, offset 10, loop [2, 6) plays 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 12, 13, ... forward and 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 15, 14, ... reversed.
   - **Regression fixture (legal):** F = 1000, offset 100, q = 1, loop [20, 84), length 300. Forward: 100 to 183, then 120 to 183 each cycle. Reversed: 203, 202, ..., 184 (the 20-sample intro, from the material after the loop), then 183, 182, ..., 120 each cycle (computed).
   - **Refused** when the mirrored intro would address centers outside the file (`the reversed intro would read past the end of the file; shorten the intro or move the loop`), so the domain stays closed (5.5). Only a clip whose loop ends within `ls·q` frames of the file's end is affected.
   - **The alternative not chosen, whole-clip reversal** (play exactly what the forward clip plays, backward in time). Unlooped clips are the same under both. A looped clip is the same under both exactly when `ls ≤ 0` and `length − 2·ls` is a multiple of the cycle (computed: the condition predicted equality or difference in 12,483 random cases without exception). That is rare: with loop [0, 64) and length 100, rule (i) starts at the cycle's last frame (63) and whole-clip reversal at frame 35, where the forward clip ended. Without an intro the two differ only in the phase at which the reversed cycle starts, and whole-clip reversal is expressible in the one mapping by moving the loop range by that phase within `[ls, le)` (computed: 2,248 random cases, all expressible); with an intro it turns the intro into an outro, which the loop model (an intro, then cycles) cannot express without a mirror stage before the wrap.
   - **Why rule (i):** the reversed cycle always starts at the cycle's last frame, whatever the clip's length, so trimming a reversed clip's end never shifts its loop, and every operation stays in the one mapping. Ableton flips the region and loop settings of its reversed file; whether its intro behaves like rule (i) is unverified.
5. **Fades, gain, the envelope and the sync point stay in clip time** (Ableton keeps clip envelopes in time).
6. **Refusals.** Not an audio clip; edit-locked; a Sampler-shared source (#477); a disk-backed source until slice 6c (the streamer's descending reads) lands, said in the refusal; a mirrored intro outside the file (6.4). A batch with any refusal is refused whole.
7. **Disk-backed sources.** The streamer fills a ring with reader output already mapped (ADR 0035 decision 2), so the audio thread's work is unchanged; decision 8.6 lists what the streamer must do. A toggle while playing re-cues the ring (one counted underrun, as a slip does).
8. **Older builds.** A `clip_reverse` need when any clip is reversed. A build without it plays the clip forward from `offset`: wrong audio. Builds since the marker warn.

### 7. Transpose and fine tune (no warping)

1. **Pitch is a rate change, as a sampler plays it.** `Clip::transpose` (semitones, −48 to 48) and `Clip::fine` (cents, −50 to 50), both `i8`, default 0 and not written while 0 (ranges decided by the maintainer with question 3). The rate `r` is decision 5.2's, pinned by a table test for every one of the 9,797 values after ADR 0055's goldens exist (compared in process before). ±48 is Ableton's clip-envelope range (verified); ±50 cents is this record's choice (Ableton's manual names cents but gives no range; not verified against either program). Pitch that keeps the length needs a stretch, which is the warping record's (decision 12).
2. **The length follows the rate, like tape** (decided by the maintainer, question 3; kept after the convention audit). This is **not** an Ableton/Logic consensus. It **differs from Logic Pro**, whose documented Transpose is a pitch control; its fixed-length behavior and Flex Speed/Varispeed's length behavior were not verified by the audit, and **Ableton Live's behavior with Warp off is not documented by the vendor**. So the control says what it does (7.9), and **pitch-only transposition arrives with the warping record** (decision 12). What the maintainer agreed to: a transposed clip keeps the source material it plays and takes longer (down) or shorter (up) to play it; a lengthened clip stops at the next clip's start on its playlist and the status line says the end was cut; fades keep their lengths at the clip's edges; undo restores everything exactly; a transpose there and back restores the length exactly in some cases and approximately in others (7.3).
3. **No hidden memory: each transpose is computed from the clip as it is.** The first revision kept a span memory on the clip (an origin and a mark) so that a transpose there and back would be lossless; it is removed (round 2). Made complete, it needed invalidation rules for every edit, both split pieces, copies, source replacement, save, load and undo, plus its own compatibility need, and a later transpose could make a clip silently grow back to a length the user had cut; the maintainer confirmed the removal (question 7). With `ρ = q_old / q_new`:
   - **the mapping:** `anchor = 0` and `offset = round(x(0))` (`round` is half away from zero, as `f64::round`). `x(0)` is an integer whenever `anchor` was 0, so only a piece split from a rate-changed clip moves its first position, by at most half a source frame, once;
   - **the length, unlooped:** `max(1, round(length · ρ))`, then the **far-end rule:** no longer than `Lmax`, the longest length whose last center, computed with the declared mapping arithmetic (5.3), stays in `[0, Fa − 1]`. **The precondition, checked before either correction loop:** the transpose is refused when the file is empty (`Fa = 0`) or the rebased `offset` (the first bullet) lies outside `[0, Fa − 1]` (`the clip starts outside its file; slip it into the file first`). A clip outside the material invariant (inherited, relinked or imported, 5.5) can start outside its file, and then no length passes: with `F` = 1, offset 1, anchor 0 and length 1 transposed from q = 1 to q = 2, the candidate below is 0 and shortening visits centers −1, −3, −5, ... forward, and reversed the candidate is 1 and the centers are 1, 3, 5, ..., never inside `[0, 0]`, so the loop would not end (round 4, computed). Under the precondition the candidate is at least 1 and a one-sample clip's only center is `offset`, inside the file, so shortening stops at one sample at the latest. **Computing `Lmax`** (under the precondition): a candidate from the quotient, `floor((Fa − 1 − offset) / q_new) + 1` forward or `floor(offset / q_new) + 1` reversed, divided in `f64`; then verify and correct the endpoint: while the candidate's last center (clip time `L − 1`) computes outside `[0, Fa − 1]`, shorten by one; while one more sample's center would still compute inside, lengthen by one. The computed position is monotone in the length, so the result is exactly the longest length that passes. **Under the precondition, the loop is bounded by two steps:** the quotient is below 2^45, so its `f64` value is within 2^−8 of the real quotient and the candidate is within one of the real-arithmetic answer; the computed position is within 2^−11 frame of the exact one (5.3) and consecutive centers are at least 1/32 apart, so at most one length changes sides between real and computed arithmetic; an implementation asserts the two-step bound, which is claimed only under the precondition (outside it the loops never run). The quotient alone is not enough: with `F` = 625,735,960,094, offset 0 and q = 1.0594630943592953 it gives 590,616,099,254, whose last center computes to 625,735,960,093.0001, past `F − 1`; one correction step gives 590,616,099,253 (computed, round 3). Over 200,000 random and adversarial cases and 400,000 cases built so the quotient lands just below an integer (files up to 2^40 frames, every transpose rate, stretched rates too, both directions), the corrected length always equaled a bisection over the computed predicate, and no case needed more than one step (computed); round 4 added 300,000 cases under the precondition, a quarter of them on files of 1 to 8 frames and a quarter with the offset at 0 or `Fa − 1`, with the same result (14 needed one step, none more; computed). Example (round 2): a whole 100-frame file pitched down to q = 0.5 would get 200 samples with its last center at 99.5 (at −0.5 reversed from offset 99), outside the file; the rule gives 199 samples ending at 99 (at 0 reversed); at q = 1/32 it gives 3,169 samples ending at 99 instead of 3,200 ending at 99.97 (computed). What is cut is the last fraction of a frame, which only the kernel's taps into silence past the file would have played;
   - **the length, looped:** under the same precondition (refused otherwise), form the rounded half-open loop `[round(ls · ρ), round(le · ρ))` and intersect it with the integer clip-time interval whose centers, computed with the declared mapping arithmetic, lie in `[0, Fa − 1]`. Refuse before any edge-correction iteration if that intersection is empty, has fewer than `MIN_CYCLE` samples, or ends at or below zero. Otherwise use its edges and set the length to `max(1, round(length · ρ))` (a looped clip's end is not bounded by the file). **The interval in the file** (`Clip::clip_span_in_file`, 5.6): for the rebased clip (`anchor = 0`) it is a single interval, forward and reversed, because the computed position is monotone in clip time, and under the precondition it contains clip time 0. It is computed in closed form with no unbounded loop: its last clip time is `Lmax − 1` for the clip's direction and its first is `−(Lmax − 1)` for the opposite direction, each `Lmax` computed as for the unlooped length (a quotient candidate, verified and corrected in at most two steps), because the declared arithmetic gives `x(−k)` at rate `s` exactly the value of `x(k)` at rate `−s` (rounding to nearest is symmetric). Clip time 0 and the new loop's last clip time both lie in the interval, so every addressed center of the result (`[min(0, ls), le − 1]`, 5.5) is in the file. Wherever round 4's inward edge correction ended it gave these same edges; it never ended when an edge started on the far side of the interval. Evidence includes the relinked-file case `F=100`, offset and anchor zero, loop `[128,256)`, length 512, transposed from q=1 to q=2: refused unchanged (the rounded loop `[64, 128)` meets the interval `[0, 50)` nowhere, and round 4's rule moved the start edge's center through 128, 130, 132, ... without end; computed, round 5);
   - **the timeline:** a result with `start + length > MAX_POSITION` is refused;
   - **what a transpose there and back gives:** down then up (lengthen, then shorten) returns the exact length unless the far end or a neighbor cut the clip (proof in real arithmetic: `|round(L·u)/u − L| ≤ 1/(2u) < 1/2` for a step ratio `u > 1`; the smallest step, one cent, leaves a margin of 2.9·10⁻⁴, which covers the `f64` roundings of the two products and of the rate ratio, a few units of 2^−52 relative each, while the lengthened clip is below 2^38 samples, 66 days at 48 kHz; beyond that it is measured: 225,559 random cases with lengths up to 2^40, none inexact, computed). Up then down, with `u = q_up / q_down`, has two cases. **When the shortened length rounds to at least one sample** (`round(L/u) ≥ 1`, that is `L ≥ u/2`), it returns within `(u + 1) / 2` samples of the original length (proof: the shortened length is within 1/2 of `L/u`, so its product with `u` is within `u/2` of `L`, plus 1/2 for the last rounding): at most one sample for steps under a semitone, eight for four octaves (a 100-sample clip at q = 16 and back is 96, and 104 goes 104 → 7 → 112, computed; over 300,000 random cases the worst was 0.999 of that bound), and the clip may come back longer as well as shorter. **When the one-sample minimum replaced a rounded zero** (`L < u/2`), the shortened clip is 1 sample and transposing back returns `round(u)` samples, before any far-end or neighbor cut: length 1 at u = 16 goes 1 → 1 → 16, and at u = 2^(2500/1200) ≈ 4.24 goes 1 → 1 → 4 (round 3's examples, computed; all 11,685 clamped cases of the random set returned `round(u)`). A cut end, by a neighbor or by the far-end rule, is not restored by transposing back: the user trims the end out again (the trim is bounded by the file). A fade longer than a shortened clip is clamped and stays clamped; a sync point outside the clip is dropped; envelope points past the end are kept and do not sound.
4. **Neighbors, rounding, minimums, batches.** The visible length is `min(uncut, room)` with `uncut` the length of 7.3 and `room = max(length, next.start − start)`, `next` the first clip on the same playlist starting after this one (an overlap that already exists, a crossfade, is kept). The minimum length is 1 sample. A looped clip's new loop, after 7.3's intersection with the clip time in the file, must be at least `MIN_CYCLE` long and end above 0, else the transpose is refused (a short loop pitched far up, or a loop pitched past the end of a relinked file). `clip.transpose {clips, semitones?, cents?, delta_semitones?}` never moves a clip's start, so each clip's room is the same whatever order the batch is applied in; a refusal of any clip refuses the batch.
5. **What a length change does to the rest.** Fades, the gain envelope and the sync point are clip time and keep their clip times (fades sit on the clip's edges, as in both references): the fade-in keeps its length at the start and the fade-out its length at the new end; a fade longer than the new length is clamped; envelope points past the new end are kept and do not sound; a sync point outside the clip is dropped; `whole_fades` is unchanged. An existing envelope is never reinterpreted.
6. **`stretch` goes through the same reader.** The inherited `stretch` composes with the rate (`q = r / stretch`) and so gets the better interpolator, in memory and from disk at once (slice 7a carries the streamer's forward support, decision 8.6). This changes the sound of any clip with `stretch ≠ 1` once (announced); the reference for those clips is the reader itself, not today's linear read. The inherited commands that set `stretch` are refused on transformed clips (decision 4). Retiring `stretch`, `clip.conform_to_tempo` and `clip.elastic_properties` belongs to the warping record.
7. **Refusals.** Not audio; edit-locked; outside −48..48 or −50..50; not an integer; `semitones` and `delta_semitones` together; an effective rate outside decision 5.2's bounds; an empty file or a rebased start outside the file (7.3's precondition); a loop whose intersection with the clip time in the file is empty, shorter than `MIN_CYCLE` or ends at or below 0 (7.3, 7.4); a result past `MAX_POSITION` (7.3); a Sampler-shared source. A missing file does not refuse a transpose (nothing of the audio is needed); a disk-backed source does not either once slice 7a has landed.
8. **Older builds.** A `clip_transpose` need when any clip has a nonzero `transpose` or `fine`. A build without it plays the clip at its original pitch for the new length.
9. **The control says it changes speed and pitch together** (decided by the maintainer after the convention audit, 2026-11-12). A Logic user expects Transpose to keep the length; the label and tooltip say otherwise before the first drag:
   - **Label:** the Transpose and Fine fields sit in one field group titled **`Speed · Pitch`**, with the field labels `Transpose` (st) and `Fine` (ct).
   - **Tooltip,** on the group title and on both fields: **"Changes speed and pitch together, like tape. The clip gets longer or shorter. Pitch without a length change comes with warping."**
   - **Accessible names** carry the same: `Speed and pitch, transpose, minus 3 semitones`; `Speed and pitch, fine, plus 12 cents`.
   - **Help entry** (`docs/help/clip-editor.toml`, the Speed · Pitch entry): "Speed · Pitch changes the clip's speed and pitch together, like tape or a sampler. Pitching up plays the audio faster, so the clip gets shorter; pitching down plays it slower, so the clip gets longer and stops at the next clip on the track. Fades keep their lengths at the clip's edges. Transposing down and back restores the length unless a neighbor or the file's end cut it off. Transposing up and back can change the length through sample rounding; a clip shortened to the one-sample minimum can return longer. A cut end must be trimmed back out. Undo restores the previous state exactly. This is not a pitch-only transpose: changing pitch without changing the length comes with warping."
   - **User guide** (the editing section): the same in one paragraph, saying that this differs from a pitch-only transpose and that pitch without a length change comes with warping.
   - Proven by `the_speed_pitch_group_says_it_changes_speed_and_pitch` (UI, slice 7b): the group title reads `Speed · Pitch`, and the group title and both fields carry the tooltip text above.

### 8. The reader

1. **The kernel.** A Kaiser-windowed sinc, `h(u) = sinc(u) · I0(β·sqrt(1 − (u/16)²)) / I0(β)` for |u| < 16 and 0 beyond: 16 zero crossings a side, so 32 taps at q ≤ 1; β = 0.1102·(80 − 8.7) = 7.85726 ≈ 7.8573 (the Kaiser design formula for 80 dB; a design figure, not a bound: 8.4). Cutoff at half the source rate for q ≤ 1. **For q > 1 the kernel stretches:** `h_q(t) = h(t/q)/q` over |t| < 16q source frames, so the cutoff is half the output rate (0.5/q of the source rate) and the tap count is about 2·16q. The kernel is normalized by construction (h(0) = 1, unity area); no per-phase normalization.
2. **The table.** `h` at 512 points per unit of `u` (per zero crossing), from 0 to 16: 8,193 entries, built once before the first plan, off the audio thread, through `transit_math::f64::sin` and `sqrt` and a 60-term `I0` series, then rounded once to `f32`; the entry at 0 is set to exactly 1 and every entry at a nonzero integer to exactly 0. With a delta table beside it (`D[i] = T[i+1] − T[i]` in `f32`), 64 KiB, shared read-only by every clip.
3. **One output sample** at source position `x` (decision 5.3), for each channel: for every integer `k` with |x − k| < 16·max(1, q), in ascending `k`, `u = |x − k| / max(1, q)` in `f64`, `i = floor(512·u)`, `f = (512·u − i)` rounded to `f32`, coefficient `T[i] + f·D[i]` in `f32`, accumulated as `acc += source[k] · coefficient` in `f32`; then, for q > 1, `acc · (1/q as f32)`. No fused multiply-add. At q ≤ 1 every tap shares one fraction, so the 32 coefficients are 32 table reads at a fixed stride.
4. **Acceptance bounds, from a dense sweep of this specification with a convergence check** (the measurement is defined in Evidence, and `the_reader_meets_its_acceptance_bounds` runs it). Frequencies are fractions of the session rate `fs`, the rate of both the source and the output (0.42 fs is 20.2 kHz at 48 kHz and 18.5 kHz at 44.1 kHz); a source tone at `f` plays at `q·f`. **Passband error:** for source tones up to 0.42 fs (q ≤ 1) or tones that play at up to 0.42 fs (q > 1), the largest absolute difference from the exact tone at any output position, over every tone phase (the ideal band-limited resample of a tone below the source Nyquist is the tone itself). **Alias output (q > 1):** the largest output amplitude at any position, over every tone phase, for a source tone below the source Nyquist that would play at 0.58 fs or above. Each bound sits at least 0.5 dB above the worst value found: the analytic response's worst after refinement, and the full `f32` reader's figure at each refined worst case (the two are kept apart in Evidence).

| Effective rate q | Taps a channel a sample | Passband error: bound (measured worst) | Alias output: bound (measured worst) |
|---|---|---|---|
| q < 1.5 (q ≤ 1 and 1 < q < 1.5 alike) | 32 at q ≤ 1; 33 to 48 above | −73 dB (−73.50 dB, a source tone at 0.4158 fs; the same at q ≤ 1 and as q approaches 1 from above) | q ≤ 1: images fall in the error column; 1 < q < 1.5: −73.5 dB (−74.27 dB, q = 1.1686, output 0.5843 fs) |
| 1.5 ≤ q ≤ 32 | 2·16q, up to 1,024 | −78 dB (−78.63 dB, q = 1.5148, output 0.4157 fs) | −78 dB (−78.50 dB, q = 1.5001, output 0.5842 fs) |

   **Why the bands are where they are.** For a position with fractional part φ the reader's response to a tone is the kernel's spectrum at the tone plus its images at multiples of the source rate, weighted by `e^(i2πmφ)`; at q ≤ 1 the coefficients do not depend on q, so one sweep over φ covers every q ≤ 1. Just above q = 1 the image nearest the passband edge sits where it does at q = 1, so the passband error is continuous there (in the analytic response, as are the other figures of this explanation: −73.53 dB at q = 1.000001, the round's figure reproduced) and the round-2 band for 1 < q < 1.5 (−75.5 dB, from the rates 1.0595 to 1.4) was wrong near q = 1; the error falls below −76 dB by q = 1.006 and rises again toward −73.8 dB near q = 1.016. From q = 1.5 the nearest image is a full stopband away, and the worst cases (passband −78.6 dB, alias −78.5 dB) sit just above 1.5 (the round's −78.87 dB at q = 1.51 and −78.93 dB at q = 1.6 reproduced; the round-2 band of −79 dB there was wrong). A single bound for all of q > 1 would be −73 dB in the passband and −73.5 dB for aliases: the 1.5 boundary is kept because it buys 5 dB for every upward rate from a fifth up. The design's 80 dB is not reached at any rate (the worst alias output is about −80.0 dB from q = 4 up). **Sparse observations, not bounds** (the first two revisions' fixed rates and few phases, kept for reference): first revision −75.3 to −82.1 dB passband and −82.7 to −82.9 dB alias; second revision −73.7 to −79.6 dB passband and −74.9 to −80.1 dB alias (its alias figure was an RMS over the run, which averages phases; the bound above takes the worst position). **Response to a constant, measured with the full `f32` reader and rounded outward** (round 4; worst values found over the stated samples, not proofs): a constant input of 1.0 comes out within **4.2·10⁻⁵** of 1 at q ≤ 1 (worst found 4.071·10⁻⁵ over 65,536 positions at q = 0.5; every q ≤ 1 has the same coefficients), within **5.2·10⁻⁵** for 1 < q < 1.5 (worst found 5.132·10⁻⁵ at q = 1.01095, from a scan every 0.001 refined to steps of 10⁻⁵ around its worst, 32,768 positions there), and within **3.7·10⁻⁵** for 1.5 ≤ q ≤ 32 (worst found 3.654·10⁻⁵ at q = 1.50722; 3.511·10⁻⁵ at q = 1.5, 3.123·10⁻⁵ at q = 2, and 2.956·10⁻⁵ at q = 32, where position fraction 0.7249755859375 gives 0.9999704360961914). So a filtered ramp is not exact (6.3); tests compare mapped positions exactly and filtered values within these limits, relative to the value. **A coefficient figure, not a rendered-value bound:** the interpolated table differs from the exact kernel by at most 1.7·10⁻⁶ (worst found 1.6263·10⁻⁶ near u = 7.5/512, with 64 points per table step over the whole table and 1,024 per step around the worst). The round-3 figures (4.04·10⁻⁵ at q ≤ 1, 3.5·10⁻⁵, 3.1·10⁻⁵ and 2.7·10⁻⁵ at q = 1.5, 2 and 32, 1.6·10⁻⁶ for the table) were coefficient sums in `f64` or rounded down; they were not the reader's output, and the q ≤ 1 figure does not hold just above q = 1. **Stated limits:** between 0.42 and 0.58 fs is the transition band. At q ≤ 1 a source tone at 0.43, 0.44, 0.45 and 0.48 fs (20.6, 21.1, 21.6 and 23.0 kHz at 48 kHz) comes out with an error of up to −48.4, −34.1, −24.3 and −6.4 dB (analytic response, worst over 1,024 phases; zero at integer positions; observations, not bounds): content there is attenuated, and when a clip is pitched down its partly rejected mirror images land at audible frequencies. At q > 1, content between 0.5 and 0.58 fs is partly rejected and aliases only into 0.42..0.5 fs (above 20.2 kHz at 48 kHz). The help states these figures. **Why the kernel stretches:** a fixed 32-tap span with only its cutoff scaled, measured at q = 2, lets content at 0.58 fs through at −22.9 dB and at 0.62 fs at −40.0 dB (the round reproduced both); a fixed tap count cannot reach the rejection when pitching up. **Kernel unchanged:** β = 7.8573 is near the best for 32 taps: a coarser sweep of the analytic response (256 phases at q ≤ 1, 32 phases for aliases) at β = 6.5, 7.2, 8.5 and 9.2 gave a passband worst of −63.8, −68.4, −61.1 and −52.1 dB at q ≤ 1 against −73.5 dB, and worse aliases at q = 1.184, 1.5 and 2 (computed); only more taps (8.7) would lower the bounds, so they are measured, not redesigned.
5. **Taps at boundaries.** A tap at a frame outside `0..F` (before the file's first frame or past its last) reads 0, at every boundary kind. **A region edge** (the clip's start or end inside the file) is not a boundary: taps read the file's adjacent frames, as a resampled copy of the file would, which is what makes a split piece null against the unsplit clip. **A loop seam** is a jump of the center position in clip time (`content_pos`), not of the taps: the taps around a position near the loop's end read the frames that follow it in the file, and around the start of a cycle the frames before the loop start. **A loop intro** reads its adjacent frames the same way. Reverse needs no rule of its own (the kernel is symmetric). So a transposed or reversed clip's first samples may carry energy from up to 16·max(1, q) source frames outside its region; a fade-in of that length hides it. Pinned with impulses at each kind: file start and end, region start and end, loop seam, intro, reversed and from disk.
6. **What the disk streamer must do.** Computing the reader's output on the streamer thread into the ring (so gain, fades and the envelope stay on the mixer path), a halo of `ceil(16·max(1, q)) + 1` frames on both sides of the read position, a read-ahead of about `q` source frames per ring frame plus both halos, and `reverse`, `transpose`, `fine`, `anchor` and `stretch` in the ring's fingerprint and in the request check (`disk.rs:310-316`), so a change while playing is a miss, never a read of the old mapping: **slice 7a**, for every clip with q ≠ 1 on a disk-backed source, existing stretched clips included, so that no clip is read through the new reader in memory and through today's linear read from disk. Refilling the file cache in the direction of travel (a reversed clip's miss at frame `x` loads a window ending past `x`, not one starting four frames behind it, `disk.rs:337-345`): **slice 6c**, with reverse; until it lands, reverse refuses disk-backed sources. Proven by disk-equals-memory tests, bit for bit: a stretched and a transposed clip in 7a, a reversed one in 6c.
7. **The 48-tap alternative, sparse observations:** 24 zero crossings a side gave errors within −78 dB up to 0.43 fs and −60 dB at 0.45 fs at the few tones tried, for 1.5 times the work and a 96 KiB table pair. Not chosen for v0.8.0; it is the upgrade path named in Reopen when, and needs the dense measurement before it is adopted.
8. **Cost and budget.**
   - **Work:** about 2·16·max(1, q) tap contributions a channel an output sample, each a table read pair, one multiply-add for the coefficient and one for the sum, only for clips with q ≠ 1; at q > 1 the coefficients differ for every output sample. For a stereo clip at 48 kHz: 3.07 million tap contributions a second at q ≤ 1, 6.14 million at q = 2, 50.6 million at q = 16.47 and 98.3 million at q = 32, sixteen times q = 2. Reverse alone is index arithmetic.
   - **Budget, measured in slice 7a** as ADR 0055 measures (release build, median of five runs, on the named reference machine, whose name is recorded here with the figures): a stereo clip at 48 kHz through the whole clip path (read, gain, fades, mix) costs at most **max(1, q/2) percent of one core**: 1 percent at q ≤ 2, 8.2 percent at q = 16.47, 16 percent at q = 32. Measured at q = 0.5, 1.0595, 2, 4, 16.47 and 32, and for two transposed clips overlapping in a crossfade with curved fades, against the plain read.
   - **Landing rule:** if any measured figure exceeds its limit, slice 7a does not land as measured. Restructuring the inner loop (the fixed stride at q ≤ 1, vectorized table reads) changes no output and may be tried first. If the budget is still missed, the remedy is the one the maintainer decided (question 12): lower the upper bound of q to 16.47 (transpose alone; `stretch` could no longer push higher), which halves the worst case, recorded in an amendment with the figures. The kernel's stretch is not capped, because capping costs quality directly: keeping the cutoff at 0.5/q with a quarter of the taps at q = 32 leaves four zero crossings a side, and Kaiser's width formula then puts the transition band about four times as wide (estimated, not measured: flat only to about 0.19 fs, 9 kHz at 48 kHz, for the same 80 dB design).
   - The help states that a clip pitched far up costs in proportion to its rate. The reader is stateless (the position comes from `p`), so a seek, a loop wrap or a block split needs nothing reset.

### 9. Gain, fades and the envelope: placement here, arithmetic in ADR 0058

**Clip fades and gain envelopes use the per-sample, closed-form f64 gain path, rounded once, defined by the mixer-gain record. This record defines their clip-time placement and editing behavior.** The mixer-gain record is [ADR 0058](0058-one-gain-path-for-the-mixer.md) (the mixer gain path). The fix tracked in #517 (with #501), this record's slice 0 until round 3, is that record's clip slice, with its evidence, its re-pins and its cost budget. Nothing in this record prescribes an intermediate rounding, an interpolation, a product order or a conversion for clip gain, fades or the envelope; where an earlier revision did (an `f32` gain product, `f32` fade arguments, the envelope summed in `f32` through `env_value`), ADR 0058 governs.

1. **Where a fade sits.** A fade-in covers clip samples `0` to `n_in − 1` and a fade-out `length − n_out` to `length − 1`, each length clamped to `0..=length` (`clamp_fades`). Both sit on the clip's edges in clip time, as in both references, so they move with the clip's window and never with its content. The fade law is the **closed law** (decided by the maintainer, question 9): for fades of two samples or more, a fade-in runs from exactly 0 to exactly 1 and a fade-out ends at exactly 0, mirror images; a one-sample fade is the midpoint gain (9.6 d). ADR 0058 renders it, and which gain each covered sample gets under it is ADR 0058's arithmetic. Today's half-open law (Context: a fade-out never reaches 0, a fade-in never reaches 1) is the behavior ADR 0058's clip slice replaces.
2. **Where the envelope sits.** Breakpoints are `(clip time, dB)` pairs measured from the clip's first sample and added to the static gain, as today. Their stored times and values are kept as written: `sanitize` sorts them stably by time on load and drops none, so breakpoints sharing a time keep their order. How the envelope is evaluated at and between breakpoints, and how a lookup finds them, is ADR 0058's.
3. **What each edit does to fades and envelope points**, all in clip time:
   - **Slip and reverse:** nothing. Fades, envelope points and the sync point keep their clip times (decisions 3.1 and 6.5).
   - **Transpose:** decision 7.5 (clip times kept; a fade longer than the new length clamped; points past the new end kept and silent).
   - **A start trim, and the later piece of a split:** the fade-in keeps its length from the new start, clamped to the piece; envelope points before the cut are dropped, and when any was dropped and none sits exactly at the cut, one is inserted at the cut carrying the envelope's value there, stored as an `f32` dB value: until ADR 0058's clip slice lands, the value by today's evaluation (`env_value`, as `trim_start_to` does it, `clip.rs:334-339`), which slice 1 keeps; after that landing, the value as ADR 0058 evaluates it. The remaining points move earlier by the cut.
   - **An end trim:** the fade-out moves with the end and keeps its length, clamped; envelope points past the end are kept and do not sound.
   - **A split:** each piece keeps the fade on its outer edge and gets none on its inner edge (`split_clip`, `edit.rs:50-51`), so a split outside both fades leaves them unchanged and a split inside a fade clamps it to its piece. The earlier piece keeps every envelope point; the later piece is treated as a start trim.
   - **Crossfade creation and the Crossfade field:** as today (`edit.fades_create`): lengths, shapes and curvature only; when a gesture makes a pair, the fade-out's curvature is the negative of the fade-in's.
   - **Copies and duplicates:** every field kept.
4. **What the editor draws, in two states.** The waveform scaled by a gain at each drawn clip sample, the fade curves from the same function, the static gain line, and the envelope read-only (decision 3.6). At zooms coarser than one sample per column, each column uses the gain at its first clip sample (display only).
   - **Until ADR 0058's clip slice lands (the interim state):** the gain is today's nominal `Clip::gain_at` and the curves are today's fade and envelope curves, as the arrangement draws them now. No sample-exact agreement with playback is claimed: today's `mix_clip` interpolates `gain_at` over 32-sample segments that start at each block's first frame, so what renders depends on the block partition. For a 32-sample Equal Power fade-in, sample 16 has `gain_at` 0.70710677; a 32-sample mixer segment renders 0.51550734 there and a one-sample block 0.70710677 (round 4, computed), and no function of the clip sample alone matches both.
   - **After that landing:** the gain is ADR 0058's clip gain function, the function the render calls (9.6 h), so from then on what is drawn is what sounds, and drawing and envelope accuracy rest on ADR 0058's evidence.
   - Slices 1 and 3 do not wait for ADR 0058; the drawing changes with the sound when its clip slice lands.
5. **What a split promises for gain.** The source reading is exact (decision 5.4, 8.5); with 0 dB, no fades and no envelope the pieces null against the unsplit clip. Fades: as 9.3. Envelope: the earlier piece evaluates identically; the later piece starts from a breakpoint stored as `f32`, so its samples up to its next breakpoint may differ from the unsplit clip's. How much, and by which error measure near silence, is ADR 0058's to state (9.6 b), once its clip slice lands; before that, the cut value is today's `env_value` (9.3) and no accuracy beyond today's rendering is claimed. **History, not a guarantee:** round 2 measured, under its own `f32` interpolation, one set of 3,000 random splits in which 6.7 percent of later-piece samples differed, by at most 3.6·10⁻⁶ in relative gain. That is a statistic of that sample set, and a relative measure fails at the silence floor: with breakpoints `(0, −144)` and `(31, −100)`, split at 1, static gain −4.258056640625 dB, the unsplit clip renders its sample 3 at 0 and the later piece the same sample at 6.3·10⁻⁸ (round 3, computed).
6. **Requirements this record places on ADR 0058.** The editor's drawing and the envelope's accuracy rely on them once ADR 0058's clip slice lands; until then the interim state of 9.4 and 9.3 applies:
   - **a. Exact breakpoints.** At a breakpoint's clip time the rendered envelope gain is the conversion of exactly the stored dB value plus the static gain, with no interpolation residue. (Today's arithmetic, interpolating from −144 dB to `f32(0.1)`, returns 0.100006103515625 at the later breakpoint, against the stored 0.10000000149011612; round 3, computed.)
   - **b. The silence floor.** Any accuracy statement about clip gain, the split statement of 9.5 included, is relative above a stated floor and absolute below it, since a gain at or below −144 dB is exactly 0 and a relative error against 0 is undefined.
   - **c. Breakpoints sharing a time.** They are preserved with today's meaning (the value at the shared time is the first point's; after it, the segment starts from the last point's), and the per-sample work to cross a group of them is bounded independently of the group's size (a jump to the group's end through a group index built and admitted against the plan limit by the plan compiler, in ADR 0058's slice G1a, which its slice G3 depends on), proven with a large duplicate group inside one block. A binary search at the block's start alone does not bound a group crossed later in the block.
   - **d. The one-sample fade under the closed law.** The closed law defines a one-sample fade as the midpoint gain `Fade::gain(0.5)`; today's half-open law, which ADR 0058's clip slice replaces, gives it today's arguments (0 in, 1 out). The midpoint is a property of the closed law only. For an isolated pair of uncurved fades of identical shape, the one-sample gain sums in real arithmetic are 1, √2, 1, 0.25, 1.75 for Linear, Equal Power, S-Curve, Exponential, Logarithmic, respectively, under the closed law (today's half-open law: 1 for every shape). With curvature, the closed-law sum comes from the two midpoint evaluations and need not equal these figures (today's half-open sum remains 1).
   - **e. Crossfade sums per shape.** Stated for an isolated complementary pair only (the two fades of one crossfade with equal lengths, no other fade overlapping inside either clip), as identities in real arithmetic with the fade-out's argument the complement of the fade-in's:
     - **Uncurved, per shape:** Linear in with Linear out sums to 1 in gain; S-Curve with S-Curve sums to 1 in gain; Exponential in with Logarithmic out, and Logarithmic in with Exponential out, sum to 1 in gain (`1 − (1 − (1 − t))³ = 1 − t³`); Equal Power with Equal Power is complementary in squared gain, not in gain (the amplitudes sum to √2 at the midpoint); two Exponential or two Logarithmic fades have no sum law.
     - **With opposite curvatures** `c` in and `−c` out: the weighting multiplies a gain's odds `b/(1 − b)` by `2^(4c)`, so a pair whose uncurved gains sum to 1 still sums to 1: Linear, S-Curve, and the Exponential and Logarithmic cross pairs (shapes and weighting as in today's fade code, `crates/model/src/clip.rs:22-69`, which ADR 0058 keeps in `f64`; checked in exact rationals for Linear and the cross pairs at `2^(4c)` = 2, 4, 1/8, 16 and 1/16, 3,855 sums all exactly 1; round 4, computed). It does not carry Equal Power's squared-gain identity: at the midpoint with curvatures +0.5 and −0.5 the gains are 0.9061636786 and 0.3763849674, a squared sum of 0.9627982562 and a gain sum of 1.2825486460 (round 4, computed). Any guarantee for a curved Equal Power pair is ADR 0058's, from its own rule and evidence; this record states none.
     - **In rendered arithmetic** none of these is exact by itself; the bound comes from a proof or a measurement with a convergence check (at the midpoint, curvatures ±`f32(0.2632)` sum to 0.9999998807907104 in the round-2 arithmetic, so the round-2 figure "within 4·10⁻⁹", observed at ±1, is not a bound; today's `f32` code gives opposite-curvature sums up to 1.43·10⁻⁶ from 1 for the cross pairs and 1.55·10⁻⁶ for S-Curve, round 4, computed). Fades of different lengths have no sum law.
   - **f. Partition invariance and unity.** The clip source, gain and fade path is block-partition invariant for a fixed snapshot with available source data, and a clip at 0 dB with no fades and no envelope applies exactly unity: alone on its strip it renders its source bit for bit, except that a −0.0 source sample renders +0.0 (an IEEE sum) and a NaN stays a NaN with its payload unspecified (ADR 0058 decision 5; its test `unity_gain_and_no_fades_pass_the_source_bit_for_bit`).
   - **g. Cost.** A per-clip budget, with a miss handled as ADR 0058's decided item 9 states (this record's round-2 budget: at most 0.5 percent of one core for a stereo clip at 48 kHz with both fades curved and overlapping across the whole clip and an envelope; a clip with neither no slower than the base beyond 5 percent). The clip cost is measured with the fade cache disabled, once per fade shape, against the same budget (ADR 0058 decisions 13 and 17). If any shape misses, ADR 0058's decided item 9 applies: the qualified promise, not holding the slice. Under a qualified promise a bounded cache of fade factors is counted in the plan's prepared bytes, never a full per-fade table (two hour-long fades would take 2.76 GB). For the benchmark workload the 0.5 percent budget applies when both fades are resident in an admitted cache; otherwise the stated uncached budget applies, including when plan admission omits the cache. The uncached cost is stated on the claims page. A fade the cache does not hold is evaluated per sample with the same values.
   - **h. A drawing entry point.** The clip gain function callable off the audio thread for any clip sample, returning the value the render uses (9.4). The same evaluator serves every preview with temporary fades during a drag and the fade-curve overlay (which draws the fade factor at clip samples), with the clip's data, clamps, fade law and floor (ADR 0058 decision 13). Edit commands that insert clip-envelope points from the envelope's value (copy and paste clip gain, the coalescing commands, volume automation to clip gain) use the same evaluator from ADR 0058's G3 on. This changes the values they store by today's `f32` interpolation rounding: a −144 dB to `f32(0.1)` segment read at its end point stores 0.10000000149011612 instead of 0.100006103515625. Their test expectations re-pin in G3.
7. **History: the round-2 measurements of the closed law** (kept for ADR 0058 to re-establish under its own arithmetic, not normative here; `f32` gain arguments and products as round 2 specified them): Linear complementary pairs summed to exactly 1.0 for every length from 2 to 4096 (8,390,655 pairs); S-Curve within 1.8·10⁻⁷ and Equal Power's squared gains within 2.1·10⁻⁷ (every length to 256 and nine longer ones to 48,000); curvature pairs within 1.37·10⁻⁶ (Linear) and 1.79·10⁻⁶ (S-Curve) over the sample set listed in round 2's record, the witness n = 4096, k = 4078 summing to 1.0000008344650269; a 32-sample interpolated equal-power crossfade dips 3.0 dB (Context), the reason for per-sample evaluation.

### 10. The loop region

1. **The editor's loop is ADR 0019's loop.** The brace sits over the source in the `loop` hue (as the Sampler's zone waveform draws a loop), its edges drag (`clip.set_loop`), the Loop toggle sets or clears it (`clip.set_loop` over the region, `clip.clear_loop`), and its start and length are fields in samples, Min:Secs and bars|beats. The arrangement length beyond one cycle stays the arrangement's loop handle.
2. **#427, for audio that is not warped: the cycle stays a whole number of samples.** An unwarped clip plays at a fixed rate in time, not in beats (Ableton plays a sample with Warp off "at its original tempo, unaffected by the Set's current tempo"), so its loop is a time span; resampling each cycle onto the grid would change the audio of a loop the user cut on purpose. The editor shows the honest consequence: when the loop's length is within half a sample of a whole number of bars or beats but not on it, the loop fields show the drift per cycle (`+0.24 samples a cycle against the grid`). A loop that follows the grid is the warping record's. ADR 0019's "Not changed" paragraph gains a pointer to this decision when slice 5 lands, and #427 is closed by that pointer.
3. **Snapping the loop to the grid** snaps each edge to its nearest sample of a grid line; nothing rounds the cycle further.

### 11. Several clips, sources, takes

1. **One audio clip in v0.8.0.** Multi-clip property editing (Ableton edits shared properties of several clips; split handles for differing values) is a later slice. Several MIDI clips keep opening together (#329).
2. **Other clips of the same source** are not drawn in v0.8.0; a later slice may outline their regions on the source.
3. **Takes and comping.** The editor shows the active take's clip. Swipe comping (ADR 0039 slice 2) is not in the editor.

### 12. The stored form, and room for warping

1. **What this record fixes:** the mapping of decision 5 for a clip with no warp (`x(c) = offset + s·(c − anchor)` after the clip-time loop wrap), the reader of decision 8, and the clip-time semantics of fades, the gain envelope and the sync point (decision 7.5). The order in which a future warp, the loop and the reader compose is **not** a permanent contract.
2. **`warp` is reserved as an extensible mapping and render description.** A clip gains no warp field now. The warping record adds `warp: Option<...>`, written only when present, with its own `needs` entry. **Absent means decision 5's fixed-rate mapping.** When present, it replaces that mapping for the clip, and it may carry what a scalar rate cannot: source and destination anchor pairs (stretch markers), a beat-following loop placement, and a render description for pitch-preserving modes (whose engines are not a position map into this reader); it may give `transpose` the meaning of pitch at constant length, as Ableton's warp modes do. An older build ignores the field and plays the clip at fixed rate; builds since the marker warn.
3. **Left open for the warping record:** how a warped clip's loop is placed and wrapped (in beats or time), whether its reader is this one, the meaning of `transpose` and `fine` under warp, how `offset` and `anchor` relate to its anchors (and whether it needs a domain of its own beyond decision 5.5), and whether existing fades and envelopes stay in clip time under warp (this record keeps them in clip time and forbids silently reinterpreting existing automation when warp is enabled).
4. **What this record forbids**, so the warping record is not boxed in: any other meaning for a clip key named `warp`; storing a source-time position anywhere but `offset` and the warp description (every other clip position is clip time); new timing state in `edit.values` keyed by clip id (the inherited `elastic.<id>.*`, `clip.bpm.<id>` and `clip.len0.<id>` keys stay until the warping record retires them, and nothing new joins them); and a transpose that changes `stretch`.

### 13. Real time, determinism, portable math

1. **Nothing allocates or locks on the audio thread.** The reader table is built before the first plan and shared read-only; the clip gain path's state is ADR 0058's; the no-allocation callback test runs with a reversed, transposed, faded, enveloped clip playing.
2. **Work per block** is bounded by the clips playing times the block length times the reader's taps (decision 8.8), plus the clip gain path's own work, which ADR 0058 bounds and budgets (breakpoints sharing a time included, 9.6 c); nothing in the reader scales with a source's length.
3. **Disk-backed clips** move the reader to the streamer thread (decision 8.6); the audio thread's work for them is unchanged.
4. **Portable math (ADR 0055).** The rate's `exp2`, the table's `sin`, `sqrt` and Bessel series and Normalize's `log10` go through `transit-math`; fade shapes and gain conversion do too, under ADR 0058. After ADR 0055's golden slices land, an offline render of a session using every property here is a cross-platform golden; before, the tests compare in process.

### 14. Edge cases

- **Sample rates.** Timeline sources are at the session rate in the pool; the source ruler converts stored frames to seconds (decision 2.2). A Sampler-shared source (#477) is at its native rate, so its frames are not session samples: the editor shows a status tag (`shared with a Sampler`) and refuses reverse, transpose, slip and Normalize on it until #477 decides. The editor never creates such sharing.
- **Very long files.** The editor draws from the pool's mipmap, or from a disk-backed source's 1024-frame peaks; zoomed below 1024 frames a column on a disk-backed source, a background reader fills the visible span from the file (`DiskSource::with_sync` off the UI thread) and the status tag reads `Analyzing…` until it arrives (later slice; the minimum cut stops zoom at the peaks' resolution for disk-backed sources and says so in the tag). Transient detection on a disk-backed source reads the region plus one window each side, never the whole file. No second decode of a source.
- **Missing files.** The source record (and so `F`) stays; slip, trims, reverse and transpose work on the stored fields and the domain of decision 5.5; Normalize refuses (it needs the audio); the display hatches (decision 2.9).
- **A dangling source id** (no source record at all, only from a hand-edited file): every command that needs `F` refuses.
- **Wider than stereo.** Rendering folds as today (`clips.rs:106-111`); the editor draws decision 2.4.

### 17. Snapping and the time range

1. **Snap targets in order:** a transient within 8 points (when Snap to Transients is on), else the grid (the arrangement's snap toggle and grid, Ctrl+4), then, when Snap to Zero Crossings is on, the nearest zero crossing of the raw source within ±5 ms of that point (`slicing::snap_to_zero_crossing`, Logic's option). Shift bypasses all snapping, as in the arrangement. The transient and zero-crossing toggles are per-machine view settings, off by default. On a clip with q ≠ 1 a snap target found in source frames is mapped to the clip sample whose position is nearest it, so the snapped sample reads within q/2 source frames of the crossing; the rendered signal there is the reader's output and is not guaranteed to cross zero (stated in the help). At q = 1 the snapped sample is the crossing's frame.
2. **The time range is the clip's.** A plain drag in the waveform selects a range inside the open clip and sets the session's selection with `edit.select {tracks: [the clip's track], start, end, exact: true}`, so no edit group widens it and the arrangement draws the same range. The editor's range actions are **clip-local** and pass the clip explicitly: Set Loop to Range (`clip.set_loop`), Crop to Range (`edit.trim_clip` on this clip with `shuffle: false`, so the arrangement's Shuffle mode ripples nothing, decision 3.2), Fade In and Fade Out to Range (`edit.fade_to_start`/`_end` with `clips: [id]`), Split at Range (`edit.separate` with `clips: [id]` at the range's edges, so an overlapping crossfade partner is not split), Zoom to Range. None of them reaches another clip or track; the range is drawn only over the open clip in the editor. Group-wide range editing stays the arrangement's (#224); when #224 lands, the arrangement's own gesture sets the same `edit.selection`.

### 18. Audition

1. **Audition is the transport.** Space plays through the track's chain and fader, as everywhere. A click in the editor's rulers locates the transport there (Ableton's scrub area jumps playback), snapped as decision 17.
2. **Play Region** (locates to the clip's start and plays) and **Loop Range** (sets the transport loop to the range and turns Cycle on) are editor actions, in slice 8. Loop Range needs a range to act on, so it is absent until the editor's range drag (decision 17.2, slice 5) has landed. **The reader's acceptance measurement** (`the_reader_meets_its_acceptance_bounds` runs it; round 3 ran it in standard-library Python). Every number below is a binary64 value computed as written, each operation rounded once, so two implementations evaluate the same points.
- **The analytic response.** For a rate q and a position `x = 1000 + φ` (φ the fractional part; the tap distances `|x − k|` are formed in binary64), take the reader's coefficients `c_k` exactly as decision 8.3 computes them (`f32` table and delta table, `u` in `f64`, the fraction and the coefficient in `f32`, then the `f32` factor `1/q` for q > 1) and form `H(f, φ) = Σ c_k·e^(i2πf(k − x))` in `f64` for the source frequency `f`. For the coefficient-linear response (the coefficients as rounded, the tone and the sum exact) the output for a source tone `sin(2πfk + θ)` is `Im(e^(i(2πfx + θ))·H)`, so over every tone phase θ its largest error is exactly `|H − 1|` (passband) and its largest amplitude exactly `|H|` (alias). These are not exact maxima for the full `f32` reader, whose rounding of the tone samples, the products and the accumulation is nonlinear; that reader is measured separately (the full `f32` check, below). A rate's figure is the largest value over its φ and frequency points.
- **Rates.** The regular low grid `q_i = 1 + i·δ`, i = 1 to 500, with δ the binary64 value of 0.002 (i = 250 gives exactly 1.5, i = 500 exactly 2); the irregular rates 1 + 10⁻⁹, 1 + 10⁻⁶, 1 + 10⁻⁴, 1 + 10⁻³, 1.5 + 10⁻⁹, 1.5 + 10⁻⁶, 1.5 + 10⁻⁴, 1.501, 1.506 and 1.51 (each sum or literal rounded once), merged with the low grid and duplicates removed; the high grid `2·16^(i/400)`, i = 1 to 400, for the passband and `2·16^(i/300)`, i = 1 to 300, for aliases. Rates below 1 are not swept one by one: below q = 1 the coefficients do not depend on q, so one sweep (run at q = 0.5) covers every q < 1, and q = 1 itself is the direct read (decision 5.3), exact.
- **Bands and boundaries.** A rate belongs to the band q < 1.5 when it is below 1.5 and to the band 1.5 ≤ q ≤ 32 otherwise, so the grid point exactly 1.5 is in the upper band, and the bands are reported as q < 1 (with q = 1 exact), 1 < q < 1.5 and 1.5 ≤ q ≤ 32.
- **Phases.** φ = j/N, j = 0 to N − 1: N = 2048 for q < 1, 64 on the low grid and the irregular rates, 32 on the passband high grid, 16 on the alias high grid.
- **Frequencies.** Passband: output frequency `v = 0.005 + n·0.001`, n = 0 to 415 (to 0.42 fs), source frequency `v / max(1, q)`. Alias, only for rates with q/2 > 0.58 (below about 1.16 no source tone under the source Nyquist plays at 0.58 fs or above): `v = 0.58 + n·0.0005` for n = 0 to `floor((min(q/2, 1.2) − 0.58)/0.0005 − 10⁻⁹)`, then, when q/2 > 1.2, `v = 1.2 + m·0.01` for m = 1 to `floor((q/2 − 1.2)/0.01 − 10⁻⁹)`; a point counts only when its source frequency `v/q` is below 0.5.
- **Refinement.** For each kind (passband, alias) and band (q < 1, 1 < q < 1.5, 1.5 ≤ q ≤ 32), the six largest main-grid values, each refined on its own. Initial half-widths: `Δq` = 0 for q < 1 (q is not a variable there), 0.002 for 1 < q ≤ 2 and 0.007·q above 2; `Δv` = 0.001 (passband) or 0.0005 (alias); `Δφ` = 1/64 in every band. Each of five rounds evaluates the 125 points `(q0 + a·Δq/2, v0 + b·Δv/2, φ0 + c·Δφ/2)` for a, b, c in −2 to 2, φ taken modulo 1, around the round's center `(q0, v0, φ0)`; it keeps the largest value seen so far (a tie keeps the earlier point), the next round is centered on it, and `Δq`, `Δv` and `Δφ` are halved. A point outside its band or frequency range is skipped: passband v in [0.005, 0.42]; alias v ≥ 0.58 with `v/q` < 0.5; q in (1, 1.5) for the lower band and [1.5, 32] for the upper. (Round 3's run let the lower band's window include q = 1.5 itself; no lower-band refinement moved above q = 1.2, so its figures are those of this rule.)
- **Convergence check.** The main sweep repeated with the regular grids halved: δ the binary64 value of 0.001 (i = 1 to 1000), 800 and 600 high-grid rates, every N doubled, every frequency step halved (0.0005, 0.00025 and 0.005); the irregular rates are kept as they are, not halved. The halved sweep changed no band's worst value by more than 0.09 dB (0.081 dB at most, bands q < 1, 1 < q ≤ 1.5, 1.5 < q ≤ 2, 2 < q ≤ 4 and 4 < q ≤ 32, passband and alias), and the refinement raised no band's worst value above that band's main-grid worst by more than 0.16 dB (individual refined points rose by up to 0.23 dB above their own grid values). The observed convergence supports the chosen measured margins; it is not a bound over unsampled points. (The steps sample the kernel's spectral sidelobes, about 1/32 fs apart, about fifteen times each: the reason for the step sizes, not a proof.)
- **The full `f32` check.** At every refined worst case the full `f32` reader (tone samples rounded to `f32`, `f32` products and accumulation in ascending `k`, the `f32` factor `1/q`, the tone phase chosen so that the position shows the analytic worst case) was run; it differed from the analytic figure by no more than 0.02 dB. The bounds of decision 8.4 sit at least 0.5 dB above both the analytic and the full `f32` figures. The response to a constant and the table figure of decision 8.4 were measured separately (round 4): the first with the full `f32` reader, the second on the coefficients only.
- **Reproduced for the round:** passband −73.53 dB at q = 1.000001 (0.416 fs), −75.38 dB at q = 1.01 (0.414 fs), −78.88 dB at q = 1.51 and −78.93 dB at q = 1.6 (0.4155 fs); alias −80.49 dB at q = 2, output 0.584 fs, phase 0.5, as an RMS over a run (−79.55 dB as the worst position, the measure used now).

**Method of the rounds' other computations** (standard-library Python with IEEE binary64 arithmetic, no fused multiply-add; `f32` emulated by rounding each intermediate to binary32; shapes with `sin` or `cos` evaluated in binary64 and rounded, which approximates the portable `f32` functions within one unit in the last place): the fade and crossfade sums by enumeration as stated (the curved sums added in `f32`); the envelope by today's `env_value` arithmetic in `f32` and `db_to_gain` in `f64` rounded once; the mapping and its domain with exact rational arithmetic over random edit sequences (decision 5.5); the transposes by decision 7.3's rules.

## Consequences

- An audio clip has an editor: the clip's window onto its file, its gain, fades, loop, reverse and pitch, all non-destructive and all one undo step.
- One mapping from clip time to source position serves every edit, so a split, trim, slip or reverse is exact at any rate, and today's defects for stretched clips (split, heal, bounds, AudioSuite) are fixed on the way.
- Existing renders change once, announced as a Change: clips with `stretch ≠ 1` through the reader, in memory and from disk together (slice 7a). Faded and enveloped clips change with ADR 0058's clip slice, not with this record; the editor's gain drawing moves from today's nominal curves to ADR 0058's function in that same landing.
- The stretch defects of #554 are fixed ahead of the editor (slice 1, v0.7.2).
- Transposing a clip changes its speed and length together, like tape, and the control is labeled `Speed · Pitch` and says so; this differs from Logic Pro's documented pitch-control model (fixed-length behavior unverified), and pitch-only transposition arrives with the warping record. A pitched-down clip stops at its neighbor; transposing down and back restores the length exactly, up and back within `(u + 1)/2` samples (a clip shorter than half the step ratio, held at the one-sample minimum, comes back at `round(u)` samples); a cut end is restored by trimming, not by transposing back. No hidden state remembers an earlier length.
- A clip pitched far up costs in proportion to its rate (98.3 million tap contributions a second for a stereo clip at q = 32), within a measured budget that blocks the landing if missed.
- Three new `needs` entries; an older build plays a reversed, transposed or re-anchored clip wrongly, and builds since the markers warn. Older builds drop the fields on save (K40's class).
- The piano roll starts following a single-clip selection while open.
- The clip inspector strip goes only after the editor has its fields and behavior.
- Unwarped audio loops keep whole-sample cycles; the drift is shown, not hidden.
- **On Windows, Ctrl+Shift drag on a clip body stops making an unsnapped copy and slips instead; the unsnapped copy moves to Alt+Shift drag.** On macOS, Shift+Option drag stops making an unsnapped copy and slips instead (Ableton's chord); the unsnapped copy is Cmd+Shift drag, as today. The release notes must say both.
- The editor header shows the file's own sample rate, bit depth and channels, with a note when the file plays converted to the session's rate.
- Editor trims never ripple, whatever the arrangement's edit mode.

## Alternatives rejected

- **A separate Audio Editor tab** beside the piano roll: two tabs for one job, and a MIDI clip and an audio clip would open in different places; both references use one editor area.
- **Reverse by rendering a new file** (Ableton, and #181's plan): a new file per toggle, and editing would produce audio against the project's rule.
- **Reverse as a mirror of the file** (`F − 1 − (offset + p)`, the first draft): needs `F`, is wrong at any rate but 1, and cannot keep a split exact; the signed rate needs neither.
- **An integer `offset` alone**, or a rounded `f64` start position per piece: cannot keep phase through splits and reversals at a non-unit rate (computed: the rounded origin mismatched in 296 of 300 random splits); `anchor` keeps every operation in integers.
- **A destructive file editor** (Logic's Audio File Editor): the maintainer decided non-destructive; the offline tools already cover file processing without overwriting.
- **Transpose that keeps the clip's length** (the window stays, more or less material shows): with no stretch it hides or reveals material on every pitch change, and a pitched one-shot does not play whole; pitch at constant length is the warping record's. The maintainer chose the length that follows the rate (question 3).
- **A span memory for transposes** (the first revision: an origin and a mark on the clip, so a transpose there and back, or one cut by a neighbor, would be lossless): complete, it needs invalidation rules for every edit, both split pieces, copies, source replacement, save, load and undo, its own compatibility need even when the clip is otherwise plain, and a later transpose could silently restore a length the user had cut. Removed in round 2; the cost is a there-and-back that is exact only going down first (7.3), and a cut end that the user trims back out.
- **A stored anchor bounded by ±`MAX_POSITION`** (the first revision): not closed under reverse and start trims at low rates (the round's counterexample); the bound of decision 5.5 follows from the material invariant instead.
- **Letting a transposed clip's last center overhang the file** (rounding the length to nearest at the file's end): reads up to nearly a frame past the file into silence and breaks the material invariant; the far-end rule cuts that fraction instead.
- **Reading silence for a reversed intro that leaves the file:** would leave the domain open; reverse is refused there instead (6.4).
- **Interpolating the envelope's linear gain over 32-sample segments** (the first revision): a −144 to +36 dB ramp over 31 samples renders 30.53 where the exact value is 0.001428, and a split moves the segments; per-sample dB evaluation costs one `db_to_gain` per sample.
- **Transpose through today's linear varispeed** (`stretch`): unfiltered two-point interpolation aliases audibly; it would make the flagship sound worse than both references.
- **The Sampler's cubic reader:** #474's aliasing when pitched up.
- **A fixed 32-tap kernel with only its cutoff scaled for upward rates:** measured at q = 2, content at 0.58 fs aliases at −22.9 dB. Capping the stretched kernel's tap count at high rates fails the same way (decision 8.8).
- **Fades interpolated over 32-sample segments** (today, and the first draft): a 32-sample equal-power crossfade dips 3.0 dB; per-sample fades cost one shape evaluation per sample inside a fade.
- **The mirrored fade law of the first draft:** breaks every crossfade sum (linear `(n − 1)/n`).
- **An editor axis in file time only** (a sample editor): trims and slips could not be seen against the bars they land on.
- **Resampling each audio loop cycle onto the grid (#427's other option):** changes a loop the user cut on purpose; musical loops are warping's.
- **Gain envelope editing in the minimum cut:** the references disagree on the form; it is a design of its own.
- **An editor-local range model:** a second selection model beside `edit.selection`; the editor sets the same selection, exact and on one track, and its actions take the clip explicitly.
- **Ctrl+Alt drag for slip** (the first draft, from memory): it is a copy-drag in transit, and Live documents Ctrl+Shift.
- **Cmd+Shift drag for slip on macOS** (the accepted record, transit's command modifier): an Ableton user on a Mac Shift+Option-drags to slip, and in transit that made an unsnapped copy. Replaced by Ableton's Shift+Option after the convention audit.
- **The stored session rate in the editor header** (the accepted record, question 13): a user checking whether a file is 44.1 or 48 kHz saw the session rate. Replaced by the file's own format after the convention audit.
