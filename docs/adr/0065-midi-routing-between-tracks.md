# ADR 0065: MIDI routing between tracks

- **Status:** accepted 2026-11-27 (proposed 2026-11-27, issue #671; the record ADR 0064 decision 11 names as its next record; builds on ADR 0057, ADR 0059 and ADR 0064, all accepted; revised during review the same day, Changed during review; seven product choices decided 2026-11-27 by delegation, each marked where it is written and listed under For the maintainer to confirm or overturn). Acceptance does not remove the maintainer's right to overturn any of those choices. External MIDI ports are the follow-up record "Routed MIDI to external ports" (decision 7), named here and not yet written. The slices depend on slices of ADR 0064, ADR 0057 and ADR 0059 in the order the Slices section gives.
- **Date:** 2026-11-27
- **Implementation:** not started. Slice cards in the Slices section; a contributor can take any slice whose needs have landed.
- **Reopen when:** the follow-up record "Routed MIDI to external ports" (decision 7) is accepted (it then admits external-output tracks as destinations and may amend decision 1.5); a destination needs more than one routed source (fan-in, decision 1.4); a tap before the chain or after a given stage is wanted (decision 1.2); a plug-in that writes several note outputs meant for several destinations is supported (ADR 0064 decision 3 reads one); ADR 0036's rack stage lands (racks as sources and destinations, decision 1.5); the MIDI effect chain runs on an external output (ADR 0057, Where this goes), which would let an External Instrument track or a track with an external output be a source; ADR 0043's clock sending is built and a route is asked to carry clock; the alignment stage (decision 4) or a route recovery barrier (decision 3.4) is measured happening in ordinary use, or users report held chords cut under load; a route's same-block ordering is measured costing more render time than a block can afford; or Ableton Live and Logic Pro are found, in their documentation, to agree on a behavior this record chose differently.

## Context

**The request.** Issue #671's reporting user has a note-writing plug-in (a generative chord performer with no sound of its own) on one track and wants its notes to drive synths on other tracks, and eventually external MIDI gear. This record decides routing between tracks; external gear is the follow-up record's (decision 7). ADR 0064 delivers a hosted MIDI effect's notes only to its own track's instrument (decision 11) and names this record as the next one. Its decision 11 sets the constraints this record keeps: the chain's output after its last stage is a per-block stream a second consumer can read without changing what the source instrument receives, and it is not fully identified (bypassed hosted stages pass their input's identities, built-in generators write anonymous notes), so a route must qualify identities by source; ownership stays per hop, a destination owning what it accepted by its own tables; a source barrier must reach every destination; admission at a destination is bounded; the tap is before the source instrument's admission. The record is written for whole classes of note source (hosted stages, built-in devices, clips and live input passing an empty chain) and is not designed around any reported plug-in.

**What the code does today** (verified against code on the branch carrying ADR 0064, head 4ca4f772; line numbers are that tree's).

- **A track's MIDI input** is `Track::midi_input: MidiInput { port: MidiPort, channel: Option<u8> }`, `MidiPort` one of `All` (the default), `None` or `Device { backend, name, ordinal, id }` (`crates/model/src/midi_input.rs:6` to `26`; `crates/model/src/track.rs:243`), set by `midi.input`, which refuses kinds other than MIDI and instrument tracks (`crates/engine/src/cmd/midi_input.rs:49` to `88`). The only chooser is the mixer strip's input row (`crates/ui-studio/src/mixer/channel.rs:262` to `295`, `crates/ui-studio/src/midi_input.rs:127` to `182`). The #760 "MIDI handoff" carries open MIDI inputs across an audio reopen (`crates/playback/src/player/device/midi_handoff_api.rs`); it does not choose inputs.
- **Monitoring.** Record Enable and Input Monitoring are runtime state, "never saved, and false whenever a session is opened ..., so opening a session never activates an input path" (`crates/model/src/mixer.rs:691` to `697`); undo and redo never change them (`keep_runtime_state`, `crates/engine/src/lib.rs:730` to `745`), and their commands are not undo steps (`crates/engine/src/cmd/mix.rs:43` to `61`). For **MIDI**, playback opens a live hardware route to an instrument or MIDI track while it is active and armed **or** monitored, whether the transport plays, records or is stopped (`crates/playback/src/player/native.rs:582`); Audio > Monitor Armed Tracks does not enter that rule. For **audio**, a track hears its input with Input Monitoring on, or while armed when Monitor Armed Tracks is on and the transport records or the engine is monitor-only (`hears_input`, `crates/mix/src/strip.rs:86`; `crates/engine/src/cmd/options.rs:89`). The Input Monitoring button is enabled in the mixer for audio tracks only (`crates/ui-studio/src/mixer/channel.rs:409`) and in the arranger for audio tracks and racks (`crates/ui-studio/src/arrange/tracks.rs:150`); the `mix.input_monitor` command itself has no kind check.
- **Hardware MIDI over playing clips.** An armed instrument track plays its clips and its keyboard at once: the live route opens while armed (`native.rs:582`), and in the same block the strip schedules the track's clips and then delivers the remaining live events, "including when stopped" (`crates/mix/src/strip.rs:177` to `191`), clips first at a tie (`crates/mix/src/midi.rs:199` to `209`). Overdubbing over the clips works this way.
- **The mix's note table, per instrument,** matches by source: `Source { port: u16, channel: u8, generation: u64 }`, with reserved ports `Source::UI` and `Source::CLIP` (`crates/mix/src/events.rs:101` to `112`), and "Matching includes the whole source, then the sender id when there is one, else the key" (`NoteEntry::matches`, `:456` to `459`). It holds 224 notes and 32 pedals (`:376` to `381`), assigns every note a voice id of its own (`:27` to `28`), coalesces a same-source same-key re-strike (`:15` to `16`), releases everything one source generation holds with `release_sender` (`:1040` onward) and everything with `release_all` (`:1092`), and is the single exit to the chain or the instrument (`send`, `:811` to `821`); `release_sender`'s releases pass through `send`, so with an active chain they enter its first stage.
- **The chain lives in the instrument's event state.** In a strip: `fx.begin`, retries, clip scheduling interleaved with earlier live events, `deliver`, `fx.flush`, then the instrument's `process` (`crates/mix/src/strip.rs:164` to `194`). The event state exists only for an instrument cell (`crates/mix/src/compile.rs:961` to `963`); an empty instrument slot, a MIDI track and an External Instrument have no cell (`crates/mix/src/instance.rs:67` to `78`). A hosted instrument on a track with an external MIDI output still renders and gets note-offs only (`strip.rs:173` to `186`).
- **Render order and loops.** The route graph has edges Output, Send, Input and Key (`crates/model/src/route_graph.rs:39` to `49`). Commands refuse an edit that closes a loop (L22: `refuse_loop`, `crates/engine/src/cmd/track.rs:908` to `915`; backstop `crates/engine/src/lib.rs:911` to `918`). A loaded loop is cut by `RouteScratch::solve` (`route_graph.rs:442` to `534`): per round, in each strongly connected component, the edges into the bus that is the input of the component's first track (lowest track index, `:427` to `434`), else the Key edges into that first track; Key cuts are reported dropped (`:529`). Phase-1 strips render in layers by depth (`crates/mix/src/compile.rs:322` to `351`): serially (`crates/mix/src/active.rs:600` to `632`), or through the render workers' task graph with an edge from every key source in an earlier layer (`crates/mix/src/graph.rs:269`), where a missed source skips its dependents.
- **Delay compensation is audio only** (`head_latency`, `strip.rs:523` to `527`; `route_into`, `crates/mix/src/routing.rs:75` to `200`). ADR 0057 decision 7 (slice P4) adds `L_midi`, with a requested figure for planning and a provisioned figure for rendering.
- **Mute does not stop a strip.** `strip.muted` comes from mute, mute automation, a VCA and solo (`strip.rs:323` to `362`, solo at `:326`) and sets the fader to -144 dB (`:362`); the instrument is processed regardless (`:194`). External clip delivery suppresses non-release messages of a muted or solo-excluded track and lets releases pass (`crates/playback/src/player/audio/external.rs:190` onward).
- **Freeze** turns a frozen track into an audio track with no instrument in the plan (`crates/model/src/session.rs:558` to `582`); the freeze render keeps a frozen track's sidechain key feeders running (`key_feeders`, `crates/mix/src/offline.rs:303` to `370`). Freezing an External Instrument track is refused (known issue K64).
- **External MIDI output** (ADR 0022): `Track::midi_output: Internal | External { port, channel, offset_ms }`. External clip events come from a traversal of the clips (`crates/mix/src/external_midi.rs:52` to `70`), stamped on the audio thread with the presentation time of their frame at the current speed, into a playback ring of 4,096 slots, 256 reserved for releases, whose push can refuse even a protected event when the ring is full (`Producer::push`, `crates/playback/src/player/midi_out.rs:115` to `128`). The output thread keeps each track's held notes and the sustain pedal (CC 64) only (`:1015` onward), sends at Stop and panic a note-off per held key, CC 64 off and CC 123 (`:1153` to `1170`), ends a destination's held playback notes at a loop wrap or locate (`:925` to `944`), rejects stale echo events by a route generation (`:130` onward), and on device loss forgets the port's held state without sending anything (`lose`, `:904` to `913`). No MIDI clock is sent; ADR 0043's clock is not built.
- **MIDI output across an audio reopen.** On this branch the replacement output thread reopens the ports (`crates/playback/src/player/native/handoff.rs:95` to `147`); unlanded work carries them across instead (decision 7).
- **Recording** captures on the audio thread every live packet the mix buffered for an armed track while recording or counting in, at the output frame it was given (`crates/playback/src/player/audio.rs:996` to `1014`): hardware input before the chain, refused or not.
- **Cross-track references** are stored by `TrackId`; a key naming a missing track is inert. A session with an unknown field loads in an older build with the field dropped; an unknown enum variant fails the load (`Session::from_json`, `session.rs:781` to `792`).

**What other hosts document.** Ableton documents a destination track taking "MIDI From" another track and choosing the plug-in as the source ([Accessing the MIDI output of a VST plug-in](https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/209070189-Accessing-the-MIDI-output-of-a-VST-plug-in); that page blocks automated reading and is cited for that claim only). Ableton's routing chapter ([Routing and I/O](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/routing-and-i-o/)) documents a track's Monitor setting (Auto: the input is heard when the track is armed, and suppressed while the track's clips play; In: the input is heard permanently and the clips are suppressed; Off), and a MIDI track taking input from another track tapped after that track's MIDI effects and before its instrument. Logic's guide ([Route MIDI internally to software instrument tracks](https://support.apple.com/guide/logicpro/lgcp1efa7c4d/mac)) is cited only for this: the routing is chosen on the receiving track ("Internal MIDI In") and can be recorded there. No other decision rests on another host's behavior; where this record differs from Ableton's Monitor setting (5.1), it says so.

## Changed during review (2026-11-27)

- Routed input follows the destination's Listen switch or Record Enable instead of always sounding; Listen is saved with the route and undoable, and decides exports and freezes (decision 5).
- A route barrier is defined at the receiving chain and always raises the destination's own tap epoch, so it reaches every downstream track (3.3).
- Every new boundary has bounded ownership and an automatic recovery barrier; a tap block that lost an event is damaged and never delivered (2.3, 3.4).
- A route latency change ends every note the destination delivered; requested and provisioned figures are kept apart (4.3).
- The loop cut is extended to MIDI edges (2.1); source mute stops its routed output and deleting a source leaves its destinations disconnected (decision 6).
- The note table's route generation is a per-event-state ordinal, deterministic for fingerprints (3.1).
- External MIDI ports moved to their own record; decision 7 keeps the agreed direction and its requirements, and a track with an external output is not a route destination until that record is accepted and built (1.5).

## Decision

### 1. The model

1. **What is routed: the chain's output after its last stage.** The **tap** of a track is the stream its MIDI effect host offers the instrument after the last stage, before the host's delivery filter and the instrument's admission (ADR 0057 P3b; ADR 0064 11.4): with stages, what the last stage's output buffer accepted; with no stage, the stream the mix's note table sends (the track's clips, live input, chase and seek releases). The tap does not depend on what the source instrument accepts or refuses, and reading it changes nothing the source instrument receives. This is the same tap point Ableton documents for a MIDI track taking another track's input (after its MIDI effects, before its instrument; Context).
2. **No tap before the chain or per stage.** Out of scope: a destination that wants the source's raw input can take the same hardware input; a per-stage tap would put a destination inside every stage's edit and barrier rules.
3. **The choice lives on the destination.** A **route** goes from one source track to one destination track; the destination's MIDI input is either a hardware choice (today's `midi_input`) or one source track. Ableton and Logic both document the choice on the receiving track (Context), and it keeps one rule for a track's input: one input, one channel filter.
4. **One source per destination, any number of destinations per source.** Fan-in is out of scope. A source never admits on a destination's behalf: each destination reads the source's fixed tap (2.3) and admits by its own tables (decision 3), so fan-out multiplies no storage at the source; each destination's admission work is its own.
5. **Which tracks.**
   - **A source** is an instrument track that is not a rack, whose `midi_output` is `Internal`, whose instrument slot is not an External Instrument and which is not frozen; its instrument slot may be empty (2.5). Not sources: MIDI tracks (no chain, ADR 0036), tracks with an external output or an External Instrument (the chain does not run on an external output; ADR 0057, Where this goes), racks (the rack's chain runs once per member until ADR 0036's rack stage), and every other kind.
   - **A destination** is an instrument track that is not a rack, whose `midi_output` is `Internal` and whose instrument slot is not an External Instrument (a hosted, built-in or empty slot); it plays routed notes through its own chain and instrument. **A track with an external MIDI output is not a route destination** in any slice of this record: a MIDI track whose output is external, an External Instrument track, and an instrument track sending to a port are refused as destinations, the chooser offers them no Tracks section, and while a track takes a route its MIDI output cannot be set to a port and an External Instrument cannot be put in its slot ("Bass takes MIDI from Keys; routing to a hardware port is not available yet"). The prohibition holds on **every path that can install an external output or an External Instrument on a routed destination, or a route on a track that has one**, not only on `midi.output` and `instrument.external`: applying a track preset (which installs an instrument directly, `crates/engine/src/cmd/track_preset_kit.rs:65`), duplicating a track, undo and redo of whole-session snapshots, changing a track's kind, and loading a hand-edited session. Each such path either refuses with that message before any change or, for a loaded session or a restored snapshot that already holds the combination, keeps the route inert with its load note (decision 8); a command's validation (the engine's edit validation, as for loops) is the backstop for paths that write the session directly. That rule stands until the follow-up record of decision 7 is accepted and built; no interim surface reaches a port.
   - A track is never its own source. A track may be both a source and a destination (a chain of routes); it then follows both sets of rules, and the freeze prohibition of a source (6.3).
6. **Session field.** `Track::midi_from: Option<MidiFrom>`, `MidiFrom { track: TrackId, listen: bool }`, `#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "Option::is_none")]`. While it is `Some`, the track's hardware choice in `midi_input.port` is kept and not used (playback opens no hardware route for the track); `listen` is the route's Listen switch (5.2), saved, `true` for a new route. A separate field, not a `MidiPort` variant, because an older build fails to load an unknown enum variant and loads an unknown field (Context), which the `needs` marker warns about (decision 8).
7. **Commands.** `midi.input` gains `from` and `listen`. `from` with a track id sets the route (Listen on unless `listen` is given); `from: null` clears it and the track takes its kept hardware choice again; a `port` given clears the route and sets that port instead; `listen` alone sets the current route's Listen. Each call is one undo step. Refused, with the reason, before any change: a source or destination outside 1.5 ("<track> cannot send MIDI to other tracks: <reason>", the reason one of "it is a MIDI track", "it sends to an external port", "it holds an External Instrument", "it is a rack", "it is frozen"; "<track> cannot take MIDI from another track: <reason>", the reason one of "it is not an instrument track", "it sends to an external port; routing to a hardware port is not available yet", "it holds an External Instrument; routing to a hardware port is not available yet", "it is a rack"); setting a port output (`midi.output`) or an External Instrument (`instrument.external`) on a track that takes a route; the destination itself; `listen` on a track with no route; a route that would close a loop with any edge of the route graph (`refuse_loop`'s wording, "that route would create a routing loop: Bass → Keys → Bass").
8. **The channel filter.** `midi_input.channel` gates the routed **starts and other non-release events** (a generator writing bass on channel 2 can drive a bass track filtered to channel 2); routed note-offs and pedal-ups always reach the destination's table, which consumes those that match nothing. Changing the filter therefore needs no ownership transition.

### 2. Timing: same block, after the source

1. **A route is an edge of the route graph, and the loop cut is extended.** `EdgeKind::Midi` goes from the source track to the destination track; depth gains the MIDI edges, so a destination renders in a later layer than its source. An edit that closes a loop through any mix of edge kinds is refused (L22, 1.7). For a loaded loop, `solve`'s rounds become: in each component of more than one node, cut (a) the edges into the bus that is the input of the component's first track, else (b) the Key edges into the first track, else (c) the MIDI edges into the first track, "first" being the lowest track index in `Session::tracks` (not the lowest `TrackId`), as today. Every track in a loop receives from inside it through a bus input, a key or a MIDI route, so each round cuts at least one edge and the rounds end. Exact cuts: a pure MIDI loop Keys → Bass → Keys with Keys at index 2 and Bass at index 5 cuts Bass → Keys; a MIDI and key loop (a route Keys → Bass, a key from Bass into an insert on Keys) cuts the key, so the route survives (a disabled key detects on its own input and still sounds; a disabled route silences its destination); a loop through a bus keeps today's cut. A cut MIDI edge is reported dropped like a cut key, the route is inert, and the load note names it: "MIDI from Bass to Keys is disabled: it made a loop (Keys → Bass → Keys)." Key-only and bus loops cut exactly as today.
2. **Same-block delivery with exact offsets.** A destination renders after its source in the same block and receives the source's tap for that block at its offsets. Rejected: one block later (a delay that depends on the block size). The render paths follow ADR 0015 decision 4: the serial path runs layer after layer; a plan with a route renders through the task graph as a keyed plan does (`strip_graph` gains the MIDI edges), where ready tasks are published and no worker waits on a prerequisite. Long chains of routes reduce parallelism and can miss a deadline, which decision 3.4 recovers from at a stated cost. The offline renderer is the same renderer.
3. **The tap.** Each source's event state keeps a fixed tap of 512 events (ADR 0057's per-block chain bound), a monotonic **tap epoch** (u64) and a **sequence** (u64, the count of events the tap has been offered since the event state was made, kept or not). Per block it publishes, as one snapshot read by destinations after the source's layer has finished (the key views' borrow): the epoch, the sequence at the block's start, the number of events offered in the block, the events kept, and whether the block is **damaged**. Rules:
   - **A source barrier empties the tap and raises the epoch before any event of the new epoch is written.** A barrier at the start of a block (an edit, ADR 0064's barriers) and one between blocks (Stop, panic) leave nothing of the old epoch in the tap, so no old-epoch start, pending between-block events included, can be delivered after it.
   - **A damaged block** is one in which the tap could not keep an event it was offered (above ADR 0057's bound; counted, `tap_overflow`), or at whose end the source ran a barrier (P3b's safety reset runs after a block's last event): its epoch is raised at its end and its events are never delivered (decision 3.4).
   - Events the host runs between blocks (ADR 0057 P2's call in which no time passes) are kept in the tap and reach destinations at offset 0 of the next block, ahead of its events, unless a barrier emptied it.
4. **Track order and cycles.** Decided by 2.1: never a run-time order between a destination and its source.
5. **The silent sink.** A source or destination whose instrument slot is empty gets a plan-only instrument cell holding a built-in sink plug-in that accepts every event, renders silence and reports no latency, so the event state, the note table, the chain and the live binding exist exactly as for any instrument cell. It is made only where the track has no instrument cell, never in place of one. Its id is reserved for the plan, never stored in a session or listed in a menu.

### 3. Note ownership across a route

1. **Routed notes enter the destination's table as a source of their own.** A new reserved port, `Source::ROUTE`, with `generation` the destination's **route ordinal**: a u64 kept in the destination's event state, set to 1 when that state is made and increased by one at every route barrier there (3.3). It is never reused within that state; a recreated event state starts again at 1 safely, because its note table starts empty, so no entry of the old state can match it. Being a function of the state's own history, the ordinal is deterministic: two equivalent renders in one process give their tables the same ordinals, and the MIDI state fingerprints, which record `Source::generation` verbatim for held notes, pedals, queued events and controller writers (`source_record`, `crates/mix/src/fingerprint.rs:124`; `crates/mix/src/events/seam.rs:155`), stay equal with no change to those writers, while an entry left under an older ordinal (a retained release of an old route generation) still shows in the fingerprint as an older value than the current one. An unchanged-plan adoption keeps the ordinal (it travels with the event state). No process-global value enters the mix's event state. Matching is the table's: the whole source, then the note id when there is one, else the key, so a routed note never matches the destination's clip, live or audition notes, and a release from before a route barrier never matches a note after it. Same-key anonymous routed notes keep the table's existing re-strike coalescing. Routed events are given to the table in the block, merged in time order with the destination's other events; at one offset: the chase, clips, routed events, then live events, each stream keeping its own order, pedal and note ties included.
2. **Bounded admission and refusal.** Routed starts use the table's ordinary admission: a note-on needs a free note entry (224), a pedal press a free pedal entry (32); releases are reserved (ADR 0059 below the table). Routed events reach the table directly in the block, as clip events do, so the queue's non-release quota does not apply; past the table, the destination's chain input admits by ADR 0057 P3b. A refused start is counted per destination by cause (`route_refused_notes`, `route_refused_pedals`, `route_refused_downstream`, in `EventStatus` and the diagnostics report), its release is consumed, and nothing else changes. Route recovery barriers (3.4) are counted apart (`route_recoveries`), never mixed with refusals. The destination's input label shows the refusals since the last Stop ("From Keys · 12 refused"), and its tip names the causes ("Bass refused 12 notes from Keys: 10 because 224 notes were already held, 2 because its MIDI effects' input was full") and the recoveries ("3 times notes were ended because a block was missed").
3. **The route barrier.** Every way a route stops delivering is a **route barrier** at the destination, applied at offset 0 of the block in which the destination observes it, before that block's routed events:
   - **At a destination with at least one active (unbypassed) MIDI effect stage:** a chain barrier of the destination, exactly ADR 0057's edit barrier and ADR 0064 decision 6 (every note the host delivered ended from its `out` table, every pedal the instrument holds released and none pressed again, every stage reset, a new host epoch), followed by `release_sender` of the route's generation in the table, whose releases reach the host after its epoch change and are swallowed as old-epoch entries, so nothing is sent twice. **This also ends the destination's own notes** (clips, audition) at that moment, because a transforming stage can latch, delay or replace its input and no table can tell which of its outputs came from the route.
   - **At a destination with no active stage:** `release_sender` of the route's generation only (its releases go straight to the instrument; nothing can transform them). The destination's own notes are untouched.
   - **In every case,** the route ordinal increases and the destination's own **tap epoch** increases (its tap is emptied, 2.3), so every track routed from it observes a route barrier in turn (3.4), whether or not this destination needed a full chain barrier. So in A → B → C with no active effect on B and a latching effect on C, deleting A releases B's routed notes selectively and raises B's tap epoch; C then runs a full chain barrier and its latch is reset. Ordinary releases leaving B are never relied on to reset a transformer downstream.
4. **What raises a route barrier, and what the destination acknowledges.** At each block the destination renders, it compares what it last acknowledged (the source's cell identity, the tap epoch, and the sequence) with the source's published snapshot (2.3), and applies a route barrier when any of these holds; the comparison is of monotonic state, so a destination that missed blocks still sees it:
   - the route is set, changed or cleared, or its source is deleted, replaced by another cell or absent from the plan;
   - the tap epoch differs from the acknowledged one: a host barrier of the source (a chain edit or bypass, Stop, the end of a play range, the All MIDI Notes Off action, P3b's safety reset, ADR 0064's barriers: an instance arriving or leaving, a lifecycle generation change, a silent state, a failing block; the source instrument's failure containment), or a damaged block;
   - the snapshot's block-start sequence differs from the acknowledged sequence: the destination missed a block of the source with events in it (a render deadline, a skipped strip), so a release may have been lost;
   - the route's delivery gate closes (5.1, 6.1: the source muted, inactive or frozen; the destination neither armed nor listening);
   - the route was cut as part of a loaded loop (2.1).
   Then it acknowledges the snapshot's identity, epoch and end sequence (block start plus events offered), and delivers the block's kept events **only if the block is not damaged and the gate is open**: a damaged block's events, starts and releases alike, are never delivered (the barrier has already ended everything that route delivered, so its releases have nothing to end and its starts would be notes whose releases may be among the dropped events). Replay: the tap keeps a note-on, fills and drops that note's off in the same block; the block is damaged, so the destination runs the barrier and does not deliver the note-on; the next block is contiguous and nothing is left sounding. A gate that opens starts delivery with the tap's next events (a note already sounding at the source is not delivered, and its later release matches nothing and is consumed). With this, every note a destination accepted from a route is ended by its own release, by Stop, or by a route barrier in the destination's next rendered block after the release was lost, while the host keeps executing; no manual action is needed.
   - **Cost under load, stated.** A missed block with any events in it, even controllers, is enough to trigger recovery, and at a destination with an active MIDI effect recovery ends every note delivered through its chain, clips and live playing included; held keys do not sound again until struck again. So render overload can repeatedly cut held chords on a routed destination, and long chains of routes raise that exposure. The help says so, and the recovery count is shown apart from refusals (3.2).
5. **What does not end routed notes.** A destination's own chain edit ends them as it ends every note of its chain (ADR 0057 2.13). Solo on either track, a parameter change, a change of the channel filter (1.8) and an unchanged-plan adoption end nothing.

### 4. Latency compensation across a route (slice MR4)

1. **The figures.** For every track, `L_out = L_in + L_midi`, where `L_in` is its source's `L_out` when it takes a route, else 0. Two figures are kept, as ADR 0057 decision 7 keeps them: the **requested** recursive figure (from each stage's reported latency), which planning and storage sizing use; and the **provisioned** recursive figure (from each stage's provisioned latency, set when the plan that compensates it is adopted), which rendering, the alignment stage, runtime compensation and key alignment use. A routed event at the destination's render frame `f` belongs to timeline frame `f − L_in` (provisioned). The source instrument's audio latency is never part of a route.
2. **The destination.** Its compensation latency becomes `L_in + L_midi + head + inserts`, `L_in` entering wherever P4 enters `L_midi` and never the note readers' alignment. Its own events (clips, the chase, edit and seek releases, audition) pass an **alignment stage** that delays them by exactly `L_in` render frames before its chain; routed events do not pass it. The stage is host code at the head of the destination's MIDI host, present only while the provisioned `L_in > 0`, with a fixed schedule of 1,024 events: a note-on or a pedal press is admitted only with room for its release, which is reserved; another event while the schedule holds fewer than 768; a refusal is a refusal of the chain's input (ADR 0057 P3b), which the table already handles. Pending events are delayed across loop wraps and locates alike, as a delay line; a host barrier of the destination cancels pending starts with their reserved releases. Rejected: reporting `L_in` without aligning (own clips early by `L_in`); not compensating (routed notes late by `L_in`); reading the destination's clips `L_in` earlier on the timeline (wrong across a loop wrap).
3. **A change of the provisioned route latency is a latency barrier, stronger than a route barrier.** At the adoption that changes a destination's provisioned `L_in`, at offset 0, whether or not the destination has an active MIDI effect:
   1. the alignment stage's pending events are discarded: pending starts with their reserved releases, and pending releases and pedal-ups of notes and pedals already delivered;
   2. the destination's table runs `release_all` (every note and pedal of every source: clips, live, audition, the route), and its host runs a chain barrier (every note it delivered ended, every pedal released, every stage reset, a new epoch), so every note and pedal the destination delivered, its own and the route's, is ended once;
   3. the route ordinal and the destination's tap epoch increase (3.3);
   4. the new delay applies to later events, and at 0 the stage is removed.
   Pinned cases, `L_in` 100 adopted to 0 at frame 10: an own note-on taken at 0 and still pending is never delivered, and the clip's note-off at 20 matches nothing and is consumed; an own note-on taken at −150 (delivered at −50) whose note-off is pending is ended at 10 and the pending note-off discarded; a pedal press pending, and a pedal press delivered with its pedal-up pending, likewise; a delivered note with nothing pending is ended at 10; a routed note sounding is ended at 10. Nothing is reordered: no release can wait behind a delay its start no longer has. The control shows the new value at once; the sound follows at adoption (ADR 0057 decision 7). Cost, stated: a route latency change cuts every note of the destination.
4. **Before P4** every `L_midi` is 0, so every `L_in` is 0 and MR1 to MR3 need none of this. A hosted stage's reported latency may describe its audio rather than its notes (ADR 0064 10.1); the destination inherits that error, and the help says so.

### 5. Hearing, Listen, recording and the destination's own chain

1. **When a routed destination is heard (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation; its control amended 2026-11-27 by delegation, 5.2).** A routed destination plays its route live while it is active and **Record Enable is on or its route's Listen is on**, whether the transport plays, records or is stopped; with both off the route delivers nothing (its gate is closed, 3.4), and the user stops listening by turning Listen off, without removing the route. Arming follows transit's existing rule for hardware MIDI input (heard while armed; Monitor Armed Tracks does not apply to MIDI; Context). **Over the destination's clips:** a heard route sounds on top of the destination's playing clips and takes, because that is what transit does today for a hardware keyboard on an armed instrument track (`native.rs:582`; `strip.rs:177` to `191`); Ableton's Monitor setting never plays both (Auto suppresses the input while the track's clips play, In suppresses the clips; Context), so this record does not claim agreement with Ableton here; it follows transit's own overdub rule. **Recorded:** while armed and the transport records or counts in (5.5). **Played back afterwards:** the destination's clips and takes play as usual, with its route on top while it is armed or Listen is on. After recording a take with Listen still on, the take and the live route both play; the user turns Listen off (one click, one undo step) and disarms the track to hear the take alone.
2. **Listen: a saved route switch (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation, as an amendment of the control terminology of the two choices above).** The first two choices delegated on 2026-11-27 said a routed destination is heard through its monitoring and that export follows the persistent monitoring state. They did not authorize saving Input Monitoring, which an earlier draft of this record did; that design is withdrawn. Instead:
   - **Listen** is part of the route (`MidiFrom::listen`), stored with it, shown attached to the route in the input chooser and on the track's input label ("From Keys · Listen On"), and changing it is an undo step (`midi.input { listen }`).
   - Hardware Input Monitoring and Record Enable keep their runtime-only persistence (never saved, not undo steps). Input Monitoring stays hardware-only: on a routed destination it has no effect (the track has no hardware input). Record Enable arms the routed destination: it records the route and makes it heard while armed, as it does for hardware input.
   - **A new route has Listen on**: a user who routes a generator to a synth expects to hear it and to have it in the export.
   - After reopening a session, the track shows "From Keys · Listen On" or "Listen Off" as saved, with Record Enable and Input Monitoring off; Listen On restores the arrangement's routed sound and its export, Listen Off leaves the destination's clips and takes playing alone until it is armed.
3. **Export and freeze: the saved state decides (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** An offline render (export, bounce, freeze, Capture) renders a route exactly when its Listen is on, and ignores Record Enable: a route heard only because its destination is armed is live input and is not in an export, as any live input. That is the play-equals-export rule for routes: playback with the same routes listened to and no track armed gives the same events at the same frames as an export, under the conditions every export already has (the same effective processing, the source's plug-ins reproducible from their stored state, ADR 0064 10.3; live input not rendered; Low Latency's bypasses not inherited).
4. **Before its chain.** Routed events enter the destination's note table and pass its own MIDI effect chain, then its instrument: a route is the destination's input (and Ableton's documented tap feeds the receiving track before its instrument; Context). A destination that wants the notes raw has an empty chain.
5. **Recording a route is input recording.** While the destination is armed and the transport records (or counts in), every routed event of a non-damaged block that passes its channel filter is captured for its take before the destination's table admits it, as hardware capture records what the mix buffered: a note the destination refused is recorded too. It is placed at its **musical position**, the timeline frame `f − L_in` (4.1), at the output frame the capture uses for that render frame. Takes, punch, loop recording, pickups and the pre-count zone follow the existing MIDI recording rules (ADR 0052 decision 8); the take stores ticks, so a recorded clip's fidelity limits apply (positions to the tick, same-key overlaps as the clip renderer plays them); note ids are not kept. **Timing after the input changes.** The take replays at its musical position. While the destination's audio is delay-compensated to Main (delay compensation on, and no Low Latency bypass on its path), that is also where the route was heard, to the tick: live, a routed note at render frame `f = S + L_in` was compensated by the destination's `L_in` and reached Main at `S + A`; replayed with Listen off or the route cleared, the take's note at `S` reaches Main at `S + A`. Where compensation was off or Low Latency bypassed a path, the live performance was heard displaced by those rules, and the take keeps the musical position, not the displaced timing.
6. **Input recording keeps the offered performance, not a guaranteed copy of the sound.** For a source with free-running or random behavior, a recorded route keeps the notes that were offered live, which Capture MIDI Effect Output (ADR 0057 decision 9; ADR 0064 10.3) cannot, since it renders a new performance offline. Replaying the take through the destination's own stateful or random MIDI effects can sound different from what was heard, as for any recorded input.

### 6. Mute, solo, freeze, bypass, deletion

1. **Source mute stops its routed output; solo never stops a route (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** A source whose own mute is on (its Mute button, its mute automation, or its VCA's mute) delivers nothing to any destination: turning mute on is a route barrier at every destination (3.4), and nothing is delivered while it stays on. Reason: a generator track often has a silent instrument (2.5), so muting it would otherwise do nothing anywhere. Solo exclusion is not mute for this rule: a source silenced only because another track is soloed keeps feeding its destinations, so soloing a destination keeps its sources running and a soloed destination keeps hearing its route. A destination's mute and solo act on its audio as today; its route keeps delivering (its instrument still runs). Mute at a hardware-port destination is the follow-up record's (decision 7).
2. **Inactive.** An inactive source is not rendered: its routes take a route barrier and carry nothing until it is active again. An inactive destination is not rendered; its table is voided as today.
3. **Freeze.** A track that is the source of a route cannot be frozen ("Keys sends MIDI to Bass; remove that route or freeze Bass instead"), whether or not it is also a destination; no route can be made from a frozen track. A destination can be frozen: the freeze render keeps the sources of a route whose Listen is on running, transitively through routes and keys (`key_feeders` gains the MIDI edges), their audio cut, so the frozen audio holds the routed notes (a route with Listen off is not rendered, 5.3). While frozen the destination takes nothing from its route (it has no instrument in the plan); unfreezing resumes it. Parity: the frozen audio equals playback under the conditions of 5.3. A session loaded with a route from a frozen track keeps the route, inert, with a load note. Flattening a destination clears its `midi_from` as part of the same undo step; undo restores the instrument track and its route with the saved Listen value. A track referenced as a route source cannot be flattened until those routes are removed.
4. **Bypass.** Bypassing a source stage is a source barrier, so a route barrier at every destination (3.4). Bypassing a destination stage is the destination's chain edit (3.5).
5. **Failure.** A source instrument contained for non-finite output or a reported processing error takes no events until Stop (ADR 0059 decision 2), so its chain takes nothing and its routes carry nothing until Stop (the containment is a source barrier). This is distinct from the source instrument refusing events, which never changes the tap (1.1). A failed hosted stage on the source is silent until Stop (ADR 0064 7.4). A destination's failure is its own containment.
6. **Deleting a source leaves its destinations disconnected (Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation).** Deleting a track leaves `midi_from` naming it on every destination; such a destination is **disconnected**: it takes no routed input (route barrier) and no hardware input, its input label reads "No input" with the tip "The track it took MIDI from was deleted", and its chooser marks the kept hardware choice "(previous)" so one choice restores it. Undo of the deletion brings the track back and the route works again, with its Listen as it was. A session loaded with `midi_from` naming no track is disconnected the same way, with a load note: "Bass took MIDI from a track that is not in this session. Its input is disconnected until you choose one." Deleting a destination needs nothing.

### 7. External MIDI ports: their own record

Routing a chain's output to a hardware MIDI port is the follow-up record **"Routed MIDI to external ports"**, to be written; its issue is filed when the maintainer schedules it. The review of this record settled its direction and found its hard requirements; they are kept here so that record starts from them, and none of them is decided as a design here.

1. **The direction already agreed.**
   - A route reaches a port only through a destination track whose output is an external port (a MIDI track with an external output, an External Instrument track, an instrument track sending to a port): one destination model (ADR 0022 decision 1), one output thread, its "device missing" state, no second path to a port. Such a destination's chain does not run (ADR 0036), so routed events go to the port as they are; a hosted instrument on such a track keeps rendering and getting note-offs only, as today.
   - A muted or solo-excluded external destination sends no routed non-release events and lets routed releases pass, as its clips do today (`crates/playback/src/player/audio/external.rs:190`).
   - The output thread tracks all four pedal kinds (CC 64, 66, 67 and 69) for every origin, not CC 64 alone, so Stop, the panic, a destination change and a route revocation release every pedal it holds before its CC 123.
   - Shared channels: MIDI 1.0 carries no source identity, so when a destination's clips and its route play the same key or move the same pedal on one channel of one port, a release from either ends the other's voice or pedal at the hardware; the output thread does not suppress it. A stated limit.
   - Physical safety is conditional on the device: the host can end what it owns, but on device loss the output thread forgets the port's held state without sending anything (`lose`, `crates/playback/src/player/midi_out.rs:904`), so a powered synth whose connection drops can keep sounding.
   - Timing: routed events stamped for their timeline frame `f − L_in` through the player's frame-to-time conversion at the current speed, with the track's usual offset; an External Instrument destination is late by any excess of `L_in` over the render-ahead time (a causal limit). MIDI clock is not part of a route (ADR 0043).
2. **Requirements the follow-up record must meet.**
   - **Identity before generation.** An event reaches a port on a track's behalf only if it carries that track's identity as the output thread's current occupant; a previous occupant's events are rejected by identity whatever their generation.
   - **Revocation before any scheduled start, with guaranteed capacity.** A route change or barrier is visible to the output thread before it sends any start of the old route, including starts already scheduled for a future deadline, without needing a slot in a ring that can be full (`Producer::push` can refuse even a protected event, `midi_out.rs:115`); its storage is bounded whatever the number of barriers.
   - **A generation or incarnation protocol whose lifetime covers every queued, scheduled and held event** across: outlet recreation by a plan change; output-slot reassignment when `set_destinations` rebuilds its vector (`midi_out.rs:763`); a track leaving external output and re-entering it; an old audio plan still running during a control-side replacement; and session open reusing track ids. This replay, which defeated an earlier draft, must fail: the old outlet on cell C0 at 20; control removes and re-adds A, creates C1 at 21 and the output thread installs it; the old audio plan still runs, a source barrier makes its outlet draw 22, store it in C0 and enqueue an event tagged 22; the output thread finds A by identity and compares 22 with C1's 21; "older" is the only rejection, so the stale event passes.
   - **Nothing process-global in mix state or fingerprints.** The MIDI state fingerprints record `Source::generation` verbatim (`crates/mix/src/fingerprint.rs:124`, `crates/mix/src/events/seam.rs:155`); whatever the follow-up uses at the port stays out of the event state (this record's route ordinal, 3.1, stays as it is).
   - **Eventual release delivery under consumer progress.** A routed release refused by a bounded buffer or the ring is kept and delivered once the output thread drains, never lost while the host keeps executing; a start that cannot be sent in time may be dropped with its release.
   - **Note safety as this record's.** Route barriers, recovery after missed or damaged blocks, and the latency barrier (decisions 3 and 4) reach the port with the same guarantees, and a loop wrap on the destination's timeline does not end routed notes (they belong to their source and route).
   - Deterministic tests with the fake output backend, including the replays above.
3. **Internal routing needs neither the MIDI output thread nor its playback ring**: every route in this record is delivered inside the mix, from the source's tap to the destination's note table.
4. **Until that record is accepted and built,** a routed track cannot send to hardware: a track with an external MIDI output is not a route destination (1.5), the chooser lists no sources for it, and nothing in slices MR0 to MRV reaches a port.

### 8. Session format

- `Track::midi_from` (1.6), written only when set. `Session::needs()` lists `midi_routes` when any track has it, and `Session::KNOWN_NEEDS` gains it; an older build that knows markers warns, drops the field and plays the destination from its kept hardware choice. Alpha: no migration.
- A missing source (disconnected, 6.6), an ineligible source or destination (1.5, a frozen source included) and a route cut from a loop (2.1) each load with the field unchanged, the route inert, no hardware fallback, and a load note naming both tracks; saving such a session writes its canonical serialization unchanged (the same canonical JSON a save of the loaded session produced before, not the input file's whitespace or field order).
- A loaded route whose destination has an external MIDI output (a hand edit, or a build that admits them) keeps the field, the route inert, with a load note: "Bass takes MIDI from Keys, but routing to a hardware port is not available yet."

### 9. The user interface (slice MRU)

1. **The chooser.** The destination's MIDI input chooser (`InputView::popup`) gains, after the ports and before Channel, a "Tracks" section listing every other track in session order, with name and color: an eligible source enabled; one that would close a loop disabled with the tip "Would create a routing loop: Bass → Keys → Bass"; an ineligible one disabled with its reason. A track that cannot be a destination (1.5, a track with an external MIDI output included) shows no Tracks section. Choosing a track sends `midi.input { from }` (Listen on); choosing a port sends `port` (clearing the route); a disconnected destination marks its kept port "(previous)". While a route is set, the chooser shows a "Listen" toggle directly under the chosen track, with the tip "Play the MIDI from Keys on this track, and include it in exports".
2. **On the destination.** The input label reads "From Keys · Listen On" or "From Keys · Listen Off" (" · Ch n" when filtered); the arranger header gains a chip with the same text and an inline Listen toggle, opening the chooser, drawn only while a route is set. With Listen off and the track not armed, the chip and label are dimmed with the tip "Not heard: turn Listen on, or arm the track."
3. **On the source.** The arranger header gains a chip "→ Bass" (or "→ 3 tracks"), destinations in its tip, drawn only while the track feeds a route; clicking selects the first destination. The Devices pane shows the same marker at the end of the MIDI FX group, before the instrument card; its placement and size are for the panel design hand-off (ADR 0057 K2a's `deviceChain` tokens), and until the hand-off places it the marker uses the tokens' smallest strip and every test reads them. A muted source's chip reads "→ Bass (muted)".
4. **States**, amber, with a tip, on the destination's label and chip: "Keys is inactive: no notes arrive"; "Keys is muted: no notes arrive"; "No input" (disconnected, 6.6); "Keys cannot send MIDI: <reason>"; "Route cut on open: it made a loop (Keys → Bass → Keys)"; refusals and recoveries (3.2); frozen: "Frozen: the route plays again when you unfreeze".
5. **Help.** One entry: what is routed, one source per destination, Listen and arming (when a route is heard, that Listen is saved and decides exports, that a heard route plays over the track's clips and takes, and that turning Listen off and disarming after recording plays the take alone), same-block timing, source mute against solo, that routed notes pass the destination's chain and that a route barrier there ends its own notes too, that render overload can repeatedly cut held chords on a routed destination, input recording and how it differs from Capture, freeze rules, that a track sending to a hardware port cannot take a route yet, and that a hosted plug-in's reported latency may not describe its notes.
6. **No interim surface.** No routing controls or routing help appear in the studio until MRV. Before MRV, `midi.input` accepts `from` and `listen` through programmatic entry points, including the control channel and headless CLI/scripts, but does not advertise them in command listings or generated help. Session loading preserves routes and reports invalid routes as decision 8 requires. This visibility gate does not identify or reject callers.

## Out of scope

- Taps before the chain or after a given stage (1.2); several sources into one destination (1.4).
- Racks as sources or destinations, until ADR 0036's rack stage.
- MIDI tracks, tracks with an external output and External Instrument tracks as sources.
- A plug-in's second and later note outputs (ADR 0064 decision 3).
- Routing to external MIDI ports: the follow-up record "Routed MIDI to external ports" (decision 7).
- MIDI clock, transport, MTC and system exclusive over a route (ADR 0043, ADR 0022).
- Sample-exact recording of a route (recorded clips store ticks).
- Automating a route or its Listen, or changing them by MIDI Learn.

## Consequences

- A note-writing plug-in, a built-in MIDI device or a track's clips on one track can drive any number of synths on other tracks. Routed notes do not reach hardware ports until the follow-up record (decision 7).
- A routed destination is heard while armed or while its route's Listen is on; Listen is saved with the route, is undoable, and decides whether the route is in exports and freezes. A heard route plays over the destination's clips and takes, as a keyboard does on an armed track; after recording, turning Listen off and disarming plays the take alone.
- A routed destination renders after its source in each block, ordered like a keyed strip, at the same cost.
- Every routed note is owned by its destination, under a route ordinal; every way a route stops is a route barrier, detected across missed and damaged blocks and propagated to every track downstream. At a destination with active MIDI effects a route barrier also ends the destination's own notes, so render overload can repeatedly cut held chords there.
- A latent MIDI source delays its destinations' compensation and their own clips are aligned to it; a change of that latency cuts every note of the destination.
- Recording an armed destination keeps the routed input as it was offered.
- Muting a source stops its routes; solo never does. A source cannot be frozen; a destination can.
- Deleting a source leaves its destinations disconnected until the user chooses an input.

## Alternatives rejected

- **The choice on the source (an output chooser).** Both reference workstations document the choice on the receiving track (Context).
- **A `MidiPort::Track` variant.** An older build fails to load a session with an unknown variant.
- **Delivering a block later.** A delay that depends on the block size.
- **A route that sounds whatever the destination's state.** No way to stop listening short of removing the route.
- **Saving Input Monitoring for routed destinations.** The same button would have two persistence behaviors, and a saved edit that changes the arrangement's sound would be immune to undo; Listen is a route switch of its own, saved and undoable.
- **Rendering a route heard only because its destination is armed.** Arming is transient recording state; an export would depend on which tracks happened to be armed.
- **Following Ableton's Monitor setting (input suppressed while clips play, or clips suppressed).** transit's existing rule plays an armed track's clips and its keyboard together (overdubbing); a route follows the track's existing rule rather than introducing a second one.
- **A route barrier that releases only the route's notes through a destination's MIDI effects.** A transforming stage can latch, delay or replace them; only a chain barrier ends what it holds.
- **Propagating a barrier downstream as ordinary releases.** A latch downstream ignores them; only the tap epoch tells it to reset.
- **Delivering the kept events of a damaged block after the barrier.** A kept start whose release was dropped in the same block would never be released.
- **A routing table of its own at the destination.** The note table already qualifies by source and is reached by Stop, failure and inactivation.
- **Packing a route serial and a source epoch into the generation.** Two unbounded counters do not pack into 64 bits.
- **Dropping an overflowing release and waiting for Stop.** Leaves a note sounding until the user acts.
- **Keeping pending aligned events at their frames across a latency change.** A release can then precede its pending start.
- **A process-global draw as the note table's generation.** The MIDI state fingerprints record it verbatim, so two equivalent renders with different draw histories would differ.
- **Muting a source leaves its routes running.** A generator track with a silent instrument could not be muted anywhere.
- **Restoring the kept hardware input when a source is deleted.** An armed destination would start listening to a keyboard without being asked.
- **Routed notes after the destination's chain.** The destination's chain would not process its input.
- **Specifying external ports in this record.** Each fix of the hardware-port path during review opened another lifetime hole while routing between tracks stayed settled; the port path gets its own record (decision 7) and routing between tracks ships without waiting for it.
- **No latency alignment, or reporting `L_in` without aligning.** Late routed notes, or early own clips (4.2).

## Choices made without a rule to follow (for the review)

1. One source per destination; fan-in out of scope (1.4).
2. The tap with an empty chain is the mix table's output, so a track's clips and live input can be routed (1.1).
3. Eligible sources and destinations, a track with an external MIDI output not a destination until the follow-up record; a silent sink only where no instrument cell exists (1.5, 2.5).
4. The channel filter gates starts and passes releases (1.8).
5. Same-block delivery through the route graph's layering and the task graph (2.2); the loop cut's priority bus input, key, MIDI (2.1).
6. Routed events at one offset after clips and before live events (3.1).
7. A route barrier at a destination with an active stage is a full chain barrier that ends its own notes too; every route barrier raises the destination's tap epoch (3.3).
8. Recovery by epoch and sequence comparison; damaged blocks delivered not at all (2.3, 3.4).
9. Refusal and recovery counts by cause, shown until Stop (3.2).
10. The alignment stage, its capacity and its delay-line behavior; the latency barrier ending every note of the destination (4.2, 4.3).
11. Recording captures offered routed input at its musical position (5.5).
12. Freezing a source refused; a frozen destination renders the sources of a route whose Listen is on (6.3).
13. The table's route ordinal per event state (3.1).
14. Routing's mix slices after HM3 and behind any ready hosting slice (Slices).

## For the maintainer to confirm or overturn

Decided 2026-11-27 by delegation, each in one sentence:

1. A track that takes MIDI from another track plays it while the track is armed or while the route's Listen switch is on, and turning Listen off stops listening without removing the route (this replaces "Input Monitoring" in the original wording: Listen is a switch of the route, and hardware Input Monitoring and Record Enable stay as they are).
2. Listen is saved with the route and is an undo step, and exports and freezes include a route exactly when its Listen is on, while a route heard only because its track is armed is treated as live input and is not exported.
3. A new route starts with Listen on, so a generator routed to a synth is heard and exported until you turn Listen off.
4. A heard route plays on top of the receiving track's own clips and takes, as a keyboard does on an armed track today, so after recording a take with Listen still on you hear the take and the live route together until you turn Listen off and disarm the track.
5. Muting a source track stops everything it sends to other tracks, while soloing never stops a route.
6. Deleting a source track leaves the tracks it fed with no input until you choose one, rather than switching them back to a keyboard, and undo brings the route back.
7. Routing to hardware MIDI ports is not in these slices: it gets its own record, and until then a track that sends to a hardware port cannot take a route.
