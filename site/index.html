    First start: open Audio Devices, pick your audio interface, and set the buffer size. transit measures its own latency and shows you the number.

    Why transit

    Record and pick your best take

    Record a part again and again. Every take stacks up in its own lane, so you choose the one that plays. Bounce in Place turns a track into audio when you are done.

    Use the plug-ins you own

    Plays CLAP and VST3 plug-ins. Each one loads on its own, so a slow or stuck plug-in cannot freeze the window.

    Instruments and effects included

    Comes with synths such as the Macro Oscillator, Resonator and Modal Voice, a sampler, plus compressor, EQ, reverb and more.

    Low delay, and it shows the number

    On Windows, transit talks to your audio interface directly for low delay, and the Audio Devices window shows how much you really have.

    Also in the box

    • A mixer with buses, sends and sidechains.
    • Automation for volume, pan and plug-in knobs.
    • A piano roll with scale and quantize tools.
    • MIDI controllers that learn: move a knob, and it maps to what you picked.
    • Plays hardware synths and records them.
    • Export to WAV, AIFF and FLAC.
    • Runs on Linux too.

    How we decide

    For the curious: how changes get into transit.

    Measure first

    We change a setting only after measuring it on real hardware, and we publish the numbers.

    Mistakes become checks

    When a bug slips through, we write what happened and add a check so it cannot happen again.

    Decisions are written down

    Every design change gets a short written decision with its evidence, and every change is reviewed by someone trying to break it.

    Known problems are public

    What we know is broken is in the known issues list.
